
## 2470 Appendix

- Daguesse, Louis Jacques Mandé (1787–1851), French inventor. Invented the daguerreotype, the original photographic process.
- Dale, Henry Hallett (1875–1958), British pharmacologist and physiologist. Isolated vitamin B1 and recognized its effect to be similar to that brought about by parasynthetic nerves; Nobel Prize, 1936.
- Dalén, Nils Gustaf (1869–1937), Swedish physicist. Invented automatic gas lighting for unserved lighthouses and railroad signals; Nobel Prize, 1912.
- Dalitz, Richard Henry (1925– ), Australian-born British theoretical physicist. Research on proper- and of mesons and baryons; developed the Dalitz plot, a method of identifying strong and weak decays; proposed the Dalitz model for elementary particles; introduced the Dalitz plot. Fields Medal, 1998.
- Dalli, John (1766–1842), English chemist and physician. Proposed the atomic theory of chemical reactions; developed the law of partial pressures; studied color mixing.
- Dam, Carl Peter Henrik (1895–1976), Danish biochemist and nutritionist. Discovered vitamin K; studied its role in human hemming-disease, Nobel Prize, 1943.
- Dancowes, Peter Victor (1916–1984), British chemical engineer. Proposed the surface-renewal model of liquids.
- Daniell, John Frederic (1790–1845), English physicist and chemist. Invented the Daniell cell.
- Darwin, Charles Robert (1809–1882), English naturalist. Proposed far-reaching theory of evolution of species and theory of natural selection in his Origin of Species.
- Darwin, George Howard (1848–1921), English mathematician and astronomer. Applied detailed mathematical analysis to celestial and geological problems.
- Daubesset, Jean (1916– ), French biologist and medical scientist. Studied the structure and function of the role in transplast membrane transport to receptors. Nobel Prize, 1980.
- Davis, Raymond, Jr. (1914– ), American physiologist. Developed pioneering experiments to detect slow neitrons, and observed delict in their number. Nobel Prize, 2002.
- Davison, Clinton Joseph (1881–1958), American physicist. Studied magnetics, radiant energy, and electricity. Independently of G. P. Thompson, discovered electron diffraction by crystals; Nobel Prize, 1937.
- Davy, Humphry (1778–1829), English chemist. Discovered potassium and sodium; invented the Davy safety lamp for use in coal mines; proposed theoretical explanations of electricity and voltaic action.
- Debye, Peter Joseph Willim (1884–1966), American physicist and chemist. Born in the Netherlands. Worked on dipole moments; established the theory of x-rays in gases; formulated Debye-Huckel theory on the behavior of strong electrolytes; Nobel Prize, 1936.
- Dedekind, Julius Wilhelm Richard (1831–1916), German mathematician. Worked in number theory and analysis. Created the ideal theory of rings, algebraic functions, and ideals; defined real numbers by Dedekind cuts.
- De Duve, Christian René (1917– ), Belgian biochemist and cytologist. Refined centrigue technique for studying cell components; discovered lysosomes; Nobel Prize, 1974.
- de Forest, Lee (1872–1961), American physicist and inventor. Pioneer in radio technology; invented audio amplifier and the four-electrode valve; incorporated the grid into the thermionic valve.
- de Genes, Pierre-Gilles (1932– ), French physicist. Applied physical principles to the study of complex systems, including liquid crystals and polymers; Nobel Prize, 1991.
- de Haas, Wander Johannes (1888–1960), Dutch physicist. Demonstrated the magnetic effect of electrons, working on production of extremely low temperatures by adiabatic demagnetization; with P. Van Alphen, discovered de Haas-Van Alphen effect.
- Dahnemist, Hans Georg (1922– ), German-born Austrian physicist. Developed the Penning trap, which uses magnetic and electric fields to hold ions in a small volume, used to take measurements of atomic properties; Nobel Prize, 1989.
- Deisenhofer, Johanna (1943– ), German chemist. With R. Huber and M. Hartman, elucidated the structure of a bacterial protein that performs photosynthesis: Nobel Prize, 1988.
- Delbrück, Max (1906–1981), German-born American biologist. Pioneered in molecular biology, the interaction of genetic viruses with cells; Nobel Prize, 1969.
- Deligne, Pierre René (1943– ), Belgian mathematician. Wrote and completed work on proving the Riemann hypothesis, which is to find functions—work which brought together algebraic geometry and algebraic number theory. Fields Medal, 1978.
- Democivus, Abraham (1665–1748), French-born English mathematician and astronomer. Wrote treatises on expansions of trigonometric expansions; proposed methods to approximate functions of large arguments, and the concept of the normal distribution curve.
- De Morgan, Augustus (1806–1871), English mathematician. Wrote textbooks and treatises on arithmetic, algebra, and trigonometry; formulated the De Morgan theorem.
- Desargues, Gérard (1591–1662), French mathematician. A founder of modern geometry; proposed the theory of involution and transversals.
- Desargues, René (1596–1650), French mathematician. Originated conic, coordinates, and geometry.
- De Sitter, Willem (1872–1934), Dutch astronomer. Worked on the theory of Einstein's theory to the problem of the universe; computed the size of the universe.
- de Vries, Hugo (1848–1935), Dutch botanist. Suggested the mutation theory of evolution.
- Dewar, James (1842–1923), British chemist. Made studies of low-temperature physics; first to liquefy hydrogen; invented the Dewar vacuum flask.
- Dick, George Frederick (1881–1967), American physicist and bacteriologist. Isolated scald fever pathogen, and developed Dick test for streptococcus.
- Dicke, Robert Henry (1916–1997), American physicist. Developed new relativistic theory of gravitation with C. Niss; investigated cosmic blackbody radiation; worked on development of radar.
- Diezel, Otto Paul Hermann (1870–1954), German engineer. Invented the compressed-gas-ignition engine (diesel synthesis); worked on stereo chemistry; discovered carbon suboxide. Nobel Prize, 1950.
- Diesel, Rudolf (1882–1973), German inventor. Designed and built the diesel engine.
- Deisenhofer, Johann (1943– ), German chemist. With R. Huber and M. Hartman, elucidated the structure of a bacterial protein that performs photosynthesis; Nobel Prize, 1988.
- Dirichlet, (2d century n.c.), Greek mathematician. Contributions to the theory of cones and geometry.
- Diophantus of Alexandria (3d century), Greek mathematician. Known as the father of algebra; the first to use conventional algebraic notation.
- Dirac, Paul Adrien Maurice (1902–1984), English physicist. Worked in quantum mechanics; his theory of negative-energy holes predicted the existence of the positron; Nobel Prize, 1933.
- Dirichlet, Carl Gustav Lejeune (1805–1859), German mathematician. Applied higher analysis to the theory of numbers; work on definite integrals.
- Diursanti, Carl (1923– ), American-born American chemist. Synthesized the first oral-contraceptive.
- Dobzhansky, Theodosius (1900–1975), Russian-born American zoologist. Developed the basic mechanisms of heredity and variation through studies of Drosophila.
- Dohrn, Peter C. (190– ), Australian-born American immunologist. Collaborated with R. M. Zinkernagel in the discovery of specificity of cell-mediated immune defense; Nobel Prize, 1996.
- Dolsy, Edward Adelbert (1893–1986), American biochemist. Isolated a protein that is essential to important to human health, such as some compounds and vitamins; Nobel Prize, 1943.
- Dorsch, Gerhard (1893–1964), German biochemist. Discovered sulfinamide antibiotics, the first synthetic microbial of broad clinical usefulness; Nobel Prize, 1937.
- Donsadon, Simon Kiron (1957– ), British mathematician. Worked on topology of four-dimensional and showed that there exist exotic manifolds that are topologically equivalent to standard Euclidean four-space. Fields Medal, 1992.
- Donati, Giovanni Battista (1826–1873), Italian astronomer. Studied stellar spectra; discovered six sunspots.
- Donnan, Frederick George (1870–1956), Irish chemist born in England. Research in chemical kinetics; originated the Donnan theory of membrane equilibrium.
- Doppler, Christian Johann (1803–1853), Austrian physicist and mathematician. Formulated Doppler's principle, relating the frequency of wave motion to velocity; described the Doppler effect.
- Douglass, Jesse (1897–1965), American mathematician. Solved the Plateau problem (the problem of determining the existence of a minimal surface with a given space curve; also studied the same problem with singularities). Fields Medal, 1936.
- Draar, Frank Donald (1930– ), American astronomer. Research on solar system, 21-centimeter radio band, search for extraterrestrial intelligence (introduced Drake equation), and radio telescope development.
- Draper, Henry (1837–1882), American scientist. Research in spectroscopy; the Draper catalog is named in his honor.
- Drinfeld, Vladimir Gershonovich (1954– ), Ukrainian mathematician. Worked in algebraic geometry, number theory, and the theory of quantum groups; proved a special case of the Langlands conjecture. Fields Medal, 1990.
- Drude, Paul Karl Ludwig (1863–1906), German physicist. Attempted to correlate and account for optical phenomena; developed the chemical properties of substances; developed the theory of the properties of metals based on free electrons treated as a gas.
- Dudgeon, William (1872–1935), American physicist and radiologist. Developed treatment of cancer by radioisotopes and x-rays; with F. L. Hunt, discovered Duncan-Hunt law.
- Du Bois-Reymond, Emil (1818–1896), German physicist. Developed Fowler–DuBois–Riedel theory of living matter; formulated an equation for emission of radiation.
- DuBridge, Lee Alvin (1901–1994), American physicist. Developed Fowler–DuBois–Riedel theory of living matter; formulated an equation for emission of radiation.
- Ducrocq, Augusto (1860–1940), Italian dermatologist. Discovered Hemophilius ducreyi, the agent of chancroid.
- Dufay, Charles François de Cisternay (1688–1739), French chemist. Discovered potash and oxalogue types of electricity.
- Dulbecco, Renato (1914– ), Italian-born American virologist. Developed techniques for studying animal viruses; investigated interaction between devirous, cells, and tumor viruses and genetic material; Nobel Prize, 1975.
- Du Long, Pierre Louis (1758–1838), French chemist and physicist. With A. P. Petit, formulated the law of the constant of atomic heats; developed the Du Long formula for heat value of fuels.
- Dumas, Jean Baptiste (1800–1884), French chemist. Research on organic compounds; determined many atomic weights.
- Dürer, Albrecht (1471–1528), German painter, graphic artist, and mathematician. Work on scientific perspective and mathematical proportion.
- Du Vignaud, Vincent (1920–1978), American biochemist. Synthesized a polyphasic hormone; cofounders; worked on other biologically important sulfur compounds; Nobel Prize, 1955.
