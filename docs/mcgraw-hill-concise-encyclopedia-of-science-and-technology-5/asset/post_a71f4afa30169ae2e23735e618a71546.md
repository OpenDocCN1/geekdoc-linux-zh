- **Northrop, John Howard** (1891–1987), American biochemist. Isolated several enzymes and proved them to be proteins: developed the first bases and viruses. Nobel Prize, 1946.

- **Novikov, Sergei Pavlovich** (1903– ), Russian mathematician. Worked in algebraic topology; proved the topological invariance of the Poincaré homology group and the homology of Thom spaces. Fields Medal, 1970.

- **Noyce, Robert Norton** (1927–1990), American physical, electronic engineer and inventor. Participated in the invention of the integrated circuit.

- **Noyori, Ryoji** (1939– ), Japanese chemist. Developed chiral catalyzed hydrogenation reactions; Nobel Prize, 2001.

- **Nurse, Paul M.** (1949– ), British biologist. Identified, cloned, and characterized a key regulator of the cell cycle, cyclin-dependent kinase; Nobel Prize, 2001.

- **Nüsselt, Ernst Karl Wilhelm** (1822–1897), German mechanical engineer and physicist. Used dimensional analysis to derive functional form of solutions to equations for heat flux in a flowing fluid.

- **Nüsslin-Volhard, Christiane** (1942– ), German developmental biologist. Using Drosophila, she and Eric Wieschaus generated and classified a small number of genes that are important in determining the body plan and the formation of body structures, which control early development. Nobel Prize, 1995.

- **Nyquist, Harry** (1889–1976), Swedish-born American physicist and engineer. His work on conditions necessary to keep feedback-controlled circuits stable; determined Nyquist rate for communication channels.

- **Ochoa, Severo** (1905–1993), Spanish-born American biochemist. Discovered a bacterial enzyme that synthesizes ribonucleic acid from nucleoside diphosphates; first to synthesize a ribonucleic acid; Nobel Prize, 1959.

- **Ockham, William of** (ca. 1284–1347), English philosopher and theologian. Developed nominalist school of thought; postulated Ockham's razor.

- **Oersted, Hans Christian** (1777–1851), Danish physicist, chemist, and politician. Discovered that electric current produces a magnetic effect around it and that the magnetic force is inversely proportional to the distance squared.

- **Ohm, Georg Simon** (1787–1854), German physicist. Discovered Ohm's law relating electrical resistance to voltage and current.

- **Olah, George A.** (1927– ), Canadian-born American chemist. Discovered new reaction; Nobel Prize, 1994.

- **Olbers, Heinrich Wilhelm Matthiass** (1758–1840), German astronomer. Described two methods for determining parallax and angular diameter of the Sun. Olbers' paradox.

- **Onsager, Lars** (1903–1975), Norwegian-born American chemist. Laid the foundation of irreversible thermodynamics; contribution to theories of electrolytes, hydrodynamics, and cooperative phenomena; Nobel Prize, 1968.

- **Oort, Jan Hendrik** (1900–1992), Dutch astronomer. Research on the structure and dynamics of the galactic system; investigated the origin of comets.

- **Oppenheimer, J. Robert** (1904–1967), American theoretical physicist. Researched properties of the strong nuclear force; Manhattan Project; Nobel Prize, 1949.

- **Or, John Boyd** (1880–1986), Scottish physiologist and nutritionist. Work on animal nutrition; pioneer in science of human nutrition; Nobel Prize, 1949.

- **Osheroff, Douglas D.** (1945– ), American physicist. Studied with D. Lee and C. C. Richland, discovered superfluidity in helium-3; Nobel Prize, 1996.

- **Ostwald, Friedrich Wilhelm** (1853–1932), German chemist born in Latvia. Research on affinity and mass action; discovered the Ostwald dilution law; worked on the catalytic oxidation of ammonia; Nobel Prize, 1909.

- **Otto, Nikolaus August** (1832–1891), German inventor. Built the first four-stroke internal combustion engine.

- **Page, James** (1814–1899), English surgeon and pathologist. Studied pathology of the uterus; developed Page's test for detecting carcinoma in the female genital tract.

- **Palade, George Emil** (1912– ), Rumanian born American cytologist. Improved electron microscope technique; developed cell fixation and central techniques to study cell membranes; discovered ribosomes; Nobel Prize, 1974.

- **Papanicolaou, George Nikolaos** (1883–1962), Greek-American cytologist and anatomist. Described the papanicolaou test used in diagnosis of uterine cancer; Nobel Prize, 1949.

- **Pappus, Alexandros** (ca. 3d–4th century), Greek mathematician. Wrote Mathematical Collection; an account of Greek geometry; formulated Pappus's theorem.

- **Parsal, Philippus Aureolus, real name Theophrastus Bombastus von Hohenheim** (1493–1541), Swiss physician. Emphasized use of chemical medicines; discovered that diseases are not just caused by specific viruses but that humoral imbalances are important; developed the tripartite system of medicine.

- **Pâté, Ambroise** (1549–1590), French surgeon. Advocated the treatment of wounds by tying arteries; advocated ligatures rather than cauterization; proposed new methods for amputations and for the care of burn patients; wrote a manual of surgery.

- **Parkinson, James** (1815–1842), English physician. Described Parkinson's disease; Nobel Prize, 1986.

- **Parseval, Jean Châtes, Marc Antoine** (1836–1836), French mathematician. Introduced an important formula that now bears his name as Parseval's theorem is derived.

- **Pascal, Blaise** (1623–1662), French mathematician. Discussed Pascal's theorem, and proved Pascal's relation to the geometry of conics; formulated Pascal's law, relating to the pressure of a fluid; invented Pascal's triangle and the calculation of probability; developed the Pascal's wheel.

- **Paschen, Louis Carl Heinrich Friedrich** (1865–1947), German physicist. Established Paschen's law; with C. R. Black, discovered Paschen-Back effect; verified predictions of relativistic fine structure made by Bohr; Nobel Prize, 1902.

- **Pasteur, Louis** (1822–1895), French biologist. Founder of microbiology; discovered the role of bacteria in fermentation; discovered anaerobic bacteria; developed the pasteurization process; demonstrated the efficacy of vaccination, especially for rabies.

- **Patterson, Arthur Lindo** (1902–1966), New Zealand-born American crystallographer. Developed Patterson-Harker method of x-ray diffraction analysis of crystal structures; Nobel Prize, 1946.

- **Pauli, Wolfgang** (1913–1958), German physicist. Discovered the Pauli exclusion principle, stating that no two particles can have the same quantum state; Nobel Prize, 1945.

- **Pauli, Wolfgang** (1900–1958), Austrian-born American physicist. Worked on quantum theory; formulated the Pauli exclusion principle; contributed to nuclear structure and chemical bonding; Nobel Prize, 1945.

- **Pavlov, Ivan Petrovich** (1849–1936), Russian physiologist. Discovered the nerve fibers affecting heart action and the secretory nerves of the digestive glands; used conditioned reflexes; Nobel Prize, 1904.

- **Pickering, Edward Charles** (1846–1919), American chemist. Pioneered the meridian photometer; pioneered in stellar spectroscopy.

- **Pierce, George Washington** (1812–1956), American physicist and electrical engineer. Devised the standard units of electrical communication; developed the concept of rational engineering.

- **Peltier, Jeanne** (1785–1845), French physicist. Discovered the Peltier effect, the production of a temperature gradient at the junction of two different conductors when current is passed through them.

- **Petroche, Jeanne** (1914–1997), American chemist. Pioneered the development of a variety of new organic compounds that have made possible the preparation of new polymers and other chemical substances.

- **Planck, Max Karl Ernst Ludwig** (1858–1947), German physicist. First to state the law that all matter consists of quanta, and the law that radiation can be emitted only in discrete packets of energy. Won the Nobel Prize in Physics for his work on the laws of radiation.

- **Planck, Gaston** (1834–1889), French physicist. Co-discovered the Law of Inverse Square for electricity and magnetism. Showed that electric and magnetic fields could not penetrate the interior of a conductor.

- **Plateau, Joseph Antoine Ferdinand** (1801–1883), Belgian physicist. Experimented with soap films to study the shape of surfaces; determined the existence of a minimal surface with a given space curve as its boundary.

- **Podolsky, Boris** (1896–1966), Russian-born American physicist. Co-authored in 1935 the paper on the EPR paradox, which led to the development of the Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen paradox.

- **Poggendorff, Johann Christian** (1796–1877), German physicist. Demonstrated that electric currents produce magnetic fields; invented the galvanometer.

- **Poisson, Henri Henri** (1801–1857), French mathematician. Developed a mathematical theory of elasticity and fluid mechanics; discovered Poisson's effect, which describes the relationship between strain in different directions in an elastic object.

- **Poincaré, Henri** (1854–1912), French mathematician. Wrote one of the greatest of the classical expositions of the theory of potential and of the theory of functions.

- **Poiseuille, Jean Lesieur Marie** (1799–1869), French physiologist. Developed the theory of flow of fluids through tubes; known for Poiseuille's law, which describes the relationship between the pressure gradient and the flow rate of a fluid through a tube.

- **Poisson, Simon Denis** (1781–1840), French mathematician. Made major contributions to the mathematics of statistics and probability, and to the theory of differential equations. Developed the Poisson distribution.

- **Politzer, H. David** (1949–), American physicist. Discovered a way of cooling atoms to near absolute zero; co-discoverer of laser cooling and trapping of atoms. Nobel Prize in Physics, 1997.

- **Poloni, John C.** (1929–), Canadian chemist. Studied radiochemistry; discovered the elements Rn and Fm, and their isotope Rn-211, which is used in the treatment of cancer. 1986 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

- **Pomeranchuck, Isaak Yakovlevich** (1918–1966), Russian physicist. Pioneered research into nuclear reactions involving heavy particles and high energies. Made a major contribution to the theory of the weak interaction.

- **Pons, Jean Louis** (1761–1831), French astronomer. Made the first detailed observations of the transverse nature of light.

- **Porter, George** (1920–2006), British chemist. Made major contributions to the understanding of enzymes and the development of enzyme inhibitors. 1998 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

- **Porter, Rodney Robert** (1917–1985), British physicist. Discovered the effect of the nuclear spin on the magnetic moment of a nucleus. 1960 Nobel Prize in Physics.

- **Poulet, Cecil Frank** (1905–1969), British chemist. Made major contributions to the understanding of catalysis and the development of new catalysts. 1953 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

- **Prandtl, Ludwig** (1875–1953), German engineer. Developed the theory of boundary layers in fluid dynamics; also known for his work on turbulence. 1955 Nobel Prize in Physics.

- **Predt, Fritz** (1880–1953), Austrian chemist. Discovered several important reactions in inorganic chemistry. 1931 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

- **Prelog, Vladimir** (1906–1998), Yugoslavian-born chemist. Developed an important new method of preparing chiral compounds. 1991 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

- **Priestley, Joseph** (1733–1804), English chemist. Discovered oxygen, carbon dioxide, ammonia, nitrous oxide, and sulfur dioxide. Showed that plants give off oxygen in sunlight. 1774 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

- **Proust, Jean Baptiste** (1789–1850), French chemist. A major contribution to organic chemistry and to the understanding of chemical stoichiometry. 1850 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

- **Raman, Chandrasekhara Venkata** (1888–1970), Indian physicist. Investigated the scattering of light by molecules; discovered the Raman effect. 1930 Nobel Prize in Physics.

- **Ramsey, William** (1852–1931), British chemist. Discovered the noble gases; developed the fractional distillation of liquid air. 1904 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

- **Ramsden, Jesse** (1735–1800), English instrument maker. Developed a more accurate form of the micrometer screw; improved the telescope. 1800 Nobel Prize in Physics.

- **Raman, Norman Foster** (1915–), American physician. Discovered a new method for measuring the diffusion of molecules across cell membranes. 1983 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

- **Ranke, William John Macorn** (1820–1872), Scottish engineer. Contributed to the theory of the steam engine and to the development of the steam turbine. 1872 Nobel Prize in Physics.

- **Raulot, François Marie** (1830–1901), French chemist. Studied the physical properties of liquids and solutions. 1901 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

- **Rauthe, Martin Heinrich** (1939–1983), German physicist. Developed a new method for detecting the presence of a gravitational field. 1983 Nobel Prize in Physics.

- **Ray or Ryle, Lord** (1919–2006), British astronomer. Discovered the existence of radio galaxies; made major contributions to radio astronomy. 1977 Nobel Prize in Physics.

- **Riccati, Jacopo Francesco** (1743–1788), Italian mathematician. Developed the Riccati equation, a second-order nonlinear differential equation. 1788 Nobel Prize in Mathematics.

- **Rice, Frederick** (1915–1998), American chemist. Studied the theory of chemical kinetics; discovered the Rice-Herzfeld mechanism. 1998 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

- **Reynolds, Osborne** (1842–1912), British engineer. Developed a method for the determination of the Reynolds number, a dimensionless quantity used in fluid mechanics. 1912 Nobel Prize in Physics.

- **Richardson, Theodore William** (1868–1928), British physicist. Investigated the properties of cathode rays; discovered the electron. 1928 Nobel Prize in Physics.
