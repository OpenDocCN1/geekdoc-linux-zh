
## Appendix 2469

- Chaplygin, Segel Aleksandrovich (1869–1942), Russian physicist, engineer, and mathematician. Made contributions to fluid mechanics, particularly aerodynamics
- Chapman, Sydney (1888–1970), English mathematician and physicist. Discovered (independently of D. Enskog) gaseous thermal diffusion; studied the daily variations of the geomagnetic field and magnetic storms
- Châtelet, Jean Antoine Claude Comte de Châtelet (1756–1832), French chemist. Wrote on technical chemistry; introduced the metric system about the Revolution
- Charcot, Jean Martin (1825–1893), French neurologist. Director of the Salpêtrière clinic, where he made systematic clinical studies of chronic nervous disorders, including cerebrospinal disease
- Charles, Jacques (1743–1823), French physicist, chemist, and inventor. Formulated Charles' law, relating gas volume to pressure
- Chapnik, Georges (1924– ), French physicist. Invented the multiple proportional chamber, used as a detector in high-energy physics experiments; Nobel Prize, 1992
- Chebyshev, Pafnuty Lvovich (1821–1894), Russian mathematician. Research on convergence of Taylor series, prime numbers, probability theory, quadratic forms, and integral theory
- Chladni, Ernst Florens Friedrich von (1756–1827), German physicist. Studied Chladni's figures and used them to study vibrations of solid plates
- Christofel, Elwin Bruno (1825–1900), Swiss mathematician. Worked in hyperbolic analysis, geometry, mathematical physics, and geodesy
- Chu, Ching-Wu (1911– ), Chinese-born American physicist. Initiated the French concept of magnetic motion in molecules in transition from one energy level to another
- Chu, Steven (1948– ), American chemist. Developed a system of opposed laser beams to cool atoms to extremely low temperatures, and a magnetic trap to capture them; Nobel Prize, 1997
- Ciechanow, Aaron (1947– ), Israeli medical doctor and scientist. Discovered ubiquitin-mediated protein degradation; Nobel Prize, 2004
- Cleriavart, Alexis Claude (1713–1765), French mathematician. Studied the shape of the moon, excluding Clairaut's theorem; made astronomical calculations concerning Halley's comet
- Clairaut, Alexis Claude (1713–1765), French mathematician. Studied the shape of the moon, excluding Clairaut's theorem; made astronomical calculations concerning Halley's comet
- Claisen, Ludwig (1853–1930), German organic chemist. Developed Claisen condensation; contributed to understanding of tautomerism; worked on rearrangement of allyl and aryl ethers into phenols
- Clapeyron, Benoît Paul Émile (1799–1864), French engineer. Developed P. L. S. Carnot's concept of a universal function of temperature
- Clarke, Alexander Ross (1828–1914), British geodesist. Worked on the triangulation of the British Isles; proposed Clarke ellipsoids as geodetic standards
- Claude, Albert (1899–1983), American cytologist, born in Luxembourg. Pioneered in applying electron microscopy to cell studies and in using centrifugation to separate cell components; Nobel Prize, 1964
- Clausius, Rudolf Julius Emanuel (1822–1888), German physicist. A founder of thermodynamics; worked out the Clausius-Clapeyron equation for the universal temperature function
- Clebsch, Rudolf Friedrich Alfred (1833–1862), German mathematician. Contributions to theory of invariants and mathematical physics
- Cockcroft, John Douglas (1897–1967), English physicist. With E. T. S. Walton, split nucleus by bombarding them with accelerated protons
- Cohen, Paul Joseph (1934– ), American mathematician. Proved that the axiom of choice is independent of the other axioms of set theory and that the continuum hypothesis is independent of the axiom of choice; a result with profound implications for the foundations of mathematics; Fields Medal, 1966
- Cohen, Stanley (1922– ), American biochemist. With R. Levison. Materials, landamark's study of nerve growth factor and its functions; Nobel Prize, 1986
- Cohen-Tannoudji, Claude (1933– ), French physicist. Helped develop methods to cool and trap atoms with laser light, and explained how atoms could be cooled to temperatures lower than the previously calculated theoretical limits; Nobel Prize, 1997
- Cohn, Ferdinand Julius (1828–1898), German botanist. A founder of bacteriology; did research in plant physiology; first to classify bacteria according to their cell and spore structures
- Cole, Kenneth Stewart (1900–1984), American physiologist. Research on structure and function of living cell membranes and nerve membranes in particular, concentrating on electrical approach; with brother, R.H. Cole, introduced Cole-Cole plot of dielectric behavior
- Cole, Robert Hugh (1914–1990), American chemist and physicist. Research on dielectric properties of matter and intermolecular forces; with brother, K.S. Cole, introduced Cole-Cole plot of dielectric behavior
- Collins, Samuel Cornette (1898–1984), American engineer. Invented Collins helium liquefier
- Compton, Arthur Holly (1892–1962), American physicist. Discovered the Compton effect of x-rays; studied cosmic rays; helped develop the atomic bomb; Nobel Prize, 1927
- Conant, James Bryant (1893–1978), American chemist. Studied free radicals, homophilic and chlorophilic; contributed to atomic energy de-zonization
- Condon, Edward Uller (1902–1974), American physicist. Contributed to the Franck-Condon principle, by extending and giving quantitative treatment to E. Franck's concept of magnetic motion in molecules in transition from one energy level to another
- Connes, Alain (1947– ), French mathematician. Did foundational work on the theory and application of operator algebras, particularly von Neumann algebras; Fields Medal, 1982
- Coolidge, William David (1873–1975), American physicist. Invented Coolidge tube; discovered method for melting tungsten strong and ductile
- Cooper, Leon N. (1930– ), American physicist. Showed that electrons could form Cooper pairs; with J. R. Schrieffer and J. Bardeen, formulated a theory of superconductivity; Nobel Prize, 1972
- Copernicus, Nicolaus (1473–1543), Polish (Prussian) astronomer. Proved that the Copernican theory, with the Sun as the center of planetary orbits
- Corby, Elias James (1828– ), American chemist. Developed theories and methods of organic chemical synthesis that have made possible the production of a wide variety of complex biologically active substances and useful chemicals; Nobel Prize, 1947
- Cort, Carl Ferdinand (1896–1984), and Cort, Gerty Theresia Radetz (1896–1957). Czechoslovak-born American biochemists. Discovered an enzymatic mechanism of glucose-phosphoryl inversion mechanism and the effects of hormones on this mechanism; Nobel Prize, 1947
- Cortot, Gasparel Gustavo (c. 1792–1943), Italian physicist. Contributed to theoretical and applied mechanics; clarified and supplied new theories of work and kinetic energy; devised Cortois acceleration
- Cormack, Allan Macleod (1924–1998), American physicist. Contributed to the development of computerized axial tomography; Nobel Prize, 1979
- Cornell, Eric Alvin (1961– ), American physicist. Made major contributions to the first time in producing Bose-Einstein condensates in a dilute gas of alkali (rubidium) atoms, and carried out studies of the novel properties of condensates, including studies of collective excitations and vortex formation in condensates; Nobel Prize, 2001
- Correr, George Washington (1889–1981), American medical biologist. Contributed to an understanding of anatomical details of menstrual cycle and functions of estrogen and progesterone
- Cotton, John Jacob Wacup (1917– ), Australian-born British chemist. Pioneered stereochemistry of enantio-catalized reactions; Nobel Prize, 1975
- Cottni, Marie Alfred (1941–1902), French physicist. Used a laser spectroscopy determination of intensities in interstellar phenomena
- Coster, Dirk (1889–1950), Dutch physicist. Worked in x-ray spectroscopy, and of heavy, discovered Hartman; with R. Krong, discovered Coster-Kroning transitions
- Cotrell, Frederick Gardner (1877–1948), American medical scientist. Developed the Cottell process for precipitation of particles from gas; researched nitrogan and nitrogen fixation, liquefaction of gases, and recovery of rare gases
- Coulomb, Charles Augustin de (1736–1806), French physicist. Formulated Coulomb's law of electric forces
- Courant, Richard (1888–1972), German-born American mathematician. Research in geometric function theory, differential equations of mathematics physics, and transition by limiting processes from finite difference equations to differential equations
- Cournard, André Frédéric (1895–1988), French-born American physicist. Studied normal and abnormal human cardiovascular and pulmonary function; Nobel Prize, 1956
- Cowan, Clyde L. Jr. (1919–1974), American physicist. With F. Reines, made first detection of the neutrino, a fundamental subatomic particle
- Craigs, James Mason (1839–1917), American chemist. With C. Freidel, discovered the Freidel-Craigs reagent, and with other chemists the Freidel-Craigs reaction which involves aluminum chloride as a catalyst
- Cram, Donald J. (1919–2001), American chemist. Contributed to the field of molecular recognition, by developing new methods to synthesize structures that mimic the action of biological molecules; Nobel Prize, 1988
- Cramer, Gabriel (1704–1752), Swiss mathematician. Contributed to Cramer's rule for solving linear equations
- Crick, Francis Harry Compton (1916–2004), English molecular biologist. With J. D. Watson, proposed a double-helix structure for deoxyribonucleic acid molecule; Nobel Prize, 1962
- Cronstedt, Axel Fredrik. Born (1772–1765), Swedish mineralogist. Discovered nickel; developed a chemical classification system for minerals
- Crookes, William (1832–1919), English physicist and chemist. Made major contributions to study electrical discharges in high vacuum, and to radium discovery; thallium
- Crutzen, Paul J. (1933– ), Dutch chemist. Demonstrated that nitrogen oxides react catalytically with ozone, thus accelerating the rate of reduction of the ozone layer; Nobel Prize, 1995
- Curie, Marie, born Marya Sklodowska (1867–1934), Polish physicist and chemist in France. Explained of radioactivity, co-discoverer of radium, and first to separate polonium; Nobel Prize, 1903 and 1911
- Curie, Pierre (1859–1906), French chemist and physicist. Co-discoverer of radium; formulated the Curie point, relating magnetic properties and temperature of a substance; discovered the piezoelectric effect; Nobel Prize, 1903
- Curti, Robert F. Jr. (1933– ), American chemist. Made major contributions to the discovery of fullerenes; Nobel Prize, 1996
- Cushing, Harvey (1869–1930), American surgeon. Developed new techniques in neurosurgical techniques to reach on function and diseases of the pituitary gland
- Currier, Georges Léopold Christian Frédéric Dagobert, Baron (1769–1838), French naturalist. Made a detailed classification of the animal kingdom; wrote on comparative anatomy
