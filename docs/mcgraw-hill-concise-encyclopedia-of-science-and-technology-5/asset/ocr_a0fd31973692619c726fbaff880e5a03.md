# 2468  Appendix

+   *   **Bravais, Auguste** (1811–1863), French physicist. Studied relationships between crystal form and structure; derived Bravais lattices.
+   *   **Bratt, Gregory** (1899–1911), Russian-born American chemist. Research and synthesis of inorganic and actinide electromagnetics, hyperfine structure, and ionophosphorescence.
+   *   **Bremen, Sydney** (1927– ), South African-born British molecular biologist. Established the *namel* *Caenorhabditis elegans* as an experimental model system for studying genetic regulation of cell division, cell speciation, and organ development in multicellular animals. Also credited with the identification of messenger ribonucleic acid (mRNA). Nobel Prize, 1963.
+   *   **Bruer, Josef** (1842–1925), Austrian neurologist. Established a treatment for neuromotor disorders.
+   *   **Brester, David** (1781–1868), Scottish physician. Formulated a law on polarization of light; codesigner, with E. B. Biot, of Biot-Savart laws.
+   *   **Branchton, Charles Julien** (1783–1864), French mathematician. Proved Bézout's theorem.
+   *   **Brigman, Percy Williams** (1882–1961), American physicist. Worked in high-pressure physics, thermodynamics of liquids. Nobel Prize, 1946.
+   *   **Briggs, Henry** (1561–1631), English mathematician. Prepared logarithmic tables (later known as common logarithms); devised sophisticated interpolation techniques.
+   *   **Bright, Richard** (1789–1858), English physician. Made biochemical study of disease; researched Bright's disease of the kidney.
+   *   **Briot, Léon** (1869–1969), French physician. With G. Wantz and H. A. Kramers, devised the Wantz-Kramers-Briot method, originally conceived of Brillouin zones.
+   *   **Brioloi, Louis Marcel** (1854–1948), French physician. Work on cryosurgery; developed methods and uses, radiotreatment, and relativity.
+   *   **Brini, Johann August** (1849–1925), Swedish engineer. Invented the Brixton machine to measure the hardness of alloys and metals in terms of the Brixton scale.
+   *   **Brockhouse, Bertram Norfleet** (1918–2003), Canadian physicist. Developed slow neutron spectroscopy techniques for studying dynamics of atoms in solids and liquids. Nobel Prize, 1994.
+   *   **Brogile, Louis Victor de** (1892–1987), French physicist. Worked in nuclear physics; first to link wave and corpuscular theory. Nobel Prize, 1929.
+   *   **Brogniou, Thomas John l'Anson** (1875–1929), English mathematician. Showed how the Hessian calculation could be carried out in a manner acceptable to pure mathematicians through use of contour integrals.
+   *   **Brotsted, Johannes Nicolas** (1879–1947), Danish chemist. Researched kinetic properties of ions, catalysis, and nitrile; formulated the Brested theory of acid-base reactions.
+   *   **Brown, Herbert Charles** (1912– ), British-born American chemist. Developed methods for chemical synthesis of inorganic compounds. Nobel Prize, 1979.
+   *   **Brown, Michael S.** (1941– ), American biochemist and geneticist. With Joseph L. Goldstein, discovered low-density lipoprotein receptors and their function in cholesterol metabolism. Nobel Prize, 1985.
+   *   **Browning, John Moses** (1855–1926), American inventor. Invented the Browning machine gun.
+   *   **Bru, Viggo** (1885–1978), Norwegian mathematician. Worked in number theory, introducing what is now known as the B-n, those who made some progress in the resolution of two problems from Goldbach's conjecture and the two prime problem.
+   *   **Branauer, Stephan** (1903–1996), Hungarian-born American chemist. Contributed to surface and colloid chemistry. With P. H. Emmett and E. Teller, developed the Brunauer-Emmett-Teller equation for surface area measurement, BET equation.
+   *   **Brunet, Isambard Kingdom** (1806–1859), English engineer. Constructed great bridges in England; designed important steamships.

+   *   **Bucher, Eduard** (1860–1917), German chemist. Studied anaerobic fermentation of sugars.
+   *   **Buck, Linida** (1947– ), American molecular biologist. Recognized along with Richard Axel for pioneering research of the olfactory system, which led to the discovery of the large gene family encoding for the different odorant receptor proteins on the membrane of olfactory receptor cells. Later independent work clarified the olfactory system, from the odorant's level to the organization of the organ. Nobel Prize, 2004.
+   *   **Buchingham, Edgar** (1867–1940), American biologist. Worked on thermodynamics and dimensional analysis; derived Buckingham-π theorem.
+   *   **Buffon, Georges Louis Leclerc, Comte de** (1707–1788), French naturalist. *Complété* *Histoire Naturelle*, a monumental work on natural history.
+   *   **Bullen, Keith Edward** (1906–1976), Australian applied mathematician. Career in mathematical studies of the Jerky-wave table; with H. Jeffreys, proved one of the Jeffreys-Bullen tables on seismic travel time.
+   *   **Buren, Robert Wilhelm** (1811–1899), German chemist. Discovered, with G. R. Kirchhoff, spectroscope: invented the Bunsen burner. Bunsen and Kirchhoff, and formulated the law of spectrally with E. H. Roscoe.
+   *   **Burbank, Luther** (1839–1926), American horticulturalist. Experimented on crossing, in-breeding of plant varieties.
+   *   **Burnet, F. Macfarlane** (1899–1985), Australian immunologist. Studied with P. B. Medaway, called the baby's tolerance of antigenic substances. Nobel Prize, 1960.
+   *   **Bush, Vannevar** (1890–1974), American electrical engineer. Originated the concept of hypertext.
+   *   **Butenandt, Adolf Friedrich Johann** (1875–1950), German chemist. Researched sex hormones, such as male hormone (depleted). 1939.
+   *   **Buys-Ballot, Christoph Hendrik Didericus** (1817–1890), Dutch meteorologist. Devised a system of storm signals; formulated Buys-Ballot's law for determination of wind direction.
+   *   **Byron, Augusta Ada, Countess of Lovelace** (1815–1852), English mathematician. Daughter of the poet Lord Byron; wrote the first program for Charles Babbage's analytical engine; later described as the first computer programmer.
+   *   **Cailletet, Louis Paul** (1832–1913), French chemist. Researches on gas liquefaction; first to obtain liquid oxygen, hydrogen, nitrogen, and air.
+   *   **Calden, Hugh Longbrough** (1863–1930), English physicist and anatomist. Developed plant-tissue resistance metabolism and continuous-flow calorimetry.
+   *   **Callow, John Michael** (1867–1940), English-Born American mining engineer and metallurgist. Invented the Callow flotation cell and Callow screen.
+   *   **Calvin, Melvin** (1911–1997), American chemist. With J. A. Bassham, traced the path of carbon in photosynthesis. Nobel Prize, 1961.
+   *   **Cattaneo, Stanislao** (1802–1910), Italian chemist. Promulgated Avogadro's law as related to atomic weights; discovered Cannizzaro's reaction in organic chemistry.
+   *   **Cantor, Georg** (1845–1918), Russian-born German mathematician. Founded set theory; introduced fundamental concepts in topology; worked on the theory and replications of real numbers.
+   *   **Carathéodory, Constantin** (1873–1950), German mathematician. Developed calculus of variations; studied complex analysis; introduced Carathéodory outer measure; gave mathematical formulation of second law of thermodynamics (Carathéodory's principle).
+   *   **Cardan, Gerolamo, or Jerome Cardan** (1501–1576), Italian physician and mathematician. Work on mathematics, medicine, and astronomy; invented the Cardan shaft.
+   *   **Carlson, Arvid** (1923– ), Swedish pharmacologist. Did research on opiate receptors; led to the discovery of his role as a key neurotransmitter in the brain and in the control of movement; the development of L-DOPA, a precursor of dopamine; into

+   *   a drug to treat Parkinson's disease; elucidated the mode of action of endorphin and antipyrine, which affect synaptic transmission by blocking dopaminergic receptors; did work (along with that of Paul Greengard and Eric Kandel) leading to the elucidation of the molecular mechanisms involved in slow synaptic transmission in the nervous system. Nobel Prize, 2000.
+   *   **Carnot, Nicolas Léonard Sadi** (1796–1832), French physicist. Formulated Carnot's theorem in thermodynamics.
+   *   **Carrel, Alexis** (1873–1944), French surgeon and biologist. Worked on transplanting organs, suturing blood vessels, treating diseased organs, and promoting tissue life. Nobel Prize, 1912.
+   *   **Carrington, Richard Christopher** (1826–1875), English astronomer. Investigated motions of sunspots.
+   *   **Carver, George Washington** (1864–1943), American botanist. Did research on industrial uses of the peanut.
+   *   **Casségnave, N. (17th century)**, French physicist. Designed the Casségnave reflecting telescope.
+   *   **Cassini, Jean Dominique** (1625–1712), Italian-born French astronomer. Director of the Paris Observatory; discovered four new satellites of Saturn and the Cassini division in Saturn's rings; conducted pendulum experiments related to the shape of the Earth.
+   *   **Castiglion, Gaudio Alberto** (1847–1894), Italian physiologist. Proved Castiglion's theorem.
+   *   **Cauthy, Augustin Louis, Baron** (1789–1857), French mathematician. Wrote extensively on wave propagation; studied and applied the mathematics of causation, Henry (1731–1801). English physicist and chemist. Determined the density of the Earth and the composition of air; discovered the properties of carbon dioxide and hydrogen.
+   *   **Cayley, Arthur** (1821–1895), English mathematician. Among other work, wrote a classic treatise on the theory of invariants and covariants; worked on quantities and the theory of groups.
+   *   **Cech, Thomas R.** (1947– ), American chemist. By studying the single-cell organisms *Tetrahymena* and *Tetrahymena*, discovered that molecules of ribonucleic acid (RNA) have catalytic properties similar to those of enzymes. Nobel Prize, 1989.
+   *   **Celsius, Anders** (1701–1744), Swedish astronomer. Devised the thermometer, then the thermometer using the Celsius (Centigrade) scale.
+   *   **Cerenkov, Pavel Alekseevich** (1904–1990), Soviet physicist. Discovered the Cerenkov effect of radiation; devised the Cerenkov counter for particle detection. Nobel Prize, 1958.
+   *   **Cet, Nion G.** [1943– ], American computer scientist. Discovered the Cerenkov counter for particle detection. Nobel Prize, 1958.
+   *   **Transmission Control Protocol/Internet Protocol (TCP/IP)**.
+   *   **Cesalpino, Andrea** (1519–1603), Italian physician and botanist. First to attempt to classify plants according to characteristics of fruit and seed in his *De Plantis*.
+   *   **Cesaro, Ernesto** (1859–1906), Italian mathematician. Formulated an intrinsic geometry; introduced the Cesaro summation method.
+   *   **Ceva, Giovanni** (1647–1734), Italian mathematician. Formulated a theorem on the concurrency of straight lines passing through the vertices of a triangle.
+   *   **Chadwick, James** (1891–1974), English physicist. Established experimentally the existence of the neutron. Nobel Prize, 1935.
+   *   **Chain, Ernst Boris** (1906–1979), German-born British biochemist. With H. W. Florey, worked on the chemical structure of penicillin and its first clinical trials. Nobel Prize, 1945.
+   *   **Chamberlain, Owen** (1920– ), American physicist. With E. S. Singh, discovered the existence of the antiproton. Nobel Prize, 1959.
+   *   **Chamberlin, Thomas Chrowder** (1843–1928), American geologist. Studied the fundamental geometry of the solar system.
+   *   **Chandler, Seth Carlo** (1856–1943), American astronomer. Discovered Chandrasekhar's stellar variable.
+   *   **Chandrasekhar, Subrahmanyan** (1910–1995), Indian astrophysicist. Developed a theory of white dwarf stars. Nobel Prize, 1983.
