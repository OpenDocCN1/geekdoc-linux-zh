# 2474 Appendix

Haidt, René Just, Abbe (1734-1822), French mineralogist. Formulated the geometrical law of crystallization; pioneer in the science of crystallography.

Havner, C. (1895- ), French zoologist. Proposed the first full discussion of Haversian lamellae and Haversian canals.

Hay, Walter N. (1883- ), American scientist. Synthesized ascorbic acid; studied carbohydrates, including the structure of sugars; Nobel Prize, 1937.

Haysides, Oliver (1850-1925), English zoologist. Proposed the Haversian layer in the upper atmosphere.

Heardman, Alan J. (1934- ), American physicist. Developed superconductivity and did work on the superconductor.

Heberer, Friedrich (1877-1963), German chemist. Discovered the existence of the Hagerer ring and developed new analytical methods for organic chemistry.

Heberer, Franz (1884-1963), Austrian chemist. Pioneered methods for detecting radioactive elements and discovered polonium.

Heiberg, G. (1899- ), Norwegian mathematician. Made contributions to functional analysis and the theory of integral equations.

Heilbronn, Hans (1852-1945), German zoologist. Pioneered research on protoplasm and animal behavior.

Heine, Hermann Eduard (1821-1881), German mathematician. Formulated the concept of uniform convergence.

Heisenberg, Werner (1901-1976), German physicist. Pioneer of quantum mechanics; studied molecular structure of the atom and nucleus; proposed the uncertainty principle of indeterminacy in atomic physics; Nobel Prize, 1932.

Heitler, Walter (1904- ), Swiss physicist. Worked in theoretical physics; developed a quantum mechanical theory of chemical bonding.

Hemlock, Ludwig Ferdinand von (1812-1884), German chemist. Developed a new method for the synthesis of organic compounds and improved dyeing techniques.

Henley, Philip (1886-1952), American neurologist. Investigated the function of the brain and its role in mental processes; studied epilepsy.

Hensel, O. (1895-1984), German mathematician. Pioneered work on p-adic numbers and made contributions to abstract algebra and number theory.

Hertz, Gustav (1887-1975), German physicist. Studied effects of electron impacts on atoms; Nobel Prize, 1925.

Hertz, Heinrich Rudolf (1857-1894), German physicist. Discovered Hertzian waves in the ether; formulated the equations of electrodynamics and magnetism.

Hertzsprung, Ejnar (1873-1967), Danish astronomer. Discoverer of the main sequence and parallax of stars; made contributions to spectroscopy and stellar classification.

Hess, Victor (1883-1964), Austrian physicist. Studied alpha particles from radium; discovered cosmic rays; Nobel Prize, 1936.

Hess, Walter Rudolf (1881-1973), Swiss physiologist. Discovered the originator function of the mammal brain in coordination of body movement; established the chemical control of respiration; Nobel Prize, 1949.

Hevesy, Gyorgy (1885-1966), Hungarian chemist. Experimented with radioactive indicators, leading to the development of the tracer technique; Nobel Prize, 1943.

Hicks, George (1886- ), American mathematician. Made significant contributions to differential geometry and the theory of Lie groups.

Higgins, John G. (1911- ), British physicist. Pioneered the development of the particle accelerator; contributed to nuclear physics; Nobel Prize, 1962.
