A small but significant flux of cosmic rays is present at ground level. This radiation produces minute amounts of certain radioactive isotopes such as 14C. Because the concentration of these isotopes in the atmosphere is known, the flux of cosmic rays can be measured by comparing the concentration of these isotopes in the atmosphere with the concentration of these isotopes in a sample of atmospheric gas.

# Cosmology

Cosmology is the study of the origin and development of the universe. It is a branch of astronomy, and it is concerned with the origin, evolution, and ultimate fate of the universe. Cosmology is also the study of the large-scale structure of the universe.

# Big bang model

The big bang model is the explanation for the origin and evolution of the universe. It is based on the idea that the universe began as a very hot, dense state and has been expanding and cooling ever since. The model is supported by observations of the expansion of the universe, the cosmic microwave background, and the abundance of light elements.

# Relativity

The big bang model is based on the general theory of relativity, which describes gravity as a curvature of spacetime. The theory of relativity was developed by Albert Einstein in the early 20th century.

# Redshift

The redshift of distant galaxies is evidence that the universe is expanding. The farther away a galaxy is, the more its light is redshifted, and the faster it is moving away from us.

# Cosmic microwave background

The cosmic microwave background is the thermal radiation left over from the big bang. It is a nearly uniform background of electromagnetic radiation that fills the entire universe.

# Abundance of light elements

The abundance of light elements, such as hydrogen, helium, and lithium, is also evidence for the big bang. The model predicts that the universe began with a high temperature and pressure, and that the light elements were formed in the first few minutes of the universe.
