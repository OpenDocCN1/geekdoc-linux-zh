The quark content of the nucleons is given by Eqs. (1).
*\[p=(uud)\qquad n=(udd)\]  (1)*
The replacement of a d quark in the nucleon by an s quark produces a baryon state with spin parity 1/2+ and strangeness number s = −1, the latter being defined by [n(s) − n(ū)], where n(q) denotes the number of quarks of type q in the system considered. The states thus reached have the flavor structures of Eqs. (2), the
*\[(Ξ^+ Ξ^0 Λ) = (uus,(ud+dus)/√2, dds)\] (2a)*
*\[Λ = (ud − du)s/√2\] (2b)*
other factors in their wave functions being identical with those for the nucleons: thus the isotriplet Σ and isosinglet Λ states are obtained. If a u quark and a d quark are each replaced by an s quark in Eqs. (1), the isodoublet Ξ states of Eq. (3) are
*\[(Ξ^0, Ξ^−) = (uss, dss)\] (3)
obtained. The quark has I = 0, being unaffected by the SU(2)_3 transformations in the (u, d) space. The flavor wave function (sss) is necessarily symmetric and cannot occur with total spin S = 1/2. Baryonic states with s ≠ 0 are collectively termed hyperons. See Hyperconic: Strange Particles.

These eight baryon states [p, n, Σ^0, Σ^+, Λ, Ξ^0, Ξ^−] all have the spin parity 1/2+ and the same internal wave functions. It is helpful to use the quantum number Y = (B + s), named hypercharge. Then, if the states are arranged in the I_3 − Y plane (Fig. 1), the symmetry of their relationship is evident.
The mass difference \[mΣ − [mΛ] = [mΣ] − [mΛ]\] is quite large, and so SU(3)_3 symmetry is much more strongly violated than SU(2)_3 symmetry. The baryon mass values vary widely over the octet; the leading variation is proportional to the strangeness s, which counts the s-quark content of each baryon. The approximately 75-MeV difference between the mean Ξ mass m(Ξ) and m(Λ) has a more subtle origin, but is well accounted for on the basis of the quark-quark (qq) potential from quantum chromodynamics, described above. The small mass differences within each isospin multiplet are believed to be due to the intrinsic (u, d) mass difference and to electromagnetic effects.
