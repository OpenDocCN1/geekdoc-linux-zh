# ## Biographical Listing

**Abbe, Ernst** (1840–1905), German physicist. Developed optical instruments, such as an apochromatic objective and a crystal spectrograph.

**Abel, Frederick Augustus** (1827–1902), English chemist. Expert on the chemistry of explosives; originated the Abel test for determination of the flash point of petroleum.

**Abel, John Jacob** (1857–1938), American pharmacologist and physiologist. Isolated epinephrine, and insulin in crystal form.

**Abel, Niels Henrik** (1802–1829), Norwegian mathematician. Contributed to the theory of elliptic functions.

**Abell, George Ogden** (1927–1983), American astronomer. Research in problems relating to organization, structure, and distribution of galaxies; observational cosmology; and planetary nebulae.

**Abney, William de Wiveleuse** (1843–1920), English photographic chemist and physicist. Photographed the infrared solar spectrum.

**Abrikosev, Alexei A.** (1928– ), Russian-born American physicist. Applied the Ginzburg-Landau theory to explain the behavior of type II superconductors; Nobel Prize, 2003.

**Adams, John Couch** (1830–1892), English astronomer. Discovered, independently of J. J. Leverrier, Neptune.

**Adanson, Michel** (1727–1806), French naturalist. Classified plants in *Les Familles Naturelles des Plantes*.

**Addison, Thomas** (1713–1860), English physician. Identified pernicious anemia and Addison’s disease of the adrenal cortex.

**Adler, Alfred** (1870–1937), Austrian psychiatrist and psychologist. Founded the school of individual psychology.

**Adrian of Cambridge, Edgar Douglas Adrian, Baron** (1889–1977), English physiologist. Investigated physiology of nervous system; showed that change in electric potential in electroencephalogram is due to multiple activity of motor cortex; Nobel Prize, 1932.

**Atzeilow, Adam** (1750–1837), Swedish botanist. Founded the Linnaean Institute.

**Agassiz, Jean Louis Rodolphe** (1807–1873), Swiss-born American naturalist. Wrote books on ichthyology; especially important in modificationism.

**Agnesi, Maria Gasetna** (1718–1799), Italian mathematician. Author of *Istitutioni Analitiche*, a complete treatment of algebra and curve calculus.

**Agnew, Peter** (1949– ), American medical doctor and scientist. Discovered water channels in cell membranes; Nobel Prize, 2003.

**Agricola, Georgios**, real name Georg Bauer (1494–1555), German physician and mineralogist. Known as the father of systematic mineralogy.

**Ahffors, Lars Valerian** (1879–1966), Finnish-American mathematician. Did research on covering surfaces related to Riemann surfaces of inverse functions of entire and meromorphic functions; opened up new fields of analysis; Fields Medal, 1936.

**Airy, George Biddell** (1801–1892), English astronomer. Discovered inequalities in the motions of Venus and the Earth; determined the mass of the Earth.

**Aitken, John** (1839–1919), Scottish physicist. Studied dust particles in the atmosphere, known as Aitken nuclei.

**Aitken, Robert Grant** (1864–1951), American astronomer. Discovered more than 3000 binary stars.

**Alder, Kurt** (1902–1958), German chemist. Co-developer of the Dieck-Alder reaction for diene synthesis; contributed to stereochemistry; Nobel Prize, 1950.

**Almenard, Jean le Rond** (1717–1773), French mathematician. Developed d’Alembert’s principle and the calculus of partial differences.

**Alferov, Zhores Ivanovich** (1930– ), Russian physicist and electronics engineer. Developed semiconductor heterostructures used in high-speed and optoelectronics, including fast transistors, laser diodes, and light-emitting diodes; Nobel Prize, 2000.

**Alfven, Hannes Olof Gösta** (1908–1995), Swedish physicist. Studies in magnetohydrodynamics, planetary physics, antimatter dynamics, and shortwave magnetism; Nobel Prize, 1970.

**Alhazen** (965–1038), Arab mathematician and astronomer. Provided the first accounts of atmospheric refraction and reflection from concave surfaces; constructed spherical and parabolic mirrors.

**al-Khwarizmi** (780–850), Arab mathematician. Wrote treatises on arithmetic and algebra, which were important in the mathematical knowledge of medieval Europe.

**Allen, Edgar** (1892–1943), American biologist. Discovered estrogens; investigated hormonal mechanisms controlling female reproductive cycle.

**Allen, William Murray** (1904–1993), American physician. With G. W. Corner, discovered progesterone; and proved it necessary for development of embryo in early pregnancy; with O. Winterstein, synthesized crystalline progesterone.

**Altman, Sidney** (1939– ), American chemist. Discovered an unusual enzyme that contains ribonucleic acid (RNA) in addition to a protein, leading to the discovery that RNA molecules have catalytic properties similar to those of enzymes; Nobel Prize, 1989.

**Alvarez, Luis Walter** (1911–1988), American physicist. Pioneered in building liquid hydrogen bubble chambers, and in developing electronics and computers to analyze data from these chambers; discovered large numbers of previously unknown subatomic particles; Nobel Prize, 1968.

**Amagat, Emile** (1841–1915), French chemist. Investigated relationship of pressure, density, and temperature in gases and liquids, particularly at high pressure.

**Amici, Giovanni Battista** (1786–1863), Italian astronomer, optical artisan, and scientist. Invented the Amici microscope; designed parabolic mirrors for reflecting telescopes.

**Ampère, André Marie** (1775–1836), French physicist and mathematician. Founder of electrodynamics; formulated Ampère’s law; invented the astatic galvanometer.

**Anaximander** (611–547 B.C.), Greek astronomer and mathematician. Developed the first known map.

**Anaximenes** (585–525 B.C.), Greek philosopher; formulated the concept of the universe as infinite (apeiron).

**Anderson, Carl David** (1905–1991), American physicist. Discovered the muon in cosmic rays; discovered the positron; Nobel Prize, 1936.

**Anderson, Phil Warren** (1923– ), American physicist. Demonstrated existence of electric- or charge-localized, or localized, magnetic states; Nobel Prize, 1992.

**Andrade, Eduardo Neville da Costa** (1887–1971), English physicist. Discovered Andrade’s creep law and a new governing variation of viscosity of liquids with temperature.

**Andrewes, Roy Chapman** (1864–1940), American naturalist. Discovered many plant and animal fossils.

**Andriesse, Christian Boerhm** (1916–1995), American biochemist. Discovered how three-dimensional structures of ribonucleic acid and other substances were formed; Nobel Prize, 1972.

**Angström, Anders Jonas** (1814–1874), Swedish physicist. Mapped the solar spectrum; discovered lines in the solar atmosphere.

**Appollonius of Perga** (247–205 b.c.), Greek mathematician. Wrote about conic sections; coined the terms parabola, ellipse, and hyperbola.

**Appleton, J.J.** (1892–1965), English physicist. Demonstrated the existence of the ionosphere and discovered its region known as the F-layer.

**Appleton**, later, contributed to the development of radar; Nobel Prize, 1947.

**Arago, Dominique François** (1786–1853), French astronomer and physicist. Discovered the magnetic properties of nonferrous materials, and the production of magnetism by electricity.

**Arbuthnot, Werner** (1929– ), Swiss molecular biologist. Determined the molecular mechanism of host-controlled restriction modification of bacterial viruses and discovered the restriction enzymes; Nobel Prize, 1978.

**Archimedes** (287–212 A.D.), Greek physicist and mathematician. Formulated Archimedes’ principle; invented the compound pulley and Archimedes’ screw.

**Arc, Jean Robert** (1768–1822), Swiss mathematician. Developed the Arand diagram.

**Argelander, Friedrich Wilhelm August** (1799–1875), German astronomer. Prepared a star catalog; introduced decimal division of stellar magnitudes.

**Aristarchus of Samos** (310–250 B.C.), Greek astronomer. Invented the hemispherical sundial; determined the movement of the Earth around the stationary Sun; added a correction factor of 1/823 of a day to the length of the year.

**Aristotle** (384–322 B.C.), Greek philosopher. Extensive works on the classification and division of sciences; contributions to physics, astronomy, meteorology, psychology, and biology.

**Arkwright, Richard** (1732–1792), English inventor. Developed the first water-driven mechanized spinning frame; utilized rollers.

**Ardenne, Emile August** (1859–1927), German physicist. Invented the theory of electric discharges; investigated ozone and viscosity of solutions; Nobel Prize, 1903.

**Arsonval, Jacques Arsene** (+1851–1904), French physicist and physiologist. Pioneered in electrotherapy; invented d’Arsonval galvanometer.

**Astor, Francis William** (1877–1945), English physicist and chemist. Discovered isotopes in radioactivity elements by using the mass spectrograph; Nobel Prize, 1922.

**Atiyah, Michael Freeden** (1929– ), British mathematician. Worked on the interrelation between geometry and analysis; developed K-theory in collaboration with I.M. Singer; proved the index theorem concerning elliptic differential operators on compact differentiable manifolds, which was proved to be applicable to theoretical physics; Fields Medal, 1986; Abel Prize, 2004.

**Atwood, Thomas** (1746–1807), English mathematician. Invented the Atwood machine.

**Audubon, John James** (1785–1851), Haitian-born American ornithologist and artist. Made drawings and paintings of birds and animals.

**Auger, Pierre Victor** (1898–1993), French physicist. Discovered the Auger effect.

**Aurelius, Avicenna** (979–1037), Persian physician. Wrote the medical text *Canon Medicinae*.

**Avogadro, Amedeo** (1776–1856), Italian physicist. Formulated Avogadro’s law.

**Axel, Richard** (1946– ), American molecular biologist. Recognized along with Linda Buck for pioneering research of the olfactory system, which led to the discovery of the genes and proteins involved in the transmission of olfactory information. Later independent work clarified the cellular and molecular mechanisms underlying the olfactory system. Nobel Prize 2004.

**Ackland, John** (1912– ), American biochemist and pharmacologist. Showed that many drugs act by modifying storage of neurotransmitters in the brain; the release of stored neurotransmitters; metabolism, and mechanism for formation and inactivation of norepinephrine; Nobel Prize, 1970.

**Arton, William Edward** (1847–1908), English physician and electrical engineer. Invented the amperemeter, voltmeter, and other electrical measuring instruments.
