# 2446 Appendix

Acadé, Walter (1893–1960), German-born American astronomer. Formulated concept of stellar populations; increased distance scale of universe by factor of 2.

Abaigbe, Charles (1792–1851), English mathematician. Devised a prime computer to calculate and print mathematical and astronomical tables.

Abacock, Harold Delos (1882–2003), American astrophysicist. Invented the Babcock magnetograft, discovered weak solar magnetic fields, and discovered stellar magnetic fields.

Abacock, Stephen Newton (1943–1931), American agricultural chemist. Found nitrogenase. Developed the Abacock test to measure fat content in milk.

Abalment, Jacques (1734–1872), French physician. Invented a polariscope and a goniometer.

Back, Ernest E. A. (1851–1899), German physician. Developed improved spectrograph; made spectroscopic observations leading to Paschen-Richard Blacket.

Badger, Richard McConnell (1896–1974), American physical chemist and spectroscopist. Studied structures of polymetallic molecules; formulated Badger’s rule concerning vibrational energy levels.

Baekeland, Leo Hendrik (1864–1944), Belgian-born American chemist. Invented the phenol-formaldehyde polymer, Bakelite, the first commercial synthetic polymer.

Baer, Karl Ernst von (1728–1876), Estonian embryologist. Discovered the mammalian ovum and the notochord; developed the theory of embryogenesis.

Baeyer, Johann Friedrich Wilhelm Adolf von (1835–1917), Nobel Prize winner. Synthesized indigo and hydrometallic compounds; Nobel Prize, 1905.

Bailey, Francis (1774–1844), English astronomer. A founder of the Royal Astronomical Society; first observed phenomenon of Baileys beads.

Baile, René Louis (1874–1939), French mathematician. Contributed to theory of linear real variables; introduced concept of Banach functions.

Baker, Alan (1939– ), British mathematician. Provided a generalization of the Gefland-Schneider theorem; from this, worked general transcendental numbers not required on Banach spaces and solved problems in the theory of Diophantine equations.

Fields Medal, 1970.

Baldor, Francis Maitland (1851–1882), English biologist. Founder of comparative embryology.

Balmes, Johann Jakop (1825–1898), Swiss physician. Explored the mathematical formula for absorption of hydrogen lines in the visible spectrum.

Baltimore, David (1938– ), American virologist. Investigated interaction of viral and cellular tumor viruses and genetic material; independently of M. M. Tyms, discovered reverse transcriptase; Nobel Prize, 1975.

Banach, Stefan (1892–1945), Polish mathematician. Laid foundations of contemporary functional analysis; introduced concept of Banach space and discovered fundamental properties.

Bang, Bernhard Laurits Frederik (1848–1932), Danish veterinarian. Developed method of eradicating bovine tuberculosis; discovered Brucella abortus, the agent of contagious abortion (Bang’s disease) and brucellosis.

Bantring, Frederick Grant (1891–1941), Canadian physician. With J. R. Macleod and C. H. Best, discovered insulin and its role in diabetes; Nobel Prize, 1923.

Barling, Robert (1876–1936), Austrian physician. Developed new methods of diagnosing ear diseases; Nobel Prize, 1914.

Barden, John (1906–1991), American physicist. With L. N. Cooper and J. R. Schrieffer, formulated theory of superconductivity; developed transistor; Nobel Prize, 1972 and 1962.

Barkhausen, Heinrich Georg (1881–1956), German electronic engineer. Invented Barkhausen oscillator; contributed to theory and application of electron tubes; discovered Barkhausen effect.

Barkla, Charles Glover (1877–1944), English physicist. Described absorption of X-rays and other short-wave emissions of elements.

Barnard, Edward Emerson (1857–1923), American astronomer. Discovered 16 comets, the first satellite of Jupiter, and dark nebulae; contributed to celestial photography.

Barnet, Samuel Jackson (1837–1892), American physicist. Discovered Barnett effect and used it to measure the gyromagnetic ratio of ferromagnetic materials; gave experimental proof of existence of the Barnett effect.

Bar, Murray Llewellyn (1908–1995), Canadian anatomist. Discovered the Bann body on the nucleus of the hypoglossal nerve.

Bartholin, Kaspar (1655–1723), Danish physician. Described Bartholin’s glands of the vagina and a salivary duct.

Bartholin, Thomas (1616–1680), Danish physician. Described lymphatic system; developed the Bartholin’s glands of the vagina and a salivary duct.

Bartlett, James Holly (1894–2000), American physicist. Introduced concept of Bartlett force; did research on nuclear shell model, electrochemical potential, and restricted three-body problem.

Barton, Derek Harold Richard (1918–1998), British chemist. Developed and expanded concepts of conformation to include large molecules with cyclized systems; Nobel Prize, 1969.

Basov, Nikolai Gennadiovich (1922–2001), Soviet physicist. Conducted fundamental studies in quantum electronics; with A. M. Prokhorov, developed quantum optical generator; Nobel Prize, 1964.

Basbamm, James Allen (1922– ), American chemist. Used to elucidate basic photophotocarbon cycle.

Bates, Henry Walter (1825–1892), English naturalist. Described Batesian mimicry among butterflies and moths.

Baute, Émile (1845–1903), French engineer. Invented an improved telegraph transmitter.

Baumé, Antoine (1728–1804), French chemist. Invented a graduated hydrometer which utilizes the Baumé scale.

Bautz, Laura Patricia (1940– ), American astronomer. Collaborated with W. W. Morgan in devising Bauer–Morgan classification of galaxy clusters.

Bayer, Johann (1572–1624), German astronomer. Changed 12 constellations; first to use Greek letters to designate star order of brightness; developed the Bayer designation system.

Bates, Thomas (1720–1761), English mathematician. First solution for stimulated emission inference.

Batyasov, William Maddock (1860–1924), English biochemist. Discovered on electrophysiology of heart action; discovered the heart as a muscle secretion.

Beaude, George Wells (1903–1989), American geneticist. With E. L. Tutin, proved that genes affect fertility by controlling cell chemistry; Nobel Prize, 1958.

Beams, Jesse Wakefield (1898–1977), American physicist. Devised vacuum-type ultramicrophone, used in purliguration and molecular weight determination of large-molecular-weight substances, isotopic separation, and determination of the gravitational constant.

Beatrixe, James Alexander (1859–1961), American chemist and physicist. Studied ionic theory and thermodynamics; with P. W. Bridgman, proposed Beattie and Bridgman equation for gases.

Beaurot, Francis (1874–1853), English hydrographer. Discovered scale of wind velocity.

Beaumont, William (1775–1853), American surgeon. Did pioneering studies of digestion and gastric juice.

Béchamp, Pierre Jacques Antoine (1816–1908), French chemist. Discovered a method of preparing glucose.

Beckmann, Ernst Otto (1853–1923), German chemist. Discovered Beckmann molecular transition; invented the Beckmann rearrangement.

Bequerel, Antoine César (1788–1878), French physicist. Pioneer in electrochemistry; first to extract metals from ore by electrolysis.

Bequerel, Antoine Henri (1852–1908), French physicist. Discovered radioactivity.

Bednorz, Johannes Georg (1950– ), German physicist. With K. A. Müller, discovered high-temperature superconductors in copper oxide-ceramic materials; Nobel Prize, 1987.

Beebe, James William (1877–1962), American naturalist. Pioneered in deep-sea exploration; made ornithological collections.

Beer, August (1825–1863), German physician. Discovered Beer’s law.

Beersbøe, Emil Ludwig von (1854–1917), German bacteriologist. Produced diptheria and tetanus antitoxins; Nobel Prize, 1901.

Bell, Alexander Graham (1847–1922), Scottish-American physician inventor. Invented the telephone, phonograph, graphophone, and one of the earliest photophone, telephone, and one of the earliest graphophone, and one of the earliest graphophone.

Bell, Charles (1774–1842), Scottish anatomist. Described that sensory and motor nerves are anatomically and functionally distinct.

Bellman, Richard Ernest (1920–1984), American mathematician. Research in analytic number theory, differential equations, stochastic processes, dynamic programming, and mathematical biosciences; discovered Bellman’s principle of optimality.

Benacerraf, Baruj (1920– ), American immunologist. Discovered immune-response (lr) genes that control specific immune responses to thymus-dependent antigens; Nobel Prize, 1985.

Beniøf, Hugo (1899–1968), American geophysicist. Investigated earthquakes, particularly through instrumental means; Nobel Prize, 1980.

Benihum, George (1800–1884), English botanist. With J. Hocker, wrote Genera Plantarum.

Bering, Paul (1824–1920), American chemist. Investigated the biochemistry of dideoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) and devised a technique for gene splicing; Nobel Prize, 1959.

Berger, David Hendricks (1860–1937), American bacteriologist. Authority on classification of bacteria.

Bergius, Friedrich (1884–1949), Polish-born German chemist. Developed Bergius process for hydrogenation of coal to a petroleumlike oil; Nobel Prize, 1931.

Bergerström, Sune Carl (1916–2004), Swedish biochemist and medical scientist. Studied the metabolism of uric acid; formulated the Bergestral and determined the chemical structure of prostaglandins; Nobel Prize, 1982.

Bernard, Claude (1813–1878), French physiologist. Studied digestion; discovered that glycogen is produced by the liver.

Berners-Lee, Tim (1955– ), British-born physician and software engineer. Proposed the World Wide Web, and then invented hypertext markup language (HTML), Hypertext Transfer Protocol (HTTP), the Internet address scheme (Unir), and Resource Locator or URL; the first Web browser.

Bernoulli, Daniel (1700–1782), Swiss mathematician born in the Netherlands. Founder of mathematical physics; worked on hydrodynamics and differential equations; formulated the Bernoulli equation.

Bernoulli, Jacques or Jacob (1655–1705), Swiss mathematician. Contributed to mathematics of curvilinear, calculus, and probability; developed the Bernoulli number.

Bernoulli, Jean or Johann (1667–1748), Swiss mathematician. A founder of calculus of variations; contributed to exponential calculus, complex numbers, geodesics, and statistics of mathematics.

Berthelot, Pierre Eugène Marcellin (1827–1907), French chemist. Founder of thermochemistry; first to synthesize organic compounds; demonstrated nitrogen fixation.

Bertrand, Joseph Louis François (1822–1900), French mathematician. Contributed to analysis, differential geometry, and probability theory.

Berzelius, Jons Jakob (1779–1848), Swedish chemist. Discovered the elements carbon, sulfur.
