- **Bessel, Friedrich Wilhelm** (1784–1846), German astronomer and mathematician. Developed Bessel functions; determined parallax of the star 61 Cygni; postulated existence of Neptune and dark stars.
- **Bessemer, Henry** (1813–1898), English engineer. Invented the Bessemer method, the first method for manufacturing steel on a large scale.
- **Best, Charles Hedrich** (1899–1978), Canadian physiologist and medical researcher. Associated with F. G. Banting and J. J. R. Maccleod in the discovery of insulin.
- **Bethé, Hans Albrecht** (1906– ), German-born American physicist. Formulated the theory of energy production in stars; researched on nuclear physics; Nobel Prize, 1967.
- **Bhabha, Homi Jeehanga** (1909–1966), Indian physicist. Won by H. J. Hertz. Developed techniques for showers of cosmic rays; discovered showers of decay rates of high-velocity mesons.
- **Bianchi, Luigi** (1856–1928), Italian mathematician. Contributed to differential geometry and study of nonuniform geometries; discovered Bianchi identity.
- **Bichat, Marie François Xavier** (1771–1802), French anatomist and physiologist. Founder of animal histology; originated the term "tissues" and distinguished 12 tissues in his particular scheme.
- **Bieberbach, Ludwig** (1886–1922), German mathematician. Research on complex functions, theory, differential equations, geometry, and algebra; postulated Bieberbach’s conjecture.
- **Bienaymé, Irène** (1768–1840s), French mathematician. Calculated distribution of probability and its application to financial theory; with P. L. Chebyshev, discovered Bienaymé-Chebyshev inequality.
- **Billet, Felix** (1805–1891), French physicist. Invented Billet split lens.
- **Binet, Adolphe** (1823–1911), French psychologist. Investigated intelligence and measurement of intelligence.
- **Binning, Gerd** (1947– ), German physicist. At R. Hofer, developed scanning tunneling microscope; Nobel Prize, 1987.
- **Biot, Jean Baptiste** (1774–1862), French mathematician and physicist. Discovered circular polarization of light; invented a polarimeter; with D. Boussier, discovered Biot-Blancardi law; helped formulate Biot-Savart law.
- **Birkhoff, George David** (1884–1944), American mathematician. Investigated topology and dynamics systems, ergodic theory, logician of foundations of relativity and quantum mechanics.
- **Bishop, John Michael** (1936– ), American virologist and biochemist. With H. Varmus, discovered the genetic basis of human cancers; their work led to identification of over 50 cellular genes that can become oncogenes; Nobel Prize, 1989.
- **Birkeland, Wilhelm Ferenc** (1862–1951), Norwegian physicist. Research on electric waves; originated the polar-faron theory in electromagneticism.
- **Black, James** (1749– ), British pharmacologist. Developed the first beta blocker drug, propranolol; also credited with the discovery of another important class of drugs, the H2 antagonists; Nobel Prize, 1988.
- **Black, Joseph** (1728–1799), Scottish physicist and chemist. Established the concepts of latent heat and specific heat, and discovered carbon dioxide.
- **Blackett, Patrick Maynard Stuart** (1897–1974), English physicist. Built an improved cloud chamber to photograph tracks of a nuclear disintegration and a cosmic-ray event; discovered positron; Nobel Prize, 1948.
- **Blödel, Günther** (1936– ), German-born American cell and molecular biologist. Discovered that proteins carry signals that help direct their movements among the organs of the cell; Nobel Prize, 1999.
- **Block, Félix** (1905–1983), Swiss-born American physician. Discovered a technique for studying magnesium of atomic nuclei in normal matter; Nobel Prize, 1952.
- **Block, Konrad Emil** (1910–2000), German-born American biochemist. Traced the transformations of fat and carbohydrate metabolism to cholesterol; Nobel Prize, 1982.
- **Blodgett, Katharine Burr** (1895–1979), American chemist. Studied surface science and is best known for her work with Irving Langmuir on the development of the Langmuir-Blodgett film.
- **Bloembergen, Nicolaas** (1920– ), Netherlands-born American physicist. Contributed to development of maser; made extensive contributions to theoretical and experimental development of nonlinear optics; Nobel Prize, 1981.
- **Blumberg, Bausch, Samuel** (1925– ), American physician and biotechist. Research leading to a test for AIDS in blood and tissue is an experimental hepatitis vaccine; Nobel Prize, 1976.
- **Boas, Franz** (1883–1942), American anthropologist. Pioneer in physical anthropology; contributed to stratigraphic archeology in Mexico; emphasized importance of linguistic analysis.
- **Bolltilier, Étienne** (1776–1840), French mathematician and physicist. Contributed to probability and statics; discovered Bolltilier’s law.
- **Bode, Johann Elert** (1747–1826), German astronomer. Made a celestial map following an orbit; in 1773 formulated Bode’s law.
- **Boerhaave, Hermann** (1668–1738), Dutch physician. A great teacher at the University of Leiden; wrote the physiology textbook *Institutiones Medicae*.
- **Bohm, David** (1917–1992), American-born British physicist. Research in quantum theory and New Models of description in physics.
- **Bohm, Leon** (1925– ), Danish-born American physicist. With B. R. Motelson, developed theory which unifies short- and liquid-drop models of the atomic nucleus, and which explains non spherical nuclei; Nobel Prize, 1975.
- **Bohr, Niels** (1885–1962), Danish physicist. Made an atomic model; developed the quantum theory, applying it to atomic structure in Bohr’s theorem; Nobel Prize, 1922.
- **Bohm, Ludwig** (1844–1906), Austrian physicist. With K. R. Hergess, improved the theory of gases; demonstrated the Stefan-Boltzmann law of blackbody radiation; Boltzmann’s law of energy distribution; Boltzmann constant.
- **Bolayai, János** (1802–1851), Hungarian mathematician. Independently of K. F. Gauss and N. I. Lobachevsky, originated a system of non-Euclidean geometry.
- **Bolzano, Bernard** (1781–1848), Czechoslovak-born mathematician. Logician and mathematician. Contributed to theory of real functions; proved Bolzano’s theorem and Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem.
- **Bombieri, Enrico** (1940– ), Italian mathematician. Made major contributions to the study of prime numbers, partial differential equations and minimal surfaces, invariant functions and the local Bieberbach conjecture, and functions of several complex variables; Fields Medal, 1974.
- **Bond, George Phillips** (1875–1965), American astronomer. Introduced concept of Bond albedo, and pioneered observational photography.
- **Boole, George** (1815–1864), English mathematician and logician. Developed new system of mathematical logic, which is now as Boolean algebra.
- **Borchardt, Richard Ewan** [1955– ], British mathematician. Works in algebraic geometry; proved the monochrome conjecture, which relates to the so-called monochrome group and elliptic curves, using methods from number theory; Fields Medal, 1998.
- **Borda, Jean Charles** (1733–1799), French physicist and mathematician. Introduced Borda mouthpiece; designed instruments for navigation, geodesy, and determination of weights and measures.
- **Bordet, Jules Jean Baptiste Vincent** (1870–1961), Belgian pathologist. Made discoveries in immunology; with O. Gengou, developed the technique of the complement fixation reaction; Nobel Prize, 1919.
- **Borel, Félix Édouard Emile** (1871–1956), French mathematician. Work in infinite-dimensional calculus and the calculus of probabilities.
- **Born, Max** (1882–1970), German-born British theoretical physicist. Pioneered in the development of quantum mechanics; Nobel Prize, 1954.
- **Bosch, Carl** (1874–1944), German chemist. Developed chemical high-pressure methods, and the Haber-Bosch process for ammonia synthesis; Nobel Prize, 1931.
- **Bose, Jagadish Chandra** (1858–1937), Indian physicist, physicist and physician. Founded the Bose Research Institute in Calcutta; developed the concept of "nervous mechanism" of plants and other plant services.
- **Bose, Satyendra Nath** (1894–1974), Indian physicist. Originated Bose-Einstein statistics to describe photons.
- **Bothe, Walther** (1891–1957), German physicist. Devised the coincidence method for the investigation of nuclear reactions and cosmic radiation; Nobel Prize, 1954.
- **Bouger, Pierre** (1698–1758), French geodesist, and hydrographer, and physician. Laid foundations of phonomy; discovered Bouguer-Lambert law of light attenuation.
- **Bourdon, Eugene** (1808–1884), French inventor. Invented Bourdon pressure gauge.
- **Bourn, John** (1958– ), American mathematician. Worked in several areas of mathematical analysis, including the geometry of Banach spaces, operator algebras, and harmonic analysis, ergodic theory, and nonlinear partial differential equations; Fields Medal, 1994.
- **Bouvet, Josse-Philippe** (1842–1929), French mathematical physicist. Research in hydrodynamics; introduced Boussinesq approximation.
- **Bovet, Daniel** (1907–1992), Swiss-born Italian pharmacologist. Research on synthetic compounds that inhibit the action of the vascular system and skeletal muscles; Nobel Prize, 1957.
- **Bowditch, Nathaniel** (1773–1838), American navigator and mathematician. Produced navigational guide; translated and improved P. S. Laplace’s *Mécanique céleste*.
- **Bowen, Norman Levi** (1887–1956), Canadian-born American geologist. Studied physical chemistry of geological processes and physical solubility of silicates; discovered significance of reaction principle in petrogenesis.
- **Bayer, Paul D.** (1915– ), American chemist. Made major contributions toward elucidating the enzymatic mechanism underlying the synthesis of protein. His work, by proposing the two-site models, led to development of the two-state model of protein folding; Nobel Prize, 1997.
- **Boyle, Robert** (1627–1691), British physicist and chemist. Contributed to the scientific study of the air; his law concerning gases is named for him; advanced the atomic theory of matter.
- **Brewster, David** (1781–1868), English astronomer. Discovered aberration of light due to Earth's atmosphere; discovered and demonstrated the phenomena of reflection, motion, and rotation of the Earth's axis; prepared astronomical tables and star catalogues.
- **Brage, William Henry** (1862–1922), English astronomer. Co-developed, with W. L. Bragg, the X-ray spectrometer; used X-ray diffraction to determine crystal structure; Nobel Prize, 1915.
- **Bragg, William Lawrence** (1859–1971), British physicist. With W. H. Bragg, developed X-ray analysis of crystal structures; developed in stellar studies; Nobel Prize, 1915.
- **Brabe, Tycus** or Tyve [1560–1610], Danish astronomer. Made improvement in the construction of the planetary system; wrote *Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata*.
- **Bramson, Walter Houser** (1902–1987), American physicist. Investigated properties of semiconductors; researched on surface properties of solids; Nobel Prize, 1956.
- **Braum, Karl Ferdinand** (1850–1918), German physicist. Research on cathode rays and wireless telegraphy; Nobel Prize, 1909.
