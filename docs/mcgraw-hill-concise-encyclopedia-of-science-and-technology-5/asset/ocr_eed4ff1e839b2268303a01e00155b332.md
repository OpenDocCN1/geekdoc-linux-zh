# ## 4742 Appendix

+   Forrey, Howard Walter (1898-1968). British chemist. Born in Australia. Contributed with E. B. Chain to development of penicillin as a chemotherapeutic agent. Nobel Prize, 1945.

+   Foster, Paul John (1918- ), American physical chemist. Developed analytic techniques to explore properties and molecular structures of long-chain molecules. Nobel Prize, 1973.

+   Fock, Vladimir Aleksandrovich (1898-1974), Soviet theoretical physicist. Contributions to quantum electrodynamics, quantum field theory, electromagnetism, diffusion, and propagation and general relativity; developed Hartree-Fock approximation of wave functions.

+   Foucault, Scott Elizabeth [1951- ], American geophysicist. Discovered the worldwide decrease in comma-ray intensity associated with sunspot magnetic storms.

+   Forsyarnan, Werner Theodor Otto (1904-1973), German physicist. Developed the techniques of cardiac catheterization. Nobel Prize, 1956.

+   Foucault, Jean Bernard Leon (1819-1868), French physicist. Accurately determined the velocity of light; constructed the Foucault pendulum and the Foucault prism; determined experimentally the rotation of the Earth.

+   Fourier, Jean Baptiste Joseph, Baron (1768-1830), French mathematician and physicist. Pioneered the Fourier series on arbitrary functions; formulated the law of heat conduction.

+   Fowler, Ralph Howard (1889-1944), English astronomer. Applied statistical mechanics to matter of high temperatures and pressures; explained the structure of white dwarf stars; with E. A. Guggenheim, R. F. Peiers, and others, developed the fermion and spin models.

+   Fowler, William Alfred (1911-1995), American geophysicist. Fundamental contributions to understanding of nuclear reactions in stars. Received a prize for discovery of stars and synthesize the elements of the universe. Nobel Prize, 1983.

+   Franck, James (1882-1964), German physicist. With G. Herz, studied energy transfer in collisions of molecules; formulated Franck-Condon principle of transition from one energy state to another. Nobel Prize, 1925.

+   Frank, Illya Mikhailovich (1908-1990), Soviet physicist. With I. Y. Tamm, formulated a theoretical interpretation of Cerenkov radiation. Nobel Prize, 1958.

+   Franklin, Benjamin (1706-1790), American inventor, oceanographer, inventor, and inventor. Formulated a theory of general electrical 'action'; introduced principle of conservation of charge; showed that lightning is an electrical phenomenon; invented the lightning rod.

+   Fraunhofer, Joseph von (1787-1826), German physicist and physician. First studied the dark lines in the solar spectrum (Fraunhofer lines); improved the spectroscope.

+   Fredholm, Eric Ivar (1866-1927), Swedish mathematician. Developed the theory of integral equations (Fredholm equations).

+   Freeman, Michael Henry (1951- ), American mathematician. Developed new methods for topological analysis of four-manifolds and applied them to prove the Poincare conjecture for dimensions 4-7. Fields Medal, 1996.

+   Fresnel, Jean Frederick (1816-1900), French mathematician. Helped to develop Fresnel-Soret photography.

+   Frenkel, Yakov Hych (1894-1954), Soviet physicist. Pioneered in modern atomic theory and solids; developed quantum electrodynamics excitations for electron energy free path in metals. For paramagnetism and terrmomagnetism, postulated the exciton. Frenkel excitations, and Frenkel defects.

+   Fresnel, Augustin Jean (1788-1827), French physicist. Investigated effects (Fresnel's fringes) due to the interference of light; developed a wave theory of light; originated Fresnel's reflection formula.

+   Fresnel, Sigmund (1856-1929), Austrian psychosociologist. Founder of psychoanalysis, with emphasis on dream interpretation and free association; developed a theory of personality involving id, ego, superego, and the importance of the libido.

+   Friedel, Charles (1832-1899), French chemist and mineralogist. With J. M. Crafts, described the Friedel-Crafts reaction; worked on the fractional crystallization of minerals; studied crystals, isotopes, and isotopic differences.

+   Fiehneman, Jerome Isaac (1930- ), American physicist. Collaborated in experiments that demonstrated that protons, neutrons, and similar particles are made of quarks.

+   Fiehe, Karl (1774-1842), Austrian zoologist. Discovered means by which bees communicate information about the distance and direction of food. Nobel Prize, 1973.

+   Frobenius, Georg Ferdinand (1849-1917), German mathematician. Developed representation theory of finite groups and showed how to solve linear homogeneous ordinary differential equations.

+   Froude, William (1810-1879), English engineer. Developed the law of comparison, concerning the driving of an object in a fluid.

+   Fubini, Guido (1879-1943), Italian mathematician. Developed front orbital theory, a quantum-projective property; proved Fubini's theorem.

+   Fuku, Kenichi (1918-1998), Japanese chemist. Worked in algebras, analysis, and differential projective geometry; proved Fubini's theorem.

+   Fuller, R. Buckminster (1895-1983), American engineer and architect. Designed geodesic domes; the carbon molecular form C$_{60}$ was named a buckminsterfullerene because of its structure resembling a ball, and the name Fullerene was given to any closed-cage molecule containing an even number of carbon atoms.

+   Furchgott, Robert F. (1916- ), American pharmacologist. Discovered endothelium-derived relaxing factor (EDRF), a significant molecule in the cardiovascular system that makes vascular smooth muscle cells relax. Louis I. Ignaro, working independently with Furchgott, later concluded that EDRF was nitric oxide. Nobel Prize, 1998.

+   Gabor, Dennis (1900-1979), Hungarian-born British physicist and engineer. Invented holography. Nobel Prize, 1971.

+   Gäldel, Daniel Curleton (1923- ), American physician and virologist. Discovered causative virus and transmission mechanism of kuru. Nobel Prize, 1976.

+   Galeni, 22nd century), Greek physician and medical writer. Wrote treatises long used as textbooks; experimented on animal nervous systems; made anatomical descriptions of structure and functions of body parts.

+   Galilei Galileo (1564-1642), Italian astronomer. First to use telescope for astronomical purposes; made many discoveries related to the planets and the Sun; did theoretical work on classical mechanics.

+   Galois, Evariste (1811-1832), French mathematician. Developed Galois theory of polynomials.

+   Gamow, George (1904-1968), Russian-born American physicist. Made theoretical contributions to nuclear physics, astronomy, and biology, with E. Teller, formulated the selection rule for beta emission; proposed theoretically the genesis of deuterium.

+   Garvey, Gerald Thomas (1935- ), American physicist. Researched in experimental nuclear physics, particularly nucleon reactions, isobaric spin states, and weak interactions in nuclear systems.

+   Gasser, Herbert Spencer (1888-1963), American physiologist. With J. Erlanger, provided a method for recording electrical nerve impulses of nerves; studied functions of nerve fibers. Nobel Prize, 1944.

+   Gattermann, Friedrich August Ludwig (1869-1906), German chemist. Developed Gattermann-Koch synthesis of aldehydes, isolated and analyzed vitamin thiocticidolic and aromatic carbocyclic acids; thiophenols, and thioacetic acid. Nobel Prize, 1912.

+   Gauss, Carl Friedrich (1777-1855), German mathematician, astronomer, and physicist. Formulated the Gauss theorem on the mathematics of electricity, discovered the many contributions to pure and applied mathematics; determined orbits of planets and comets from observational data.

+   Gav-Lassaigne, Jean Baptist (1797-1850), French chemist and physicist. Discovered the law of expansion of gases by heat, and the law of combining volumes of gases; studied chemistry of iodine and cyanogen.

+   Geiger, Hans Wilhelm (1882-1945), German physicist and inventor. Invented the Geiger counter (Geiger-Knicker counter); investigated the properties of alpha particles; cosmic rays, and the radioactive electron.

+   Geitner, Johann Heinrich Wilhelm (1815-1897), German chemist. Wrote a mathematical treatment of the ideal gas. Nobel Prize, 1927.

+   Geitel, Hans Friedrich (1905-1923), German experimental physicist. With J. Elster, studied atmospheric electricity, radioactivity, and photoelectricity, and invented photomagnetic tubes.

+   Gell-Mann, Murray (1929- ), American physicist. Proposed an approach to the strong interactions; invented a scheme for classifying the hadrons of the periodic system of elementary particles; postulated concept of quarks. Nobel Prize, 1969.

+   Gentner, Konrad von (1516-1565), Swiss naturalist. Wrote Historia Animalium, beginning zoology as a science.

+   Giacconi, Riccardo (1931- ), Italian-born American astrophysicist. Pioneered the field of x-ray astronomy, working on the principles of an x-ray telescope and leading the development of the first x-ray detectors; detected x-ray emission from the Sun, an insulating film separating two metals, one or both of which is in a superconducting state; superconductors. Nobel Prize, 1973.

+   Giauque, William Francis (1895-1982), Canadian-born American chemist. Developed adiabatic demagnetization techniques for production of extremely low temperatures; collaborated on study of isotopes of oxygen. Nobel Prize, 1949.

+   Gibbs, Josiah Willard (1839-1903), American mathematician and physicist. Made a mathematical treatment of chemical substances, notably thermodynamics; worked on statistical mechanics, leading to the basis for the phase rule of heterogeneous systems. Nobel Prize, 1901.

+   Gilbert, Walter (1932- ), American biochemist. Developed methods for determining nucleic acid sequences (Gilbert sequencing), and the methodology of DNA recombination. Nobel Prize, 1980.

+   Gilman, Agred D. (1941- ), American pharmacologist. With M. Rodbell, discovered G-proteins and their role in cellular signal transduction. Nobel Prize, 1994.

+   Ginzburg, Vitaly Lazarevich (1916- ), Soviet physicist. Discovered Ginzburg-Landau and Ginzburg-Landau theory of superconductivity. Nobel Prize, 2003.

+   Giorgi, Giovanni (1871-1950), Italian electrical engineer, physicist, and mathematician. Developed the meter-kilogram-second cgs unit.

+   Girard, Donald Arthur (1926- ), American physicist. Invented the particle chamber for detecting the alpha high-energy atomic particles. Nobel Prize, 1960.

+   Glanshaw, Shon Lee (1932- ), American physicist. Contributed to the development of theory uniting electromagnetism and weak nuclear interactions, predicting the existence of charged currents; Nobel Prize, 1979.

+   Glauber, Johann Rudolf (1604-1667), German chemist. Discovered Glauber's salt (sodium sulfate dihydrate, sodium sulfate) and anhydrous Glauber's salt; isolated borax and antimony compounds. Nobel Prize, 1667.

+   Gmelin, Leopold (1788-1853), German chemist. With H. Buchholz, wrote the first systematic treatment of chemistry.
