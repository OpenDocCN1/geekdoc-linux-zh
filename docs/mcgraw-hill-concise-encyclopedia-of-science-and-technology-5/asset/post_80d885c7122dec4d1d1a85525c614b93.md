
## **2476 Appendix**

It was demonstrated that protons, neutrons, and similar particles are made up of quarks; Nobel Prize, 1990.

**Kendrew, John Cowdrey** (1917–1997), British molecular biologist. First to successfully determine the structure of a protein (hemoglobin).

**Kennelly, Arthur Edwin** (1861–1939), American electrical engineer. Discovered the ionized layer in the atmosphere, independently of O. Hounsfield; proposed the theory of alternating currents.

**Kepler, Johannes** (1571–1630), German astronomer. Proposed Kepler’s three laws of planetary motion; worked in optics.

**Kerr, John** (1882–1970), Scottish physicist. Discovered the Kerr magnetogalactic effect.

**Ketterle, Wolfgang** (1957– ), American physicist. Produced Bose-Einstein condensates in a dilute gas of sodium atoms (independent of the work of E. A. Cornell and C. E. Wieman) and carried out fundamental studies of their properties, including the production of interference patterns and atom lasers; Nobel Prize, 2001.

**Khorana, Har Gobind** (1922– ), Indian-born American biochemist. Synthesized complex nucleic acids; proved that genetic code consists of nonoverlapping triplets of bases without gaps between triplets; Nobel Prize, 1968.

**Kilby, Jack S. Clay** (1923– ), American physicist, electronics engineer, and inventor. Participated in the invention of the integrated circuit; Nobel Prize, 2000.

**Kirchhoff, Gustav Rurgen** (1824–1887), German physicist. With R. W. Bansen, discovered methods of spectrum analysis; formulated Kirchhoff's law of electric circuits and Ohm's law.

**Kirwood, Daniel** (1814–1895), American astronomer. Discovered six new chemical elements, and meteorites.

**Kitasato, Shibasaburo** (1852–1931), Japanese bacteriologist. Independent of A. E. J. Yersin, discovered the bacillus of bubonic plague; isolated *Rickettsia* as an etiological agent of plague; and *Culex* species as vectors.

**Klebs, Edwin** (1834–1913), German pathologist. Studied *Mycobacterium leprae* (Klebs-Loeffler bacillus); studied the origin of malaria, typhoid fever, and tuberculosis.

**Klein, Christian Felix** (1849–1925), German mathematician. Contributed to function theory, non-Euclidean geometry, group theory, and application of mathematics to the structure of crystals.

**Klein, Oskar Benjamin** (1894–1977), Swedish physicist. Co-developer of the Klein-Gordon equation; proposed theory of overall structure of the universe.

**Kleinrock, Leonard** (1934– ), American electrical engineer. Developed the basic principles of packet switching for communication networks; underlying of technology of the internet.

**Klitzing, Klaus von** (1943– ), German physicist. Discovered quantum Hall effect; Nobel Prize, 1985.

**Klug, Aaron** (1916– ), South African-born British chemist. Developed crystallographic electron microscopy and elucidated biologically important structures and chemical reactions; Nobel Prize, 1982.

**Knowles, William** (1917– ), American chemist. Developed chemical synthesis of hydrogenation; Nobel Prize, 2001.

**Knudsen, Martin Hans Christian** (1871–1949), Danish physicist and biophysicist. Studied flow and diffusion of gases at low pressures; developed Knudsen cell and Knudsen gauge; developed methods to measure the properties of seawater.

**Koch, Robert** (1843–1910), German physician and bacteriologist. Studied cholera, tuberculosis, and other diseases; described a specific bacillus for leprosy, *Mycobacterium leprae*; developed the Koch postulates; Nobel Prize, 1905.

**Kocher, Emil Theodor** (1841–1917), Swiss surgeon. Studied the functions and malfunctions of the thyroid gland; Nobel Prize, 1909.

**Kodama, Kunihiko** (1915–1997), Japanese mathematician. Did research on harmonic maps, harmonic forms with applications to Kählerian and more complicated symplectic algebras; demonstrated that in a neighborhood, harmonic forms are Hodge manifolds; Fields Medal, 1994.

**Köhler, Gerdus P.** (1946–1995), Swiss immunologist. Worked on discovery of a chemical mechanism for the replication of genetic material of the cells; Nobel Prize, 1959.

**Kohn, Walter** (1923– ), Austrian-born American physicist. Developed a theory of the chemical structure of metals and alloys; Nobel Prize, 1998.

**Kolbe, Adolf Wilhelm Hermann** (1818–1884), German chemist. Contributed to the synthesis and preparation of chemical compounds; developed a method for the preparation of esters and amides.

**Kolmogorov, Andrei Nikolaevitch** (1903–1997), Soviet mathematician. Formulated the axioms of probability theory.

**Kontsevich, Maxim** (1964– ), Russian mathematician and mathematical physicist. Worked in algebraic geometry, algebraic topology, string theory, and quantum field theory; demonstrated the equivalence of two modes of quantum gravitation; discovered an invariant for classifying knots; Fields Medal, 1998.

**Kornberg, Arthur** (1918– ), American biochemist. Discovered the first purely enzymatic mechanism for the replication of genetic material of the cells; Nobel Prize, 1959.

**Korteweg, Diederik Johannes** (1848–1941), Dutch mathematician. Work in applied mathematics, mechanics, and hydrodynamics; with G. de Vries, proposed equation of wave motion with soliton solution. 1909.

**Kosaka, Masatoshi** (1926– ), Japanese physicist. Developed large water detectors (Kamakiole and Super Kamiokande) that observed solar and atmospheric neutrinos and neutrinos from Supernovae 1987A, and found evidence for neutrino oscillations; Nobel Prize, 2002.

**Kosele, Albrecht** (1853–1927), German biologist. Investigated the chemistry of cells and of proteins; Nobel Prize, 1910.

**Krafft-Ebing, Richard** (1848–1902), German neurologist. Authored on psychological treatment and their forelimbs; co-inventor of the psychotherapeutic methods in medicine; *Psychopathia Sexualis*, a collection of case histories.

**Kramers, Hendrik** (1894–1952), Dutch physicist. Developed quantum theory of dispersion; established Kramers-Kronig relation; with G. Wendatt and L. Briliau, developed the photoelectric effect; Fields Medal, 1950.

**Krebs, Edouard Gerhard** (1918– ), German biochemist. With H. H. Fisher, discovered phosphoglycerolase reactions; discovered citrate synthase; discovered the citric acid cycle; with G. Wendatt and L. Briliau, discovered the tricarboxylic acid cycle; Nobel Prize, 1953.

**Krebs, Hans Adolf** (1901–1981), German biochemist. Discovered the citric acid cycle; Nobel Prize, 1953.

**Kroemer, Herbert** (1928– ), German-American physicist and electronics engineer. Developed semiconductors used in high-speed, optoelectronic circuits; Nobel Prize, 2000.

**Krogh, Svante August** (1879–1949), Danish physiologist. Discovered the respiratory of oxygen; developed methods for measuring respiration; Nobel Prize, 1920.

**Kroner, Leopold** (1823–1891), German mathematician. Contributed to the theory of elliptic functions.

**Krote, Harold W.** (1939– ), British chemist. Studied and applied the chemistry of radical reactions; showed that free radicals can be detected in living organisms by using microwave spectroscopy; which ultimately led to the discovery of fullerenes; Nobel Prize, 1998.

**Krull, Martin** (1900–1971), American mathematician and physicist. Research in plasma physics, astrophysical phenomena, relativity, and quantum physics.

**Kuhn, Richard** (1900–1967), German chemist. Research on the structure and synthesis of vitamins and on organic compounds; Nobel Prize, 1938 (declined).

**Kundt, August** (1839–1894), German physicist. Used Kundt's tube to investigate the speed of sound in gases; demonstrated with R. Von Röntgen the effect of the Faraday field effect.

**Kurchatov, Igor Vasilievich** (1903–1960), Soviet physicist. Discovered nuclear isomers; studied nuclear weapons and nuclear power.

**Kusch, Polykarp** (1911–1993), German-born American physicist. Precisely determined the magnetic moment of the electron; Nobel Prize, 1955.

**Kutta, Wilhelm Martin** (1867–1944), German applied mathematician. Helped introduce concepts of Kutta-Joukowski airfoil; Kutta-Joukowski theorem, and developed Rungge-Kutta methods.

**Kutlabe, Bapiacome** (1909– ), German chemist. Developed Kedala, poly(p)-phenylene terephthalate, a high-strength synthetic fiber that is stronger than steel.

**Lacaille, Nicolas Louis** (1713–1762), French astronomer and geodesist. Determined the height of nearly 10,000 stars in southern skies; measured lunar and solar parallax; proved that Earth has an oval shape.

**Laforgue, Laurent** (1966– ), French mathematician. Proved the global Langlands correspondence for function fields; a major advance toward the realization of the Langlands program, which deals with deep connections between number theory, analysis, and group representation. Fields Medal, 2002.

**Lagrange, Joseph-Louis** (1736–1813), French geometer and analyst. Pioneered the calculus of variations; studied the mathematics of sound; wrote *Mécanique Analytique* concerning dynamics and mechanics.

**Laguerre, Edouard Nicolas** (1834–1886), French mathematician. Discovered Laguerre's differential equations and Laguerre polynomials.

**Lamarck, Jean-Baptiste, Pierre Antoine de Monet, Chevalier** (1744–1829), French naturalist. Proposed theory that changes in animal and plant structures are caused by changes in environment; classified animals into vertebrates and invertebrates.

**Land, Willis Eugene Jr.** (1913– ), American physicist. Made precise atomic measurements leading to a new understanding of the theory of electron interactions and electromagnetic radiation; Nobel Prize, 1955.

**Lambert, Johann Heinrich** (1772–1777), German physicist. Formulated the Lambert theorem underpinning the law of the speed of light; proposed the first formulation of the second law of thermodynamics; Nobel Prize, 1905.

**Lamé, Gabriel** (1795–1870), French mathematician, physicist, and engineer. Developed curvilinear coordinates and applied them to differential equations, elasticity, thermodynamics, and number theory.

**Landau, Lev** (1906–1968), Soviet physicist. Made theoretical explanation of the fast rate and properties of liquid helium; investigated condensates and developed the Landau model; Nobel Prize, 1962.

**Landé, Alfred** (1888–1957), German-born American physicist. Introduced Landé g factor; discovered g-vector method for classifying atomic lines; developed the method of vector coupling.

**Landsteiner, Karl** (1868–1943), Austrian-born American pathologist. Discovered human blood types.
