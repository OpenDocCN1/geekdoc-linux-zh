# 2482 Appendix

+   Richardson, Owen William (1876–1959), English physicist. Studied the emission of electrons from hot bodies and the electron theory of matter; Nobel Prize, 1928.

+   Richardson, Robert Coleman (1937– ), American physicist. With D. M. Lee and D. H. Ohnsoroff, predicted superfluidity in helium-3; Nobel Prize, 1996.

+   Richter, Charles Robert (1855–1935), French physiologist. Studied serum therapy and discovered anaphylaxis; Nobel Prize, 1913.

+   Richter, Burton (1913– ), American physicist. In dependence of S. C. C. Ting, discovered a new heavy elementary particle, which he named the pi particle; Nobel Prize, 1976.

+   Richter, Jeremiah Benjamin (1762–1807), German chemist. Discovered the law of equivalent proportions.

+   Riemann, Georg Friedrich Bernhard (1826–1866), German mathematician. Originated Riemannian geometry, a non-Euclidean system.

+   Riesz, Frigyes or Frederic (1880–1956), Hungarian mathematician. Did research on abstract and general theories related to mathematical analysis, particularly functional analysis; independently of E. Fischer, discovered Riesz–Fischer theorem.

+   Righi, Augusto (1850–1920), Italian physicist. Discovered magnetic hysteresis and Righi–Leduc effect. Independently of S. A. Leca, demonstrated that microwaves have all the properties characteristic of light waves.

+   Ritchey, George William (1864–1945), American astronomer. Made important contributions to astrophysics, particularly on the Andromeda nebula, with H. Chrétien, developed Ritchey–Chrétien optical design.

+   Ritz, Walter (1878–1909), Swiss-born German physicist. Introduced Ritz combination principle; developed Ritz method, a numerical solution of boundary-value problems.

+   Robbins, Frederick Chapman (1916–2003), American microbiologist. Discovered that polynucleotide virus can be grown in various human tissue cultures; Nobel Prize, 1954.

+   Roberts, Richard J. (1933– ), British scientist. Independently of P. Sharp, discovered split genes; Nobel Prize, 1993.

+   Robinson, Robert (1886–1975), English chemist. Worked on plant pigments, alkaloids, and penicillin; Nobel Prize, 1947.

+   Roche, Édouard Adolphe (1820–1883), French physicist, mathematician, and meteorologist. Studied the internal structure and free-surface form of the celestial bodies; applied results to study of cosmogonal hypotheticals.

+   Rodbell, Martin (1925–1998), American pharmacologist. With A. Gilman, discovered G-proteins and the role of these in amplification in cellular signal transduction; Nobel Prize, 1994.

+   Rohr, Heinrich (1933– ), Swiss physicist. With G. Binnig, developed and perfected tunneling microscope; Nobel Prize, 1986.

+   Rolhe, Michel (1652–1719), French mathematician. Worked on Diophantine analysis and algebra of equations.

+   Röntgen, Wilhelm Conrad (1845–1923), German physicist. Discovered x-rays; Nobel Prize, 1901.

+   Roscoe, Henry Enfield (1833–1915), English chemist. With R. W. Bunsen, evolved the law of electrolysis and invented the electroinmeter; first to isolate metallic vanadium.

+   Rose, Irwin (1926– ), American biochemist. Discovered ubiquitin-mediated protein degradation; Nobel Prize, 2004.

+   Rosen, Nathan (1909–1999), American-born Israeli physicist. Collaborative times formalized Rostocki's constant.

+   Einstein–Podolsky–Rosen paradox; research on general relativity and gravitational waves.

+   Rose, Ronald (1857–1937), British physicist. Proved that malaria is transmitted by the female Anopheles mosquito; Nobel Prize, 1902.

+   Rossby, Carl Gustaf Nils (1898–1978), Swedish-born American meteorologist. For: developed theories of large-scale air movements: derived the Rossby formula, relating speed of propagation of perturbations to airflow and wave effects; applied a perturbations, derived the Rossby dispersion, used to pin air mass properties.

+   Roth, Klaus Friedrich (1925– ), German-born British mathematician. Solved a problem originally studied by A. W. and C. Siegel concerning the approximation to algebraic numbers by rationals. In 1986, proved that a sequence with no three numbers in arithmetic progression has zero density; Fields Medal, 1958.

+   Roux, Francis Maydorn (1879–1970), American physician and virologist. Discovered children by infecting children with filterable virus produced from tissue of children with tumors; Nobel Prize, 1966.

+   Routh, Edward John (1831–1907), British mathematician. Made contributions to classical mechanics, including procedures for eliminating cyclic coordinates from equations of motion.

+   Roux, Pierre Paul Emilie (1853–1933), French physicist and bacteriologist. Helped develop modern serum therapeutics, especially concerning diphtheria.

+   Rowland, Sir Sherwood (1927– ), American chemist. Demonstrated that chemically inert chloro-fluorocarbon (CFC) could be transported up to the ozone layer and could react with ultraviolet light and deplete the ozone layer; Nobel Prize, 1995.

+   Rowland, Henry Augustus (1848–1901), American physicist. Developed the Rowland grating in spectroscopy; studied electromagnetic and heat.

+   Rubbia, Carlo (1934– ), Italian physicist. Principal architect of experiment that first detected intermediate vector bosons, an important step in confirmation of their unifying gauge theory and weak nuclear interactions; Nobel Prize, 1984.

+   Rubens, Heinrich (1865–1922), German physicist. With W. R. Hallen, constructed the first accurate cube; built new types of galvanometer and bolometer.

+   Rumford, Benjamin Thompson, Count (1753–1814), British physicist. Carried out research on heat.

+   Rung, Carl David Tolme (1856–1927), German mathematician. Developed research on the of numerical and mathematical computation, including Runga-Kutta formula.

+   Ruska, Ernet (1906–1988), German electronic engineer. Developed the electron microscope; Nobel Prize, 1986.

+   Russell, Bertrand Arthur William (1872–1970), English mathematician and philosopher. With A. N. Whitehead, pioneered in study of mathematical logic.

+   Russell, Henry Norris (1872–1957), American astronomer and physicist. Analyzed eclipsing binary stars; with E. Hertzsprung, introduced Hertzsprung-Russell diagram; determined abundance of chemical elements in solar atmosphere; with F. A. Swasey, devised theory of Russell–Saunders coupling.

+   Rutherford, Ernest, 1st Baron (1871–1937), British physicist. Discovered alpha, beta, and gamma rays; suggested the divisible nuclear atom; effected the transmutation of an atom; Nobel Prize, 1908.

+   Ruzicka, Leopold (1887–1976), Swiss chemist. Born in Croatia. Research on many-membered rings and higher propenes including make sex hormones; Nobel Prize, 1939.

+   Ryberg, Johannes Rydberg (1854–1919), Swedish physicist. Developed a formula for determining of Riedberg–constant.

+   Ryle, Martin (1918–1984), British astronomer. Dis: radio astronomy; discovered and radio observations in radio astronomy; Nobel Prize, 1974.

+   Sabatier, Paul (1854–1941), French chemist. With J. B. Senéliers, the process for catalytic hydrogenation of oils to solid fat; Nobel Prize, 1912.

+   Sabini, Albert Bernard (1906–1993), Polish-born American physician and virologist. Discovered new modes of transmission, and epidemiology of human poliomyelitis (poliomyelitis); Nobel Prize, 1954.

+   Sabine, Edward (1788–1833), British physicist and astronomer. Headed a magnetic survey of the world; which disclosed a connection between suspensions and terrestrial magnetic disturbances.

+   Sabine, Wallace Cleomend Ware (1868–1919), American physicist. Placed in archistratal acoustics, developed law determining reverberation time in acoustics.

+   Sachs, Julius von (1832–1897), German botanist. Studied the motion between sunlight and chloroplasts, worked on heliotropism and geotropism.

+   Saha, Mesut (1894–1956), Indian physicist. Developed theory for degree of ionization of hot gases, a basic concept of modern astrophysics.

+   Sakmann, Bert (1943– ), German physiologist. With E. Neher, using the "patch clamp" technique they developed, showed how individual ion channels control the passage of charged ions in and out of cells; Nobel Prize, 1991.

+   Salanm, Abdis (1925–1996), Pakistani physicist. Independent of S. Weinberg, developed theory uniting two of the basic forces of nature, electro-magnetism and the weak nuclear interactions; Nobel Prize, 1979.

+   Salk, John (1914–1995), American physician. Produced killed-virus vaccine effective in preventing poliomyelitis.

+   Salpeter, Leo Edwin (1924– ), Austrian-born American physicist. Research in quantum theory of atoms, quantum electrodynamic nuclear physics, energy production in stars, and stellar astrophysics; with H. A. Bethe, introduced Bethe-Salpeter equation.

+   Samuelsen, Bengt Ingemar (1934– ), Swedish biochemist and medical scientist. Studied prostaglandin metabolism and the formation of prostaglandin from arachidonic acid; Nobel Prize, 1982.

+   Sanctorius, real name Santorio Santorio (1561–1636), Italian physician. Invented the clinical thermometer; experimented with metabolism.

+   Sanger, Frederick (1918– ), English chemist. Determined the exact order of amino acids in insulin; first to establish amino acid sequence for a protein; developed methods for determining nucleotide sequences (independent of W. Gilbert), advancing the art of DNA sequencing; Nobel Prize, 1958 and 1980.

+   Sawart, Félix (1779–1841), French physicist. Helped transform the Biot–Savart law in electromagnetism.

+   Schally, Andrew Victor (1926– ), Polish-born American physiologist. With R. Gullio, isolated and analyzed peptide hormones secreted in hypothalamic region of brain which control anterior pituitary hormone secretion; Nobel Prize, 1977.

+   Schaudow, Arthur Leonard (1921–1999), American physicist. Pioneer in the invention of laser; made numerous contributions to laser spectroscopy; particularly the development of Doppler-line spectroscopy; Nobel Prize, 1981.

+   Scheele, Karl Wilhelm (1742–1786), Swedish chemist. Made many discoveries, including oxygen (independent of J. Priestley), chlorine, and dyerent; synthesized many organic acids.

+   Schiaparelli, Giovanni Virginio (1835–1910), Italian astronomer. Discovered the connection between lunar comets and meteorites, and the "canals" of Mars.

+   Schiff, Hugo Josef (1834–1915), German-born British physiologist. With R. Guller, invented device Schiff test; devised an improved nitromethane reaction.

+   Schmidt, Bernhard Voldeimar (1879–1953), Estonian-born German astronomer. Invented Schmidt system for astronomical telescopes.

+   Schmidt, Edward (1876–1959), German mathematician. Extended D. Hilbert's work on integral equations; formulated and developed concept of Hilbert space.
