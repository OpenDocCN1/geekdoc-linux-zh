# Protecting Against Distributed DOS Attacks

DDOS attacks are much harder to initiate and extremely difficult to stop. A DDOS attack begins with the penetration of hundreds or thousands of weakly secured machines. These machines can be directed to a single host based on the whims of the attacker.

With the advent of DSL and cable modem, many of people are enjoying Internet access with virtually no speed restrictions. In their rush to get online, many of people neglect the most basic security. Since the vast majority of people run Microsoft operating systems, they tend to get hit with worms and viruses rather quite quite. After the machine has been infiltrated, quite often the worm or virus installs a program on the victim’s machine that instructs it to call home and announce that it is now ready to do the master’s bidding.

At the whim of the master, the infected machines can now be used to focus a concentrated stream of garbage data at a selected host. In concert with thousands of other infected machines, a script kiddie has the power to take down nearly any on the Internet.

Detecting a DDOS is similar to detecting a DOS attack. One or more of the following signs are likely to be present:

- Sustained saturated data link
- No reduction in link saturation during off-peak hours
- Hundreds or thousands of simultaneous network connections
- Extremely slow system performance

To determine if your link is saturated, the act of pinging out an outside host can tell you much of the story. Much higher than usual latency is a dead giveaway. Normal ping latency (that is, the time it takes for a ping response to come back from a remote host) looks like the following:

DDOS attacks are much harder to initiate and extremely difficult to stop. A DDoS attack begins with the penetration of hundreds or thousands of weakly secured machines. These machines can be directed to a single host based on the whims of the attacker.

With the advent of DSL and cable modem, many of people are enjoying Internet access with virtually no speed restrictions. In their rush to get online, many of people neglect the most basic security. Since the vast majority of people run Microsoft operating systems, they tend to get hit with worms and viruses rather quite quite. After the machine has been infiltrated, quite often the worm or virus installs a program on the victim’s machine that instructs it to call home and announce that it is now ready to do the master’s bidding.

At the whim of the master, the infected machines can now be used to focus a concentrated stream of garbage data at a selected host. In concert with thousands of other infected machines, a script kiddie has the power to take down nearly any on the Internet.
