# Software Patents

Few will argue that it is illegal for someone to copy a software's code and redistribute it without permission. The concept of being able to patent an idea that many company might incorporate in its code could be a major point of contention in years. Can someone patent the idea of clicking an icon to open a window?

Software companies are scrambling to find thousands of patents related to how software is used. While these companies may never create products based on those patents, the restrictions those patents might place on other software companies or open source software development is a major issue.

The EuroLinux Alliance is a group dedicated to "protecting software freedom based on copyright, open standards, competition, and open source, such as Linux." EuroLinux is filing a petition in the European Parliament and European Council to warn about the dangers of software patents. To find out more, go to http://eurolinux.org.

# Other Linux Issues

Particularly contentious legal issues surround audio and video software. In Red Hat Linux 8, Red Hat, Inc. removed support for MP3 and DVD players because of questions licensing associated with music and movie formats. Red Hat's advice at the time was to download and install the players yourself for personal use. Red Hat did not want to distribute those players because companies owning patents related to audio and video encoders asked Red Hat to pay fees for distributing those players (see www.redhat.com/advice/speaks_80mm.html).

Check with an attorney regarding any legal issues that concern you.
