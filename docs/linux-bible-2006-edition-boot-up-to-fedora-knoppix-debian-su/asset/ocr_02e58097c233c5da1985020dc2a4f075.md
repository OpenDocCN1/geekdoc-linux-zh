# Slackware Users

From a purely subjective perspective, my friends who use Slackware tend to be technically oriented, but not the extreme overclockers and tweakers who might be drawn to Gentoo, for example. They like Slackware because it works so simply and so that they believe it gives them more time to slack.

Slackware users often think of themselves as losers, despite the fact that they hang out at LAN parties and Internet cafes. They like the more unique, less commercial approach of Slackware. For their personal desktop, gaming box, or small-office server, they see no need for the graphical tools that you get with Red Hat or SUSE Linux systems. They are comfortable with commands and managers.

I've often heard users refer to Slackware as being easier to use than other Linux distributions. To someone coming from a UNIX or BSD background, this is probably true. You don't have to wait for graphical tools to pop up and everything is covered on a page.

# Slackware Internet Sites

The Slackware home page (www.slackware.com) is a good place to start looking for information about Slackware. There are two main mailing lists plus IRC channel available through the Web site, as well as links to download sites, some documentation, and the Slackware Store (store.slackware.com).

There's Slack Linux Essentials online book (slackware.com/book) and four FAQs (slackware.com/faq) available from the Slackware site. There's also no "news" to speak of at the Slackware site, the best way to keep up on what's happening with the project is to read the changelog (slackware.com/changelog).

Outside of the official site, a lot of new sites have been popping up recently to provide information about Slackware. A good place to bring questions about Slackware is http://linuxquestions.org (follow the links: Linux Distributions: Slackware). The Linux Packages site (www.linuxpackages.net) offers some active Forums on different aspects of Slackware.
