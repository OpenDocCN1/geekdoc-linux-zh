## GeeBoxX

GeeBoxX (www.geebox.org) is another bootable multimedia player distribution. From the screen that appears after GeeBoxX, you can use your cursor to select the location of the content you want to choose. Like MovXi, you can play a variety of audio and video content. It boots up on the network, so you can use audio and video content.

Because GeeBoxX is so small (just a few megabytes), you can fit easily on a mini-CD, bootable business card, or a pen drive (provided your computer can be booted from those media). There is no graphical interface; you just use the keyboard to select content and simple controls from menus.

Arrow keys to move among the few GeeBox selections (Option, Controls, Options, Help, and Quit). Press Enter to make a selection. You can open a file from hard disk, a music playlist, directory of images, or removable media (DVD, VCD/XCD, or audio CD) containing video content. Press M to show or hide menus and P to pause.

## KnoppMyth

I've added KnoppMyth here as it represents a new and interesting class of bootable Linux distributions. MythTV is a fairly complex set of software used to configure an entertainment center that can include a personal video recorder (complete with downloaded local TV listing and tools for managing recording and playback), music player, weather center, and tools for getting news and other information.

KnoppMyth is a CD database based on KNOPPIX that is intended to help simplify getting a MythTV installation and running. Boot up KnoppMyth, answer a few questions, and MythTV is installed on your hard disk.

KnoppMyth also includes another nice feature: a MythTV front. With MythTV configured on your LAN, you can use the KnoppMyth disk to boot up a MythTV front. That way you can use your MythTV entertainment center from any TV on your local area network.

## Dynebolic:

The Dynebolic GNU/Linux live bootable CD (www.dybolic.org) provides a full range of multimedia production tools on a single bootable operating system. The distribution is intended for artisans who want to create and work with a variety of digital media: audio, video, digital images, HTML, and so.

Tools included with Dynebolic include Muse, TerminatorX, GDam, SoundTracker and PD (for mixing and streaming audio). Video editors include Kino, Cinerella and LIVES. For 3D modeling, there is Blender. For image manipulation, Dynebolic includes GIMP. Bluefish is for creating web pages.

## Tiny Desktops

A small CD in the shape of a business card, can fit in your wallet. A USB drive can hang from your keychain. There are whole bootable Linux distributions that let you boot a device with you you can connect to the Internet, browse the Web, play music, and receive e-mail, do instant messaging, write documents, and work with spreadsheets. And they can do all that in 50MB of space removable media.

> CD databases are really regular CDs that have been cut into the shape of a business card. Depending on the one you choose, it can hold from 40MB to 52MB of data. A mini-CD can hold about 180MB of data. You can purchase these CDs in bulk from many locations that sell regular CDs, and you can play them in any CD drive. (However, it's best to use these CDs in trays that have a mini-CD inset because they have been known to loose and break CDs.)

Many loose Linuxes are either based on KNOPPIX or the Bootable Business Card project (www.lnx-bbc.org). I know of several Linux Users Groups that have tailored their bootable business card projects from the lnx-bbc org project to hand out to represent their groups. Many bootable Linux distributions for media having capacity that is larger than that of a business-card-size CD to be based on KNOPPIX.

Two examples of tiny Linux distributions are Damn Small Linux and Puppy Linux.

Dann Small's default desktop is pretty simple. The window manager is the powerful, yet efficient, FluxBox window manager (based on BlackBox). Right-click the desktop to see a menu of features you can select. Here are a few things you want to do when you first boot up Dunn Small:

- * Enhance your desktop—Right-click to see the Dunn Small menu, and then select Desktop Full Enhanced Desktop. This adds some icons to your desktop applications, some apples in the lower right corner to display system information, and a workspace editor. Select Desktop again if you want to change the Styles (colors and window borders), or Configuration to change desktop behavior.

- * Get a network connection—If you don't automatically get the Internet at boot time, select System Net Setup from the Dunn Small menu. Then you can configure to your Ethernet card, DSL connection, dial-up modem, or wireless card.

- * Browse the web—Dann Small comes with the Dillo web browser. The browser is simple, fast, and can run on any X manager because it doesn't require GNOME libraries.

- * Configure and read email—The Spyeed client is very close to perfect and runs fast. Select Apps Net Spyeed to open it. Configure it and you can be up and reading your e-mail within a few minutes.

- * Try other applications—Right-click and look through the menu for applications that interest you. See descriptions of those applications, visit http://damsmallux.org/applications.html.

- * Get other applications—Select the MySQL on the desktop to select of applications you can choose from. If you are connected to the Internet, you can see lists of applications in each category to choose for downloading and installing.

> Note You can get other DSL files that let you download other applications from your desktop as well. Visit www.damnsmall.org and select the link to packages to my MySQL repository.

Dann Small Linux has recently added some excellent customization features. For example, packages you download, desktop settings, and configuration information can be saved across reboots. By creating a list of packages and files you want to save, these files and packages can be saved to a backup file that is stored on your hard disk or any removable medium. The next time you reboot, you can tell Dunn Small Linux to find that backup file and all settings and applications will be inserted into your current Dunn Small session.

More information about using Dunn Small Linux is available at the project FAQ page, www.damnsmall.org/faq.html.
