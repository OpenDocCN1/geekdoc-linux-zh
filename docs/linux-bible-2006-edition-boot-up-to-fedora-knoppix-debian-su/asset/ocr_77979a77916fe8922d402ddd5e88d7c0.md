Fair-use rule is a principle that permits someone other than the owner of the copyright to use the copyrighted material in a reasonable and limited manner without his consent.

+   Users can sell copyrighted works that they own. You can sell books, DVDs, audio, and other materials as long as you are not retaining a copy for yourself, or (of course) selling copies of the work without permission from the copyright holder. Some people arguing in file trading with copyrighted materials claim that the DMCA infringes on their ability to “share” contended “own”. In fact, under existing copyright law they do not “own” the copyrighted material and certainly do not possess the right to redistribute the content unless they are reselling in an allowed manner.

+   Copyright will expire at time in the future and fall into the public domain. This point raises the same issue as with the first item: Your DVD movie enters the public domain (eventually), but to freely copy the content you may circumvent the protection inherent on the DVD and by doing so, you run of all of the DMCA.

It is important to realize the DMCA is very vague how it defines many of the acts that are illegal. What is a “protection scheme?” Some argue that it could be nearly anything. Many pundits fear that the DMCA can be used to curtail the use of nondirectional copyrighted works such as books because the law is vague in defining its bounds.

While the courts are trying to clarify what the legal line is in any particular situation, there's a problem in that often, the company seeking to protect its copyrights is a large corporation or the defendant is a new company or an individual user. and the broad scope of the DMCA essentially prevents the “little guy” from making his case, because he cannot afford to.

> In 1998 a law known as the Sonny Bono Copyright Term Extension Act or CTEA was passed. This act actually the lengthy copyright protection period (generally 70 years) and extended it by another 20 years, preventing several valuable properties, including film and images of Steamboat Willie (the first Mickey Mouse), from entering the public domain.

The question of practicality is very important when examining copyright issues. In other words, what does this mean to you as a Linux user? Well, it means that you have to use any trickery to copy MP3s off your CD collection, you could be breaking the law.
