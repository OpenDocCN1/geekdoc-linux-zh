# Watching TV with Vptime

The vptime program (vtime command) enables you to display video output—television channels, in particular—on your desktop. You can change the channels, adjust volume, and fine-tune your picture. In addition, vptime sports a slick onscreen display and support for a widescreen display.

> > Vptime will not display output from some low-quality Webcams. To use your Webcam, consider obtaining the xawt package, which is available for most Linux distributions.

The following sections describe how to choose a TV capture card and use vptime to watch television on your desktop.

# Getting a Supported TV Card

Video4Linux (V4L1/V4L2) is the video interface available for Linux. It supports a variety of TV capture cards and cameras, and is included in some distributions. If your distribution does not include V4L or V4L2, you can install it on your own, although it is not the easiest task to accomplish. For more information about and installing V4L and the appropriate driver, visit http://linux.bytex.org/v4l2/index.html.

To see a list of supported TV cards that you can use with vptime, refer to the CARDLIST and Cards of your V4L installation. You will find the kernel-source package installed. In /src/linux*/Documentation/video4linux/btt/Cards on your Linux system. The Cards file applies to the Video4Linux btt driver. In addition, look at all files starting with CARDLIST in /src/linux*/Documentation/video4linux/CARDLIST*.

Video4Linux is designed to autodetect your TV capture card and load the proper modules to activate it. Install the TV-card hardware (with the appropriate connection to your TV reception), boot Linux, and run the vptime command as described in the next section. See video displayed on your vptime window.

If your card appears to be working, here are a few things you can try:

+   - Check that your TV card is properly seated in its slot and detected by Linux by typing:
 /sbin/lspci

This shows you a list of all PCI cards on your computer. If your card doesn't show up, you probably have a hardware problem.

+   - It is possible that the card is there but the right card type is not being detected. Improper detection is most likely if you have a card for which there are several revisions, each requiring a different driver.
