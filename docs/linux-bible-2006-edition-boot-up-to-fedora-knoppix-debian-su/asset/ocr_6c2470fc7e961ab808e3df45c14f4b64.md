The brief history of Lininspire is colorful and has been portrayed as a “David and Goliath” story, at least in some of the news media. In this case Lininspire is David and a PC desktop market is Goliath. While Goliath seems to have gotten away, to some extent, David is still breathing.

Lininspire is a commercial, desktop-oriented Linux system that has made a play for the retail market. Lininspire, Inc. has marketed itself as a direct competitor to Microsoft Windows by offering boxed sets of Lininspire, as well as preinstalled versions on sub-$300 PCs.

This chapter describes what Lininspire is, its major features, how to install it, and where to find more information.

# Overview of Lininspire

Lininspire began as Lindows, founded by Michael Robertson in 2001, after his tenure with MP33.com. His goal was to bring Linux to the desktop once and for all.

The general concept was to develop an operating system that would be inexpensive, easy to install and use, and a competitive alternative to Microsoft Windows on the desktop PC. To accomplish that, Lindois needed to be simple enough for a non-technical user (even someone who has never really used a PC before) to install, configure, and use. While this is, indeed, a laudable goal, most of this product’s press as the result of its tension with Microsoft Corporation.
