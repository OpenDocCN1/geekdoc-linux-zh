# Exploring Codecs

If you want to play a video or audio file, you need the appropriate codec installed and ready for your media player. A codec is a software-compressor-decompressor to use existing digital audio/video data and reduce the size of the content while retaining the quality of the output.

If you encounter a media file that you know is a working, playable file and you cannot play the file, you might need to identify and install the proper codec. This often involves installing the proper playback application, such as DivX 5.0.5 for Linux, which installs the MPEG4 for video and audio playback.

There are many codecs available, getting the ones you need is usually not an issue. Advances in codec technology have continued to increase the quality of the encoded content, while reducing file size. Fortunately most widely distributed videos and audio files (from News sites, for example) are created using a few common codecs.

While there are many using encoding standards, there are also a slew of proprietary codecs in use today as well. This is a battleground of sorts with each vendor/developer trying to produce the superior standard and obtain the spoils of market share that can follow. For the end, this means you might have spent time chasing a variety of playback utilities to handle multiple video and audio formats.

Another debate continues: Can digital media match the quality of analog formats? This hardly seems of a question anyone because DVD has shown the potential for high-quality digital video, and MPEG codecs have made huge strides in digital audio fidelity.
