# Setting Up Audio Cards

To start your "quadraphonic wall of sound," you need to have a sound card in your PC. A sound card can be an add-PCI (or even ISA card), or it can be integrated on your motherboard. Your card will have a ton of uses—from gaming to audio/video playback. Having a multimedia system just't the same without sound.

Fortunatelyately most PCs include a sound card, often of the integrated variety. In the rare case that one isn't included (the slightly more common case where it's not supported in Linux), you can add a supported sound card starting for a few dollars. If you're really pinched, check out ebay, where you probably can get a decent SoundBlaster (the *standard*) compatible card for next to nothing.

> Note
If you try the procedures in this book but still don't have a working sound card, visit the Advanced Linux Sound Architecture at www.alsa-project.com, home of the ALSA sound architecture. ALSA is the preferred sound architecture for Linux and is built into the Linux kernel itself (with the 2.6 kernel). The ALSA offers support, information, and help.

The following list summarizes the basic features that are included in the popular SoundBlaster family of sound cards:

+   * Sound recording and playback — The card can convert analog sound into 8-bit or 16-bit digital numbers. To convert the sound, the board samples the sound in waves from 5 KHz to 48 KHz, or 5,000 to 48,100 times per second. The higher the sampling rate, the better the sound and larger the output files.
