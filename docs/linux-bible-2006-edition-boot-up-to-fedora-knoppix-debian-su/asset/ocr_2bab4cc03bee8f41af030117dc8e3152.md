**Network** — MovXi² boots onto the network if a DHCP server is detected.
Although the friendly user interface doesn't appear to support it yet, software in MovXi² should enable you to get content from your LAN or the Internet to play back using NFS (UNIX file sharing) or FTP (standard Internet file sharing facility) file server.

MovXi² boots right up to MPlayer, so you can eject the MovXi² disk, insert a CD, DVD, or VCD into your drive and play any supported content. Right-click the top to see your choices for selecting content.

If you are comfortable moving around in Linux, you can go to virtual terminals using eMovXi. Press Ctrl+Alt+F2 to view a sound mixer or Ctrl+Alt+F3 to go to a Linux shell. Press Ctrl+Alt+F4 to get back to the main screen (with MPlayer). Select Switch MovXi² from the menu, and you can choose to run your audio player, slide show, or TV (the latter if you have a television installed).

If you think MovXi is cool, you'll really like the idea of eMovXi project. eMovXi, you put a mini-MovXi distribution on a CD or DVD with your video, so that your video content comes with its own bootable player!(See http://sourceforge.net/Docs/eMovXi for details.)

At the time of this writing, MovXi² had recently released 1.0. That it has reached a fairly stable form. While there are still some rough edges to it, eMovXi is fun to play.

> Note
BothMovXi and MovXi² are described in the book Toys II by Christopher Negus, in a project devoted to making your own bootable movies.

# GeeBoxX

GeeBoxX (www.geebox.org) is another bootable multimedia player distribution. From the screen that appears after GeeBoxX, you can use your cursor to select the location of the content you want to choose. Like MovXi, you can play a variety of audio and video content. It boots up on the network, so you can use audio and video content.

Because GeeBoxX is so small (just a few megabytes), you can fit easily on a mini-CD, bootable business, or a pen drive (provided your computer can be booted from those media). There is no graphical interface; you just use the keyboard to select content and simple controls from menus.

Arrow keys to move among the few GeeBox selections (Option, Controls, Options, Help, and Quit). Press Enter to make a selection. You can open a file from hard disk, a music playlist, directory of images, or removable media (DVD, VCD/XCD, or audio CD) containing video content. Press M to show or hide menus and P to pause.
