GnomeMeeting does not support the NetMeeting shared whiteboard functions, just videoconferencing.

To be able to send, you need a Webcam supported in Linux—you’ll find a few dozen models from which to choose. The following sections show you how to set your Webcam and use GnomeMeeting for videoconferencing.

# Getting a Supported Webcam

As with TV capture cards, Webcam support is provided through the video412 interface. If you see if your webcam is supported, check the `/src/linux*/Documentation` directory. A few parallel-port cameras are described in the video412 subdirectory; however, the bulk of the supported cameras are listed in the usb directory.

After some research, I purchased a Logitech QuickCam Pro 3000. The driver for this Webcam was made for a Philips USB Webcam, but it also works for Webcams from Logitech, Samsung, Creative Labs, and Askey. After making the purchase, I checked out the driver’s description at www.saillard.org/linux/pwd.

Supported USB cameras should be autodetected, so that when you plug them in, the necessary modules are loaded automatically. Start up GnomeMeeting (gnomemeeting command), and you should see video from your Webcam on your Linux desktop.

You can check that your Webcam is working by typing the following:

```
$ l1smod 43392 1
pwc 5120 2 [pwc]
usbcore 59072 1 [audio pwc usb-hci]
```

The output from l1smod shows that the pwc driver is loaded and associated with the videodev module and usscore module.

# Opening Your Firewall for GnomeMeeting

You need to open a variety of ports in your firewall to use GnomeMeeting. In particular, you need to open TCP port 1720 and TCP port range 30000 to 30010. For UDP ports, you must open ports 5000 through 5007 and ports 5010 through 5013. Examples of exact iptables settings you can use to open these ports are contained in the GnomeMeeting FAQ (www.gnomemeeting.org/faq).
