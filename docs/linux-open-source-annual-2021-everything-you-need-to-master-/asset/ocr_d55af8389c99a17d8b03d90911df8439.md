# OPEN SOURCE GAME ENGINES

Like the Revenant in Doom II. Jonni Bidwell brings the corpses of lost video games back to life with a lil' bit of FOSS magic

one of us Linux Format are as young as we used to be. But our considerable age means we remember some fantastic games.

Some of these go really back: Exile, Elite, Reptun, and the model Bundium and Maniac Mansion on the C64. Most of our youth playing Arena games, Monkey Island taught us to sizeof, and who could forget those Bitmap Brothers

classics: Xenon2, Speedball 2, and The Chaos Engine? Just talking about them makes us want to fire up an emulator and relive halcyonydays.

But emulation can be clunky, and getting hold of ROMs and the like is tricky. Even with DOSBox the magic of open source though we can do better. Many classic titles have had their engines reverse engineered or revamped, so that the original game

assets can be used seamlessly in a modern system. This can make for a completely authentic experience, or allow new features (graphics, proper network play) to be added.

More importantly, it enables us to show those young deppers what gaming was like back in the day. Before the days of running in a slick 3D environment, chasing in game purchases, and to swear in Russian by listening to in-gamer chat.
