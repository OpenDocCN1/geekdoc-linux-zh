We installed all the desktops inside an Arch installation. Most of them are in of the officially supported repositories, though some in the community-powered Arch User Repository (AUR). We've rated the desktops on various fronts, such as their availability and cache of default apps. Maintainability is also another important criteria when choosing a desktop: one that ships with loads of tweakable settings will score higher than one that has a limited number of modifiable parameters, people usually swap their defaults DEs because of a rigid configuration. # Availability

Can you find it in a repository near you?

With the exception of a couple, all the desktops in this round have options in the repositories of mainstream desktops. Arch, however, leads the pack, in that gives you access to all the options via official and community-supported repositories. You'll also get mileage thanks to flat in which implements its repositories.

Similarly, Ubuntu with its PPAs enables you to get your hands on a wide number of DEBs. One word of advice though: the quality of the community-maintained repositories varies widely from anywhere between rock-solid to barely usable. You might not get the best experience when using a community-supported repository as compared to the official.

LXQt and Openbox are the options that remain in every distribution. LXQt has an enumerable list of applications it supports natively, and you can find them in Fedora. Openbox is also supported in the repositories.

Lumina is primarily developed for Debian, but also has ported to Linux distributions. The project lists some that are available in Fedora, but you can't find them in the Cop repository.
