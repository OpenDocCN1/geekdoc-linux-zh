However, this should be your consideration unless you want to go beyond technical diagrams or business charts. If you are interested in more artistic use, advanced features and other tools will come to fore.

Productivity is, of course, particularly important, especially for business users. However, such as ease-of-use and the provision of user documentation and tutorials, when together, there doesn't one clear leader in this area.

Licensing will concern some users personally, as all are free except LibreOffice is open source. There is also the question over the future of the free version of Gravit Designer, which has now been joined by the commercial product

On the subject of portability, the availability of both online and offline function can help, but Gravit Designer and Vectr offer both, a locally installed copy of Vectr can only be used with a connection to the internet.

Collaboration may be relevant to some users, which differ in how well they support by a wide range of import and export files, being available on all operating systems. Inkscape is here and shoulders above all of the others.

In terms of specific support for collaborative editing, though, Google Drawings is the clear winner, despite its basic drawing facilities somewhat behind the competition.
