# Welcome to Linux & Open Source Annual 2021

Free and Open Source Software (FOSS) has become an international phenomenon in recent years. The FOSS philosophy of protecting user freedoms sets it apart from many commercial software providers. And by constantly updating and improving, it pushes the boundaries of what can be achieved with software, allowing you freedom in other ways: to create, to hack (in the tinkering sense, behave yourself!), to explore new ways of doing things. We've crammed as much exciting information as we possibly can into this annual, including the hottest stories and FOSS for a wide range of fun and practical applications. This collection is truly an abundance of Linux and open source knowledge, ready for you to jump right into. So, what are you waiting for?

# Linux milestones

Many battles were fought and won – to get the distros to where they are today. Here's a quick recap...

Linux distributions have come a long way since the good old days. In the beginning, of course, there were no distributions, then there was a big bang! You'd start with the kernel, some bare bootstrap system, fetch GNU tools, with mass files, compile packages. They install, realise you'd got your Makefile wrong, mess up to the max. Rinse, repeat, and it was great.

More often than not to get these things on unless you had CD or floppy disk to access the internet (or a friend in a computer science department). Then in 1992 some SLS, which inspired Slackware and later followed creating Debian. The first live CD was launched shortly after SLS, which was a generous 512MB of RAM and a gargantuan amount of disk space. The first version of Debian didn't appear until 1996, by which time Red Hat was on the scene and all of a sudden people realised there was money to be made from Linux.
