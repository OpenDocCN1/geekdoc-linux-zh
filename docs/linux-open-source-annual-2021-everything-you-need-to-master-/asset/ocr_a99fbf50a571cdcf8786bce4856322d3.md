# HOW WE TESTED...

![](bbox=[0.313, 0.413, 0.94, 0.743])

Many of the OSes in Roundup suggest that users first experiment with them inside the safe environment of a virtual machine. We need their advice and run all of them configured per the specification mentioned on their websites, before subjecting them to physical hardware.
Our intention is to hunt for an alternate OS that enables us to use the computer productively. Sure, we don't expect to stream movies via games via Netflix, but they can play multimedia the web, do word processing and without without unexpected errors. We test various parameters such as installation and app, with the sole intention of helping regularly open OS that takes us out of our zone of familiarity – with the least of compromise with our comfort.

> Being a regular reader, you'll know full well that Linux isn't the only operating system that you can pluck from the internet for free. Notwithstanding our love for Linux, we regularly review open OSes as well, particularly the BSDs.
Non-linux open OSes are designed for niche uses and won't be of much in specialized environments. They might not possess the dexterity of Linux, which can scale from your notebook to a server, a few of them perform well in multiple environments, including the desktop. We'll introduce you to

some of the best options for desktop tasks in Roundup.
Despite the fact they've been development over two decades, many of these alternative OSes are actually in the list of favorites primarily because they don't get the same attention from the open source ecosystem as many Linux distributions. Most of the OSes in Roundup have a really (and an even smaller band of dedicated users), and none of them have the backing of non-profit corporations to accelerate their development – yet continue to.
