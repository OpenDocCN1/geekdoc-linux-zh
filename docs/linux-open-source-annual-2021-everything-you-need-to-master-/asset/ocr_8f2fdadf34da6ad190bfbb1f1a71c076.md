# # Photo editors

Keen photographer Alexander Tolstoy keeps his imperfect snaps, for fixing later, using some of the best photo editors available for Linux.

# HOW WE TESTED...

Editing photos isn't the same as editing general images. Therefore we decided to compare some of the best editors for Linux with a focus on fixing common faults, enhancing colour and lost details that might be considered gone for.

The aim here is help you select the most feature-packed and practical software that can bring dull images back to life. We'll take a closer look at their advanced features, such as automatic enhancements, the ability to fix dull, blurry, or over-exposed images, and whether or not they handle RAW files.

It's not the first time we've run a Roundup on image photo editors, but this current group includes a different selection of software. Out of the five open source tools, Ufraw had a better application in the past. The fifth is a commercial edition, which is well known to the reader, but should be of great interest to photo-editing enthusiasts. And to find out more!

With comes to select an editor, there's plenty of choice. There are general-purpose editors like Gimp, Pinta, image managers with such as Digikam or Darktable, and finally software for artists like Krita. Our interest lies in fixing photos and processing RAW files, so we grabbed Gimp with its set of built-in elements, Ufraw plugin, and lots of third-party plugins from FX Foundry and GIMP. Then we included

Darktable and Rawtherape – two classic darkroom editors tools for dragging out barely visible details. Lightzone is similar to these two programs, but has a more consumer-friendly interface. Last but not, Pixeluvo is a program with a limited set of editing tools. Gimp is the only one that does use a UI theme by default. Although a theme adds a 'pro' feel to the program, it won't hide the truth in our Roundup!
