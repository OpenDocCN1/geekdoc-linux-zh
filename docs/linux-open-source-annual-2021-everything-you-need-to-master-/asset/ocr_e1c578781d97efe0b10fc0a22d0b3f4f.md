# Photo editors

# The Verdict

The winner of this month's Roundup was easy to identify. GIMP Image Manipulation Program, Gimp, is an undisputed champion and the best choice for photographers, regardless of their workflow. Gimp is the best for painting and retouching, but it's also the most reasonable choice for repairing, replacing and adding variety with third party extensions from GMIC and FX. If you need to process RAW images, Gimp-can integrate with Ufraw and Rawtherape to use them as image sources. It's a versatile and comprehensive software package.

Deciding on the positions of this month's programs was more difficult because they came close to each other. Securing second place is Rawtherape, with its superb colour correction tools and a stronger denoising algorithm. It is hard to tell if Rawtherape is better than Darktable – both are excellent RAW files. Darktable is versatile but doesn't have a capable white balance fix, which is why it lost a few points.

The next is Lightzone which brings us up, though not that it's a bit perfect. However, we were disappointed that Lightzone—which has a poor noise level RAW processor—is well equipped with tools for cloning and removing spots, but denoising could just be a little better.

So, for most cases we recommend using Gimp and Rawtherape, either separately or by running Rawtherape as a RAW import for Gimp. If you need better noise level and grain, we have at Pixeluv, which has everything for amateur photo processing. We don't dislike Lightzone; it has certain advantages as a good second-tier photo-editing program.

# ## ALSO CONSIDER

Some people will argue that *After Shot* from Corel, as well as some heavy weight open source software with a price to touch, and we certainly won't turn our group test with OSIMP versus proprietary software.

*Digikam* or *Krita* are also worth mentioning. *Digikam* is very good and delightful to work with, but it focuses on editing and shooting photos other than editing and processing. *Krita* is a great tool for

artists, comics, writers and artists, but it's less robust when used for editing photos. Other alternatives as well exist, and you may have read about them in issues of *Linux Format* (for example, *Photo* (see UKFOB) and Fotoxx, to just two). We of course, acknowledge these programs and other than one free software for editing photos, but for this *Roundup* we selected the most powerful and feature-rich titles that can use on a daily basis.
