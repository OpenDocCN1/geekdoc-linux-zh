# # Lightweight desktops
nThe Verdict

Taking account launch times in an all, the options in this Roundup are equally lightweight and responsive, and performance. It also isn't give us much to choose in terms of distribution. We will concentrate on their other aspects and determine our winner using the tried-and-tested method of elimination.

Lumina loses out the simple reason that it wasn't designed for Linux. It also is not available in the repos of many distributions, which makes installation a task for most users.

We are really paid to deify. Quite a polish finish, especially it performs extremely well and doesn't score badly in terms of usability. However the project isn't as developed as its peers, and you can't find it in our favourite lists.

Moksha gets on the podium for its beautiful enlightenment pedigree. It not only good but very responsive and light on resources. With most desktops designed for a specific distribution, Moksha doesn't always pretty across other distributions.

Both LXQt and Openbox are mature projects that have been around for several years. Openbox is surely faster of the two, but it's a bare-bones window manager; with its usability you will have to spend some time assembling various components and writing a custom configuration file to create a desktop environment. While it doesn't as much effort as that, it's still an much better solution compared to LXQt.

LXQt is the result of the merger of Lumina and Razor-Qt desktops. It boots very quickly and makes use of available resources. Despite consuming a fraction of the resources of its mainstream rivals, LXQt looks good thanks to its Qt underpinnings, which are good for its slightly higher resolution than others.

That said, LXQt will feel at home on a modern machine, but it is still enough to push an out-of-commission computer back into active.
