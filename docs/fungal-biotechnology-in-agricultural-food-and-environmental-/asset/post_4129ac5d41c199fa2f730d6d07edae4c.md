## 5 CONCLUSIONS

The original purpose of isolating beneficial microorganisms from soil, from the rhizosphere of plants, was to obtain microorganisms, which showed a growth effect in plants. In this effect, such studies have investigated direct suppress effects on pathogens. Due to the large amount of research undertaken over the years, we know that this view is limited. This chapter has focused on mycorrhizal fungi all of which show growth promotion and disease suppress, and the mechanisms of mineralization, hormone production, antagonism, and induced resistance have been demonstrated. These are considered mechanisms by PGPR, mycorrhizal fungi, and to some degree the fungi studied. These fungi are symbiotic to plants, and so offer advantages for keeping the plant healthy for long durations. In addition, mycorrhizal hormone production has the potential to improve agricultural productivity and reduce environmental costs. The reduction in nitrogen fertilizer use due to the use of these fungi can be expected to reduce nitrate pollution and soil erosion. In addition, these fungi have induced resistance in plants to the potential range of pathogens that may be controlled. This resistance with long-term colonization could provide important tools for pest control with minimal environmental impact.
