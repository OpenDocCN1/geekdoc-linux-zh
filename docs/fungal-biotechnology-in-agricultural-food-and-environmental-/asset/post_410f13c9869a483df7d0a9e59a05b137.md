# 2.1 Additional Functionalities

In addition to the nutritional effects, consumption of mycopcoprotein under both controlled and free-living studies in akeys has been shown to significantly reduce total and low density lipoprotein (LDL) cholesterol levels. Studies by Homma et al. (1995); Turnbull et al. (1992); Udal et al. (1998) and others also concluded that mycopcoprotein is effective in serum lipid levels. Post-meal glycogen has shown to be reduced by consumption of mycopcoprotein, 13% with respect to controls. On the other hand, insulin remains thirty minutes after meal (Turnbull and Ward 1995). Finally, a mycopcoprotein has a significant effect on the sensation of satiety, which would help control the appetite of dieting patients, by Burley et al. (2003); Turnbull et al.

# 2.1.3 Nucleic Acid Content

Nucleic acids are a necessary component of all cells, and found in highly levels in rapidly dividing cells. The nucleic acid content of yeast (around 10% of weight) is approximately five times greater than the average human. When nucleic acids are degraded by nucleases, the resulting nucleotides are attacked by nucleotases in the intestine, resulting in nucleosides and nucleotides. These are further degraded into purines and pyrimidines. The degradation of purine bases in means in the production of uric acid. Accumulation of uric acid beyond the capacity of the kidney results in deposits in skin and soft tissues, leading to gout manifestations and calculi in the urinary tract. Pyrimidines are degraded in order. The administration of microbial protein is limited by the amount of nucleic acid contained. The administration of 130 mg of daily for one week results in uric acid levels ranging from 4.8 to 8.3mg/100 ml in humans. Normal levels of uric acid range between 2 and 7mg/100 ml in males (Riviere 1977).

Quorn is produced from a filamentous yeast which proliferates at slower rate than yeast (Trinci 1994; Galde and Castell 2002). Then, the starting-nucleic content subject to removal is slightly lower (8%). # 2.1.4 Texture

A noteworthy feature which differentiates Quorn products is the advantage taken from the filamentous nature of the microorganism in product design. Fusarium venenatum A35 filaments are aligned in parallel by a specially designed mechanical process which renders the texture very similar to that of meat fibers set in a light matrix of egg white protein and. The final product has a bland taste, light color which renders it susceptible to the absorption of flavoring and coloring agents (Anderson et al. 1975).
