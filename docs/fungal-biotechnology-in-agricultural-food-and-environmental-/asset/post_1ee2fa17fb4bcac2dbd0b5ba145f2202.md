The surface coat is probably the part of the nematode surface most relevant to recognition and adhesion of nematophag fungi; since proteolytic removal of this structure results in reduced adhesion of spores and endoparasitic nematophag fungi (Bird 1988).

The trap organ of A. oligospora contains an adhesive material. Upon contact with the nematode surface, a recognition step possibly modified, at least partly, by lectin binding, induces changes in the structure of the adhesive leading to capture of the nematode (Venhuis et al. 1985). The adhesive undergoes changes from an amorphous material to a fibrillar structure, more organized with fibrils perpendicular to the nematode surface. This may anchor the nematode to the trap thus facilitating infiltration. In contrast, the endophagous fungus D. comispoora does not appear to change and has a fibrillar structure even in the absence of nematodes on the nematode cuticle and forms an attachment bulb (Figure 3 bottom middle), from which hyphae grow out to digest the nematodes (Venhuis et al. 1985).
