## c. Removal of Mycotoxins During Processing

While processing generally does not destroy mycotoxins, mycotoxins can be removed by certain kinds of food. For example, cooking appears to be effective for detoxifying DON but not AFB. AFB1 can form Schiff's bases with reducing sugars such as under conditions (Murphy et al 1995) and its hetero-cinogen (Li et al. 2001); but the method was found to be not effective (1996). Loss of AFB occurs during extraction and cooking of corn-based foods with sugars and nitrosylation (alkaline cooking) and in the preparation of tortilla chip and masa (Doming-Kurzman 2000; Vos et al.). PT can be effective for various types of carbons (Legott et al. 2007). The effect of food processing on various mycotoxins has been reviewed by several authors in an ACS symposium (DeVries et al. 2002).

## 4.2.2 Detection and Screening of Mycotoxins

Because of the diverse chemical natures of mycotoxins, the presence of traces of toxins in complex matrices that interfere with analysis, and the uneven distribution of the toxins in the sample of mycotoxins is a tough task (Chu 1995; Richard et al. 1993). Many methods have been established in the analysis, it is not uncommon that the aflatoxin can account up to 20%-30% (Horwitz et al. 1993).

To obtain reliable results, an adequate sampling protocol is required for various analytical methods (Whitaker et al. 1994; 1995; Wilson et al. 1998). To minimize the error, the studies have led to many improved various analytical methods for mycotoxins over the years (Maragos 1997; Maragos et al. 2001). Sensitive and high resolution MS and GC/MS or MS are used to detect the market methods. New chemical methods are emerging and have gained popularity (Maragos analysis 1997).

## 5 CONCLUSIONS

Mycotoxins are low molecular secondary metabolites of fungi that can contaminate agricultural commodities, foods, and feeds. Food product that produces these toxins do so prior to harvest and during storage. Although contamination of mycotoxins by toxic fungi frequently occurs in areas with a hot and humid climate (conditions favorable for fungal growth), they can also be prevented. Prevention of mycotoxins depends on the type of producing fungi and environmental conditions such as the substrate, water (moisture and relative humidity), duration of stress and microbial activity. Although outbreaks of mycotoxins have been documented, several of them have not well characterized; however, the correlation between mycotoxins and toxic effect has been established. Induction of cancer by some mycotoxins is a major concern as chronic effect of these toxins. The toxicity of mycotoxins varies depending on the animal species and the extent of exposure. It is nearly impossible to eliminate mycotoxins from food and feed in spite of regulatory efforts.
