### 4.1.2 Saprophytic Fungi and Yeast as Biocontrol Agents of Botrytis cinerea

The leaf surface of plants (phyllosphere) is frequently controlled by a range of saprophytic fungi and yeasts, which rely on plant nutrients and a range of other carbon-nitrogen sources for their survival, d.g., damaging or sensed tissues, pollen grains, insect herbivore. If present in the same niche these fungi compete with the pathogen, reducing growth and root structure of the pathogen through competition or antagonism (Folkkema 1993).
