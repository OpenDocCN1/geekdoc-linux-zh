
## 7.2 The Pilot Bioreactor

The fermentation was conducted in a nylon 20 aerated polyethylene drum, with 2 cm³ (Scotchbrite) cubes (Lonergan et al. 1995 b). A reactor cycle of 25 days was used in this instance with the medium continually recycled at 36 ±1°C. P. cinabarrus colonized the colony. Veratryl alcohol (4 mM) was used as an inducing agent. The RBBR was added (500 mg/ml) to the medium on day 3 after inoculation. Daily samples were analyzed for glucose, nitrogen, and laccase activity. A sample was also taken 30 min after addition of the dye and is termed D3 + 30.

## 7.3 Decolorization in Bioreactor

The dye (395) was decolorized by 80% within 24h and almost completely removed within 48h after addition. The nitrogen of the reactor was removed in 7 days and the glucose in 12 days wherein more glucose was added to the reactor (Lonergan et al. 1995b). A small increase in nitrogen occurred through the end of the cycle due to outliers.

Laccase activity was detected on day 2, peaked at a large 60 U/ml on day 8 and to a minimum on day 12. It then rose again towards the end of the cycle as the glucose was added.

In this series of experiments laccases were purified from 100 ml removed during the reactor operation on day 1, 3, D3 + 30, 20, 23, and 25 by migration to the method described (Schlippake et al. 2000). The profiles for all samples were similar to those of the laccase purification carried out on the Mono Q anion exchanger. The elution pattern for days 4 and 23 are shown in Figure 4. The anomaly in elution times (day 4, 18 min and day 23, 15 min) was explained.

## 7.4 Variation in Enzyme

The apparent molecular weight of laccase, previously estimated at 63 kDa by SDS-PAGE (Schlippake et al. 2000), was consistent in the laccase from days 1, 3, D3 + 30, and 20. The proteins from samples on days 23 and 25 migrated in the gel and gave a molecular weight of 54,620.

On the electrophoresis of particular samples, under native conditions, significant laccase activity was observed in the enzyme through the gel from difference. Electrophoretic migration of proteins under nondissociating buffer conditions occur on the basis of size and charge (Hames 1990). In this variation indicated that physical transformation of the enzyme may have occurred during the reactor cycle until the loss of enzyme activity.

Purified laccases were subjected to IEF and stained for activity. The banding pattern showed variation on days 4, 23, and 25. The activity stain showed that on day 3, proved that to addition of the dye to the reactor, bands for laccase in the strongest bands were observed on the anode. The band and the band close to the cathode were observed on day 20 of the cycle. On day 25 and, however, there were four bands and the band near the anode was the only band. A strong band of laccase on day 20 was observed on IEF (Eggert et al. 1996). The variation, with the migration on the PAGER dye, did not indicate modification of the enzyme over the reactor cycle.

The decrease of activity after a 7h incubation lasted for the different days investigated (Figure 5). Day 4 laccase decolorized RBBR at the fastest rate. This particular enzyme was isolated from the reactor one day after addition of the dye. The most active laccase was isolated from day 3, ie, before.

## CONCLUSIONS

A strain of cinabarris isolated decolorized an industrial waste as well as the disazo dye CSB and the vinyl sulfonyl RRBR. On purification of the phenoloxidase the enzyme showed all the characteristics of a laccase. The purified laccase decolorized from the white-root fungus rapidly there appeared to be some change to size variation of laccase during the reactor cycle. It may be hypothesized that the enzyme may have adsorbed laccase molecules in a way that activated the protein. This protein could induce a conformational change, which may optimize the catalytic site of the enzyme in a way that it is easily accessible. As the amino group, there could be a reason occurring between a possibly free carboxyl group of an amino and laccase of the dye. This would mean that such amino-containing molecules could behave like amino acid. Such modulation could explain the presence of an acidic band appearing closer to the cathode upon exposure to the dye.
