
# Fungi and Fermented Food

T. B. Ng The Chinese University of Hong Kong, New Territories, Hong Kong, China

Fermentation has been used by the food industry for centuries, adding nutritional value, creating aromas and flavor. A lack of knowledge of microbiology may result in health problems.

## 1 INTRODUCTION

Fermentation is a process in which complex compounds including carbohydrates, proteins, and fats are broken down into simpler forms under aerobic or anaerobic conditions and have been used by many. The products of fermentation can be used as a food source or as an additive. Fermentation has many roles including spoilage prevention, nutrition, flavor and taste, reducing cooking time and fuel consumption, some fermented foods may even improve health value.

Some fungi are pathogenic to plants and animals but bring about economics, many of them are beneficial to mankind in food production. In this chapter, types of fermented food with fungal involvement in the fermentation process are described. It is worth noting that different countries may have similar indigenous fermented food as well as varieties that are unique to that country.

## 2 ACID-FERMENTED LEAVENED BREAD AND PANCAKES

These are widely accepted as food. The acidification makes the food resistant to spoilage and development of food toxins and less likely to transfer pathogens.

(a) Indian Idli: Idli is the Indian equivalent of Western sourdough bread. It differs from sourdough as first activity is achieved by bacterial instead of yeast and wheat or rice is not used as source of protein to retain bacteria. Idli is made by fermentation of batters produced from soaked, rice and dehul black gram. After fermentation overnight around 30°C, the batter is poured into cups of idli steamer, placed in a pan and steamed for about 15 minutes until the idli is soft spongy. The texture can be achieved by replacing rice with cereal grains, and a variety of legumes can be used instead of black gram. The lactic acid bacterium Lactostoc mesophilus is important for batter layering. Yeasts are not essential, although when used in fermentation, they increase the levels of vitamins B and riboflavin. The yeasts used in production of food for idli include Saccharomyces cerevisiae, Debaryomyces ceberiae, Hansen hansenii, Hansella anomala, Trichoderma anianus, Edulis anianus (Soni and Sandhu 1999), Torulopsis kapit, and Trichosporpora pullullans (Soni and Sandhu 1999); (b) Philippine Puto: Puto is similar to Indian Idli except that there are no legumes in Puto, so the flavoring is excluded. It is produced by grinding rice grains in water, sugar and a starter culture containing L. mesenteroides, Streptococcus faecalis, and S. cerevisiae, so allowing the mixture to ferment. This fermentation process lasts for about 15 h, before adding sodium hydroxide (lye) and water sugar. A second fermentation for about 5h steaming for 30 min is a minor component until the final stage of the fermentation in which it can reach as much as 8% of the total population, resulting in a small amount of ethanol. The yeast with L mesenteroides may play an important role in leavening the batter (Rosario 1987); (c) Sri Lankan Hopper: Rice and wheat, either alone or mixed, are added to sugar, starch, coconut water, honey to make a cake fermented by bacteria and baker's yeast (S). After the mixture is a soft batter, it is covered with a piece of cloth and allowed to stand at room temperature for 12h to allow carbon dioxide to be produced by the yeast. Sodium bicarbonate may be added to raise the batter dough. The mixture is poured out and baked for 4 min. A longer fermentation period produces a better flavor.
