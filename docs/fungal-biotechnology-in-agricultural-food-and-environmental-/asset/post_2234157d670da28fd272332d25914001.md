Soil receptivity to inoculum of nematophagous fungi should be assessed by the delivery of large amounts of inoculations to be carried out (Wakelin 1999).

The effects of nontarget organic agents on the rhizosphere, e.g., mycorrhizal fungi and rhizobacteria, have not yet been characterized. Such studies would enable the development of synergistic methods of approaching control of root diseases caused by nematodes and other pathogens.

## 7 CONCLUSIONS

To use nematophagous fungi for biological control we need to know much about their biology, on physiological as well as ecological levels. This is especially true for the tri-trophic interactions between the fungi, nematodes, and plant roots. The invasion of roots by plant-parasites is fairly well established, little is known about root colonization by nematophagous fungi. The growth process can be further investigated using molecular, cellular, as ecological studies, to find out the nematophagous fungus can induce instance, systemic resistance in plants and other plant fungi. If this can develop, it will need more properties of nematophagous fungi after their nematode-debiting and selection of nematophagous fungi for root coloring may have equal importance to nematodes in soil.

Combinations of different types of nematophagous fungi, e.g. parasitic and nematode-trapping fungi, which infect various stages of the nematode life cycle, have been rarely studied biocontrol experiments. Good practices will be needed to determine nematode eggs. Root-galls induce traps may capture juveniles entering or leaving roots. Such experiments need to be conducted in both laboratory and field investigations for development of BCAs as a practical way to control plant-nematodes.

In the soil environment, nematophagous fungi are difficult to study due to lack of appropriate methods. New techniques have been developed and used, to assess efficacy in fungi from soil or hyphal surfaces have been tried, but usually show results between species and are difficult to use. Molecular techniques properly need to be employed. This could include, for transformation with marker green fluorescence protein (GFP), or development of molecular markers or similar approaches based on DNA sequences.

Development of proper delivery systems (formulation, application, etc.) and the effect on nontarget organic systems are key factors involved in the good performance of nematophagous fungi as BCAs. A fair deal of research should be invested in these fields. These efforts will also find applications in other fields of root health.
