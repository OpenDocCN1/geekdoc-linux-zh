### 5.1 Endophytes

Endophytes are defined as organisms that grow within a living plant but not without harming living cells, thereby requiring nutrition from the host. It is believed that the stems, roots, and leaves of many plants contain endophytes, but because their nature is ambiguous, and their presence remains difficult to detect such as surface infiltration and histological studies (Petrini 1991). Numerical endophytes may be potentially associated with an individual host (e.g., 85 different fungi have been isolated from items of Asteraceae). Although resident endophytes do not produce disease symptoms, their occupation is without a supporting cost of up to 0.5% of annual needle production in the case of endophytes (Carroll 1992).
