Some of the important issues related to AM biofertilizer commercialization:

These bio-inoculants are now formulated in different formulations. They are designed on the bases of their application to different crops and locations. They are available in the form of powders/pellets, grains/beads, and mycelia. Intramural filaments of Glomus sp. (species and mycelium) are entangled in algae and used as inoculum. The material remains viable and germinate under conditions (Declerck 1996a,b; Stuart and Penchette 1991). Glass beads have been used as inoculum with spores and mycelia inside (Declerck et al 2001) in much more activity using pellets or tablets just below the seeds and small plantlets initiating mycorrhization in the hardening phase. Alternative approaches for inoculum disbursement include broadcasting in the field or mycorrhizoral products often contain other ingredients to increase the effectiveness of the mycorrhizal spores. For example, organic matter is often to encourage microbial activity, soil structure, and root growth. Stress vitamins improve nutrient uptake and root biomass. Water absorbing gels help get to roots and encourage favorable soil moisture conditions for mycorrhizae to form and grow. Organic biosolids, in general, are effective in inducing mycorrhizal fungi. By promoting competitiveness, stress resistance, and nutrient efficiency, biolulants reduce barriers to mycorrhizal formation during the critical period following root initiation or transplant.
