## 7 HYDROCARBON DEGRADATION BY MIXED CURES

Several experiments have shown that the mineralization of hydrocarbons in nature may require the combined efforts of both fungi and bacteria (Juhasz and Naidu 2000). Pyrene mineralization by *Dichotomy squalens* and *Pleurotus sp.* was enhanced by soil microorganisms (in Wiesche et al. 1996). The mineralization of benzo[a]pyrene by *Bjerkandera sp.* more doubled after the addition of activated sludge (Kotter et al. 1998). *P. janhiniline*, when mixed with the bacterium *Streptomyces multiphilia*, mineralized pyrene, benzo[a]anthracene, and dibenzo[a]anthracene (Bochman et al. 2000).

## 8 BIOTECHNOLOGICAL IMPORTANCE

At one time, the industrial research on hydrocarbon metabolism of attempts to produce young yeast-cell protein from petroleum, which was cheap and widely considered to be (Lindley 1992). Little of that type is being done now, but there has been progress in the transformation of terpenes for the production of flavors, fragrances, and other intermediates (Trudgill 1994) and on the degradation of PAHs and toxic hydrocarbons (Atlas and Cremgmina 1995).

Many of the hydrocarbons contaminating soils and groundwater near leaking fuel tanks, oils, and chemical waste have been degraded by microorganisms (Atlas and Cremgmina 1995; Sutherland 2001; Coleman et al.). Most of the recent work in this field has been on BTEX compounds, but some of it is on PAHs (Compounds and Reddy 1993) and on alkanes (Nakwuchwa 2000).

The bioremediation of soils may be accomplished by either (Juhasz and Naidu 2000) or in-site (in Wiesche et al. 1996). The mineralization of benzo[a]pyrene by *Bjerkandera sp.* more doubled after the addition of activated sludge (Kotter et al. 1998). *P. janhiniline*, when mixed with the bacterium *Streptomyces multiphilia*, mineralized pyrene, benzo[a]anthracene, and dibenzo[a]anthracene (Bochman et al. 2000).

## 9 CONCLUSIONS

Fungi are capable of metabolizing hydrocarbons. However, only a few are known to grow on hydrocarbons, such as methane-hexadecane, toluene, styrene, or as carbon sources. Various fungi have been selected to carry out specific, biotrophic transformations to produce higher-value products. Many fungi transform hydrocarbons to oxidized derivatives and are few to show potential for bacteria, the degradation of toxic wastes in the environment. Several lines of research on the degradation of hydrocarbons are likely to be productive in the future: (a) investigating the roles of yeasts and fungi in transforming hydrocarbons usually found in the environment. (b) Finding ways to utilize, including by-products from agriculture and industry, to transform soils contaminated with mixtures.
