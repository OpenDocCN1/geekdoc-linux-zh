Straight-chain alkanes, from n-decane to n-octadecane, are utilized for growth by various yeasts (Ekedubu and Obukey 2000; Lida 2000). They are hydroxylated by cytochrome P450 monooxygenases in tropical Malassezia sp., M. apicola (2000), and Yarrowia lipolytica (Lida 2000). Various genes encoding cytochrome P450 have been identified in the yeast methyl-hydroxylating yeasts (den Brink 1998; lida et al. 2000). The insect Metarhizium anisoplia grow on n-tetracene, n-undadecane, and octane by β-oxidation (Napolitano and Juarez 1997; Crepo et al. 2000).

Cyclohexane, which occurs in petroleum and is used as a solvent, is transformed metabolically by several fungi (Griffin and Cooney 1979). A Cladosphora sp. has been
