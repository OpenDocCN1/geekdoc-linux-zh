## 3 BIOACTIVE ENTOMOPATHOGENIC FUN

While the focus on control of entomopathogenic fungi has been on using organisms, either as invasive or putative agents (Eilenberg et al. 2001), these are known to produce a number of toxins and enzymes. Some of these extracellular metabolites have been studied with the use of them against insect pests. Biological control using entomopathogenic fungi can be demonstrated by the discovery and formulation of sporulations. From the discovery of a strain actinomycete in Cairns, Dow Agro Sciences has shown success in producing insecticide products, such as Success™ and Naturalyt™. It is possible to utilize other compounds in a similar or novel fashion.

It is worth noting that entomopathogenic fungi produce extracellular toxins and enzymes. These compounds are required to both assist penetration of the host cuticle and overcome other defenses, which includes killing microbes. Proteins produced by entomopathogenic fungi degrade and assist entry into the host by proteases used to degrade their cuticle during molting (Melson and Parsons 1995). A number of enzymes are known from entomopathogenic fungi, such as toxins and chitinases that cause rapid breakdown. These toxins can be enhanced by addition of bacteria and there has been increased interest in the use of these in pest control. Street and Lee (2000) have reported the use of pyrines and chaotins in M. anisoplia. The toxins are not found in genomic sequences for yeast, bacteria, archaea, and mitochondria which they inherited by chr1 from horizontal gene transfer.

In the future, biot from entomopathogenic fungi may have a role in pest control, either formulated as pesticides, or through direct application. Direct application may be the only aim, so toxins or metabolites may have antifeed activities (e.g., http://www.ba.cn/ubopesti.htm).
