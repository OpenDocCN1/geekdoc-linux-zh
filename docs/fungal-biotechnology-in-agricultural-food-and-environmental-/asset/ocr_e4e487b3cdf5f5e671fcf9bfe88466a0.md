# 3. Mixed Cultures

Some research was done on the use of mixed cultures of nutrients in silage to improve better yields of biomass. Comparison of different yeasts, bacteria, and yeasts and molds from different substrates showed better biomass. The yield of the mixed cultures was significantly higher than that of pure cultures. A two-step fermentation process was used to produce large biomass. Yeast was used in the first step to produce large amounts of filaments which reduced the COD of the effluent (Azzam and Heikal 1988; Bottaro Castilla et al. 1984; Nudel et al. 1987). In whey wash was a three-mixed culture of G. Candida, hansenella aurantia, and kravis was used. The results of the three organisms showed on the dilution rate during continuous fermentation (Barker et al. 1982). Twelve different species, mainly Candida and Trichosporon were used. Yeast was effective for the treatment of two fermentaceous molasses (Malois et al. 1987).
