# Fungi as Plant Growth Promoter and Disrupor

M. Hyakami & M. Kubota Gifu University, Yanagido Gifu, Japan

# 1 INTRODUCTION

Reduction of the use of fertilizers and fungicides in agricultural production is necessary to help maintain ecosystems and develop sustainable agriculture. The use of both bio-fertilizers and biocontrol systems can be particularly present around roots, with minimal effect on the environment and such strategies have been widely researched. In soils, microorganisms co-exist with roots, or on plant root surfaces, and can have many effects on performance of the plant and may also affect the structure of the plant community. A unique microbiota is particularly present around the root surfaces, where fungi distributed around plant root play a large role in the decomposition of organic matter and may suppress deleterious microorganisms. Some of the root-associated microorganisms promote plant growth. Some fungi have been called “plant-promoting rhizobacteria” (PGPR; Klopper et al. 1980) or “plant-promoting fungi” (PGF; Hyakami 1994).
