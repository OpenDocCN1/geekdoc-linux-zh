## 4.2 Costs and Benefits of Ethanol Production

The capital cost analysis given in Table 6 shows that fact hydrolysis, SSF, and acid hydrolysis all have fairly similar total costs; the small differences are probably within the error of estimates. Likewise, the operating cost analysis presented in Table 7 also shows similar numbers, although the cost of ethanol is lowest with the SSF process.

The production of ethanol must be low for it to be competitive with other existing fuels on the market. Presently, the competition comes from methyl tertiary butyl ether (MTBE) in gasoline blend, but in the target for comparison will shift. Fluctuation in the prices of other energy sources makes it difficult to predict the future of bioethanol. Although from time to time the cost of ethanol can provide incentives to jump-start an interest in renewable energy resources—which in practice is synonymous with ethanol biomass, the production process must be inherently competitive for it to be standing in the long term. Table 8 shows a study of ethanol production in California. It gives the price of feedstocks at near-term and mid-term operations on a large scale. However we must take into account the costs, debt, and return on investment. The target price decreases from near-term to mid-term, as the technology improves and forces the production cost. When the cellulosic feedstock is inexpensive, conversion into ethanol may be costly. Cellulase enzymes at 5 cents/gal of ethanol and, therefore, expensive at the commercial level.

The critical factors that contribute to the cost of ethanol are summarized below:
(a) The cost of collected feedstock such as municipal and commercial wastes must be low. Then, production facilities should be located near the source of biomass material. Furthermore, we can cross the break-even point with higher moisture content in the municipal and commercial wastes,
(b) Ideally, the feedstock should be close to the consumption site to minimize shipping. Ethanol absorbs moisture quickly and attacks rot spots in the transportation system. ## 5 CONCLUSION

The most important commercial product of cellulose degradation is ethanol, which has the potential of filling energy needs. Furthermore, making ethanol an integral part of all energy forms: natural, gas, coal, electricity, and others is a prudent choice that the government should encouraged to take. Energy plays a central role in economic health of society. Finally, production of ethanol from biomass impacts both positively and negatively on the environment. Growing forests generates carbon. By reducing the number of trees, the severity of wildfires will be reduced; so the consequent emission of hydrocarbons and carbon monoxide generated by the fires. When with gasoline, ethanol reduces the harmful emissions.
