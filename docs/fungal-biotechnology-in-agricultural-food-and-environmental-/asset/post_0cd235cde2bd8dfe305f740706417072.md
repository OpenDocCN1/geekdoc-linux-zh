## 2.2.2 Nitrogen Source

Nitrogen is a major constituent of any organism, and fungi are certainly not. Any successful medium must carefully balance microorganism's need for nitrogen. Nitrogen limitation leads to slow or even death of growth, causing compromise in ethanol production. Table 2 summarizes the differences of nitrogen sources used in fungal media in a laboratory setting. In a defined medium, typical nitrogen sources are ammonia, nitrate, and urea. In a complex medium, peptone, tryptone, yeast extract, wheat bran, and addition of protein digests cellulose far more carbon than nitrogen sources. Nonetheless, nitrogen sources' effect on fungal biomass can be promoted. For example, Singh (1967) reported that ammonium ions inhibit inhibition of pyruvate kinase, and they stimulate phosphate pathway by deregulating glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase. However, the effects of nitrogen sources are not well understood. From economic feasibility viewpoint, supplementing nitrogen from complex sources is undesirable because adds significantly to the cost. A further challenge is to eliminate the need for nitrogen addition and to satisfy nitrogen demand entirely from lignocellulosic sources. At least, one would consider cheaper sources of nitrogen.
