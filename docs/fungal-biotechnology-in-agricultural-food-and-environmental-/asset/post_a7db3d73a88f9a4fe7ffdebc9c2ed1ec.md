Sequences of boninae species showed little variation, and a short sequence of 16 bases in the ITS2 region was found to be unique to the species. A PCR primer has been derived from the combination of this and the ITS3 sequence of G. boninae from cultures, species, and infected palm material (Bridge et al. 2001). It is therefore possible to study the spread and dynamics of fungal populations on the same occurring on secondary hosts, in the soil. Markers may be used to determine the genetic identity of host or variety species, and provide information on between pathogenic races. This information is fundamental to understanding the spread of fungal diseases, and is important to consider which marker controls the particular fungus under study and which markers may also provide information to the role of species or particular mating patterns in variation. Variation in fungal pathogenesis is the best method to identify these races using PCR. Strains/specific molecular markers/primers have been developed for plant pathogenic fungi. There is unfortunately no single system that can be guaranteed to provide the desired level of discrimination for all fungi, and some initial screening of different methods may be required to reveal a full story.
