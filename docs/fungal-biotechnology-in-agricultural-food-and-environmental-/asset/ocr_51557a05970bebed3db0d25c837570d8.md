One aim of biotechnology is to put that special physiology to use and produce a commercial product (cell or cell component) for remediating heavy metal contamination. Biological materials that can accomplish this may be termed biotraps and be grouped into three major categories:

+   a. Absorption or the process by which individual cells or cell components bind or take up metals
b. Adsorption of ions onto a surface, including ion exchange with ligands, and
b. Uptake into the cell and sequestration by cytoplasmic components;
c. Transformation by oxidation/reduction into a less toxic or volatile form, and
c. Precipitation by insoluble salts, or by a combination of these processes.

Discussion will consider heterotrophic microorganisms, primarily fungi and as possible candidates for development as heavy metal biosensors or biotraps. "Biotraps" are defined as either a (living or nonliving) or component of an organism, which can alter the form or bind with a toxic metal or ion allowing its removal and recovery from a waste stream, or rendering it harmless.

This review discusses heavy metal processes rather than have to deal with each group of organisms individually.

Biosorption is a process that describes the removal of heavy metals (or other substances) from solution, which can also be used in a sense to describe uptake by dead (detritus or living biomass) by physically-chemical processes such as adsorption or ion exchange (White et al. 1995). Processes however in living biomass may have the capacity to uptake the metal. This mechanism has the ability to remove and sequester theed metal ions for recovery or separate disposal.
