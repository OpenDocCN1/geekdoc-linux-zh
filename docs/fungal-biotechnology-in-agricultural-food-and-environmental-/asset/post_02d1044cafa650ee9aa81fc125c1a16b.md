Biological control agents are generally effective when applied as a preventative treatment, prior to the onset of disease, and multiple applications may be needed to provide lower-disease suppression. At high levels of pressure, biological control agents can be anticipated to perform less well. Some of the agents may be used in combination with, or in alternation with, chemical controls if it can be demonstrated that survival is not affected. Similarly, the use of biocontrol agents may be more effective than single organisms although research has been in this area. Biological control agents that more than one-target a particular disease. It is not clear whether different plants may have an influence on the efficacy of these biocontrol agents.

Notwithstanding these conditions, the use of yeast and other fungal control agents has generated significant interest in both the scientific and product development of means that can be commercially viable to the market.
