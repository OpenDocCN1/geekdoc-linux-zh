## 5.3 Fermentation

All technologies developed for industrial scale have same aims: maximum production of ergot, minimum amount of contaminants (other alkaloids, toxins, etc.), the shortest possible time of fermentation, minimization of cost, energy, and efficiency. For the production of ergot alkaloids, different techniques have been employed (a) stationary culture on liquid media (Kybbal and Vleck 1970; Rezezo-Hernandez and Lozanski 1993), (b) submerged cultures (Koppel and Singer 1986) adapted to semicontinuous or continuous processes (Koppel and Rehn 1984). In some cases the medium used for production of ergot under condition of submerged fermentation (Koppel et al. 1985; Kopp and 1983; Kern 1991).

### 5.3.1 Stationary Culture

In the beginning of the days, development of processes for production of ergot alkaloids under conditions of stationary culture on solid media (Adams et al. 1962; Kybbal 1960; Moleych 1965). The stationary culture on agar plates was commonly used for preparation of cultures. When transformed to industrial scale this technique showed limitations mostly due to difficulties in maintaining aerobic conditions of large surface. Kybbal and Vleck (1974) development technology for stationary culture of C. purpurea in plastic bags partially filled with liquid medium. This procedure was used for production of toxins and later for production of asexual spores of C. purpurea.

### 5.3.2 Submerged Culture

Submerged fermentation in laboratory scale, it is a primary step in getting knowledge of microorganism physiology, biosynthesis, production, stability, and influence of medium composition on production of alkaloids. In industrial scale, submerged fermentation in shaker culture is mostly used for preparation of C. purpurea, as the purpose of alkaloids. Producing Ergotamine and their derivatives are the best-studied processes due to direct therapeutic use of ergot. Industrial technologies were developed for the following strains: (a) shaker culture (6 g/l) and 47 production 1.5 g/l of ergotamine containing 75% of ergotamine (Baumert et al. 1979, 2013; Koppel et al. 1985). Other type of toxins produced by submerged culture belongs to ergot, ergocrine, and α-ergotrope. By Malinka 1999 the process developed for C. purpurea resulted in relatively high production of toxins (2.4 g/l). ### 5.3.3 Solid Substrate Fermentation

Robinson (2001) has recently proposed solid substrate fermentation (SSF) for the production of enzymes and secondary metabolites. The production of ergaloids by C. foriformis using SSF procedure was 3.9 times higher than obtained by submerged liquid fermentation (SLF) (Her et al 1993). Some problems could be the necessity of using antifungal chemicals and the shear stresses caused by stirring SLF. Also, better circulation can be achieved in SSF further increasing the ergaloid yields (Balakrishnan and Pandey 1996). The SSF has been shown to produce more metabolites but in smaller fermenters with easier downstream processing. Also, the cost and trouble associated with antifungal agents and maximizing yield production, may be seen as a viable option for industrial scale production of ergaloids.

The growth of the fungus and alkaloid formation in submerged batch fermentation was described by mathematical model, which can be used in automatic process control and optimization. Grom et al. The activation-inhibition of alkaloid biosynthesis observed for C. foriformis strains (Fliger et al. 1988) and it was found that feed-back inhibition can be improved (more than 100%) of the fermentation process was also when induced of cytochrome P-450 were used (Rylo et al. 1988).

## 5.4 Optimization, Control, and Modeling of Ergot Alkaloids Fermentation

The following facts have been taken into account optimization and scale-up of fermentation processes for the production of ergot alkaloids (Kobel and Sangler (1978)), the production strains should be continuously maintained to maintain the optimal quality of selected production strains and must be monitored by factors of biology, culture, engineering, and other factors, b) long cultivation period in aspects of biotechnology require high standard of stability in operation and equipment, c) balanced aeration and stirring are required due to the sensitivity of Claviceps to stress use of antifungal agents could cause considerable loss in alkaloid. The classical problem of the large-scale fermentation is an optimal supply of oxygen to growing Claviceps. High oxygen demand in the growth phase can be met by addition of different hydrocarbons (Gilmanov et al. 1996) or perfluorcarbons (Menge et al. 2001) to shake flask/or stir which respectively affected by other microorganisms (Low et al. 1998).

## 6 CONCLUSIONS

The biological relevance of ergot alkaloids is for their therapeutic use, unquestionable. On the other hand, they play very important role as toxins in agricultural industry as products of endophytic fungus of the genus Neothyrium of infested grasses. These examples show the importance of understanding of ergot alkal biosynthesis. It could help on one side, to develop strategies for rational design of ergot alkal drugs, and on the other one, to control and regulate the production of ergot alkaloids by endophytic fungi. The following points need more attention for production of ergot alkaloids and its (a) nonproductive phase of alkaloid production, (b) inhibition of cells, (c) construction of pathways which can carry stability markers, (d) development of systems with drug resistance, (e) study of membrane methods in the productivity, (f) use of mathematical modeling for description of phenomenon observed culture growth and alkaloid production, and (g) development of solid state fermentation and application of oxygen vertices.
