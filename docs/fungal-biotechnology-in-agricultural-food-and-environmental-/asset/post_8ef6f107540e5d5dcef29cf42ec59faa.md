## 7 FERMENTED SOYBEAN PRODUCTS

The soybean products described in the following are used in making soup or side dishes. Japanese Miso and Indonesian soya have economic significance compared to Doanjang and Chang.

- (a) Japanese Miso: This is prepared by fermentation of soybeans, with or without addition of rice or barley, A. oryza and A. soya. The use of Aspergillus, Mucor spellus, Penicillium, Rhizopus, R. flavus, and D. tattilla are all of essential microorganisms used in fermentation (Heseake and Shibai 1993).

- (c) Indonesian Tahu: Soybeans are boiled, boiled again to encourage growth. Alternatively, they are dried in the sun, put in salt brine, fermented for several days, cooled or packed for 1–4 weeks (Winarno et al. 1977).

## 6 SOY SAUCE

It is a dark-colored liquid that adds meaty and salty flavor as well as color to food. It is made by hydrolysis of soybeans or without added heat. The yeast is involved in fermentation.

- (a) Japanese Sauce: Cooked soybeans are mixed with roasted water and the mixture is inoculated with A. oryza. Fermentation proceeds at ambient temperature for a period up to months, which is followed by filtration, pasteurization, and bottling. Aspergillus, Penicillium, Rhizopus, and Co. porolaris are used in brine (Lee and Cho 1977); Bacillus subtilis, B. pumillus, and D. dentarius as well as Rhizopus flavus are added. (c) Chinese soy: The species type is used, steamed soybeans are fermented for a long period with inoculum, transferred to earthenware jars, with salt brine and kept in the sun. After 3 months of fermentation, the sauce can be extracted. Brine is added to the remaining mash for further extractions. Sugar, molasses, and lactic acid may be added to the sauce.

## 8 LEGUME-FERMENTED FOODS

Legumes are used for food production in Asia. Fermentation improves digestibility of legumes by hydrolyzing proteins, and breaking down antritional molecules

- (a) Temp (Tempeh) Kede: This is a fermented soybean-based food, prepared with vegetables and also available in the first index, in Thailand, Indonesia, and Malaysia. It is prepared in the form of a white cake. The soybeans are cleaned, soaked, dried, partially cooked, drained, packed in leaves or perforated bags, and fermented for 2 days to produce. A variety of fungi are used to ferment the soy, including species of Aspergillus, Penicillium, and Rhizopus by Yeoch and Merck (1977). In term of other bacteria such as Bacillus and Micrococcus sp. may also be used; (b) Tempeh is prepared from broad beans and soybeans.
