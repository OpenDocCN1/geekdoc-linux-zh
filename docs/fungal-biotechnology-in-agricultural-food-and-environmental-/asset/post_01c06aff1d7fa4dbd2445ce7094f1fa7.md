## 4.6.4 Production and Spoilage of Types of Cheese

Besides the important role yeast play in surface-ripened and mild-ripened cheese, may also have a difference in the quality and final hardness of hard and soft cheese. This was discussed by Chevreudoux, Gouda, and Parmentiers (Diena et al. 1994; Viljak and Grouping 1995). Yeast used to accelerate the ripening process (Diena et al. 1984), but the role of yeast in these types of cheeses is not clear. A variety of yeast types, including D. hansenii, Y. lipolytica, S. kefir, K. marxarius, and T. minella, have been isolated from these types of cheese and seem to proceed to e.g., uncontrolled matter, flavor blowing and defects of the cheese, and unintended pigment formation.

## 5 YEAST AS PRODUCTS

According to the classical definition probiotics regulate the microbial colonization in the digestive tract with a benefit on human health (Gedek 1991). In current and future probiotic products that dairy definitions to be known by Jankob and Narsbih (1998), a probiotic starter culture should be added a third dimension, i.e., extra physiological values to a more general improvement of human health (Kurman 1993; Labyet et al. 1992). The role of yeast in dairy products has been overlooked. Studies of probiotic yeast starter culture in dairy products have shown that yeast may occur in many dairy-related products. Historically, yeast may occur naturally with livestock feed and in the genus Saccharomyces. As reported by Gedek (1991), S. boulardii is nonpathogenic and used as a preventive and therapeutic agent for the treatment of various diarrheal diseases (MacFarland and Ferrand 2003; Surawic et al. 1989). S. boulardii was first isolated in Indonesia in 1950. In the fourth edition of the classification, the "Yeasts" edited by Kurtman and Fell (1998), S. boulardii is listed under the name S. cerevisiae.

Among few studies on the use of yeast in dairy products Loures-Hatting and Viljoen (2001) investigated the potential of adding S. boulardii to yogurt. The result showed that S. boulardii was able to survive four weeks at 4°C and even reach a level of 10⁸ in yogurt. Gas and alcohol products were not reported, which might be a concern for S. boulardii (Loures-Hatting and Viljoen 2001).

Several interactions between yeast and enteric pathogens, e.g., Escherichia, Salmonella, and Staphylococcus were reported. However, the yeast is reported to bind enterobacteria to the surface of the yeast through a mannose-specific (Gedik 1991). It is reported that S. boulardii is able to survive the passage with the intestinal tract, with live cells in the small intestine, which emphasizes its potential as a probiotic.

## 6 CONCLUSION

The present study has shown that the negative aspects of yeast in dairy industry, and the focus on yeast in dairy products to be more intensive than in the past.

The role of yeast spoilage is still a problem and in all kind of dairy products. The most effective way to solve this problem is hygiene and sanitation, and specific requirements for industries

The technological characteristics of important yeast and the complexity of the microbiota of many dairy products have been discussed in recent publications. Especially, the positive role of yeast in manifestation of spoilage is checking, but to a very limited extent beyond to the point of active use of yeast, the application of yeast as starter culture. New information on the technological characteristics of yeast and the diversity among microorganisms species in dairy will provide better background for the selection and use of yeast starter culture in dairy industries. The advantages of deliberate yeast are fermented and matured dairy products.

The taxonomy for yeast is very complex. New methods, including molecular techniques are used increasingly, but for the many yeast related to dairy products are not yet developed enough to distinguish yeast strains at species or species. However, these are a valuable supplement to the traditional methods of identification and powerful tools in ecological studies including the microbiota from indigenous foods and typing of yeast. New approaches for methods testing yeast with potential starter culture and characterization of yeast spoilage in dairy products should be given priority in future research with development of yeast cultures and the use of yeast as probiotics.
