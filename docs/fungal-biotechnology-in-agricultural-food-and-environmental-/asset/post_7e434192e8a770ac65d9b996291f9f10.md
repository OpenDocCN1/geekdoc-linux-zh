## 11 BIOLOGICAL SPOILAGE IN MAIZE CORES

Storage of cereal meals for animal feed is quite common in temperate regions. To achieve this, cereals must be kept below a certain O2 level. With low O2 concentrations, spoilage enables to proceed rapidly, especially by myophilic species such as P. roquefort, P. carneum, and P. paneum. This results in poor quality feed with mycotoxins (Peterson 1998). We have approached this problem by the use of accidental and competitive yeasts such as Aichia maxima which have been inoculated onto wheat, barley, or oats prior to storage. This effectively colonizes the stored cereal and prevents spoilage fungi. A. maxima has been found to be effective in controlling growth of P. roquefort (Petersen and Summer 1998). Recent studies indicate that yeasts may also affect A. ochraceatum and A. verrucosa, producing toxicogenic species in cool temperate climates (Peterson et al. 1998). This approach has not been examined for moist cereals destined for animal feed where fungi can prevail and thus may prevent such control. This could be a promising area for commercialization of natural treatments, for animal feedfeeds free of mycotoxin contamination.
