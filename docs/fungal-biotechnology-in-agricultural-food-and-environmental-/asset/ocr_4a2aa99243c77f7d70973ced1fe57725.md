# 5 MYCORRHIZAL RESPONSE TO DISTURBANCE

The fundamental role of mycorrhizal hypha is to provide a direct link between the plant community and surrounding soils (Miller and Jastro 1992). Soil disturbances always alter soil in ways that affect the balance between soil fungi and plants, organic matter, and nutrient pools (Allen and MacMahon 1985; Allen et al. 1999). Major disturbances reduce mycorrhizal inoculum density. Disturbances can occur through physical damage to fungal hyphae (Allen and MacMahon 1985) and recovery or loss of plant cover. The impact of disturbance on mycorrhizal fungi has been the topic of modern studies (Jasper 1989; Jasper 1991; McGie et al 2000).

# 6 RECOVERY OF MYCORRHIZAL FUNGI

The reestablishment of mycorrhizal fungi following disturbance has been extensively studied (Allen et al. 1999). Factors that are most influential to the recovery of mycorrhiza include the dispersal of plant and fungal propagules, the physicochemical environment, and survival of fungal individuals. Different fungal types propagate independently of one another and they meet each other in a more suitable environment to survive. Plant and fungal dispersal at thatroots might also be viable and germinating (Cooke and Willey 1993). Soil, particularly high levels of nitrogen (N) and P, could inhibit formations in mycorrhizae and the survival of recovery of invasive radical fungal propagules would greatly enhance mycorrhizal reestablishment (Allen and MacMahon 1985; Allen et al. 1992).
