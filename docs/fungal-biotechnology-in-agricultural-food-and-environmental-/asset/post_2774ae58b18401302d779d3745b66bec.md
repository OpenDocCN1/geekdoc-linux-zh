## Challenges and Strategies for Development of Mycoherbides

Susan M. Boyetchko / Gary Peng Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada, Saskatoon, Canada

### 1 INTRODUCTION

The application of biological control for the management of weeds has generally been viewed as an environmentally sound alternative to chemical (Boyetchko et al. 2002; Makowski 1997; Roskopf et al. 1999). In classical, biological control involves the import of natural enemies and relies on the survival, dissemination, and self-perpetuation of them. The classical approach is often more appropriate for low management systems such as pasture and range where distance is minimal, while herbicides are ideal for single-season management of agricultural forest and the norm. Despite economic, social, and environmental benefits are attributed to biological control, it is unreasonable to ask whether myherbicide or mycoherbides are to become widely available. Researchers have identified several additional agents as potential bioherides and innovative improvements in mass-production, formulation, and application of living organisms. Despite these accomplishments by researchers worldwide (Boyetchko et al. 2002) remains whether we have made significant advancements in bioherb research that would facilitate increased adoption. Several reviews have discussed in detail some of the limitations associated with bioherb (Auld and Morin 1995; Makowski 1997; Mortensen 1998), including biological, environmental, and technological constraints. Critics have attributed these constraints to the lack of further development of many biocontrol agents. This review will update the status of mycoherbides, summarize some points on potential approaches that may be used to address these challenges in order to advance the development of promising herbicide candidates.
