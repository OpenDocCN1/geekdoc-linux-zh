# CONCLUSIONS

The use of fungi for bioconversion distillery waste into biomass or some metabolites is reviewed. Stillage remaining in alcohols, although the products of fungi have the common characteristic that they are highly loaded effluents, which are difficult to purify. They are harmful to the environment due to the high content of organic substances and low pH. These properties facilitate their use for sugar waste. Sugarcane molasses has been used to grow various fungi, especially C. utilis, to produce more extent filamentous fungi, including Basidiomycetes, as mixed fungal and bacterial cellulases have been used. Laboratory, pilot, and industrial scale experiments have been performed using single or two-step methods. The products of bioconversion are fungal biomass and partially deficient effluents. A balanced amino acid composition makes the biomass as suitable feed supplement. Other valuable products can be produced, such as ethanol, alcohols, polysaccharides, pigments, and plant growth hormones. The economics of bioconversion of fungi depends on many factors and can vary from country to country. However, the distillery waste has a great potential in helping to solve pollution problems and, at the same time, producing marketable products.
