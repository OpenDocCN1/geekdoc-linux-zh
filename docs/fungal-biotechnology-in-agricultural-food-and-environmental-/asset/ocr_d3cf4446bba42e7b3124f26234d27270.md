# 5.1 The Composition of the Waste

The initial problem was to decolorize the complex water from an industrial site. The waste originated in a plant and and indicated that it contained low molecular weight (acyl) polyester esters derived from reaction of triglycerides with aliphatic anhydride, from 비롯, amino acids and formaldehyde. Lesser quantities of higher molecular weight, from polymerization of vinyl, and methacrylate monomers were also present. The effluent had a total organic carbon content (TOC) of 270 mg/l and a total suspended solid (TOC) of 749 mg/l.

The strain of P. cinnamabaricus was inoculated into a nylon web cube support (0.5 cm³ of Scotchbry). The effluent was prepared in a defined medium (Tien and Kirk 1988) which was modified by increasing the concentration of diammonium sulphate to 0.8 g/l to a carbon to nitrogen ratio of 38:1. It stimulated decolorization; तेव्हा alcohol (40 mM) was omitted in this trial because it retarded growth, inhibited glucose and nitrogen utilization and delayed decolorization. Addition of veryl alcohol might interfere with metabolism (Tonon and Order 1988).

The medium was circulated through the reactor at 0.45 l/h and samples were taken daily and analyzed for decolorization, available nitrogen, and oxidative activity at this stage was termed peroxidase. A control reactor regulating the effluent was also used.

# 5.2 Decolorizing Organisms

# 5.4 Decolorization in the Reactor

In view of the numerous results of involvement in dye decolorization and lignin degradation (Sections 2 and 3) the white-rot fungi were considered the organisms of choice for this work. It was argued that the organism could either grow on metabolize the effluent. Plates of malt extract (MEA) were prepared and the effluent diluted to contain 65, 1389, 2083, and 2270 mg/TOC, respectively. The fungi were first collected in western Victoria, Australia, and some other states. The fungi were grown under high humidity and examined daily.

The most appropriate organism was judged to be a test strain of P. cinnamabaricus according to Fuhring (1985) and Marshall and Western (1979). In the presence of a primary carbon source (and a TOC of 1385 mg/l) this fungus covered the effluent in 96 h. The TOC concentration was also used in bioreactor experiments.
