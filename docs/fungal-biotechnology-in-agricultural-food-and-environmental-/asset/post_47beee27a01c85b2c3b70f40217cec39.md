### 4.2.1 Properties

Manganese peroxide exists as a series of glycosylated isomers with rings from 4.2 to 4 and molecular masses ranging 45 to 47kDa. Each isomer contains one mol of iron per mole of protein (Leisola et al. 1987; Paszynski et al. 1986). Five isomers have been detected in *P. chrysosporium* MP-1 (Kirk and Cullen 1998).

### 4.2.2 Catalytic Cycle

Manganese peroxidase, like others, has the same catalytic cycle involving a two-electron oxidation of the heme by H2O₂, followed by one subsequent-electron reduction of the native ferric enzyme. The reducing substrate in the Mn2+ cycle is Mn2+, which reduces compound 1 and 2, generating Mn3+, which then serves to oxidize phenolic groups to radicals (Hatanaka).

Whereas it has shown that cationic radicals of aromatic substrates such as VA can activate the heme iron by oxidatively converting compound III to the native enzyme and preventing H2O2-dependent inactivation (Bart and August 1994). It is similarly shown to convert MnP compound III to native enzyme (Tofievski et al. 1998). ### 4.2.3 Role of MnP in Lignin Degradation

In many fungi, MnP seems to play a crucial role in the primary attack on lignin, because it generates Mn3+, a strong oxidant able to penetrate the "molecular" pores between cellulose fibrils, triggering the cleavage of lignin etheridic bonds (Flourny et al. 1993). Organic acids, such as oxalic (Kuan and Yuan 1993), are also produced (Hofman and Hoffiler 1999), which form mobile complexes that delay the oxidizing power. MnP does not oxidize lignin structures during normal rot, these structures have been shown to be co-oxidized when Mn2+ produces unsaturated fatty acids (Jenkins et al. 2002). ## 4.3 Versatile Peroxidase

A heme peroxidase different from other microbial, and fungal peroxidases, VP has been discovered in *Pleiotus* and *Bjerkandera* species (Cameroeno et al. 1999; Meyer and 1998; Ruiz-Duenas 1999, 2001). VP is characterized by catalytic affinity for MnP and LIP. The enzyme has a high affinity for both phenolic substrates, and, does also oxidizes VA, dihydroxybenzene, and manganese lignins (Ruiz-Duenas et al. 2001). Upon binding aromatic substrate, VP shows a long-range electron transfer from an exposed heme to lignin, similar to that of LIP (Ruiz-Duenas et al. 2001). Modification and chemical modification of lignin by the residues forming the Mn3+-binding site confirmed their role in catalysis.

## 4.4 Laccase

### 4.4.1 Properties

Laccases are copper oxidases that oxidize lignin and aromatic amines. Rather than heme uses oxygen as an oxidant, reducing it by four electrons to H2O (Call and Mock 1997). LACs are generally larger than dioxygenases, possessing molecular weights of approximately 60kDa (and Call and Mock 1997). With other extracellular enzymes, LACs are glycosylated.

It is believed that natural mediators, such as the mediation of nonphenyl substrates by LAC (3-hydroxyanthracinic acid in the white rot *P. cinnabarin*), or synthetic oxidizing agents like 2,2'-aminobis(3-tert-ethylbenzothiaz-6-sulfonate) (ABTS), LAC has been shown to oxidize nonphenyl lignin, VA and PAH (Bourbonnais et al. 1995; Collins et al. 1996; Eggert et al. 1996; Majerczyk et al. 1998; 1999).

## 4.5 H₂O Generating Enzymes

In the enzymes mentioned earlier, the lignolytic structure of white fungi includes extracellular H₂O generating enzymes, essential for peroxidase activity. A number of enzymes have been shown to lead to the production of H2₂, including glyoxal oxidase (GLOX), glucose oxidase, methanol oxidase, and aryl-alcohol oxidase (AO) by Zhao and Jane (1995). White fungi that lack H₂O generating enzymes may rely on the oxidation of organic acids such as oxalate and glyoxalate, which indirectly produces H2.

## 4.6 Cellobi Dehydrogenase

Cellobi dehydrogenase (CDH) is an extracellular enzyme found in many white rot fungi. It oxidizes cellobiose to glucose and hydrogen peroxide, which can then contribute to the degradation of lignin. The role of CDH in lignin degradation is still a subject of active research, but it is known that it may play a significant role in the process.
