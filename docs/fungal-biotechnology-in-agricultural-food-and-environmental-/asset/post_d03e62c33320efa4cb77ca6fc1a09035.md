## 5 MOLECULAR IDENTIFICATION AND TRACKING

In addition to improving our understanding of the genetics of disease caused by entomogenic fungi, molecular techniques have also been used in identification, classification and monitoring of fungi. It is now a standard practice to perform some form of genetic characterization of fungal strains for biotontrol. However, however, there are a few reviews on the uses of molecular characterization of entomopathogenic fungi literature.

An example of molecular characterization was the classification of the taxonomic position of M. anisopliae. Driver et al. (1998) used IM30189 and strain in the LUFIBA program for control. While described as a M. bassiana strain by the morphology of the field and some others, the correct classification was debated. Recently, Driver et al. (2000) published a review of the specific relationships between M. anisopiae and M. bassiana strains, used to identify them. They demonstrated IM30189 and strain formed a discrete clade on the M. anisopiae branch of the Metarhizium tree, a branch that appear to have been working on local control. Driver et al. (2001) described as well as other subspecies, which may be only presents as distinguish by ITS sequencing. This demonstrated a problem with molecular characterization, as fungal species cannot be solely erected from genetic data and morphological descriptions.

Molecular markers have been used to identify genotypes of individual fungi by strain generation, but new products have also allowed direct examination of variation. DNA locus has been used to study karyotype variation in other fungi, and could be used with entomopathogens. Several studies have examined the number of chromosomes and genes used. Vla et al. (1996) studied the level of chromosomal polymorphism in B. bassiana to obtain a more extensive knowledge of the genome organization. While extensive use of molecular characterization has proved useful, there are currently no standard techniques or agreement on how many regions of the genome should be sampled to provide taxonomic data.
