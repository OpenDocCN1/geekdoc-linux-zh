# ## 5.4 Fermentation Technology and Downstream Processing

Yeasts and fungi were traditionally employed in the production of alcoholic beverages and fermented foods over centuries (Hui and Khatourians 1995; Rajak 2000). Yeast (Saccharomyces) has been used worldwide for brewing and ethanol production; fungi have been traditionally used for producing-molded cheeses (Penicillium sp. in.) and soybean-based fermented foods (Aspergillus niger). In the Orient, the other edible mushrooms (such as Agaricus) have been used worldwide since ancient times (Hutter 1998; Pointing and Hyde 2001; Rajak; Singh and Aneja 1999). With passing, these fermentation techniques were scaled up and made more efficient with respect to engineering theories and practices. Main outcomes of the evaluation of food process production activities have been the development of interdisciplinary science and engineering, for example, better equipment design, heat and mass transfer systems, feedstock pretreatment system, recovery efficiency and quality management (Schiehpak et al., 2001), for an adequate coverage in this volume, computation and final analysis control control (HACCP) and quality assurance.

Fungi, because of their unique properties have challenged biologists to reinvent fermentation and downstream processes. Recent research aimed at better fungal uses for food production have synergistically aided many other aspects of fungal biotechnology from life molecules to a variety of polymer sciences.
