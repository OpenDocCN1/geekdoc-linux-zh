## 3.2 Ochratins

### 3.2.1 General Considerations

Ochratins, a group of dihydrocoumarin-containing mycotoxins (2), are produced by fungi in the genus Aspergillus and Penicillium [reviewed in Bhatnagar et al. 2002; Chu 2000; Ono et al. 1995; Petzinger and Ziegler (2000)]. Most OA producers can grow in a range from 4°C to 37°C and, as low as 0.8°C, optimal conditions for toxin production are narrow with $a_w$ and $a_v$ values >0.97 (where $a_w$ for production is below 0.85). Ochratins are produced in cereal foods (grains, barley, rye, wheat) and mixed food stored during stable temperature conditions with low humidity. OA can be carried through the body because of the presence of OA residues in animal products as a result of its binding with serum albumin. Natural occurrence of OA in kidneys, blood serum, blood and other sausage made from meat has been reported [reviewed in CAST (2003)].
