# 11 CONCLUSION

Mycorrhizal fungi are ubiquitous in their evolutionary associations with plants. All trees used for fuel, fiber and food form mycorrhiza with a diverse variety of fungi. Each, excluding a small number of animals, and all plants that decorate our homes and gardens form mycorrhiza. Known benefits range from soil stability to fertilizer and reduction of pollutants. These also sequester toxins and may be important in land rehabilitation and as restoration of lands for grazing or conservation.

Disturbance to plant and soil systems can reduce the density, diversity and function of mycorrhiza. However, the natural re-establishment of this symbiosis may take decades or may never occur. Because of the influential role mycorrhiza play in stabilizing plant succession, it is important to determine how effective to restore mycorrhizal factors to enhance or inoculated fungal populations.
