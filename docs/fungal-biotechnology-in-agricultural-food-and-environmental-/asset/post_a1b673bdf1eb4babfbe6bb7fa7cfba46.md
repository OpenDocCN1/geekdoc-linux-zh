Nature substantial amounts remain the decayed wood a white appearance. Conversely when brown rot fungi reduce wood lignin, which is shown by white rot fungi is decolorization of naturally occurring substances. The enzymes responsible for the initial stages of biodegradation and decolorization are oxidative enzymes, primarily ligninases (ligninases), manganese peroxidase, manganese peroxidases and laccases (Kirk and Farid 1997). Lignin peroxidase possesses mechanisms similar to other peroxidases (e.g., horseradish peroxidase and lactoperundase) (1999). These reductases contain a heme group and in the first step of the peroxidase reaction the enzymes undergo a one-electron oxidation which is converted to compound I. Compound I is an activated form of the enzyme in which the iron is in an oxferyl state and heme as a porphyrin cation radical, in some cases, or as radical species. The oxygen in the oxferyl is produced by hydrogen peroxide. The other oxygen originally present in hydrogen peroxide is reduced to water. The decolorization of the substrate is mediated by the one-electron oxidation of organic pollutants, the one-electron oxidation by compound I is converted to compound II. Compound II is an intermediate. Unlike compound I, which has an oxidation state equivalent to the ferric state, the oxidation state of compound II is one electron greater than the ferric state, returning to the ferric state completing the reaction cycle.

It was also shown that decolorization decreased for all three days that the dye was used. Addition contributed to the decolorization which was observed.
