# 3 TRICHOTHENES

# 3.1 Occurrence and Toxicity

The trichothecenes constitute a class of over 80 members of sesquiterpenoid compounds produced by a number of fungal genera. Based on the macocyclic ester or ether-bridge between C-4 and C-15, they are generally classified into type C (or types A and B). The trichothecenes, including diacetoxys (DA), deoxynivalenol (DON), and T-2 toxin, are mainly produced by Fusarium species (Bhanagar et al. 2002; Brown et al. 2001; Chu 1997; 2000). The trichothecene biosynthesis involves multiple reactions controlled by multiple genes. Biochemical and genetic studies of trichothecene pathway, initially in F. sporotrichioides with overlapping clusters has resulted in establishing the map of the trichothecene gene cluster on a 25 kb region of F. sporotrichioides (Brown et al. 2006; John et al. 2008; Trapp et al. 2009). This gene cluster consists of ten genes with the generation, in sequential order, TRI8, TRI7, TRI13, TRIA4, TRI16, TRI5, TRI10, TRI9, TRI11, and TRI12 (Figure 2, Brown et al. 2001). In F. graminearum, a similar cluster has been identified that TRI is nonfunctional (Brown et al. 2001; Matsumoto et al. 1999; Proctor et al. 1995). The gene organization and direction of transcription is identical in both F. sporotrichioides and F. graminearum, the fumarininin gene involved in trichothecene biosynthesis, TRI101, was isolated outside the trichothecene gene cluster by Kim (McInerny, 1999a; McCormack et al. 2009). They also identified the TRI12 gene in F. graminearum, TRI102, which encodes a trichothecene.

The initial step of the trichothecene biosynthesis involves the incorporation of three molecules of molecule into the trichothecene nucleus through the cyclization of farnesyl pyrophosphate. The first categorized gene that encodes trichothecene synthesis in this initial step is TRI1 (Bohn and Bremer 1988). Desjardins identified a gene, named TRI2 for synthesis of trichothecen from farnesyl pyrophosphate. The Tox gene (named as TRI5) was found to be similar to cytochrome P450 monooxygenases (Alexander et al. 1998; Holm et al. 1993). In fact, it was predicted that TRI4 and TRI11 genes were found to catalyze the oxygenylation of trichothecene at the C-2 position to 2-hydroxytrichothecene. The most recently identified TRI6. Alexander et al. (1998) demonstrated that TRI1 is involved in the conversion of isototrichodermin to 15-deacetoxin by oxygenation at the C-5 position. McCormick et al. (1999) indicated that the acetylation of isototrichodermin is catalyzed by TRI11. This gene might also play a role in self-protection against the toxic effect of trichothecene to the fungus producing trichothecene molecule (Muth et al. 2003).

Synthesis of the more complex macrocyclic trichothecenes such as erdin, veratoxin, and bacharin B is mediated by TRI5. Holm et al. (1997) demonstrated that these trichothecenes exhibit 10-fold more toxicity than the F. fusarium species. Trichothecene biosynthesis was also identified and deposited in GenBank database (Brown et al., personal communication).
