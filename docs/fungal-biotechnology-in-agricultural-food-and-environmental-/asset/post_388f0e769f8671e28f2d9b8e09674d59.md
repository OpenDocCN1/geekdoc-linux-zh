However, the results were not correlated with those of these proteins in plants (Datta et al. 2001). Other efforts to inhibit fungi in wheat have led to silencing (Charencey et al.), clones for maize, and B-1,3-glucan from wheat spikes infected by Fusarium graminearum (Li et al. 2001) which is exciting as they are more effective in control of fungi in this tissue.

Natural and synthetic peptides have been evaluated for control of fungal damage in wheat. Alvi and Reddy (2000) showed that certain peptides for antifungal activity in vitro and vivo all were shown to have significant activity in the microbial range and prevented the growth of F. fusotrichum. A group of antifungal peptides derived from Medicago sativa was given by Gao et al. (2000) to have significant activity against Verticillium allant in vitro and showed a reduced area of lesion under most fungal plant controls. However, the results were not correlated with the levels of peptide in plant root.
