### 3.3.3 Mode of Action

Mechanistically, Fnms are inhibitors of ceramide synthase (sphinganine/sphingosine N-otransferase), a key enzyme involved in the biosynthesis of sphingolipids, which are essential in cellular regulation, causing cell differentiation, mitosis, and apoptosis (Merritt et al. 2001; Riley et al. 2001). The primary action of Fnms is for its inhibitory effects and toxicity (2001).

The inhibitory effect of Fnms on ceramide synthase synthesis is related to the inhibition of sphingolipidn biosynthesis in multiple organs, such as brain, lung, and kidney of the susceptible animals (Garen et al. 2001). Increased lipid peroxidation has also been considered as a factor for its initiation of cancer (Garen et al. 2001). The tumor suppression reported in recent studies on the effects of Fnmb1 (Zhang et al. 2001) and the tumor necrosis (TNF) pathway is initiated by inducing them (He et al. 2001; Sharma et al.; Sharma at al 2002). The ability of Fnmb1 to alter other gene expression and signal transduction pathways are necessary for cytotoxicity and toxic effects. Fnmb1 is a good example of an apparently nontoxic (non-DNA) agent producing toxins that trigger apoptosis of (Dragon et al. 2001).

Similar to other toxins, TCs are biosynthesized via intermediate pathway: the TC skeleton is formed via cyclization of farnesyl pyrophosphate via the intermediate trichothecene by an enzyme trichothecene synthase. The isoleoid chain in T-2 is derived from leucine. Several keys in the TC biosynthesis pathway have been found in the biosynthesis of these mycotoxins have been cloned, and these genes are clustered on a chromosome. Genes Tri3, Tri4, and Tri5, which encode a transferase (15-acetyltransferase), a cytochrome P450 monooxygenase, mytoxin synthase, respectively, are located within a 9kb range, Tri5 and Tri6 (the gene family) in a 5-6kb region (Proctor 2000).

The structural diversity of TCs is reported in plants and humans [reviewed] in Bhagat et al (2002). The TC mycotoxins affect many organs, including the gastrointestinal tract, nervous, immune, hepatic, and cardiovascular systems. Effects of types of mycotoxins in humans include skin lesions, scabby wheat toxin (red-mold, or akaba-disease, or barley poisoning), feed and endemic syndrome (vries), trichotheosis, hemolytic and toxic leukemia (ATE) have been reported. ## 3.4.4 The Impact of TCs on Human and Animal

### 3.4.3 Deoxynivalenol (DON)

DON is a type B mycotoxin produced by F graminearum and other related fungi such as culinearopsis. Because DON causes feed refusal and emesis in swine, the name "vomiting" is also used for this toxin. Although DON is considered less toxic than other mycotoxins, the level of contamination of this toxin in grain is generally high, sometimes 20 ppm. Contamination of DON in other commodities, including oats, sorghum, rapeseed, and mixed feeds has been reported. Worldwide frequent occurrence of DON in grain sectors was reported by authors and Trelinholm (1994). Although moisture may lead to the production of toxin, infection of wheat and corn grains are especially susceptible to a fungal infection, which can cause "scabby wheat" and stimulate the production of toxin. The optimal level for DON production is about 24°C. Outbreaks of DON in winter wheat in the United States, Finland, and Canada usually occur with chilly and humid weather following the infection followed by a humid summer for toxin production.

Toxically, DON induces anemia and emesis both in humans and animals [reviewed] by Bhatnagar et al. (2002); Chu (2002). Swine are more sensitive to contaminated DON. Whereas most TCs are immunosuppressors, expression of mRNA-2, 4, 6 in a T-cell model IL-4-2 by DON was found at levels for partial or maximal protein synthesis inhibition.
