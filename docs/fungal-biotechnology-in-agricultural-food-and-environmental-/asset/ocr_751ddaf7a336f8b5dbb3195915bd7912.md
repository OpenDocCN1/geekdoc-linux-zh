# 8 BIOTECHNOLOGICAL IMPORTANCE

At one time, the industrial research on hydrocarbon metabolism of attempts to produce young yeast-cell protein from petroleum, which was cheap and widely considered to be باسم (Lindley 1992). Little of that type is being done now, but there has been progress in the transformation of terpenes for the production of flavors, fragrances, and other intermediates (Trudgill 1994) and on the degradation of PAHs and toxic hydrocarbons (Atlas and Cremgmina 1995).

Many of the hydrocarbons contaminating soils and groundwater near leaking fuel tanks, oils, and chemical waste have been degraded by microorganisms (Atlas and Cremgmina 1995; Sutherland 2001; Coleman et al.). Most of the recent work in this field has been on BTEX compounds, but some of it is on PAHs (Compounds and Reddy 1993) and on alkanes (Nakwuchwa 2000).

The bioremediation of soils may be accomplished by either removing soil for off-site treatment (May et al. 1997) or by in situ treatment (Feeder et al. 1996). On-site bioremediation may be done by fungi that can grow to reach the mass of PAHs (Cremmina 1997; Norway et al. 1999). Since few low molecular-weight PAHs have the ability to degrade them (DuBois and Nandu 2000), and others have been added together to achieve bioremediation (Bauchean et al. 2000). Fungi have also been used to transform PAHs from water and selectively selected fungi have been used to transform other hydrocarbons from gaseous waste (Garcia-Peña 2001).

Another function of biotechnology is the transformation of renewable resources, such as terpenes α-pinene, β-pinene, and limonene into volatile metabolites that are produced by fungi (Prahata and Bhattacharya 1974; Tan et al. 1998; Fungi transform unsaturated into hydrated derivatives and ketones. For instance, α-limonene has been transformed to α-terpineol, a volatile compound (Chen et al. 2001), or oxidized to allyl alcohol, which inhibits the growth of some tumor cells but also is a precursor of the extracellular sel peroxide peroxidase (DeOliveira and Strausson 2000); α-pinene has been oxidized to camphene, which is used in the fragrance industry (Agrawal and Joseph 2000). Sesquiterpenes are transformed by fungi to a variety of products (Abraham et al. 1992; Miyazawa et al. 1995; 1997). Hydrocarbons may be transformed by fungi to produce intermediates that are used in the synthesis of pharmaceuticals

and biochemicals (Johnson et al. 1973; Uzura et al. 2001).

# 9 CONCLUSIONS

Fungi are capable of metabolizing hydrocarbons. However, only a few are known to grow on hydrocarbons, such as methane-hexadecane, toluene, styrene, or although as carbon sources. Various fungi have been selected to carry out specific, biotrophic transformations to produce higher-value products. Many fungi transform hydrocarbons to oxidized derivatives and are few to create aromatic compounds which have shown potential for bacteria, the degradation of toxic wastes in the environment. Several lines of research on the degradation of hydrocarbons are likely to be productive in the future: (a) investigating the roles of yeasts and fungi in transforming hydrocarbons usually found in the environment. (b) Finding ways to utilize, including by-products from agriculture and industry, to transform soils contaminated with mixtures of hydrocarbons.
