Chrysporium has served as a model organism for the study of the degradation of dyes and other environmentally persistent organic pollutants. The effect of carbon on the decolorization of water containing Amaranth and Orange G was demonstrated by Chao and Lee (1994). Both dyes as well as the reactive dye Azure B were degraded by two strains of P. chrysporium and an unidentified white rot fungus. Interestingly, decolorization was not observed if fungal strains were present in a high nitrogen medium in the presence of glucose. This is because high nitrogen conditions might inhibit the expression of lignin peroxidase and other enzymes that would be expected to mediate decolorization.

Results showed that some of the fungi were more effective than acid in decolorizing wastewater. Decolorization of wastewater from a textile industry was successfully achieved. Five of highly colored dyes were decolorized successfully. It is interesting to note that some experiments were performed at pH values, one as high as pH 11 and one as low as pH 3 (v/w). Absorption to mycelium was shown to be an important mechanism for wastewater decolorization. In some experiments, adsorption was followed by decolorization of the mycelium. It should be noted that decolorization of mycelium was not observed in experiments even after extended (1 month) incubation.

Knapp et al. (1995) compared the ability of white rot fungi to degrade a variety of dyes, including Amaranth, Methyl Yellow, Brilliant Yellow, Crimson Blue, Orange Red, Chlorazone Yellow, and Acid Red 106. Results were variable in all of the fungi were able to degrade some of the dyes. P. chrysporium was able to mediate 99-100% decolorization of four of the dyes and 24-67% of the other ten. Nevertheless, P. chrysporium was also the least effective of the fungi investigated. This study shows that although the decolorative abilities are limited, other white rot fungi may have with even greater abilities. These investigations also showed that no species of fungi were able to degrade all of the dyes.
