## 3.1 Recent Success in Fungicide Development from Antifungal Leads

Since alcohol and oudanin were found to be fungicidal metabolites in Basidiomycete fungi tenellus (Anke et al. 1977) and Oudemansella maculosa (Muszyk et al., 1996), structurally related compounds have reported fungicidal activity. Each member of this family incorporates a β-methoxyacrylate group or a phenyl-vinyl unit, and all the complex strobilurin A carries either one or two subunits on the benzene ring that render structural complexity (Figure 1). Their mode of action is to inhibit mitochondrial respiration, binding to other class of cycles (Sauer et al. 1995). The unique mode of action may not provide a choice of resistance between β-methoxyacrylate and other fungicides. Through a series of synthetic program to solve these problems, azostrobin (Godin et al. 1992) and kresox-methyl (Ammann et al. 1992) developed as commercial fungicides that overcome the problems of the lead compounds (Clough et al. 1995). Azostrobin is a β-methoxyacrylate toxophore, like strobilurin A whereas kresox-methyl is a β-methoxyoximacetate tetrahydro (Figure 1). Azostrobin has a wide spectrum against all four taxonomic groups of fungi and strong preventive activity, inhibition of germination (Heaney and Knight 1994). Kresox-methyl is a broad-spectrum fungicide with strong antifungal activity against powdery mildew and apple scab (Brun et al. 1996). Its mode of action and amenable to synthetic approach, strobilurins are expected to be a major fungicide in future.

Another example of a metabolite used as a lead compound, Pyrrolnitrin, a secondary metabolite which has a very simple structure, is to provide a significant role in biocontrol activity of the bacteria (Arima et al. 1964). It showed activity against Clav. cinerea and in the greenhouse of B. cinerea and M. grise, the de-control efficacy in the fields was poor because it is rapidly degraded exposed to sunlight. In the synthetic program using pyrrolnitrin as a template, fenhexamid (Newnh et al. 1998; Budox et al. 1999) were used as de-seeded agents against many fungal pathogens. The protection of the chloro substituent in the 3-position of the pyrrole ring led to a remarkable enhancement in stability (Figure 1). Its biological activity was further enhanced by methyl substitution on the phenyl ring. Their improved selectivity over pyrrolnitrin conferred the fungicidal activity against B. cinerea, Molinia spp. and Sclerotinia spp. (Nyfer and Ackermann 1992).
