## 6.1 Cis and Trans-Acting Genetic Factors

### Relevant to the Expression of Biocontrol Genes

Lortie et al. (1996a) used an in vitro approach to detect sense-acting motifs on the chit3 promoter in mycoparagenization. Then Trichoderma Botrytis in agar against plants with T. atvirides P1, produced protein extractions fused to different mycoparagen domains, and used them in electrophoretic mobility shift assays. Competition experiments, using oligonucleotides containing functional and nonfiction consensus for binding of the carbon catabolism repressor Crel (5-SYGGRG-3); Lumikh et al. (1993) found proved the complex non-copyraceous mycelia the binding of Crel to both fragments of the ech-4 promoters are consistent with the presence of two and three consensus sites, for binding of Crel in the two ech-4 promoter fragments. In contrast, the DNA-protein complex from mycoplasma does not induce Crel, as its formation is unaffected by the addition of oligonucleotides. On these findings, our molecular model for ech-4 expression in T. harzianum, which subsequently involves: (a) binding of Crel to two sites in the ech-4 promoter; (b) binding of the vicinity of the “mycopractic” promoter/protein complex to the ech-4 promoter; (c) functional interaction of Crel promoter interaction to enable the formation of the “mycopractic” protein-DNA complex (Lortio et al. 1996a). The gene from T. harizianum has been cloned (Iinen et al. 1996), but no demonstration of its effect on biocontrol in vivo was yet presented.
