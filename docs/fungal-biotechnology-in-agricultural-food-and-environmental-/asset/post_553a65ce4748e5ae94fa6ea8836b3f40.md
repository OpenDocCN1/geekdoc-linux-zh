
## 5.2 Short Method of Composting

Based on the observations of Lambert that compost came from the ruins of the pile having temperature between 50–60°C and adequate supply of oxygen. Sinden and Hauser (1950) developed the so-called short-method of composting because it takes less time than the long method. The short method of composting consists of two phases: outturning for 10–12 days (Phase-I) followed by posturing and conditioning for 6–7 days inside isolated units, called tunnels.

Based on the temperature maintained inside the tunnel, Phase-II can be divided into two phases: posturing(45–48°C for 5–6 days) and conditioning which limited the growth of microorganisms; conditioning are essential for achieving the selectivity in the compost. Short method of composting has many advantages over the long method; more compost per unit weight of the ingredients, higher productivity, less losses for pests and diseases, shorter duration and less environment pollution.

### 5.1.3 Indoor Composting

The environmental pollution related to production of sinking gases as well as short methods of composting drew the attention of the researchers to an alternative (indoor composting). The first work on such composting system used using completely indoor system, it was termed as indoor composting (Laborde 1992); other terms like unlike-controlled composting, rapid composting, and aerobic compost composting are also used. ## 6. SPECIALTY MUSHROOMS

"Specialty mushrooms" is a term given to a group of cultivated mushrooms which are less common in a particular area, but the term has been used practically to represent all mushrooms other than the button mushroom. In the United States accounted for 98% of total production was 1993–1994.

### 6.1 Pleurotus spp. (Oyster Mushrooms)

Unlike the button mushroom, Pleurotus is best known as oyster mushrooms and the epithet "ostrea" refers to its shell appearance. Pleurotus is versatile, capable of growing over a wide range of temperature (5°C-30°C) and on all lignocellulosic substrates P. ostreatus, P. sajoricus, and P. flabell are most popular species. It is a primary fungus and can degrade moistened substrates directly and does not require secondary fungus.

Growth parameters for Pleurotus differ in species, especially temperature requirement and general parameters and have been described by Jandaik (1997). There are wide variations among the growers about method and style of opening the containers for fruiting: complete removal of polycover, only open like Japanese method, and slashes or holes in polymer. Commercial yields are obtained 1 ton fresh weight of mushrooms per ton dry weight of the substrate. One and serious problem with the cultivation of the oyster mushroom is spore allergy, which for facemasks are generally used during the operations.
