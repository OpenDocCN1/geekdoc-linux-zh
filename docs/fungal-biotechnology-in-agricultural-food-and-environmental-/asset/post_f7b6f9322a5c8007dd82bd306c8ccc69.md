## 3 EXPRESSION OF MYOPARASITISM RELATED GENES (MRGS)

Expression of extracellular chitinases is induced by growing Trichoderma on fruit, fungal walls, or mycelia as carbon source. It is believed that chitinolytic enzymes could be induced by soluble chitooligos (Reis et al. 1989). This may be the case for the endo-β-glucosidase of T. harizianum and T. atrovide, which are induced not only by cellobiosases but also by 42 kDa endonuclease (Carsell et al. 1994).

In general, expression from the cell wall degradation of Trichoderma appears to be correlated with a regulatory mechanism involving substrate and repression. The expression of the system is controlled by the level of transcription induced by available sugars (Casairo et al. 1994; De la Cruz et al. 1993; Harman and Harman 2001; Flores et al. 1997; Geeria et al. 1993; Kim et al. 2003; Ramón et al. 1995; De la et al. 2001). In finding terms of signaling, the induction of at least ech2 and ech3 is regulated by a diffusible factor (Cortés et al. 1998). Currently, it has been seen that the activation of MRGS in response to the host, through such molecule depends on the basal expression of ech2 in T. atrovide. In vitro the expression of MRGS in response to cell walls is still observed. Whether a molecule produced by the host in vivo switches the expression of MRGs are repressed, as well as the level of ech2.

Trichoderma also produces oligosaccharides (such as tricholigos), which are N-acetylated amino acid containing an amino alcohol at the C-terminus (Reubart et al. 1988). ## 5 ROLE OF MRGS IN BIOCONTROL AND STRAIN IMPROVEMENT

A major challenge for researchers investigating the mechanisms in the parasitic activity of Trichoderma is to establish the role of cell-wall degrading enzymes in vitro. For fact, we have to consider all cases encoding cell-wall degrading enzymes MRGS of their apparent relation to the process until their role is defined. Intensive efforts using genetic engineering are current directed at producing transgenic T. atrovide lines carrying multiple copies of pr1. The resulting strains produced up to 20 more proteins and all strains were more effective in the control of R. solani. One strain degradation caused by S. atrovide was 6% whereas the degradation for the nontransformed strain was 3% (Flores et al. 1997), demonstrating that pr1 plays an important role in biocontrol and the feasibility of strain improvement through genetic engineering.

The role of Chit3 and Chit36 from T. harizianum in biocontrol has also been tested by expressing the corresponding gene at high levels using the strong promoter (Limon 1999; Vteri et al. 2001). Test result of Trichoderma transforms showed higher efficiency of the chit36 gene under the promoter, but this did not reach statistical significance (Viterbo et al.). The transgenic overexpressing ech42 gene showed high antagonistic activity against R. solani on cotton. However, in vivo experiments with these transgenic lines have not been reported.

In attempt to increase the effectiveness of T. harizianum with bacterial chitinase from Serratia marcesens under the control of the CamV55 promoter. Two transformed strains showed constitutive activity and expressed a protein of the expected family. When these measures against the pathogenic fungi R. solani showed high antagonistic activity, as compared to the nontransformed (Haran et al. 1993). However, in vivo experiments were reported using these strains.

The role of glucases in the interaction between T. harizianum and Pythium was studied (Benhamou and Chet 1997). Interaction between the two fungi was accompanied by the deposition of a cellulose-induced material at potential secretion sites. Trichoderma showed high levels of cellulase enzymes produced. However, cellulose production was not the only trait involved in the process. A marked alteration of the β-1,3-gluc component of the Pythium cell wall was observed, showing that β-1,3-glucases played a key role in the interaction.
