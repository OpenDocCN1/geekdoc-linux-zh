## 3.5.1 Case IV: Identification of Cereal Borne by TLC

Cereals represent a habit with a limited associated fungi, and it is relatively easy to determine Penicillium when kernels are placed on DG18 (Dicholar Yeast Extract 18%), DRES (Dicholar Yeast Extract 18%) or DYG (Dicholar Yeast Extract), YES (Creatine Sucrose agar). At this stage it is not possible to make a determination of these criteria for the following nine Penicillium found on cereals: *P. aurantinum, P. feti, P. tricolor, P. polycomum, P. viridis, P. sp. (Penicillium series Verrucosa), and P. verrucosum (Penicillium series Verrucosa).* Agar plugs from tufts on YES are placed on TLC (plate 20x20 cm) within 30 min, and the plate developed in TLC (Figure 15) shows the secondary metabolites xanthenimine and viomlein are simultaneously detected as two brown spots under visible light in samples from QA. Aurantiamantine and viridamine are both spots, however viridamine is more light blue and viridamine will also be confirmed by the presence of blue viridamine, xanthenimine, and a yellow spot. Aurantiamantine can be produced by two spots, by 3-methoxyviridacin as a blue spot under 255 nm, xanthenimine and viomlein. P. feti, viridamine is identified by a yellow spot of blue and a green spot under orange. The TLC plate should then be sprayed with HCl and then heated at 130°C for 8 min. Penicillium is visible as a blue spot and oxalate as a yellow spot very close to the origin. If metabolites are present on QA the TLC plate should then be sprayed with ANIS and then heated.
