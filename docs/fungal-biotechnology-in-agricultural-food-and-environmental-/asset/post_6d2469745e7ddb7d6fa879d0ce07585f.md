# DEVELOPMENT OF BIOREACTORS USED FOR AZO DYE WASTEWATER

White fungi have been proposed for remediation of solutions contaminated with organic pollutants like DDT, chlorinated phenols, and poly aromatic hydrocarbons (Bumpus 1993). However, decolorization and remediation of wastewater from a variety of produced colored wastewater is the most promising place for use of these fungi in bioremediation systems.

developed to remediate decolores were the subject of considerable research that has been reviewed recently by Knapp et al. (2001). Many investigations have shown the ability of white fungi to decolorize azo dyes, while few investigations have specifically focused on development of bioreactors that could be scaled up and used for this purpose. Zhang et al. (1998) showed that an undesified bacteria F259 was tolerant and rapidly the decolorization of the azo dyes. In different bioreactor configurations, compact packed-bed, fixed-bed, and fluidized-bed bioreactors were used. All of the bioreactors proved suitable for remediation of water-contamination. Dyes up to 100 mg/L were removed rapidly (1–3 days), and the mycelium could be utilized. Particular success was achieved with the fed-bed immobilized-monolith reactor. In this system average decolorization of up to 90% resulted in 97% color removal in 24hr. It is significant that rapid degradation and the fungal mycelium remain robust and unaffected by high concentration of Orange II during repeated 2 months.

# DEVELOPMENT OF BIOREACTORS USED FOR AZO DYE WASTEWATER

White fungi have been proposed for remediation of solutions contaminated with organic pollutants like DDT, chlorinated phenols, and poly aromatic hydrocarbons (Bumpus 1993). However, decolorization and remediation of wastewater from a variety of produced colored wastewater is the most promising place for use of these fungi in bioremediation systems.

Many investigations have shown the ability of white fungi to decolorize azo dyes, while few investigations have specifically focused on development of bioreactors that could be scaled up and used for this purpose. Zhang et al. (1998) showed that an undesified bacteria F259 was tolerant and rapidly the de-colorization of the azo dyes. In different bioreactor configurations, compact packed-bed, fixed-bed, and fluidized-bed bioreactors were used. All of the bioreactors proved suitable for remediation of water-contamination. Dyes up to 100 mg/L were removed rapidly (1–3 days), and the mycelium could be utilized. Particular success was achieved with the fed-bed immobilized-monolith reactor. In this system average decolorization of up to 90% resulted in 97% color removal in 24hr. It is significant that rapid degradation and the fungal mycelium remain robust and unaffected by high concentration of Orange II during repeated 2 months.

Ramsey and Navy (1990b) studied conditions, which promote dye degradation by Trametes versicolor, Bjerkandera and Phanerochromosporium in batch reactors. Only T. versicolor proved better in its ability to decolorize following repeated addition of several azo dyes and mixtures.
Wang (1998) showed the ability of T. versicolor to decolorize water containing an anthraquinone, indigo, and the azo Dye Violet 7 while all the dyes were degraded.
