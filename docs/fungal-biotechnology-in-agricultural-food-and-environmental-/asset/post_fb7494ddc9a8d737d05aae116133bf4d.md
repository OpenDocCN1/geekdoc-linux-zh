## 4.2 Sterol Biosynthesis

The biosynthesis of sterols is an essential metabolism for produce essential constituents of cellular membranes. Most fungi contain ergosterol as a principal steroid (Merz 1991). Recent advances in our understanding of mode of sterol biosynthesis inhibitors (SBI) launched a novel approach to finding inhibitors of sterol biosynthesis, which could lead to new antifungal agents (Barrette-Reay and Ryder 1990). The effects of SBIs have resulted in a great commercial success in the synthetic fungicide. The SBI fungicides being of about half of the market is now practically applied to protect fruits, vegetables, and wines from plant diseases.

## 4.1 Cell-Wall Biosynthesis

Fungal cell wall is a critical target for antifungal agents. Enzymes for the biosynthesis of fungal wall include chitin and glucan synthases (Douglas et al. 1997; Georgopopoulos 1990). Antifungal echinocandins have been discovered as inhibitors of cell wall biosynthesis (Dennying 1997). They are not inhibitors of β-1,3-glucan synthase, an enzyme in the cell wall of many fungi. β-1,3-glucan synthase uses mechanism of UDP-glucose to form the major saccharide of the fungal cell wall (Kang and Chiba) and its synthetic analog with a similar moiety (Frontmling 1994).

Recently, marked improvements in antifungal activity have been made by modifications in the side chains of echinocandins (LY3093 and caspofungin are examples of recently developed drugs, which are generally much more active *in vitro* against a variety of yeast and filamentous fungi).

The discovery of discoveryins led to an important target for screening natural products. The two structurally related compounds were first isolated from *Penicillium* (Schwartz et al. 1991) and *Pseudomonas dispersa* (O'Sullivan et al. 1992). **Figure 2** shows they showed potent antifungal activity.

Chitin, β-1,4-acetylglucosamine polymer, plays a major structural and strengthening role in fungal cell wall. Chitin is composed of microfibrils of hydrogen-bonded polysaccharide chains that are covalently linked to other polysaccharides. It has been demonstrated that chitin synthase inhibitors showed antifungal activity when applied to growing fungi (Lorin et al. 1993).

Nikkomycins are analogs of UDP-N-acetylglucosamine which show antifungal activity by inhibiting chitin synthase as competitive inhibitors (Hunter 1995). The potency of a inhibitor of chitin synthase may depend not only on the isomer's effectiveness in binding a cell wall, but also on affinity for enzyme. Recent studies revealed that chitin synthase genes of fungi have different sensitivities to the inhibitors (Munro and Gray 1995). Therefore, new antifungal compounds with high activity and specificity to chitin synthase may be generated from diverse chemical pool of metabolites.

## 4.3 Acetyl-CoA Carboxylase (ACC)

Discovery of sorafen from a mycobacteria was an important event in antifungal metabolite development, because not only increasing microbial diversity but as a novel target for antifungal agents (Gerth et al 1994). Acetyl-CoA carboxylase catalyzes carboxylation of acetyl-CoA to malonyl-CoA at the site of ATP. The subunits of ACC are usually separate in prokaryotes, which form a multifunctional enzyme complex in eukaryotes.

The reason why sorafen is to inhibit bacteria. Sorafen A is which mainly responsible for antifungal activity against *Sorghum cellulosum* strain So26 effectively controlled powdery mildew (*Erysiphe graminea* f. sp. hordei), snow mold (Galactomyces nivalis) in rye, apple scab (Venturia inaestris) on apple and gray mold (B. cinerea) Raichenberg and Holst (1995). Soridan has no effect on ACC of plants, although inducing no phytotoxicity in the field (Vahleneck et al. 1994). In contrast, ACC from rat was strongly inhibited by the soridan (Pridzan et al. 1995). Due to these effects on experimental animals, Soridan used for control of plant diseases strongly suggests that ACC could be a target site for antifungal screening. Considering the numerous diversity of natural products related to the specificity of ACC, novel antifungal compounds with high activity and specificity to chitin synthase may be generated from diverse chemical pool of metabolites.

## 4.4 Nucleic Acid

One of the areas that can be antifungal is nucleic acid metabolism. The synthesis of nucleic acids involves numerous biochemical reactions from the initial synthesis of nucleotides to the final product of ribonucleotide and deoxyribotide. A large number of compounds have been known to be inhibitors of nucleic acid metabolism. However, few of these compounds have been used in antifungal agents. ## 5 TRENDS IN FUTURE BIOTECHNOLOGY

During the last two decades, there were numerous efforts focusing on the isolation and identification of a wide range of biologically active products. As a result, hundreds of thousands of compounds became available for the evaluation as potential antifungal leads. The concept of HTS was developed to screen a large number of chemical libraries, which limits the overcome of in vitro and in vivo assays. The HTS was designed to target a specific fungal target in a reduction. By measuring the activity of the compound at the target fungus, it of considerable interest can avoid complications arising from other metabolic events. These approaches can lead to the discovery of new and effective fungicides by screening the fungal system. The method was already applied in developing new fungicides such as soridans mentioned earlier. The target-directed screening will be fortified by DNA sequence information that is exponentially increased in recent years by a number of fungal genome projects. The genomic information can provide a wealth of needs to be validated and screened for new antifungal leads (DiDomenico 1999).
