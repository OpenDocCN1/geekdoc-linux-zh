### 4.2 Fermentation/Production

There are potentially three systems that can be used for production of mycoderma agents; submerged liquid, substrate fermentation, and two-phase system (Auld 1993). At the industrial level, fermentation is the most common method for production of microbial mycoderma. Two microfungal agents, C. truncatum and Trichoderma have used this way in the States (Churchill 1982; Swoll 1991). Understanding the importance of nutrition and environmental factors on induction of fungal sporulation, spore yield, and bioher efficacy, various approaches can be developed through efficient laboratory techniques. Jackson and Bobhart (1990) reported that carbon concentrations and C/N ratios are key to sporulation of the mycoderma agent C. truncatum. Carbon concentrations ranging from 0.4 to 1.5 gave highest yield with optimal concentration. Similarly, a ratio at 1:1 was more effective for sporulation than 40:1 or 5:1.
