These aspects are neglected in the commercialization of biocontrol.

## 8 CONCLUSIONS

Work conducted on BCP demonstrates that in cases biological control can provide adequate decay control, but in others it is merged with integrated control measures. BCP is compatible with many alternatives to fungicide treatment, which can be easily adapted to control practices, and be used in a case where other control measures are insufficient. Greater effort is needed to identify synergistic mechanisms to achieve effective control. Research in advanced physiological and genetic manipulation of the yeast biocontrol agents and the development of supernatant mixtures that might increase the efficiencies of BCP are only the tip of the iceberg in terms of what can be achieved in the postharvest control system. They also indicate that, in the future, there will be additional situations where biocontrol treatment would be adequate for the control of postharvest fruits. Greater effort is needed to identify which control measures can be used alone. This includes not only replacing a fungicide treatment but also instances where fungi are registered for postharvest use and where decay is significant.
