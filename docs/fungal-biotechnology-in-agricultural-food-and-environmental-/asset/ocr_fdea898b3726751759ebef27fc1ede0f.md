# 1 INTRODUCTION

For well over a century, metal-contaminated industrial waters have been released into the environment by industry, agriculture, sewage treatment, and mining operations worldwide. Since the most, the nuclear fuel cycle in the United States, and as such represents represent unique wastes of uranium and radioactive metals. Metal ions, unlike organic chemicals, persist in the environment indefinitely (Volesky and Holman 1995). Control of such discharges has only been recently regulated in the last two or decades. Many toxic inorganic chemicals have, over time, accumulated in soils, sediments, and living organisms throughout the world. Metal-biominerallization may be generated by a single industry, e.g., electroplating waters, or in other cases may be the result of many dissolved metals and ionic compounds at various pH values and ionic strengths with solid and particulate matter. Governments are now regulating this mandating preventative actions and forcing industries to remove toxic metals before they are discharged.
