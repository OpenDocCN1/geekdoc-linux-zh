## 2 PRODUCTION OF MICROBIAL FLAVOUR

Flavoring compounds are produced either by biotransformation of precursor compounds to flavor end-products, or by de novo synthesis. Fungi play an important role in this field, as they can transform abundantly substrates as terpenes, and thereby may make the process economical with the problems related to commercialization of terpenoid products. Rensburg et al. (1997) have shown the possibility of limonene biotransformation in yeasts.

## 1 INTRODUCTION

The demand for natural flavoring substances has increased sharply in recent years, and tremendous interest has been shown in their production by microorganisms (Berge and Evans 1996; Carla 1995; Schindler and Santos 1992; Schneider et al 1997). Fungal biotransformation of low-cost substrates to flavor and aroma compounds having high value appears to have great potential. Such compounds have been accepted by the United States Drug Administration (FDA) and by the European Commission (86/588/EEC; EWG 92/191) as being natural and falling under the generally regarded as safe (GRAS) category (Janssens et al 1992; Taylor 1996). Fungal compounds obtained are typically extracellular and suitable for fermentation because of easy downstream processing and high yields. Extensive reviews on this subject have been published by Demmnetere (2001); Kaphmar (1994); Mestri (1994); and Van der Werf et al (1997). This chapter will deal with the description and discussion of fungal flavors and aromas.

## 2.1 Terpenoids

Terpenoids constitute one of the largest groups of natural products and impact a value of pleasant and floral scents. These compounds contain one or more basic isoprene units linked from head to tail. Depending on the number of units, terpenes are classified as monoterpenes (2 units), sesquiterpenes (3 units), diterpenes (4 units), triterpenes (6 units), and polyterpenes (higher units). Terpenoids, produced from filamentous fungi are used by various food industries. Readily available monoterpenes, such as α-pine and limonene, are used as substrates for conversion into flavoring compounds (Berger et al 1992; Van der Werf et al 1997).
