# 4.5 H₂O Generating Enzymes

In the enzymes mentioned earlier, the lignolytic structure of white fungi includes extracellular H₂O generating enzymes, essential for peroxidase activity. A number of enzymes have been shown to lead to the production of H₂, including glyoxal oxidase (GLOX), glucose oxidase, methanol oxidase, and aryl-alcohol oxidase (AO) by Zhao and Jane (1995). White fungi that lack H₂O generating enzymes may rely on the oxidation of organic acids such as oxalate and glyoxalate, which indirectly results in H₂O production (Urrea et al. 1995).

# 4.6 Cellobi Dehydrogenase

Cellobi dehydrogenase (CDH) is an extracellular enzyme produced by white fungi (Henrikson et al. 2000). It oxidizes cellulose, mannitose, and lactose efficiently to their corresponding sugars by a ping-pong mechanism using a spectrum of electron acceptors including quinones, phenols, radicals and triazole. The function of CDH is not obvious, but P. chrysosporium produces high levels of approximately 0.5% of the secreted oxygen on a mass basis, suggesting it plays an important role. Many functions have been shown for CDH, some of which are related to lignin degradation, but here only those that are related to lignin degradation will be discussed. It has been suggested that CDH reduces aromatic compounds by repolymerization and supporting lignin degradation (Anders al 1999; Tempier and Egg 2009). OH radicals are very reactive and can attack various units of lignin, including aliphatic C-C bonds and aromatic rings. Typical reactions of OH radical with the major glycyl-β-aryl ether structure of lignin can result in demethylation, B-O-4 cleavage, oxidation, or polymerization (Hamel et al. 2002; Tanaka et al. 2002). The lignin is expected to degrade the polymer, however it remains unclear whether any wood decay use OH• to oxidize lignin. Oxidation of both phenolic and nonphenolic units in the lignin polymer may make it susceptible to attack by LAC or MnP (Hiden and 2000; Tanaka et al. 1999a).
