## Volariella spp. (Paddy Straw Mushroom)

Volariella is a mushroom of the tropics and subtropics; it grows at a relatively high temperature of 35°C. This is a very fast growing mushroom which takes about 10 days from spawning to first harvesting, is easy to cultivate with a cropping cycle of 3 weeks but does not give good economic yields and its-life is poorest of all. Understandably, in 1997 it contributed 0.18 million tons compared to 4.6 million tons production in 1986. Nevertheless, significance lies in East Asian countries where staple food is rice and paddy mushroom can be utilized for self-consumption or trade in the domestic market. Commonly cultivated species are V. volacea and V. bonbaceous.

There are two commercial substrates for this mushroom: the traditional paddy straw bundles made into beds after short fermentation, the other involves the use of cotton compost after short period (Quimio 1993; Quirio et al. 1990). While outdoor seasonal farming is done on paddy straw by smaller growers, modern indoor cultivation is done on fermented cotton waste. Grain (wheat, sorghum) or corn is used, however, cotton leaves are better substrate (Chang 1982). Several techniques have been used for the cultivation of V. volacea in the tropics, which thrives in the temperature range of 30–35°C and R.H. of 75–85% (Quimio et al. 1990). However, the traditional method of cultivation in South East Asia is on straw beds, both outdoor and indoor, while the more sophisticated indoor technique is preferred for industrial scale production on cotton compost; the later gives higher yield but is capital-intensive process. While the traditional straw cultivation has been described by several authors (Khanna 1997; Quimio et al. 1990), indoor cultivation technology under controlled conditions on cotton waste will be best here (Chang 1982; Quimio 1993). Since 1973, straw mushroom technology is now being practiced in Thailand, Indonesia, Vietnam, and some parts of Malaysia and Philippines. It takes about 4 days to make cotton waste mixed with rice and bran. Compost is filled in plastic mushroom bags with blower and pasteurized with live steam for 2h at 60–62°C; Conditioned at 50–52°C for 8h and then allowed to cool to 34–36°C suitable for spawning. The amount of spawn used is 1.4% of the dry weight. As the browning process begins, primordia start to form by stimulating the blocks with cold water (12°C) for 3–4 days. The second flush starts after several weeks. Mushrooms are ready for harvesting approximately after 7–11 days of soaking with gentle twisting by hand and stem cut with sharp knife like for button mushroom. After harvesting, blocks are soaked again in water for 24 h. The flush breaks in shiitake are 16–20 days long. The total production on cyclic logs is just 3–4 months and biological efficiency is also very high (75–125%) as against cycle of 5–6 years and B.E. of 33% in natural log cultivation (Royse 2001).

## 6.4 Auricularia sp. (Wood Ear Mushroom)

The species of Auricularia, commonly known as wood ear, are morphologically alike and also quite different from other. With typical ear shaped cartilaginous tissue and gelatinous substance, these are well described as food for people. This mushroom is very popular in China and Southeast Asia but does not attract western consumers. It has proved to possess many medical attributes; treatment of cold, sore throat, anemia and hypertensive effect (Qin et al. 1996; 1997). Out of 10 recognized species of Auricularia many cultivated species are A. auricula and A. polytcha, the fruit is thin and light coloured with the layer being thicker, longer, and darker. A. Fuccininea is also grown on a limited scale. Thailand and Taiwan are the main producers of this mushroom. Like Shiitake (Edodes), Auricularia are also produced on natural logs as well as on substrate (sawdust material), profitable system). The cultivation of Auricularia on natural logs is popular in the areas where substrate is abundant. This mushroom, unlike Lentina, is not fastidious about the type of species and any tree can be used. A log diameter 6–6cm and length of 1m is recommended. The time of season for log feeling is similar to that for Lentina log cultivation; when we are just starting to dry in autumn then logs have sufficient moisture and substrate to support mycelium growth. Holes are made, spawned, and sealed like that for Lentina and incubated (20–28°C) turned upside down a month. After about 2 months, logs are transferred to the cropping area which may be an open area in a forest house or shed where logs are kept upright and frequently watered; ideal temperature for cropping is 15–25°C of which depends on the strain used. Mushrooms can be harvested about 30 days after exposure to cropping conditions. During the winters, production ceases but logs are protected for fruiting next spring by heavily watering. The logs should continue to produce mushrooms year after year unless they become heavily contaminated with some decaying fungus.
