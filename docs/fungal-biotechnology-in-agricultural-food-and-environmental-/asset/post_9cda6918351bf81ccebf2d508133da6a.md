## Biotechnological Potential of Ergot Alkaloids

M. Flieger | P. Mehta | Dr. H.S University, Sagar, India | Institute of Microbiology, Czech Academy of Sciences, Prague

### INTRODUCTION

Ergot alkaloids belong to the group of compounds produced by fungi, which are referred as secondary metabolites. They are produced by a number of fungi such as Claviceps but have also been found in other fungi and higher plants. In early days, the medieval used ergot from naturally infected plants. The role of ergot has undergone important changes from a toxic parasite to another source. Ergot became known by the extraction of the first alkaloid in 1875, isolation of ergot (mixture of ergamine, ergokryptin) in 1907. At the beginning of chemistry, physiology, genetics, and therapeutic applications of ergot have been studied extensively (Berde and Stummer 1978; Mukherjee and Mehta 2000; Reha and Mlade 1993; Tuzianski 2001). Present review gives an overview of biotechnological potential of ergot with a perspective for the future, recorded from outside this genus (Flieger et al. 1997; Kozlovsky 1999).
