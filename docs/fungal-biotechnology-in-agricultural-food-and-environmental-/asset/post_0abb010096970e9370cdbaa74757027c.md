## 5.2 Fungi-Root Nematode Interactions

Nematophagous fungi can infect, kill, and digest nematodes. Most of these can also live parasitically and some have mycoparasitic abilities. Most parasitic nematodes attack roots, the rhizosphere of nematophagous fungi is important from a biological control point of view. We discovered that nematophagous fungi are more abundant in the rhizosphere than in soil (Pers and Jansson 1997). ## 6 BIOLOGICAL CONTROL OF PLANT-PARASITIC NEMODES

The use of nematophagous fungi for biological control of plant-parasitic nematodes has a long history (for extensive review on earlier experts by Stirling 1991). The investigations have resulted in variable and conflicting results—sometimes excellent, others occasions no control at all. The reasons for these varying results may be many, but the major reason is probably lack of knowledge about physiology and ecology of the nematophag fungus. Their interaction in soil with nematodes, other organisms, and the environment is little understood because of difficulties of working in the complex soil matrix and also lack of good methods for these purposes. We believe it necessary to perform both laboratory experiments and field studies to understand the complexity of fungi interactions with their environment. The following section will discuss some of the possibilities to use nematophagous fungi for biological control.
