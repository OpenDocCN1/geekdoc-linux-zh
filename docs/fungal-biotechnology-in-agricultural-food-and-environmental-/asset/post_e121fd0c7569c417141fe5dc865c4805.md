## 3.2 Methods for Yeast Characterization

Methods by which strains of the species can be differentiated have been very important for yeast strain characterization. In winery, several studies have analyzed the diversity of grapes and many interesting methods have been developed (Figure 2). PCR method using specific sequences that flank introns displays polymorphism mainly at the species level (de Barros Lopes et al. 1996); PCR amplification of elements (repeat sequences that flank the TY1 retrotransposon) has been widely used to characterize yeast strains of interest (Ness et al. 1993). Another PCR-based approach, known as the amplification of random segments of DNA with a single and short (5 to 15-mer) primer of an arbitrary sequence that flank intron, interspecific, or intaspecific, depends mainly on the region targeted; however, one of the most problems with this technique is the low (non-specific) patterns obtained.

The mitochondrial DNA restriction fragment (mdDNA-FLP) has been long used as a method for characterization of yeast strains (Zenhait et al. 1998). However, due to the complexity with the amplification and insufficient sensitivity, its use for analysis was limited until recently.
