Perhaps the most important determinant to the commercial use of mycorrhizal fungi is at large-scale field trials in various agricultural soils and in absence of lack of awareness among the users. ## 5 ENVIRONMENTAL CONCERN ON CHEMICAL FERTILIZER USAGE

Fertilizer production is also an environmental concern. Every ton of phosphate produces water that can overflow into waterways. New regulations have been enacted to guard against pollution (Johnson and Twain 1996). Mycorrhiza offers an alternative to many environmentally sensitive, sustainable, and economical ways of creating fertility and facilitating poverty reduction. In situations where the native mycorrhizal fungi potential is low or ineffective, providing appropriate fungi for the plant system is working. With the current state of technology, it is better for transplanted crops and in areas where soil distance is a factor. The first step in any mycorrhizal program will be to obtain an isolate that is both effective and able to spread and grow in the root, as to enhance the growth and stress tolerance of the host. Individual isolates of mycorrhiza vary widely; screening trials are important to select isolates that will perform successfully. Screening under actual cropping conditions is best because indigenous mycorrhizal fungi, pathogens, soil chemical and physical properties influence the result. Once-scale application of the mycorrhizal spores in common masses of mycorrhizal applications needs to be developed and new methods devised. Most importantly, from large-scale field tests, cost-benefit analysis will accurately determine the economic benefit derived from the use of mycorrhizal fungi. This will be the key factor in commercialization of mycorrhizal fungi. Biological scientists are really able to assess the economic factors involved in the application of a new technology. It is important to design the technical and infrastructure facility and service requirements for the quality of the finished product before the release of the product in the market.
