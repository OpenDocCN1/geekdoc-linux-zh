
## 2.2 De Novo Synthesis

Unlike the case of biotransformation, *de novo* synthesis, no specific precursors are found in the medium. Products formed by different micro-organisms include lactones, esters, and oxygenated terpenes (Table 3; Schindler and Schaps, 1986).

The fruity flavor aroma compounds produced by *de novo* synthesis depend on the media constituents like glucose, amino acids, and salts (Quehl and Ruffott 1992). The *de novo* synthesis category of the experiments carried out till now were found only on a laboratory scale. However, they have helped in the study of the enzyme systems involved, and of their reaction pathways through the synthesis. Sarris and Latrasse (1985) demonstrated that when *Fusarium* was grown on a solid salt medium till sporulation, it produced a lactone with a peach-like aroma under insufficient aeration, 2-methyl and 3-methylbutanol produced. *D. viride* in a soft medium generated a strong coconut aroma of 6-pentyl-2-pyrone (Welsh et al. 1989).

Composition of the medium and age of culture generally influence the flavoring of compounds in *de novo* synthesis. For instance, an intense banana aroma was formed by *Ceratomyces fimbrita* when the growth medium was supplemented with several nutrients (Christian and Rimbault 1999). Clarity et al. (1999) also reported formation of 9-decalone by an optimized immobilized culture of *Sporidobolus salmenter*.

## 2.4 Dairy Flavors

Dairy flavors, primarily cheese flavors, are widely used in the food industry. Kristofansson (1973) studied the genesis of cheese flavor. Maga (1974) investigated the formation of flavor constituents by using Penicillium on milk and curd. Similarly, formation of cheese flavor, either by the enzymatic or the chemical route, has been studied by Law and Mulholland (1991) used fungal cultures.

## 4 CONCLUSIONS

In view of the increasing demand for natural flavors, the production by microbial means seems to be a good proposition. The method is effective, and also economical. Monosteres like lipase and pinene, which are produced in large quantities in nature, are used for the production of flavoring compounds of high quality. While products are produced and high enough for industrial production and many microbial processes are already operating, Newer genetic engineering techniques may still help in enhancing the product yield. There is also necessary that the effects of different parameters on a plant pilot scale will lead to many successful commercial technologies.

## 2.5 Mushroom Flavors

Sugihara and Hansfeld (1954) found that mushroom flavor could be produced by Rhacaceus when grown under submerged conditions. Goldberg (1960); Littlefield et al. (1963); and Day et al. (1974) studied the production of mushroom flavor by Marchella. Lentinas edible are produced in 3-5 days, compared to 8 days when the medium was enhanced with ethanol (Sumimoto et al. 1971). Hansfeld et al. (1992) investigated the production of mushroom flavor from Trichoderma numidum under submerged conditions. Van Vleck and Scheffer (1972) reproduced production in the mycelium of Boletus edulis. Pysallo and Honkanen (1976) found that cultivation conditions as well as many media play a vital role in the production. Mushroom flavors have also been produced from A. oryzae, Portia portifolia (Schindler and Schmid 1982).

## 3 COMMERCIAL IMPORTANCE

Production of flavoring compounds using fungal cultures is economical and industrially. Presently, many compounds are produced industrially by the use of fungal cultures; e.g., (R)-Dodecanolol using yeast
