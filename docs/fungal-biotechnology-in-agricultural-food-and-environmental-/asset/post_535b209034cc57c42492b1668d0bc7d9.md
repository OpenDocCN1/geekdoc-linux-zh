## 2.4 Factors Affecting Aflatoxin/ST Synthesis

### 2.4.1 Genetic Regulation

Since the aflatoxin and ST biosynthetic genes are found to be clustered on a single chromosome in A. parasiticus and A. flavus and in A. nidulans (Brown et al. 1996b; Woloshuk and Prieto 1998; Yu et al. 1995a), these are expressed concurrently in the genome. In both there is a regulatory gene, affR, for activating gene transcription located in the middle of the gene clusters. afl gene, sequence for a specific zinc finger DNA-binding protein, a 4-type 47kDa polypeptide, has been shown to be required for transcriptional activation of genes, the structural genes (Chang et al. 1993; 1995b, 99b et al. 1998; Flaherty and Payne; 1999; Payne et al. 1993; Yu et al. 1997; Wolosuk et al. 1994) by the binding sequence 5'-TCCGGCA-3' in the promoter region of the structural genes (Flaherty et al., 1998, 1999a) in *A. parasiticus* and *A. nidans* (Yu et al. 1996). Used in industrial fermentation, this was found to cause a defective afl gene in addition to potential other effects (Matsushina et al. 2001b; Takashi et al. 2002). Thus, the function of the gene in induction of aflatoxin was confirmed in this food grade *Aspergillus*. Additional factors involved in regulation of aflatoxin synthesis were revealed by Flaherty and Payne (1997). Adjacent to the afl gene cluster, a diverged gene was found to be involved in the regulation of metabolism (Myers et al. 1998; Chang, personal communication). The exact mechanism by which afl modifies the transcription of these pathway genes in concert with afl is unclear. A gene for transporting aflatoxin out of cell, A. parasiticus (Yu, unpublished).
