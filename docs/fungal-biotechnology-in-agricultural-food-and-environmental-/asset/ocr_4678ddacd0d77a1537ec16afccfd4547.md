Many investigations have shown the ability of white fungi to decolorize azo dyes, while few investigations have specifically focused on development of bioreactors that could be scaled up and used for this purpose. Zhang et al. (1998) showed that an undesified bacteria F259 was tolerant and rapidly the decolorization of the azo dyes. In different bioreactor configurations, compact packed-bed, fixed-bed, and fluidized-bed bioreactors were used. All of the bioreactors proved suitable for remediation of water-contamination. Dyes up to 100 mg/L were removed rapidly (1–3 days), and the mycelium could be utilized. Particular success was achieved with the fed-bed immobilized-monolith reactor. In this system average decolorization of up to 90% resulted in 97% color removal in 24hr. It is significant that rapid degradation and the fungal mycelium remain robust and unaffected by high concentration of Orange II during repeated 2 months.

# 7 CONCLUSIONS

It is clear that white fungi are able to mediate extension and rapid degradation of azo dyes. Although several micro-organisms (some fungi and bacteria) have been reported to be able to decolorize this color pollutant, it is argued that white fungi have superior biodegradative abilities in the regime. Enzymes (including peroxidase, manganese laccase, and others) tremendously the degrading enzymes are important. It is clear that other oxidative enzymes, especially cytochrome P-450 monooxygenases are also important and they require further scrutiny for a better understanding of how azo dyes (and other organic pollutants) are degraded by these fungi.
