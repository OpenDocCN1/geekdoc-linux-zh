c. Ester. Various aliphatic esters could be obtained from limonene by cells of Rhizopus oryza; such cells tolerate high substrate concentrations, and hence enhance the production of large amounts by continuous addition of substrate, e.g., geranyl butyrate (Molinat et al. 1995). 6-hydroxy limonene was used to give direct esterification of alcohols (n-) to give a high yield (98%) of hexyl caprate (Molinat et al. 1998). Agrawal et al. (2000) demonstrated the production of dihydromy acetate from nerol using Mucor sp. the metabolic pathway was not studied but it appears that 6-hydroxy geranyl pyrophosphate is led to the formation of dihydrochamvone, which is then reduced to dihydrochamvone and acetylated to dihydromy acetate. Regio-specific esters were obtained from 4-ethylcitronellol, citronellol, and linalol by basha (1998). Patel et al. (1992) described a simple method of using Geotrichum album to improve the optical purity of (S)-(-4-chloro-3-hydroxy butyl methyl ester by conversion. Cells contain a single enzyme that can catalyze the reduction to hydroxy product. Facebook et al. (1987) demonstrated the synthesis of terpenes esters through an amino precursor in C. G. Fragano. Patel (1998) worked on the possibility of esters from amino acids in mucus; this could potentially improve upon the isolation and purification steps, compared to the aqueous fermentation systems.

d. Lactones, Aldehydes, and Ketones. Biogenesis of volatile terpenes from fungi has been demonstrated by Sifard et al. (1990). Lactone formation appears to be the result of a metabolic overflow. When the regular 3-hydroxy acids expand to 3-keto or 5-hydroxy acids, lactone formation takes place. These compounds impart fruit-like, buttery, sweet, or nutty odors. Macrocycles were produced using Useilago zeae, Patterson and Eitman (1998) accomplished the bioconversion in Mucor sp. to delta-lactone formation. Facebook et al. (1990) obtained a mixture of saturated and unsaturated-γ-lactones using γ-keto acid as the substrate; the acid is reduced to γ-hydroxy acid, which ultimately cyclizes to γ-lactones. Serrano-Carreon et al. (1992) studied the formation of γ-δ and 8-hydroxy acids in Trichoderma harzianum. Sporolomyces oligos culture was able to produce an intense peach (γ-decalactone) and lemon (cis-6-dodecen-4-ol) oils by Chou (1994). P. roquet was used for producing lactones, methyl 4-dodecenol from limonene like saffron and orange (Chapter and Croft 1992). The compound 4-decano formed by γ-oxidation of the saturated substrate, or by β-oxidation of oleic acid followed by lactonization of the mentioned terpenoids. The biosynthetic pathway in S. odoraris, results in the formation of 4-hydroxy-dodecanol produced by submerged conditions. Characterization of terpene cultures was studied by Taylor and May 1996. It is possible that lactone is a result of β-oxidation of linseed acid to dodecano acid and liberated.

Production of pent-α-pyrone by Trichoderma viridis has been reported (Praparufilla and Karavth 1992). The addition of amber X-2 resin to the medium overcomes product inhibition. Kalyani et al. (2000) studied the formation of pent-α-pyrone from terpene cultures under submerged conditions. This has also been done using T. koningii (Benioni et al. 1990). Aldehydes were formed via degradation of amino acids. This involves oxidation and dehydration leading to formation of aldehyde compound produced with high yields of flavoring compounds. Among aldehydes, benzaldehyde (almond) and vanillin, were yielded by many fungi.

As only one regio-specific fatty acid, the hydroxylation was significant.
