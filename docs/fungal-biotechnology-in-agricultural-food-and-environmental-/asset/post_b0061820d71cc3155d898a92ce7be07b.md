## 3 FUNGI FOR BIOCVERSION OF DISTILLERY WASTE

The selection of the proper organisms to use for still bioconversion is of the utmost importance. The goal is to utilize the substrate effectively and at the same time its biomass has a high nutrition value, especially in its content. According to Tomlinson (1976a) the lower and higher fungi are the most suitable for bioconverting distilleries.

### 3.1 Yeasts

Yeasts have been chosen in the majority of cases for cultivation in distillery stills of different origin. They have been commonly used in production of yeasts, so there are no reports detailing their use for food or feed except in cases with high biomass growth of yeasts leads to reduction of time and cost, also with limited capacity of contamination or microorganisms. The fermentation process is easy and continuous production is possible. The use of yeast is interesting because of its yield and vitamin content. Some factors must be taken into account (Huayand et al. 1986); (a) the yeast should not have high levels of contaminants, if it is to be used for feed), (b) energy is needed for separating the yeast from the substrate, (c) effective separation is necessary, and (d) the remaining effluent needs to be treated in a treatment facility.

### 3.2 Filamentous Fungi

Compared to yeast, filamentous fungi have been used frequently for bioconversion of distillery wastes. The reasons are that they may be slower growing and more susceptible to infection. However, filamentous fungi have some advantages as they produce a series of hydrolytic enzymes and tolerate substrates without fungus; this property enables them to grow in cell and cellulose substrates. The second advantage is in their filamentous morphology which allows separation of biomass by simple filtration. Compared to yeast, filamentous fungi vary in tolerance to temperature, pH, nutrients, and they also have a lower nucleic acid content in the biomass (Araujo et al. 1997). Various species of fungi were used for distillery fermentation applications, as Aspergillus sp. are the most popular ones. ### 3. Mixed Cultures

Some research was done on the use of mixed cultures of nutrients in silage to improve better yields of biomass. Comparison of different yeasts, bacteria, and yeasts and molds from different substrates showed better biomass. The yield of the mixed cultures was significantly higher than that of pure cultures. A two-step fermentation process was used to produce large biomass. Yeast was used in the first step to produce large amounts of filaments which reduced the COD of the effluent (Azzam and Heikal 1988; Bottaro Castilla et al. 1984; Nudel et al. 1987). In whey wash was a three-mixed culture of G. Candida, hansenella aurantia, and kravis was used. The results of the three organisms showed on the dilution rate during continuous fermentation (Barker et al. 1982). Twelve different species, mainly Candida and Trichosporon were used. Yeast was effective for the treatment of two fermentaceous molasses (Malois et al. 1987).
