# Control of Nematodes by Fungi

Hans-Börje Janssen / Luis V. Lopez-Lorca
Universidad de Alicante, Alicante, Spain

# INTRODUCTION

Many nematodes are parasites of plants and animals causing severe damage. Many anthelmintics used to control plant-parasitic nematodes have created resistance in the nematode population, resulting in decreased control (Nansen 1993). The chemicals which are used to control plant-parasitic nematodes, are often toxic compounds causing environmental and health problems. Several of these have been banned in many countries and the current process of phasing out the ozone-depleting substance, methyl bromide, which is an effective fungicide and insecticide, will increase the problem in nematode control (Ordner 1992). Furthermore, the possibility of use nematophagous fungi should be considered.

To be able to use nematophagous fungi for a larger scale we need to increase our efforts to learn more about the biology of these organisms, and their interactions with nematodes, plants, and other soil organisms, on ecological as well as cellular and molecular levels. In this paper we will review and discuss some of the research, which has been performed on nematophagous fungi. Based on recent discoveries we will also speculate on many ways to use nematophagous fungi for nematode control.

# 2 WHAT ARE NEMMATODES?

Nematodes are small roundworms occurring in most environments (soil, aquatic and marine). Most nematodes are saprophytes or microbes in the soil. Other nematodes live part or most of their life cycles as parasites of plants or animals. In the current review we will concentrate on parasitic nematodes, mainly attacking plants.
