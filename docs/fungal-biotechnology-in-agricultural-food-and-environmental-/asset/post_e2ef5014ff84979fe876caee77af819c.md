## 4 FUMONISINS

### 4.1 Occurrence and Toxicity

Investigation of the association of human esophageal cancer and the consumption of Fusarium verticillioides (called F. moniliforme) infected maize by South African Researchers suggested that fumonisin were a new group of toxins (Beizidoun et al. 1998; Gelber et al. 1998). Fumonisinins were also identified from F. verticillioides, these toxins have been reported in cultures of F. antifungal, F. flavus, F. lamini, F. oxysporum, and F. proliferatum (Bennett 1993; Mukvold and Desjardin 1997). Other genera of fungi that produce fumonisins by Alternaria have also been reported (Micha et al. 1994; Moore et al. 1996). Fumonisin bacteria have been known to produce toxins with a structure closely related to fumafinin (Michora et al. 1992). The Alternaria alternata, a toxic pathogen, was found to produce AOL toxin (Tang et al. 1996). The fumonisins produced by F. verticillioides and F. proliferatum cause health problems in humans and animals since these species decolonize corn over the wheat (Bhatnagar et al. 2002; Dunston 2006; Riley et al. 1996).

Naturally produced fumonisins, including FB1, FB2, FB3, FB4, and FB5, have been identified. FB1 is the most potent, and in chemical formula, FB1 is a derivative of 1,2,3-tricarboxylic acid of A-12-amino-16-methyl-3,5,10,11,14-pentadecanoylcane (Desjardin et al. 1992; Riley et al. 1993). Other series of fumonisins such as B (FB2), B (FB3), and B (FB4), occur at lower levels. The other series of fumonisins such as A (CA, AC and C) also occur at lower levels. The other fumonisins were produced from removal of tricarboxylic acid groups (Masser et al.1996; See et al. 1996).

Fumonisins have been produced by diverse fungi with toxic effects. The mechanism of fumonisin toxicity is well understood. Studies have shown that fumonisins have been shown to be toxic and carcinogenic in rats inducing liver cirrhosis and hepatic nodules, adenofibromas, hepatocellular carcinoma, and carcinogenesis (Gelber et al. 1998). ### 4.2 Genetics and Biosynthesis of fumon

Within the last decade, significant advances have been made in mycotoxin research with respect to identification, biochemistry, and genetics of biosynthesis and regulation of toxin formation as well as mycotoxin prevention and control. This is especially true for such mycotoxins as aflatoxins, fumonisins, and trichothecenes which are economically important in agriculture and public health. The pathways, the function of these genes on the chromosomes, and the enzymes involved in the formation have been elucidated in greater details.
