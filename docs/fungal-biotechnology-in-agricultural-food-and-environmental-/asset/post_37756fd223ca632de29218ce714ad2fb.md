# 7 BIOTECHNOLOGICAL POTENTIAL OF ENTOMOPATHOGENIC FUNGI

## INTRODUCTION

Fungi have been known to attack insects and mites for thousands of years. Although the causative agent was not always understood, insects with fungi were recorded by the Chinese in the seventh century (Tanaka and Kaya 1993) with Cordyceps. The first experimental demonstration of a microbe was by Bassi in 1835–1836, with the silkworm pathogen Beauveria. He demonstrated that it be used against Phylloxera in grapes, a pest eventually controlled by copper solution. However, the Nobel Prize winning researcher, Elie Metchoff, found that fungi could be a practical control agent. In Russia from 1878, Metchkoff developed the fungus Metarhizium anisopiae for the control of the caterpillar Antisstra. Metchkoff conducted successful experiments with larvae of austri and the sugar beet weevil, and initiated mass production (Zimmermann et al. 1995). With the mass production of M. anisopiae, Metchkoff applied a biotechnological approach to entomological control for the first time, a precursor to the development of biopesticides. The first actual application of M. anisopiae in Russia was left to Krasilich (1888) who produced the fungus and applied it in the field.

Despite the early discovery of their potential, it was only recently that entomopathogenic fungi have been utilized effectively as natural control, but their activity is very dependent upon environmental conditions. Many have been selected for germination, infection, and orientation with high specificity for species. In some cases, the effect is not robust, and some of the most promising candidates for pest control have lost the ability to persist during stages such as rearing, storage and application. Variation between species or classes of fungi has been noted, thus selection is not often been or not possible. The application of biotechnology to the study and development of entomogenic fungi has the potential to overcome some of these limitations. Biotechnology has contributed to all areas of the development of entomopathogenic fungi.
