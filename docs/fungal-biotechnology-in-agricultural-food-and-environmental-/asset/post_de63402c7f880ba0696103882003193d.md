## 4.5 Herb Synergy

(a) Assessment of natural strain variation and bio-diversity within the pathogen population based on critical epidemiological characteristics, environmental adaptation, virulence, dispersal, and production efficiency.

(b) Evaluation of key areas for efficient scale-up-based production relating to nutritional and physical requirements of specific agents that facilitate selection of economical, implementation strategies, along with downstream processing procedures that are compatible with production methods.

(c) Selection of appropriate methodologies (i.e., longitudinal, studies) for mode of attack and critical capabilities of candidate agents. Over-simplification by using single ingredients will not address the key challenges that fungal agents will encounter in the environment, including competition, temperatures, and UV irradiation.

## CONCLUSION

The identification of efficacious biological controls is only the beginning in the development of hydrochloride products. Continuing strain selection is essential to ensure that ‘nature is’ best’. There will likely be limitations associated with organic notions, therefore enabling technologies such as fermentation, formulation, and application technology will be critical in determining whether a highly efficacious mycicide can be developed into economically feasible products. The ultimate goal is to incorporate mycicides into agricultural production systems. More efforts should be directed into combining biocontrol agents with other herbicides, cultural practices, and the use of multiple pathogens to enhance the effectiveness and frequency of integrated management systems and to reduce the chemical load on crops and in the environment (Joyce et al. Roskow et al. 1999). The challenge is to critically evaluate hydrochloride candidates to make realistic decisions whether they have all the essential characteristics required for successful application in agroystems.
