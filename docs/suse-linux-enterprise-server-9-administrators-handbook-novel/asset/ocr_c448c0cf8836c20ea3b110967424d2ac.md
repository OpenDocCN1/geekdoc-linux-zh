You can set up a DMZ in two ways, as illustrated in Figure 11.5. Configuration A is the traditional way of setting up a DMZ: The single firewall controls and regulates access to the three networks. This setup has a drawback similar to placing the public servers on an internal network in that the firewall is the single point of vulnerability. After a server offers its services to the public network, it becomes vulnerable to potential attackers. As a result, if an attacker penetrates your (single) firewall, he also has open access to your internal network.
