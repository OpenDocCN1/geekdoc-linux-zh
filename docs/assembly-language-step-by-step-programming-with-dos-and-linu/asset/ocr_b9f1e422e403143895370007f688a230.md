Xerox's legendary APL programmer Jim Dunn later told me that I shouldn't ever write an APL procedure that wouldn't fit on a single 25-line terminal screen."More than 25 lines and you are doing too much in order. Split it up," he said. Whenever I worked in APL after that, I adhered to that sage rule of thumb. The Martians ill struck from time to time, but when they did, it was no longer a total loss.

All computer languages have procedures of one sort or another, and assembly language is no exception. Your assembly language program may have numerous procedures. There is no limit to the number of procedures, as long as the total number of bytes of code contained by all the procedures together does not exceed 65,536 (one segment). Other complications arise at that point, but there are mechanisms in assembly language to deal sensibly with those complications.
