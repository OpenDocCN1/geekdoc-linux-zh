So... were these just registers, or new registers?

Both.

As with a lot of things in assembly language, this becomes a lot clearer by drawing a diagram. See Figure 6.6, which shows how SI, DI, BP, and SP doubled in size and got new names—without entirely losing their old ones.

Each of the four registers shown in Figure 6.6 is fully 32 bits in size. However, in each register, the lower 16 bits have a name of their own. The lower 16 bits of ESI, for example, may be referenced as SI. The lower 16 bits of EDI may be referenced as DI. If you're writing programs
