For the point is not to conquer the subject, but to live with it, and grow with your knowledge of it. The journey is the goal, and with this book I've tried hard to help those people who have been frozen with fear at the thought of starting the journey, staring at the complexity of it all and wondering where the first brick in Yellow Brick Road might be.

It's here, with nothing more than the conviction that you can do it.

I got out of school in recession year 1974 with a B.A. in English, summa cum laude, and not much in reliable prospects outside of driving a car. I finessed my way into a job with Xerox Corporation, repairing copy machines. Books were fun, but paperwork makes money—so I picked up a tool bag and had a fine old time for several years, before finishing my way into a computer programming position.

But I'll never forget that awful moment I looked over the shoulder of an accomplished technician at a model 660 copier with its panels off, to see what looked like a bottomless pit of little cams and gears and drums and sprocket chains turning and flipping switching actuators back and forth. Mesmerized by the complexity, I forgot to notice that a sheet of paper had been fed through the machine and turned into a copy of the original document. I was terrified of never learning all the little cams did and missed the comforting simplicity of the Big Picture—that copy machine makes copies.

That's Square One—discover the Big Picture. Ignore the cams and gears for a bit. You can do it. Find out what's important in holding the Big Picture together (ask someone if it's not obvious) and study for that getting down to the cams and gears. Locate the processes that happen. Divide the Big Picture into subpictures. See how things flow. Only then should you focus on something as small and lost in the mess as an individual cam or switch.

That's how you conquer complexity, and that's how I've presented assembly language in this book. Some might say I've shortened the instruction set, but covering the instruction set was never the real goal here.

The real goal was to conquer your fear of the complexity of the subject, with some metaphors and some pictures and some funny stories to bleed the tension away.

Did it work? You tell me. I'd really like to know.
