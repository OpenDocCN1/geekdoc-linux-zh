This is why if someone talks about a "ten octal," they mean 8; "one hundred octal," they mean 64; and so on. Table 2.4 summarizes the octal column values and their decimal equivalents.

A digit in the first column (the units, or ones column) tells how many units are contained in the octal number. A digit in the next column to the left, the tens column, tells how many really are contained in the octal number. A digit in the third column, the hundreds column, tells how many 64s are in the number, and so on. For example, 400 octal means that the number contains four 64s; that is 256 in decimal.
