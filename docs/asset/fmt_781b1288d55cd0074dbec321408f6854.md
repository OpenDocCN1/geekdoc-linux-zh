
People use Linux for different reasons. For many it is a matter of power, stability, multilingual capabilities, or even personal philosophy. However, for others, crass as it might sound, it is a matter of money. Just for a moment about what it usually costs to get started with an operating system. Go to wherever you go to buy software, and take a walk down the aisles. Make a list in your head all the things you want to buy and how each costs: an office suite; a game or two; maybe a graphics program with which to make yourself look better in your digital photos; and a collection of all those firewall, antivirus, antispy, and anti-adware programs that you really need to protect yourself in the Windows world. Now do the math.

After you pick yourself up off the floor, you will understand that we are talking big bucks here. On the other hand, for the price of this book you will have all of the things you wanted and more in the Linux world. Despite the worries that many people, making the move to Linux means not only savings for you, but also more computing versatility. You will not be hamstrung at some point along the way because you do not have this or program when you need it—you'll have it all from the get-go, or be able to download it easily… and at no cost!

You might counter that with the fact that there are a lot of free applications out there for other operating systems, but c'mon, let's face it—these are often limited in terms of their capabilities. The programs with a little more oops are mostly shareware, and most shareware programs are limited in some way, or they only use them for a short time unless you are paying for them. Sure, their costs are relatively low, but $25 and $35 that eventually adds up to a considerable chunk of change. There is also the problem that some of these programs, unknown to you, install backdoors, keyloggers, or make your system a a under sudden garden of adware. Finally, at least in my experience, the majority of such programs are hardly worth the money asked. The only shareware programs I ever found worth being Lemke Software's GraphicConverter and Plask's Comic Life, both for the Mac.

## Is It Just About Money?

While mine is important to the average user, it is certainly not the only reason for taking the Linux plunge; As I mentioned before, Linux is noted for its stability. Try your present system for a month without restarting and see what happens. Linux has been known to run without a reboot for a year or without a decrease in performance. With its multilingual capabilities, Linux is also a perfect choice for language students or users in a multilingual environment.

In addition, Linux is infinitely customizable: You can get your system to look and act the way you want without being wizarded to death. And then there are the applications that come with most Linux distributions. In addition to their wide variety, many are well up to industry stuff, with such as Evolution and the GIMP, being sources of envy for those outside the Linux world.

Finally, with the advent of Microsoft's new Windows system and its demanding hardware requirements (especially if you want to take advantage of its most touted features), you may find your present machine on the fast track to obsolescence. Turning it into a Linux machine will ensure several more years of working life. Shame to put good hardware out to pasture early, after all.

## Is Linux Really Ready for the Desktop?

Despite the advances Linux has made in recent years, this question still pops up quite often, and that's fact enough. But consider this: When you install a program on your present Windows system and get an error message saying that the program can't run because some DLL file missing, or when you connect a piece of hardware and can't get it to run, no one asks if that operating system is ready for the desktop.
