4. What happens when you log in incorrectly by entering either your *userid* or your *password* incorrectly? Try it.

+   * a. Type in your *userid* incorrectly by changing the case on one or more of the letters. For example, if your *userid* is **david**, change the first letter to a capital “D,” so that you type **David**.
+   * b. Press `<Enter>`
+   * c. Type in your *password* correctly.
+   * d. Press `<Enter>`

Your screen will look like Figure 3-1.

```
Login: David

Password:

Login incorrect.

login:
```

![](bbox=[0.18, 0.45, 0.88, 0.48])

Why is there no shell prompt in Figure 3-1? (Find the answer following Figure 3-2.)

Repeat your login incorrectly three more times. At least once, use the correct *userid* with an incorrect *password*. On some systems, after a specified number of consecutive incorrect logins, the system prompt will change (you will no longer see `login:` by itself), and the required login procedure from that terminal may change. For example, if you are logging in over a modem or via `telnet`, your connection to the system may be terminated. In this case, you will have to reestablish contact with the system. (It may be prudent to find out, before you try multiple incorrect logins, how your system will react in this situation.)
