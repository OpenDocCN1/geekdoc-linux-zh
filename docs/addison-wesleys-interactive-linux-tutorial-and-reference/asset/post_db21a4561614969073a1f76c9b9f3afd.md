
## vi Cursor Movement

### Introduction

With a contemporary word processor, there are many ways to move the cursor around your document. You aren't limited to using the arrow keys to move character by character or line by line.

In this module you will learn that **vi** also has many ways to move the cursor around and through your text. In fact, **vi** offers some cursor movement features that few, if any, word processors offer.

### In This Module you Will Learn

- how to move the cursor character by character.
- how to move the cursor line by line.
- how to move the cursor from word to word.
- how to move the cursor from sentence to sentence.
- how to move the cursor from paragraph to paragraph.
- how to move the cursor from screen to screen.

### Try It Live!

As we have done in earlier *Try It Live!* sections of this guide, we will omit step-by-step instructions for some of the exercises. At the very end of this chapter of the guide, you will find whatever step-by-step instructions we have omitted in the exercises.

1. Change to your directory catalog.
2. Open *vi* with the file *supply1*.
    - a. Type `vi supply1`
    - b. Press `<Enter>`
    - Your screen should look similar to Figure 11-1.

![](img/cc8b9fd5970716027fba0d0a45c13900_191_0.png)

Remember that you created the "supply" files with blank lines top and bottom. This was to make the text of the files easy to distinguish from system-generated text, such as the shell prompt. When you open *vi*, the cursor will usually be positioned in the first column of the first line, as you see on your screen and in Figure 11-1.

3. Practice moving the cursor character by character.
    - a. Press `<Esc>` to be certain that *vi* is in command mode.
    - b. Press `<j>` to move the cursor down to the first line of text.
    - c. Press `<j>` six times. This will position the cursor on the letter “g.”
    - d. Press `<0>` to move the cursor back to the beginning of the line.
    - e. Press `<6g>` to move the cursor to the letter “g” with just one command, instead of the same command repeated six times.
    - f. Press `<2j>` to move the cursor down two lines, to the line which ends with “pens.” Note that the cursor stays in the same column.
    - g. Press `<$>` to move the cursor to the end of the line. The cursor will be resting on the period.
    - h. Press `<l>` a few times to move the cursor to the right.
        - Note that *vi* beeps. The cursor does not move beyond the period after “pens.” The cursor does not wrap to the next line when you move by character.
    - i. Press `<h>` five times to move the cursor back to the space before “pens.”
    - j. Press `<0>` to move the cursor back to the beginning of the line.
    - k. Press `<k>` to move the cursor up one line. “pencils” is at the end of this line.
    - l. Press `<2k>` to move the cursor to the top of the screen. The cursor will be resting on the blank line before the first line of text.

As we pointed out in the CBT course, when *vi* was created the computer keyboard was not standardized to the extent that it is now. You can expect that newer versions of *vi* will allow you to take advantage of today’s typical cursor-movement keys. Redo the preceding exercise, and substitute the arrow keys for `<h>`, `<j>`, `<k>` and `<l>`. Use `<Home>` for `<0>` and `<End>` for `<$>`. If the arrow keys and `<Home>` and `<End>` don’t work, it could be that your version of *vi* doesn’t recognize them.

4. Quit *vi*.

5. Reopen *vi* with the file **supply**. Your screen should look similar to Figure 11-2.

6. Move the cursor forward by paragraph to the bottom of the file.
    - a. Press `<}>` eight times.

*vi* defines a paragraph as a group of characters that are separated from other characters by blank lines, top and bottom. Note that the cursor skips over each three-line grouping that represents one of the original "supply" files.

7. Move the cursor back by paragraph to the top of the file.
    - a. Press `<{>` eight times.

8. Move the cursor forward one sentence at a time.
    - a. Press `<)>` three times.

Note that the cursor now stops at the beginning of each sentence. Even though the first letter of each of these lines is not capitalized, *vi* recognizes the end of a sentence by the presence of the period and either two following spaces or—as is the case here—the carriage return and line feed (which you created when you pressed `<Enter>`).

9. Move the cursor back one sentence at a time.
    - a. Press `<(<` twice. The cursor will be positioned at the beginning of the first line of text.

![](img/cc8b9fd5970716027fba0d0a45c13900_194_0.png)

> Note that the direction of the curve reminds you which way the cursor will move. The cursor moves to the left—back—with both `(` and `{`. The cursor moves to the right—forward—with both `)` and `}`.
>
> Explore. Do the following keys and keystroke combinations move the cursor in your version of *vi*? `<Page Up>`, `<Page Down>`, `<Ctrl-Home>`, `<Ctrl-End>`

10. Exit *vi*.

None of the “supply” files will provide adequate practice for moving the cursor by words, the object of the next group of exercises. But you can create this file using *cat* and redirection.

11. Use *cat* and redirection to create the file *word_by_word*.
    - a. Type `cat > word_by_word`
    - b. Press `<Enter>`
    - c. Type `Move (the) cursor; word-by-word not word_by_word.`
    - d. Press `<Enter>` two times.
    - e. Press `<Ctrl-d>`

You may recognize this as text you used for practice in the *Linux Made Easy* CBT.

12. Open **vi** with the file **word_by_word**. Your screen should look similar to Figure 11-3.

```
Move (the) cursor; word-by-word not word_by_word.


```

13. Move forward by small words.
    - a. Press `<w>` slowly until you reach the end of the line. Count the number of times you press `<w>`.
    - You pressed `<w>` thirteen times: once for each of the small words after the first word, "Move." Note that **vi** treats each punctuation mark as a separate small word. Note also that, while the dashes separating "word-by-word" are created as separate small words, the underscores linking the grouping "word_by_word" are not treated as separate small words.

14. Use one keystroke to move the cursor back to the beginning of the line.

15. Move forward by big words.
    - a. Press `<W>` slowly until you reach the first letter of the grouping "word_by_word." Count the number of times you press `<W>`, and note the new cursor position each time you press `<W>`.
    - You pressed `<W>` five times: once for each of the big words after the first word, "Move." Note that **vi** treats punctuation marks that are physically attached to other characters as part of a big word.
    - b. Press `<W>` once.
    - This last `<W>` moves the cursor past the period at the end of "word_by_word" and wraps to the next line. **vi** considers punctuation marks attached to text to be part of a big word.

16. Move back by small words.
    - a. Press `<b>` slowly until the cursor is positioned on the first character of the first word, and note the new cursor position each time you press `<b>`.

17. Use one keystroke to move the cursor to the end of the line.

18. Move back by big words.
    - a. Press `<B>` slowly until the cursor is positioned on the first character of the first word, and note the new cursor position each time you press `<B>`.

19. Move forward to the end of a small word.
    - a. Press `<e>` slowly until you reach the period at the end of the line, and note the new cursor position each time you press `<e>`.

20. Use one keystroke to move the cursor back to the beginning of the line.

21. Move forward to the end of a big word.
    - a. Press `<E>` slowly until you reach the period at the end of the line, and note the new cursor position each time you press `<E>`.
    - b. Press `<E>` once to wrap the cursor to the next (blank) line.

In the next few exercises, you will use numbers to modify the commands that move the cursor by words.

22. Move the cursor back to the first character of the first line.

23. Move the cursor by multiple words.

    - b. Press `<h>` until the cursor is at the beginning of the line.
    - c. Press `<2W>` to move the cursor two big words to the right. Figure 11-5 shows the cursor position after this move.
