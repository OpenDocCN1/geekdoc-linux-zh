Why doesn't **<3b>** reverse **<3e>** in steps h and i in Exercise 23? The "back-a-word" **<b, B>** and "end-of-word" **<e, E>** commands consider the word they are "in" to be the first word included in the movement. Step h in Exercise 23 positions the cursor on the "i" of "cursor". The command **<b>** moves the cursor back to the beginning of the word the cursor is currently in. So the first **<b>** positions the cursor on the letter "c"; the second **<b>** positions the cursor on the ")"; the third **<b>** positions the cursor on the "t" of "the"; and the fourth **<b>** moves the cursor back to its starting point before step h: the "c".

Your screen display may look different from what you see in Figure 11-12. The simplest explanation for this is that there are many possible terminal configurations. If your **vi** terminal display is twenty-five lines, half a screen is twelve or thirteen lines. If your **vi** terminal display is fifty lines, half a screen is twenty-five lines. **<Ctrl-d>** scrolls down half a screen. This means that you can expect the screen display to change, as it probably did for you in Exercise 27. But if the file you are working on is small enough to be displayed on one screen, **<Ctrl-d>** won't change the screen display but will instead move the cursor to the approximate middle of the screen.
