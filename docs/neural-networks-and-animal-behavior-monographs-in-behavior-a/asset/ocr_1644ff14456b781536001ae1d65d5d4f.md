Note the separation between state variables, which describe which stimuli are present, and the model structure, which describes how to obtain the response from the state, i.e., through a weighted sum. Models with this structure are the so-called law of stimulus summation (Leong 1969; Lorenz 1981; Seitz 1940–1941, 1943; Tinbergen 1951) and psychological models such as Rescorla and Wagner (1972) and Brough (1975). Note that some of the $x_{i}$’s could be made to represent internal factors. In the latter case, external and internal factors would combine additively to produce the response. Of course, there are other ways in which factors can be combined (Figure 1.5; Krantz & Tversky 1971; McFarland & Houston 1981), and individual $x_{i}$’s may not just take values of 0 and 1.

Another important theoretical concept is that of thresholds. Consider first the issue of whether to perform a given behavior or not based on the value of a single motivational variable, e.g., whether to eat or not for a given hunger level. A common idea is that the behavior is performed if the motivational variable exceeds a threshold value $T$.
