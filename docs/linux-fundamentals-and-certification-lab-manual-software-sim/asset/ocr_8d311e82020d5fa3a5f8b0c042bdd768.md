Most distributions are available for free download and for retail purchase. Most distributions offer online download of installation and user manuals, FAQs, and other resources. These materials are available whether you purchase the product or opt for the free download. Additional resources are available, however, if you purchase the distribution. Additional services include:

+   *   **Phone support** Most distributions offer phone support. This is usually an additional service for negligible cost.

+   *   **Printed manuals** Purchasing the retail box will usually provide the additional printed documentation.

+   *   **Value-added software** Some distributions come with other products such as WordPerfect, StarOffice, OpenOffice.org used for word processing, spreadsheets, and more; and BRU Backup, a utility used to back up a computer system. Some software can be downloaded for free; some you must purchase. If it’s included on the distribution disk, it is much more convenient.

# Red Hat Linux

![](bbox=[0.1, 0.36, 0.26, 0.41])

Red Hat is probably one of the most popular Linux distributions. Red Hat, created by Marc Ewing, was released in October of 1994. It was known as the Halloween release. It started as a company in 1995 when Bob Young, Red Hat Corporation founder, purchased the Red Hat distribution from Marc Ewing.

Red Hat achieved revenue of $27.2 million as of the first quarter ending May 31, 2003. One of Red Hat’s biggest contributions was the Red Hat Package Manager (RPM). RPM is a command-line-driven package management system, capable of installing, uninstalling, verifying, querying, and updating computer software packages. Red Hat also offers training and certification programs.

Red Hat products include Red Hat Enterprise Linux, Red Hat Linux 9, Red Hat Database, Cygwin, and other personal and enterprise applications. The current personal version of Red Hat Linux is 9.0.

You can download Red Hat Linux 9 from [http://www.redhat.com/apps/download/](http://www.redhat.com/apps/download/).

A recent change has occurred with Red Hat Linux: The freely distributed product has separated from the commerical products and is now called Fedora. As of the printing of this book, the Fedora Linux Distribution will be maintained by the Red Hat developers. You can download Fedora Core from [http://fedora.redhat.com/download/](http://fedora.redhat.com/download/).

# SuSE Linux

![](bbox=[0.14, 0.68, 0.26, 0.74])

SuSE is one of the oldest distributions of Linux, almost as old as GNU/Linux. In business for more than ten years, SuSE has seen growth year after year.

The current version of SuSE Linux is 8.0. It contains seven CD-ROMs with more than 1,500 applications. SuSE comes with the YAST (Yet Another Setup Tool) program, an installation and confgiration utility, making it easy to maintain.

The latest version usually isn’t available for download, but a “live evaluation” version that runs completely off a CD is available. You can also install from an FTP server.

The SuSE download site is [http://www.suse.com/us/private/download/suse\_linux/index.html](http://www.suse.com/us/private/download/suse\_linux/index.html).

SuSE was recently purchased by the Novell corporation. Novell provides network servers, a network operating system, and other products.
