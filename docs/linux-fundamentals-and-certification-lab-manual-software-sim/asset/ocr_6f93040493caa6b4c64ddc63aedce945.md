# CONFIGURING SYSTEM QUOTAS

There never seems to be enough disk space. Even with the availability of inexpensive high-capacity disk drives, the demand for more disk space always seems to grow. On a home-use Linux system the hard disk is often dual-installed with the Windows operating system, and this arrangement uses twice as much disk space as Linux alone. On a small business system, the operating system, applications, and data often take large amounts of space and hard drives are quickly used. On an enterprise-wide system, sometimes hundreds of users are given disk space to use and, if usage is left unchecked, this disk space will quickly fill up.

For example, the home space of users on the systems managed at a local university was recently audited and found to contain an average of 1.7 GB of information. Some users’ home space contained over 20 GB. The amount of free disk space available was quickly becoming nonexistent.

Ideally, on a multisuer, multitasking system, restricting the amount of disk space allowed to each user will bring disk usage under control. Restricting disk space usage enables an administrator to more easily track the amount of disk space used and to plan for future hardware upgrades.

Chapter 7 discussed how to determine who is currently logged into the system and who has logged in before using the **who**, **last**, and other commands. Usually, when a user logs in to the system, he or she is given disk space to use. With only a few users the amount of disk space used may be minimal, but on systems with many users the amount of disk space needed may be huge and therefore need to be managed.

*Disk* space is managed on Linux systems using *quotas*. *Quotas* enable an administrator to limit the amount of space made available to each user or group of users that log on to the system. Usage of disk space can be strictly enforced, or, in most cases, excessive disk usage is just reported to the root user and the user is not prevented from using the space.
