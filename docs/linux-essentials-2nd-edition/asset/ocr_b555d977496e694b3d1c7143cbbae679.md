Specific projects within GNOME Office are AbiWord (word processor), Evince (document viewer), Evolution (groupware and email client), Numeric (spreadsheet), Inkscape (vector graphics and presentation creation), and Ease (presentation). You can learn more at: http://live.gnome.org/GnomeOffice.

Calligraphy This office suite was born out of a split from an earlier KDE office suite, called KOffice. While K is no longer maintained, Calligraphy (https://calligraphy.org/) is thriving. Its office suite includes Words (word processor), Stage (presentation), Sheets (spreadsheet), Flow (charting), and Kex (database). Besides office applications, Calligraphy offers Graphics and Project Management products.

Apache OpenOffice Until 2011, this office suite, located at www .openoffice.org, was the most prominent for Linux and simply called OpenOffice.org. Its corporate sponsor, Oracle, supporting commercial development of the project and donated it to the Apache group. The official name is currently ApacheOffice. It provides six applications: Writer (word processor), Calc (spreadsheet), Impress (presentation), Base (database), Draw (vector graphics), and Math (equation editor).

LibreOffice This office suite was created as a fork of the older pre-Apache OpenOffice.org. It's becoming the most popular office suite in Linux. It provides six applications: Writer (word processor), Calc (spreadsheet), Impress (presentation), Base (database), Draw (vector graphics), and Math (equation editor). You may have noticed that these applications have the exact same name as the ApacheOffice applications. You can read more about it at www.libreoffice.org.

Most of these programs support the OpenDocument Format (ODF), which is an open set of file formats that's slowly making as a standard for word processing, spreadsheet, and other office files. Although ODF is intended to easily transfer files between different applications, application-specific assumptions often hinder transfers, especially on complex documents.

Many other programs exist in this space, although they are not part of an office suite. Some are unusual. For instance, LyX (www.lyx.org) can take the place of a word processor, but it's rather unique: it's built as a way to create and edit LaTeX documents. LaTeX is a document format that's popular in computer science, mathematics, and other technical fields.
