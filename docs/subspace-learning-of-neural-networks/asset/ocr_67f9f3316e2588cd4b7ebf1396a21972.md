for all $k \ge 0$. By Lemma 3.7, there exists constants $\theta_2 > 0$ and $\Pi_5 > 0$ such that

$$|\sigma-w^T(k+1)Cw(k+1)| \le k\cdot\Pi_5\cdot\left[e^{-\theta_2(k+1)}+\max\{e^{-\theta_2k},e^{-\theta_1k}\}\right]$$

for all $k \ge 0$.

Obviously, there exists constants $\theta > 0$ and $\Pi > 0$ such that

$$\frac{\sigma-w^T(k)Cw(k)}{w^T(k)Cw(k)}\left(\sigma-w^T(k)Cw(k)\right)z_i(k) \le k\cdot\Pi e^{-\theta k},\quad (i=1,\ldots,m)$$

for $k \ge 0$.

Using Lemma 3.3 and Lemma 3.8, it follows that

$$\left\{\begin{aligned}\lim_{t\to+\infty}z_i(k)&=z_i^*,\quad(i=1,\ldots,m)\\\lim_{t\to+\infty}z_i(k)&=0,\quad(i=m+1,\ldots,n).\end{aligned}\right.$$

Then,

$$\lim_{t\to+\infty}w(k)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}z_i^*v_i\in V_\sigma.$$ (3.12)

From (3.5), after the system becomes stable, it follows that

$$\lim_{k\to\infty}Cw(k)=\lim_{k\to\infty}w^T(k)Cw(k)w(k).$$ (3.13)

Substitute (3.12) into (3.13), we get

$$\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sigma z_i^{*}v_i=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sigma(z_i^{*})^2\sum_{i=1}^{m}z_i^{*}v_i.$$

It is easy to see that

$$\sum_{i=1}^{m}(z_i^{*})^2=1.$$

The proof is complete.

The above theorem shows almost all trajectories starting from $S(H)$ will converge to an eigenvector associated with the largest eigenvalue of the covariance matrix of $C.w(0)$ could be chosen arbitrarily except for the subspace $V_\sigma^\perp$. Clearly, the condition is easy to meet in practical applications.
