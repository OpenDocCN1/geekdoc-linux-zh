for $k \ge 0$. From (3.5), (1.8), and (3.6), it follows that

$$\begin{aligned}
w^T(k+1)Cw(k+1) &= \sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_i z_i^2(k+1)\\
&= \sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_i z_i^2(k+1)\\
&= \sum_{i=1}^{p}\left[1+\xi\left(\frac{\lambda_i}{w^T(k)Cw(k)}-1\right)\right]^2\lambda_i z_i^2(k)\\
&\ge \left[1+\xi\left(\frac{\lambda_p}{w^T(k)Cw(k)}-1\right)\right]^2 w^T(k)Cw(k)\\
&\ge \min_{s>0}\left\{\left[1+\xi\left(\frac{\lambda_p}{s}-1\right)\right]^2\cdot s\right\}.
\end{aligned}$$

for $k \ge 0$. By Lemma 3.1, it holds that

$$w^T(k+1)Cw(k+1)\ge 4(1-\xi)\lambda_p,$$

for $k \ge 0$. This completes the proof. The above theorem shows that, if $w(0)\ne 0$, the trajectory starting from $w(0)$ is lower bounded, then it will never converge to zero. Clearly, the zero vector must be an unstable equilibrium of (3.5).

# **Theorem 3.2**  *There exists a constant $H>0$ such that $w^T(k)Cw(k)\le H$ for all $k\ge 0$.*

*Proof:* If $w^T(0)Cw(0)=0$, then $w^T(k)Cw(k)=0$ for all $k\ge 0$. Clearly, $w^T(k)Cw(k)\le H$. Next, assume that $w^T(0)Cw(0)\ne 0$. Define

$$\bar H=\max\left\{\sigma,\left[1+\xi\left(\frac{\sigma}{\lambda_p}-1\right)\right]^2\lambda_p,\left[1+\xi\left(\frac{\sigma}{w^T(0)Cw(0)}-1\right)\right]^2w^T(0)Cw(0)\right\}.$$

Given any positive constant $H$ such that $H\ge \bar H$, we will prove that for any $k\ge 0$

$$0\le w^T(k)Cw(k)\le H$$

implies that

$$0\le w^T(k+1)Cw(k+1)\le H.$$

Two steps will be used to complete the proof.

**Case 1.** $\sigma\le w^T(k)Cw(k)\le H$. Then,

$$\left[1+\xi\left(\frac{\lambda_i}{w^T(k)Cw(k)}-1\right)\right]^2\le 1,\quad (i=1,\ldots,p).$$
