If $w^T(k)Cw(k)\leq \lambda_p$, then,

$$\begin{aligned}
w^T(k+1)Cw(k+1)
&=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left[1+\eta\left(\lambda_i-w^T(k)Cw(k)\right)\right]^2\lambda_i z_i^2(k)\\
&\geq \left[1+\eta\left(\lambda_p-w^T(k)Cw(k)\right)\right]^2w^T(k)Cw(k)\\
&\geq w^T(k)Cw(k)
\end{aligned}\tag{2.7}$$

for $k\geq 0$.

It follows from (2.6) and (2.7) that

$$\begin{aligned}
\eta w^T(k+1)Cw(k+1)
&\geq \min\left\{(\eta\lambda_p)^3,\eta w^T(k)Cw(k)\right\}\\
&\geq \min\left\{(\eta\lambda_p)^3,\eta w^T(0)Cw(0)\right\}
\end{aligned}$$

for all $k\geq 0$. This completes the proof.

The above lemma shows that if $w(0)\neq 0$, the trajectory starting from $w(0)$ is lower bounded, then it will never converge to zero. Clearly, the zero vector must be an unstable equilibrium of (2.2).
