
where $1 \leq l \leq p$.

**Proof:** By Lemmas 4.7, 4.8, and 4.9, it follows that

$$
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}z_i^{(l)}(k)=0, & (i=1,\ldots,l-1),\\
\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}z_l^{(l)}(k)=(z_l^{(l)})^*, & \\
\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}z_i^{(l)}(k)=0, & (i=l+1,\ldots,n).
\end{cases}
$$

Then,

$$\lim_{k\to+\infty}w_l(k)=(z_l^{(l)})^*v_l. \tag{4.15}$$

From (4.4), after the system becomes stable, it follows that

$$\lim_{k\to+\infty}Cw_l(k)=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}v_i(k)v_i^T(k)Cw_l(k)+w_l^T(k)Cw_l(k)w_l(k)\right]. \tag{4.16}$$

Substitute (4.15) into (4.16), we get

$$\lambda_l(z_l^{(l)})^*v_l=\lambda_l\big((z_l^{(l)})^*\big)^2(z_l^{(l)})^*v_l.$$

It is easy to see that

$$\left((z_l^{(l)})^*\right)^2=1.$$

The proof is completed.

In the previous theorems, the condition $w_l(0)\notin V_l^\perp$ is required. Since any small disturbance can result in $w_l(0)\notin V_l^\perp$, it is easy to meet the condition in practical applications.

In our assumptions, the assumption $w_l(k)\notin V_0$ for all $k\geq 0$ is also required. Since extracted principal directions are associated with the nonzero eigenvalues and the evolution trajectories are always close to the principal directions, the condition is satisfied usually. The following lemma will further show this point.

### Lemma 4.10

Suppose that $\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}\phi_i^{(l)}(k)=0$, $(i=1,\ldots,p)$. Then, there exists a constant $\hat K_l$ so that

$$0<\sqrt{\frac{4(1-\xi)\xi\lambda_p}{\lambda_1}}\leq\lVert w_l(k+1)\rVert\leq\frac{M_l}{\lambda_p}\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_1}{4(1-\xi)\xi\lambda_p}},$$

for all $k\geq\max\{K_l,\hat K_l\}$, where $0<\xi<1$.

**Proof:** It can be checked that

$$\left[1-\xi+\frac{\xi\lambda_i}{w_l^T(k)Cw_l(k)}\right]^2\geq\left[1-\xi+\frac{\xi\lambda_p}{w_l^T(k)Cw_l(k)}\right]^2>0,$$

for $k>0$. From (4.6), (4.12), and (4.13), the following $\Gamma$ is obtained,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma &= w_l^T(k+1)Cw_l(k+1) \\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_i\left(z_i^{(l)}(k+1)\right)^2 \\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left[(1-\xi)z_i^{(l)}(k)+\phi_i^{(l)}(k)\right]^2\lambda_i+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left[1-\xi+\frac{\xi\lambda_i}{w_l^T(k)Cw_l(k)}\right]\left[z_i^{(l)}(k)+\phi_i^{(l)}(k)\right]^2\lambda_i \\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left\{\left[1-\xi+\frac{\xi\lambda_i}{w_l^T(k)Cw_l(k)}\right]\left[z_i^{(l)}(k)+\phi_i^{(l)}(k)\right]\right\}^2\lambda_i \\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left\{\left[1-\xi+\frac{\xi\lambda_i}{\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{p}\lambda_j\left(z_j^{(l)}(k)\right)^2}\right]\left[z_i^{(l)}(k)+\phi_i^{(l)}(k)\right]\right\}^2\lambda_i \\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left\{\left[1-\xi+\frac{\xi\lambda_i}{\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{p}\lambda_j\left(z_j^{(l)}(k)\right)^2}\right]\left(z_i^{(l)}(k)+\phi_i^{(l)}(k)\right)\right. \\
&\qquad\left.-\frac{\xi\lambda_i z_i^{(l)}(k)\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{p}\lambda_j\left(z_j^{(l)}(k)\right)^2}{w_l^T(k)Cw_l(k)\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{p}\lambda_j\left(z_j^{(l)}(k)\right)^2}\right\}\lambda_i \\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left[1-\xi+\frac{\xi\lambda_i}{\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{p}\lambda_j\left(z_j^{(l)}(k)\right)^2}\right]\left(\left(z_i^{(l)}(k)\right)^2\lambda_i+\Psi_i^{(l)}(k)\right),
\end{aligned}
$$

where

$$\Psi_i^{(l)}(k)=2\left[1-\xi+\frac{\xi\lambda_i}{\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{p}\lambda_j\left(z_j^{(l)}(k)\right)^2}\right]z_i^{(l)}(k)\phi_i^{(l)}(k)\lambda_i+\left(\phi_i^{(l)}(k)\right)^2\lambda_i.$$

Meanwhile,

$$\Phi_i^{(l)}(k)=\phi_i^{(l)}(k)-\frac{\xi\lambda_i z_i^{(l)}(k)\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{l-1}\lambda_j\left(z_j^{(l)}(k)\right)^2}{w_l^T(k)Cw_l(k)\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{p}\lambda_j\left(z_j^{(l)}(k)\right)^2}.$$

Since $\lim_{k\to\infty}\phi_i^{(l)}(k)=0$, $(i=1,\ldots,p)$, by Lemmas 4.6 and 4.7, we have $\lim_{k\to\infty}\Phi_i^{(l)}(k)=0$. So, it follows that $\lim_{k\to\infty}\Psi_i^{(l)}(k)=0$. Thus, there must exist a constant $\hat K_l$ so that

$$
\begin{aligned}
w_l^T(k+1)Cw_l(k+1) &\geq \sum_{i=1}^{p}\left[1-\xi+\frac{\xi\lambda_i}{\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{p}\lambda_j\left(z_j^{(l)}(k)\right)^2}\right]^2\left(z_i^{(l)}(k)\right)^2\lambda_i \\
&\geq \left[1-\xi+\frac{\xi\lambda_p}{\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{p}\lambda_j\left(z_j^{(l)}(k)\right)^2}\right]^2\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left(z_i^{(l)}(k)\right)^2\lambda_i \\
&\geq \min_{s\geq 0}\left[1-\xi+\frac{\xi\lambda_p}{s}\right]^2s,
\end{aligned}
$$

for all $k>\hat K_l$. By Lemma 4.1, it holds that

$$w_l^T(k+1)Cw_l(k+1)\geq 4(1-\xi)\xi\lambda_p,$$

for $k\geq\hat K_l$. Since $\lambda_1\lVert w_l(k+1)\rVert^2\geq w_l^T(k+1)Cw_l(k+1)$, it follows that

$$\lVert w_l(k+1)\rVert\geq\sqrt{\frac{4(1-\xi)\xi\lambda_p}{\lambda_1}}>0,$$

for $k\geq\hat K_l$, where $0<\xi<1$.

Then, from Lemma 4.6, it is easy to see that

$$\lVert w_l(k+1)\rVert\leq (1-\xi)^k\lVert w_l(0)\rVert+\xi\frac{M_l}{\lambda_p}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{(1-\xi)^{i-1}}{\lVert w_l(k-i)\rVert}.$$

for all $k \ge K_l$, where $\lambda_p < r(k) < \lambda_1$. Since $0 < \xi < 1$, it holds that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|w_l(k+1)\| &\leq (1-\xi)^k\|w_l(0)\|+\xi\frac{M_l}{\sqrt{4}}\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_1}{(1-\xi)\xi\lambda_p}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}(1-\xi)^{i-1}\\
&\leq \frac{M_l}{\lambda_p}\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_1}{4(1-\xi)\xi\lambda_p}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

for $k \ge \max\{K_l, \hat K_l\}$. This completes the proof.

This lemma gives a smaller ultimate bound when $k$ is sufficiently increased. Obviously, in this case, the condition $w_l(k) \notin V_0$ is met.

### 4.4.2 Example 2

We will further illustrate the convergence of (4.4) in a high-dimensional space. Consider the following covariance matrix $C$ generated randomly.

$$C=\begin{bmatrix}0.0267&-0.0030&-0.0139&0.0001&0.0317&0.0106\\-0.0030&0.0240&0.0016&-0.0000&-0.0036&-0.0012\\-0.0139&0.0016&0.0072&-0.0001&-0.0165&-0.0055\\0.0001&-0.0000&-0.0001&0.0020&0.0002&0.0001\\0.0317&-0.0036&-0.0165&0.0002&0.0377&0.0126\\0.0106&-0.0012&-0.0055&0.0001&0.0126&0.0243\end{bmatrix}.$$

It is easy to get the eigenvalues of matrix $C$. They are

$0.0778, 0.0236, 0.0186, 0.0020, 0.0000, 0.0000.$

In this simulation, we extract four principal directions associated with eigenvalues 0.0778, 0.0236, 0.0186, 0.0020, respectively. Figure 4.3 shows the norm evolution of four principal directions with $\xi=0.3$ or $\xi=0.7$. Clearly, four principal directions converges quickly to the unit hyper-sphere. Figure 4.4 shows the learning rate evolution of four principal directions with $\xi=0.3$ or $\xi=0.7$. From the results, it is shown that learning rates converge to constants. The learning rates make the convergence speed in all eigendirections approximately equal [131] so that the evolution is speeded up considerably with time.

To measure the accuracy of the convergent directions, the direction cosines will be simultaneously computed at the $k$th update by [32, 31]:

$$\mathrm{DirectionCosine}_l(k)=\frac{|\mathbf{w}_l^T(k)\cdot\boldsymbol{\mu}|}{\|\mathbf{w}_l(k)\|\cdot\|\boldsymbol{\mu}\|},$$

where $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ is the true eigenvector associated with the $l$th eigenvalue of $C$, which can be computed in advance by MATLAB 7.0. If $\mathrm{DirectionCosine}_l(k)$ converges to 1, the principal directions converges to the right direction. Figure 4.5 gives the result.

In above simulations, the initial vectors may be arbitrarily chosen apart from the origin. Simulations show the GHA with this non-zero-approaching learning rates is global convergent, and the convergence speed is quite fast.
