
## Subspace Learning of Neural Networks

More than just a theoretical toy, the learning algorithms of neural networks play a vital role in subspace learning with significant practical implications across many fields. Ultimately, it is the mathematical convergence of these algorithms that determines whether applications based upon them are successful or not.

*Subspace Learning of Neural Networks* keeps its primary focus on the convergence analysis of these subspace learning algorithms. It looks at ways to extend the use of these networks to a number of fields, including biomedicine, encryption, and signal processing.

Coordinating much of their own published findings, the authors mainly rely on the discrete deterministic time (DDT) method. To overcome limits inherent with this approach and enhance the quality of the convergence, they establish a relationship between an SDT algorithm and its corresponding DDT algorithm through the use of block algorithms. And to be certain that variables are accounted for, they offer up studies of the chaotic and robust properties of some algorithms, noting, as they point out, that chaotic and stable are not necessarily exclusive of one another.

*Unique in its approach and the depth of its findings, this volume*

- Provides a framework for convergence analysis of subspace learning algorithms of neural networks that can be generalized to the study of other learning algorithms
- Builds a theoretical understanding of the convergence behavior of some subspace learning algorithms through the analysis framework
- Applies real-life examples to illustrate the practical performance of learning algorithms

In addition to providing an invaluable resource for those investigating subspace learning algorithms, the authors demonstrate the importance of these findings to data mining, image processing, and signal processing across a number of fields and offer a good example of how improvements in one theoretical area of neural networks can be used to benefit any number of application communities.
