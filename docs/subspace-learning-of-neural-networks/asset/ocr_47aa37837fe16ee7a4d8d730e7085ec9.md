Proof: By Lemma 2.9, there exists constants $\theta_1>0$ and $\Pi_1\geq 0$ such that

$$\sum_{j=m+1}^{n} z_j^2(k)\leq \Pi_1e^{-\theta_1k},$$

for all $k\geq N$. By Lemma 2.10, there exists constants $\theta_2>0$ and $\Pi_2>0$ such that

$$\left|\sigma(2-\|w(k)\|^2)-w^T(k+1)Cw(k+1)\right|\leq k\cdot\Pi_2\left[e^{-\theta_2(k+1)}+\max\left\{e^{-\theta_2k},e^{-\theta_1k}\right\}\right]$$

for all $k\geq N$.

Obviously, there exists constants $\theta>0$ and $\Pi>0$ such that

$$\eta\left|\left(\sigma(2-\|w(k)\|^2)-w^T(k)Cw(k)\right)z_i(k)\right|\leq k\cdot\Pi e^{-\theta k},\quad (i=1,\ldots,m)$$

for $k\geq N$.

Using Lemma 2.9 and Lemma 2.11, it follows that

$$\left\{\begin{aligned}\lim_{t\to+\infty}z_i(k)&=z_i^*, &&(i=1,\ldots,m),\\\lim_{t\to+\infty}z_i(k)&=0, &&(i=m+1,\ldots,n).\end{aligned}\right.$$

Thus,

$$\lim_{t\to+\infty}w(k)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}z_i^*v_i\in V_\sigma. \tag{2.17}$$

From (2.12), after the system becomes stable, it follows that

$$\lim_{k\to+\infty}2Cw(k)=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\left[Cw(k)w^T(k)w(k)-\left(w^T(k)Cw(k)\right)w(k)\right]. \tag{2.18}$$

Substitute (2.17) into (2.18), we get

$$\sum_{i=1}^{m}2\sigma_i^*v_i=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sigma_i^*z_i^*v_i\sum_{i=1}^{m}(z_i^*)^2+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sigma_i^*(z_i^*)^2\sum_{i=1}^{m}z_i^*v_i.$$

It is easy to see that

$$\sum_{i=1}^{m}(z_i^*)^2=1.$$

The proof is completed.

The above theorem requires that the initial $w(0)\notin V_\sigma^\perp$ so that there is at least one $z_i(0)\neq 0\ (1\leq i\leq m)$. Since any small disturbance can result in $w(0)\notin V_\sigma^\perp$, then $w(t)\in V_\sigma$ is easy to meet in practical application. Therefore, as long as the learning rate satisfies a simple condition, almost all trajectories starting from $S$ will converge to a unit eigenvector associated with the largest eigenvalue of the covariance matrix of $C$.
