
for all $k>0$.

Since $0<\xi<1$, by Lemma 4.2, it holds that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|w_1(k+1)\| &\le (1-\xi)^k\|w_1(0)\|+d_1\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_1}{4(1-\xi)\xi\lambda_p}}\sum_{r=1}^{k}(1-\xi)^{r-1}\\
&\le \lim_{k\to\infty}\left[(1-\xi)^k\|w_1(0)\|\right]+d_1\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_1}{4(1-\xi)\xi\lambda_p}}\sum_{r=1}^{k}(1-\xi)^{r-1}\\
&=\widetilde H_1,
\end{aligned}
$$

where

$$
\widetilde H_1=\frac{d_1}{\xi}\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_1}{4(1-\xi)\xi\lambda_p}}.
$$

Obviously, $\widetilde H_1$ is a constant. The proof is completed.

From (4.8), it follows that

$$
z_1^{(1)}(k+1)=\left[1-\xi+\frac{\xi\lambda_1}{w_1^T(k)Cw_1(k)}\right]z_1^{(1)}(k), \tag{4.9}
$$

and

$$
z_j^{(1)}(k+1)=\left[1-\xi+\frac{\xi\lambda_j}{w_1^T(k)Cw_1(k)}\right]z_j^{(1)}(k),\quad 2\le j\le n, \tag{4.10}
$$

for $k>0$. Then, given any $H_1>0$, we define

$$
S(H_1)=\{w_1\mid \|w_1\|\le H_1\}.
$$

### Lemma 4.4

Suppose that $w_1(0)\notin V_{\lambda_1}^{\perp}$. Given any $H_1\ge \widetilde H_1$, if $w_1(0)\in S(H_1)$, then there exist constants $\theta_1>0$ and $\Pi_1>0$ such that

$$
\sum_{j=2}^{n}\left(z_j^{(1)}(k)\right)^2\le \Pi_1\cdot e^{-\theta_1 k},
$$

for all $k>0$, where

$$
\theta_1=\ln\left(\frac{\xi\lambda_1+(1-\xi)\lambda_1H_1^2}{\xi\lambda_2+(1-\xi)\lambda_1H_1^2}\right)^2>0.
$$

*Proof.* Since $w_1(0)\notin V_{\lambda_1}^{\perp}$, then $z_1^{(1)}(0)\ne0$. Given any $i$ $(1\le i\le n)$, it holds that

$$
1-\xi+\xi\frac{\lambda_i}{w_1^T(k)Cw_1(k)}\ge (1-\xi)>0.
$$

for $k>0$, where $0<\xi<1$. Then, from (4.9) and (4.10), for each $j$ $(2\leq j\leq n)$, it follows that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left[\frac{z_j^{(1)}(k+1)}{z_1^{(1)}(k+1)}\right]^2
&=\left[\frac{1-\xi+\dfrac{\xi\lambda_j}{w_1^T(k)Cw_1(k)}}{1-\xi+\dfrac{\xi\lambda_1}{w_1^T(k)Cw_1(k)}}\right]^2\left[\frac{z_j^{(1)}(k)}{z_1^{(1)}(k)}\right]^2\\
&=\left[\frac{(1-\xi)w_1^T(k)Cw_1(k)+\xi\lambda_j}{(1-\xi)w_1^T(k)Cw_1(k)+\xi\lambda_1}\right]^2\left[\frac{z_j^{(1)}(k)}{z_1^{(1)}(k)}\right]^2\\
&\leq\left[\frac{\xi\lambda_j+(1-\xi)\lambda_1H_1^2}{\xi\lambda_1+(1-\xi)\lambda_1H_1^2}\right]^2\left[\frac{z_j^{(1)}(k)}{z_1^{(1)}(k)}\right]^2\\
&\leq\left[\frac{\xi\lambda_2+(1-\xi)\lambda_1H_1^2}{\xi\lambda_1+(1-\xi)\lambda_1H_1^2}\right]^2\left[\frac{z_j^{(1)}(k)}{z_1^{(1)}(k)}\right]^2\\
&=\left[\frac{z_j^{(1)}(0)}{z_1^{(1)}(0)}\right]^2\cdot e^{-\theta_1(k+1)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

for all $k>0$.

Since $w_1(k)\in S(H_1)$, by Lemma 4.3, $z_j^{(1)}(k)$ must be bounded; that is, there exists a constant $b_1>0$ such that $(z_1^{(1)}(k))^2\leq b_1$ for all $k\geq 0$. Then,

$$
\sum_{j=2}^{n}(z_j^{(1)}(k))^2=\sum_{j=2}^{n}\left[\frac{z_j^{(1)}(k)}{z_1^{(1)}(k)}\right]^2\cdot(z_1^{(1)}(k))^2\leq\Pi_1e^{-\theta_1k},
$$

for $k>0$, where

$$
\Pi_1=b_1\sum_{j=2}^{n}\left[\frac{z_j^{(1)}(0)}{z_1^{(1)}(0)}\right]^2\geq0.
$$

This completes the proof.

### Lemma 4.5

Suppose that $w_1(0)\notin V_{\lambda_1}^{\perp}$. Given any $H_1\geq\widetilde{H}_1$, if $w_1(0)\in S(H_1)$, then it holds that

$$
\lim_{k\to+\infty}z_1^{(1)}(k)=(z_1^{(1)})^*,
$$

where $(z_1^{(1)})^*$ is a constant.

**Proof:** Since $w_1(0)\notin V_{\lambda_1}^{\perp}$, then $z_1^{(1)}(0)\ne 0$. From (4.9), it follows that

$$
z_1^{(1)}(k+1)=(1-\xi)z_1^{(1)}(k)+\xi\left[\frac{\lambda_1z_1^{(1)}(k)}{w_1^T(k)Cw_1(k)}\right].
$$

For all $k>0$, where $0<\xi<1$. From (4.9), $z_1^{(1)}(k)>0$ for all $k\ge 0$ if $z_1^{(1)}(0)>0$ and $z_1^{(1)}(k)<0$ for all $k\ge 0$ if $z_1^{(1)}(0)<0$. Thus, $\lambda_1z_1^{(1)}(k)>0$. By Lemma 4.2, there must exist a constant $N_1$ so that

$$
0<\frac{\lambda_1z_1^{(1)}(k)}{w_1^T(k)Cw_1(k)}<N_1.
$$

It follows that

$$
\begin{aligned}
z_1^{(1)}(k+1)&\le (1-\xi)z_1^{(1)}(k)+\xi N_1\\
&=(1-\xi)^kz_1^{(1)}(0)+\sum_{r=1}^{k}(1-\xi)^{r-1}\xi N_1\\
&=(1-\xi)^kz_1^{(1)}(0)+(1-(1-\xi)^k)N_1
\end{aligned}
$$

for all $k>0$.

Since $0<\xi<1$, it holds that

$$
\lim_{k\to\infty}\left[(1-\xi)^kz_1^{(1)}(0)+(1-(1-\xi)^k)N_1\right]=N_1.
$$

Thus, there must exist a constant $(z_1^{(1)})^*$ so that

$$
\lim_{k\to\infty}z_1^{(1)}(k+1)=(z_1^{(1)})^*.
$$

Although $z_1^{(1)}(0)>0$, the result can be obtained similarly. The proof is completed.

### Theorem 4.1

Suppose that $w_1(0)\notin V_1^\perp$. If $w_1(0)\in S(H_1)$, then

$$
\lim_{k\to\infty}w_1(k)=\pm v_1.
$$

**Proof:** By Lemma 4.4 and 4.5, it follows that

$$
\left\{\begin{aligned}
\lim_{k\to\infty}z_1^{(1)}(k)&=(z_1^{(1)})^*,\\
\lim_{k\to\infty}z_i^{(1)}(k)&=0,\qquad (i=2,\ldots,n).
\end{aligned}\right.
$$

Thus, we have

$$
\lim_{k\to\infty}w_1(k)=(z_1^{(1)})^*v_1.
$$

So, after the system (4.7) becomes stable, it follows that

$$
\lim_{k\to\infty} Cw_1(k)=\lim_{k\to\infty}w_1^T(k)Cw_1(k).
$$

Thus, it is easy to see that

$$
\left[(z_1^{(1)})^*\right]^2=1.
$$

The proof is completed.

According to the mathematical induction, next, we will prove the $w_l(k)$ converges to $\pm v_l$ under the assumptions $\lim_{k\to\infty}w_i(k)=\pm v_i$, $(i=1,\ldots,l-1)$.

### 4.3.3 Case 2: $1<l\le p$

Assume that there exist some constants $H_i$ $(i=1,\ldots,l-1)$ so that

- A1. $0<\lvert w_i(k)\rvert\le H_i$, $(i=1,\ldots,l-1)$
- A2. $\lim_{k\to\infty}w_i(k)=\pm v_i$, $(i=1,\ldots,l-1)$
- A3. $w_i(k)\notin V_0$, for all $k>0$.

We will prove $\lim_{k\to\infty}w_l(k)=\pm v_l$. According to the assumption A2, it follows that

$$
w_i(k)=v_i+\varphi_i(k),\qquad (i=1,\ldots,l-1),
$$

for all $k>0$, where $\lim_{k\to\infty}\lvert\varphi_i(k)\rvert=0$. From (4.4), we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
w_l(k+1)={}&w_l(k)+\frac{\xi}{w_l^T(k)Cw_l(k)}\bigg[Cw_l(k)-\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}(v_i+\varphi_i(k))(v_i+\varphi_i(k))^TCw_l(k)\\
&\qquad -w_l^T(k)Cw_l(k)w_l(k)\bigg],\tag{4.11}\\
={}&w_l(k)+\frac{\xi}{w_l^T(k)Cw_l(k)}\bigg[Cw_l(k)-\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}v_iv_i^TCw_l(k)-w_l^T(k)Cw_l(k)w_l(k)+\phi_l(k)\bigg],
\end{aligned}
$$

for all $k>0$, where

$$
\phi_l(k)=-\xi\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}\left[\varphi_i(k)v_i^T+v_i\varphi_i(k)^T+\varphi_i(k)\varphi_i(k)^T\right]Cw_l(k)}{w_l^T(k)Cw_l(k)}.
$$

Let $\phi_{li}(k)=v_i^T\phi_l(k)$. Substitute (4.5) and (4.6) into (4.11), for all $k>0$, it follows that

$$
z_i^{(l)}(k+1)=(1-\xi)z_i^{(l)}(k)+\phi_{li}(k), \tag{4.12}
$$

for $i=1,\ldots,l-1$, and

$$
z_i^{(l)}(k+1)=\left\{1-\xi+\frac{\xi\lambda_i}{w_l^T(k)Cw_l(k)}\right\}z_i^{(l)}(k)+\phi_{li}(k), \tag{4.13}
$$

for $i=l,\ldots,n$, where $\phi_{li}(k)=v_i^T\phi_l(k)$.

Let

$$
\widetilde{C}_l(k)=C-\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}w_i(k)w_i^T(k)C,\qquad (1\leq l\leq p),
$$

for all $k>0$. According to the assumption A1, there must exist a constant $M_l$ so that $0\leq\|\widetilde{C}_l(k)\|<M_l$ for all $k\geq0$. Denote by $V_0^{(l)}(k)$ the null subspace associated with the eigenvalue $0$ of the matrix $\widetilde{C}_l(k)$ and $\Lambda_{\min}^{(l)}(k)$ the smallest nonzero eigenvalue of $\widetilde{C}_l(k)$. Clearly, $\Lambda_{\min}^{(l)}(k)>0$ for all $k>0$. From (4.4), it follows that

$$
w_l(k+1)=(1-\xi)w_l(k)+\xi\frac{\widetilde{C}_l(k)w_l(k)}{w_l(k)^TCw_l(k)}, \tag{4.14}
$$

for $k>0$ and $0<\xi<1$.

### Lemma 4.6

Suppose that $w_l(k)\notin V_0^{(l)}(k)$ for all $k>0$. $\|w_l(k+1)\|$ is ultimately bounded; that is, constants $K_l>0$ must exist so that

$$
\sqrt{2\xi(1-\xi)\sqrt{\frac{\widetilde{\Lambda}}{\lambda_1}}}<\|w_l(k+1)\|<\widetilde{H}_l,
$$

for all $k\geq K_l$, where $\widetilde{\Lambda}=\min\{\Lambda_{\min}^{(l)}(k),k\geq0\}$ and

$$
\widetilde{H}_l=\dfrac{M_l}{\lambda_p\sqrt{2\xi(1-\xi)\sqrt{\widetilde{\Lambda}/\lambda_1}}}.
$$

*Proof:* From (4.14), it follows that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|w_l(k+1)\|^2={}&w_l(k+1)^Tw_l(k+1)\\
={}&(1-\xi)^2\|w_l(k)\|^2\\
&+2\xi(1-\xi)\frac{w_l(k)^T\widetilde{C}_l(k)w_l(k)}{w_l(k)^TCw_l(k)}\\
&+\xi^2\frac{w_l(k)^T\widetilde{C}_l(k)^T\widetilde{C}_l(k)w_l(k)}{[w_l(k)^TCw_l(k)]^2},
\end{aligned}
$$

for $k>0$. Since $w_l(k)\notin V_0^{(l)}(k)$ for all $k\geq0$ and the assumption A2, there must exist a constant $K_l$ such that

$$
w_l(k)^T\widetilde{C}_l(k)w_l(k)\geq0,
$$

for all $k>K_l$. It follows that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|w_l(k+1)\|^2
&\ge (1-\xi)^2\|w_l(k)\|^2
+\xi^2\frac{w_l^T(k)\tilde C_l(k)\tilde C_l^T(k)w_l(k)}{[w_l^T(k)\tilde C_l(k)w_l(k)]^2}\\
&=(1-\xi)^2\|w_l(k)\|^2+\xi^2\frac{\tilde\Lambda}{\lambda_1\|w_l(k)\|^2}\\
&\ge 2\xi(1-\xi)\sqrt{\frac{\tilde\Lambda}{\lambda_1}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

for $k\ge K_l$, where $\tilde\Lambda=\min\{\Lambda_{\min}^{(l)}(k),\,k>0\}$.

Since $0<\xi<1$, from the assumption A3 and (4.14), it follows that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|w_l(k+1)\|
&\le (1-\xi)\|w_l(k)\|+\xi\frac{M_l}{\lambda_p\|w_l(k)\|}\\
&=(1-\xi)^k\|w_l(0)\|+\xi\frac{M_l}{\lambda_p}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{(1-\xi)^{i-1}}{\|w_l(k-i)\|}\\
&\le (1-\xi)^k\|w_l(0)\|+\xi\tilde H_l\sum_{i=1}^{k}(1-\xi)^{i-1}\\
&\le \tilde H_l.
\end{aligned}
$$

for $k\ge K_l$, where

$$
\tilde H_l=\dfrac{M_l}{\lambda_p\sqrt{2\xi(1-\xi)\sqrt{\frac{\tilde\Lambda}{\lambda_1}}}}.
$$

The proof is completed.

This lemma requires $w_l(k)\neq V_0^{(l)}(k)$ for all $k\ge 0$. This condition can be usually satisfied. It is because $\tilde C_l(k)$ will change with time and $V_0^{(l)}(k)$ is only a varying subspace. However, $l\le p$ so that $\tilde C_l(k)$ may not converge to zero with time.
