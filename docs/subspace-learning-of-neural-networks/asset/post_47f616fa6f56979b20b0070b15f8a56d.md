
where $\psi(k)=\frac{1+\eta a}{\eta a-\eta b\Gamma_w}$. From Lemma 6.1, it is easy to see the minimum point is $\|w(k)\|^2\approx\|w(0)\|^2$ or $\|w(k)\|^2=\frac{1+\eta a}{\eta a}$ in the interval $\left[0,\frac{1+\eta a}{\eta a}\right]$.

Thus, it can be obtained that $\|w(k+1)\|^2\ge M_{w1}>0$, for all $k\ge 0$.

From (6.2), there must exist $\beta(k)$ and a small constant $\delta_{w2}$ so that

$$\|w(k+1)\|^2=\beta(k)\left|1+\eta a-\eta a\|w(k)\|^2+\eta b\Gamma_w\|w(k)\|^2\right|\|w(k)\|^2,$$

for all $k\ge 0$, where $0<\delta_{w2}<\beta(k)\le 1$. If $\|w(k)\|^2\le\frac{1+\eta a}{\eta a+\eta b\Gamma_w}$, it holds that

$$\|w(k+1)\|^2\ge\left[\beta(k)(1+\eta a)-\beta(k)(\eta a-\eta b\Gamma_w)\|w(k)\|^2\right]\|w(k)\|^2,$$

for all $k\ge 0$, where $0<\delta_{w2}<\beta(k)\le 1$. If $\frac{b\Gamma_w}{a}<0.2558$ and $\eta a<1$, it follows that

$$M_{w2}\le\|w(k+1)\|^2<\frac{1+\eta a}{\eta a+\eta b\Gamma_w}.$$

for all $k\ge 0$. The proof is complete.

The previous theorem shows that any trajectory of algorithms (6.1) and (6.2) starting from $w(0)$ in the invariant sets $S_{w1}$ or $S_{w2}$ will remain in $S_{w1}$ or $S_{w2}$. Thus, this guarantees the non-divergence of the algorithms in stochastic environment.

The invariant sets are obtained by directly studying the original SDT algorithms (6.1) and (6.2). $\Gamma_w$ is the square of the Frobenius norm and estimated from the available samples. Clearly, the size of samples does not influence the result of Theorem 6.1. However, Theorem 6.1 does not require prewhitening. Thus, the invariant sets of the original algorithms are also suitable for the data, which is not prewhitened.

Since $\|w(k)\|=\|\tilde Bz(k)\|$, from (6.3) and (6.4), a similar theorem can be obtained as follows. Let $\Gamma_z\triangleq\sup\{\|s(k)s^T(k)\|^2,k=1,2,\ldots\}$, assumed $\Gamma_z$ is finite and $\|\tilde z(0)\|^2\ne 0$. Suppose $\|\tilde z(0)\|^2\le\frac{1+\eta a}{\eta a}$. Define

$$M_{z1}=\min\left\{\delta_{z1}\left[G-\eta(a-b\Gamma_z)\|\tilde z(0)\|^2\right]^2\|\tilde z(0)\|^2,\ \delta_{z1}\frac{(b\Gamma_z+\eta b\Gamma_z^2)^2G}{\eta a^3}\right\}>0,$$

where $G=1+\eta a$, $\delta_{z1}>0$ is a small constant. Suppose $\|\tilde z(0)\|^2\le\frac{1+\eta a}{\eta a+\eta b\Gamma_z}$. Define

$$M_{z2}=\min\left\{\delta_{z2}\left[G-\eta(a-b\Gamma_z)\|\tilde z(0)\|^2\right]^2\|\tilde z(0)\|^2,\ \delta_{z2}\frac{4(\eta b\Gamma_z)^2(1+\eta a)^3}{(\eta a+\eta b\Gamma_z)^3}\right\}>0,$$

where $G=1+\eta a$, $\delta_{z2}>0$ is a small constant. Denote $\mathcal{S}_1$ and $\mathcal{S}_2$ as two invariant sets by

$$
\begin{cases}
\mathcal{S}_1 = \left\{z\mid z\in\mathbb{R}^n,\ M_{z1}\leq \|\tilde z\|^2\leq \dfrac{1+\eta a}{\eta a}\right\},\\
\mathcal{S}_2 = \left\{z\mid z\in\mathbb{R}^n,\ M_{z2}\leq \|\tilde z\|^2\leq \dfrac{1+\eta a}{\eta a+\eta b\Gamma_z}\right\}.
\end{cases}
$$

From the analysis of Theorem 6.1, this immediately allows us to state the following theorem.

### Theorem 6.2

Suppose $\eta > 0$, $a > 0$, and $b > 0$. If $\dfrac{b\Gamma_z}{a} < 0.4074$ and $\eta a < 1$, then $\mathcal{S}_1$ is an invariant set of (6.3). If $\dfrac{b\Gamma_z}{a} < 0.2558$ and $\eta a < 1$, then $\mathcal{S}_2$ is an invariant set of (6.4).

Theorems 6.1 and 6.2 give the nondivergent conditions of the algorithms in stochastic environment. It is clear the norms of the algorithms do not go to infinity under these conditions, even in a stochastic environment. In these invariant sets, the local convergence of the algorithms will be further studied in the following subsection.

## 6.3.2 DDT Algorithms and Local Convergence

The convergence analysis of algorithms (6.1) and (6.2) can be indirectly studied by analyzing the corresponding DDT algorithms in the invariant sets. The DDT algorithms characterize the average evolution of the original algorithms. The convergence of the DDT algorithms can reflect the dynamical behaviors of the original algorithms [195, 210]. Here, we only present the convergence analysis of (6.1). The convergence results of (6.2) can be obtained by using similar method. This chapter also gives the convergence results of (6.2), but the analysis process of its convergence is omitted.

Following Zufiria’s method [210], the corresponding DDT algorithms can be obtained by taking the conditional expectation $E\{w(k+1)\mid w(0), w(i), i < k\}$ on both sides of the algorithm (6.1). Since $E\{w(k)\}=BE\{\tilde z(k)\}$, the convergence analysis of the DDT algorithm of (6.1) can be transformed to studying the convergence of the DDT algorithm of (6.3). Taking the conditional expectation $E\{\tilde z(k+1)\mid \tilde z(0), \tilde z(i), i < k\}$ on both sides of (6.3), the corresponding DDT algorithm is written as

$$
z(k+1)=z(k)+\eta\left\{E\left[s(k)\left(\tilde z(k)s(k)\right)^3\right]+a(1-\|z(k)\|^2)z(k)\right\}, \tag{6.6}
$$

for all $k\geq 0$, where $z(k)=E\{\tilde z(k)\}$. The DDT algorithm (6.6) preserves the discrete time form of the original algorithm (6.3) and allows a more realistic behavior of the learning gain [210]. Since the DDT algorithm (6.6) represents an average evolution of the algorithm (6.3), the invariant set $\mathcal{S}_1$ is also an invariant set of (6.6).

The following provides the convergence of the algorithm (6.6) in detail.

### Lemma 6.4 (Hyvärinen-Oja [85])

For any \(i\in T\), it holds that

\[
E\left\{s_i(k)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tilde z_j(k)s_j(k)\right)^3\right\}=Kurt(s_i)\tilde z_i^3(k)+3\|\tilde z(k)\|^2\tilde z_i(k).
\]

for all \(k\ge 0\).

**Proof:** The following conclusion can be found in [85]. Here the proof is provided. First, it holds that

\[
\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tilde z_j(k)s_j(k)\right)^3
=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde z_i^3(k)s_i^3(k)
+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\ne i}3\tilde z_i^2(k)s_i^2(k)\tilde z_j(k)s_j(k)
+2\sum_{q=1}^{n}\tilde z_q(k)s_q(k)\sum_{i\ne j}\tilde z_i(k)\tilde z_j(k)s_i(k)s_j(k).
\]

for all \(k\ge 0\). Then, it follows that

\[
\begin{aligned}
&E\left\{s_i(k)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tilde z_j(k)s_j(k)\right)^3\right\}\\
&=E\left\{s_i^4(k)\right\}\tilde z_i^3(k)
+E\left\{s_i(k)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{j\ne i}\tilde z_i^2(k)s_i^2(k)\tilde z_j(k)s_j(k)\right\}\\
&\quad+2E\left\{s_i(k)\sum_{q=1}^{n}\tilde z_q(k)s_q(k)\sum_{i\ne j}\tilde z_i(k)\tilde z_j(k)s_i(k)s_j(k)\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Since \(s_i\) (\(i=1,\ldots,n\)) are mutually independent, it holds that \(E\{s_i^2\}=1\) and \(E\{s_i^3s_j\}=E\{s_i^2s_js_l\}=E\{s_i^2s_js_ls_m\}=0\) for four different indices \(i,j,l,m\) [85]. It follows that

\[
E\left\{s_i(k)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tilde z_j(k)s_j(k)\right)^3\right\}
=E\left\{s_i^4(k)\right\}\tilde z_i^3(k)
+3\sum_{j\ne i}\tilde z_j^2(k)\tilde z_i(k).
\]

for all $k \ge 0$. Since $Kurt(s_i)=E\{s_i^4(k)\}-3$, it follows that

$$E\left\{s_i(k)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_j(k)s_j(k)\right)^3\right\}=Kurt(s_i)z_i^3(k)+3z_i^3(k)+3\sum_{j\ne i}z_j^2(k)z_i(k)$$
$$=Kurt(s_i)z_i^3(k)+3\|z(k)\|^2z_i(k),$$

for all $k \ge 0$. The proof is complete.

By the previous lemma, for any component $z_i(k+1)$ of $z(k+1)$, it holds that

$$z_i(k+1)=z_i(k)+\eta\left[Kurt(s_i)z_i^3(k)+3\|z(k)\|^2z_i(k)\right]+\eta a\left(1-\|z(k)\|^2\right)z_i(k)$$
$$=\left[1+\eta Kurt(s_i)z_i^2(k)+3\eta\|z(k)\|^2+\eta a\left(1-\|z(k)\|^2\right)\right]z_i(k)$$
$$=\left[1+\eta a-\eta a\|z(k)\|^2+\eta b\left(Kurt(s_i)z_i^2(k)+3\|z(k)\|^2\right)\right]z_i(k), \tag{6.7}$$

for all $k \ge 0$.

### Lemma 6.5

Suppose that $z(0)\in \mathcal{S}_1$ and $\Gamma_s>\max\{Kurt(s_i)+3,3\}$. For any $i\in T$, it holds that

$$1+\eta a-\eta a\|z(k)\|^2+\eta b\left(Kurt(s_i)z_i^2(k)+3\|z(k)\|^2\right)>0$$

for all $k\ge 0$.

**Proof:** It follows that

$$1+\eta a-\eta a\|z(k)\|^2+\eta b\left(Kurt(s_i)z_i^2(k)+3\|z(k)\|^2\right)$$
$$=1+\eta a-\eta\left[a-b\left(Kurt(s_i)\frac{z_i^2(k)}{\|z(k)\|^2}+3\right)\right]\|z(k)\|^2,$$

for all $k\ge 0$. Since $\Gamma_s>\max\{Kurt(s_i)+3,3\}$ and $z(0)\in \mathcal{S}_1$, by Theorem 6.2, it follows that

$$1+\eta a-\eta a\|z(k)\|^2+\eta b\left(Kurt(s_i)z_i^2(k)+3\|z(k)\|^2\right)>0,$$

for all $k\ge 0$. The proof is complete.

Suppose that $z(0)\in \mathcal{S}_1$, $z(0)\notin V_1$ and $\Gamma_s>\max\{Kurt(s_i)+3,3\}$. For any $z_i(k)\ne 0$, $i\in P$, if $z_i^2(k)>0$ for all $k\ge 0$, it holds that

where $j\in Q$ and

$$\Pi_1=\frac{1+\eta a}{\eta a}\left[\frac{z_i(0)}{z_j(0)}\right]^2$$
