Shake-speare's Sonnets

I
From fairest creatures we desire increase,
That thereby beauty's Rose might never die,
But as the riper should by time decease,
His tender heir might bear his memory:
But thou contracted to thine owne bright eyes,
Feed'st thy light's flame with self-substantial fuel,
Making a famine where abundance lies,
Thy self thy foe, to thy sweet self too cruel:
Thou that art now the world's fresh ornament,
And only herald to the gaudy spring,
Within thine owne bud buriest thy content,
And tender churl mak'st waste in niggarding:
Pity the world, or else this glutton be,
To eat the world's due, by the grave and thee.

II

When forty winters shall besiege thy brow,
And dig deep trenches in thy beauty's field,

The point of **less** is that it lets you navigate through the file in several useful ways, such as moving one line up or down with the arrow keys, pressing space bar to move a page down, pressing **Ctrl-F** to move forward a page (i.e., the same as spacebar) or **Ctrl-B** to move back a page. To quit **less**, type **q** (for “quit”).

Perhaps the most powerful aspect of **less** is the forward slash key `/`, which lets you search through the file from beginning to end. For example, suppose we wanted to search through **sonnets.txt** for “rose” (Figure 16),<sup>17</sup> one of the most frequently used images used in the *Sonnets*.<sup>18</sup> The way to do this in **less** is to type `/rose` (read “slash rose”), as shown in Listing 13.
