Tape consists of a long flexible strip coated with magnetic material rolled on a reel and placed in a cartridge. In addition to industrystandard half-inch tape reels, there are multiple types of cartridges used with pSeries systems: the 3-inch tape cartridge, the 4-mm tape cartridge, the 8-mm tape cartridge, VXA tape, digital linear tape (DLT), Ultrium tape, and 2-inch Magstar tape. Some of these tape technologies are available for installation in the internal media bays of pSeries systems, others are available only as external tape units.

In each case, a tape unit reads and writes information on the tape much as a cassette recorder records and plays music on audio cassette tapes. The tape unit runs the tape across the read/write head, which is in contact with the tape surface. Electrical impulses in the read/write head are used to transfer information to and from the tape surface. Chapter 2 will explore some of the tape units used with pSeries systems.

# Optical Storage

The final type of storage device we will discuss is optical storage. Optical uses light and a reflective surface to store information. There are two basic types of optical storage used with pSeries systems: CD-ROM disk drives and rewritable optical disk drives. Let's briefly look at each type.

CD-ROM disk drives use the same technique to store information as audio CDs do. Rather than using magnetism, as with disks, disks, and tape, CD-ROM systems use optical techniques to achieve much higher recording density. A single disk used in pSeries CD-ROM drives can hold over 600 MB of information. That's enough storage to hold over 300,000 sheets of computer (or a stack over 90 feet high). The optical disks used with pSeries systems are read only. That is, users can view the information but can't change it. The information is preordered on the disk using specialized equipment and distributed to users. The primary use of CD-ROM storage is to distribute large amounts of information in a convenient package. For example, the entire library of pSeries and AIX 5L user manuals are stored on a single CD-ROM disk. Not only is this much easier way to distribute and store manuals, but the electronic method of looking up information is much more convenient. Other potential uses for CD-ROM include distribution of program libraries, financial reports, operations manuals, phone directories, or any large (and relatively stable) database.
