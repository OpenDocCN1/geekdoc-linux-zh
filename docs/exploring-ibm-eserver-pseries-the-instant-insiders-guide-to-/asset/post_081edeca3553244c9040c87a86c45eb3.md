# POWER GXT6500P

The POWER GXT6500P 3D Graphics Accelerator is IBM's current high-performance graphics adapter. When used in a 64-bit PCI or PCI-X slot, the GXT6500P provides extremely high performance, even for the largest MCAD models. Like the GXT4500P, it has a 128 MB unified frame buffer and supports resolution up to 2048 x 1536. Its rasterizer draws lines, characters, and smoothed polygons, and has advanced texture mapping for high image quality. Like the GXT4500P, this accelerator provides digital monitor support and can be used with the L200p and T541H Flat Color Monitors, as well as the ISO 9241-compliant P260/P275 CRT monitor.

The GXT6500P derives its power from the IBM-designed processing chip used in its design. This hard-wired logic (not a generic CPU or digital signal processor requiring microcode) implements the OpenGL and Graphics APIs by 3D graphics software natively on the adapter card, giving the maximum in performance. Offloading the graphics processing to the hardware on the GXT6500P minimizes load on the system and therefore helps provide the best overall system performance.

# Graphics Performance

Creating and manipulating 3D images requires a significant amount of computation. Every time an image is reduced, enlarged, rotated, or moved, thousands or millions of complex floating-point calculations must be done. Three-dimensional graphics accelerators designed to provide these functions so that the image appears to change immediately and smoothly require special hardware and extra memory. IBM and others use benchmark programs that are specially written to measure the speed with which an adapter and the system in it is installed create and manipulate images. It is important to understand that the graphics performance a user enjoys is determined by many different elements of the system, including the graphics adapter, the behavior or design of the operating system and application programs used, the overall system performance, and more. Even though the only way to measure actual performance is to run the programs that will be used in practice, benchmark results give a valid comparison among different system configurations.

# Printers

pSeries systems support many types of printers, and an exhaustive coverage of this topic is beyond the scope of this book. We will limit our discussion here to some representative IBM printers. Since the serial and parallel ports on pSeries systems are compatible with industry standards, printers from many other vendors work with pSeries systems. IBM offers an extensive range of printers, from high-end advanced function systems printers to low-cost models suitable for individual workstations or small workgroups. We will list a few of the printers that are suitable for medium-size to large workgroups, including printers for applications requiring continuous multipart forms and that can be shared by multiple users of a system. We will not describe low-end printers that are typically used with individual workstations or PCs.

# Continuous Forms Printers

The IBM 4247 Multi-Form Printer and the IBM 6400 Printer are dot-matrix impact printers. The dot-matrix technology forms characters, symbols, and graphics through the arrangement of discrete dots on the paper. The dots are formed by print wires or pins striking a ribbon and transferring ink to the paper just as old-fashioned typewriters do, but at much higher speeds. Although the dot-matrix technology is relatively old and less desirable than laser or inkjet printers for most applications, these heavy-duty, continuous-form printers can handle a wide variety of documents, including multipart forms. The print speed of the 4247 is 800 characters per second (cps) in data mode and 1,000 cps in fast mode. Near letter quality can be printed at 200 cps.

The IBM 6400 printer family offers speeds of 500, 1,000, 1,500, or 2,000 lines per minute (lpm), depending on the printer model. Although the 6400 printer family is designed to minimize the noise level, high-speed impact printers create much noise than laser or inkjet printers. For business environments where very high volumes of multipart forms are printed and higher noise levels are acceptable, 6400 models are available in cabinets designed for easy access.

There are also continuous forms printers that print with the laser or electrographic (EP) process like that used in office copiers. The 4370 Infoprint 62, for example, can print 62 pages per minute (ppm) of 8.5-inch wide forms with graphics at resolutions of either 240 or 300 dots per inch (dpi). The Infoprint 62 is particularly useful in manufacturing and distribution applications in which high-speed label printing with barcodes is required.

# IBM Network Printers

IBM network printers are tabletop letter-quality printers, designed to be shared by workgroups via LAN attachment. Since they print individual sheets of paper (cut sheets), they cannot produce continuous forms. The IBM Infoprint 1000 family of workgroup or network printers offers a range of prices, capacities, features, and software support for a broad range of workgroup printing requirements. Infoprint 1000 printers provide resolutions up to 1200 x 1200 PELS per square inch, yielding excellent print quality. Attachment options range from Ethernet and Token-Ring to parallel, serial, or USB, with network support for all major operating system environments.
