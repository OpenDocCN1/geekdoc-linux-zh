pSeries systems are designed with multiple and separate buses for improved I/O throughput. These separate buses can communicate to I/O devices in parallel, thus increasing the rate of information sent to and from processors. The PCI bus is multiplexed, which means that some pins are used for more than one purpose, depending on the operation being performed or the step of a particular operation. This is a bit more difficult to design, but results in fewer pins on each component, meaning that the components are less costly to produce. The PCI bus was also designed from the start to have many autoconfiguration features. This allows new devices to be added without setting switches or having conflicts between adapters. Because of its high performance and emergence in the PC industry, PCI proves to be a solid and cost-effective solution for the pSeries product line.

The most recent pSeries servers, including p615, p630, p650, p655, p670 and p690, provide the faster PCI-X I/O slots. PCI-X is an extension to the PCI architecture, doubling the transfer speed from 66 MHz to 133 MHz, enabling data transfer rates of more than a gigabyte per second. PCI-X slots are backward compatible with older 3.3-volt or universal (i.e. 3.3-volt or 5.0-volt) adapters. Also, these latest pSeries servers offer hot-plug PCI-X support, a RAS capability that allows an adapter that is not in use by an application to be removed while the system is up. In addition, the p650 and 7311-D01 (on p650) and 7040-61D (p655, p690) I/O drawers provide "blind-swap" hot-plug PCI-X slots. Blind-swap technology provides a cartridge that allows the adapter to be installed or removed without moving the system or drawer to a server position.

# Standard Ports

We have already seen that pSeries systems come standard with one or more Ultra-SCSI (or faster) ports. Other ports are provided for serial, parallel, Ethernet 10/100 Mbps or gigabit Ethernet, keyboard, mouse, and HMC interfaces. Ports are the control electronics and connectors accessible from outside the pSeries cores that provide a means of attaching external devices (such as storage, LANs, and printers) to the system without using one or more of the PCI slots.
