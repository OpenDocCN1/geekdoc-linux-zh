The extremely high storage capacity of CD-ROMs can be attributed to the technique used to store the information. When the CD-ROM is first recorded, a laser beam is used to burn tiny patterns on the reflective surface of an optical disk according to industry standards. By bouncing the low-power laser beam in the CD-ROM drive off the optical disk's surface, a series of mirrors and sensors can read back the information burned into the disk. Although the optical technology itself quite well limited speed (compared with disks) and inability to record information preclude using CD-ROM as normal disk storage. Most pSeries and RS/6000 systems have internal CD-ROM drives. For systems that do not have an internal CD-ROM drive, an external unit (7210 External CD-ROM) is available. These devices will be covered in Chapter 2. Also available on pSeries systems are DVD-ROM drives, which can read both CD-ROM formatted disks and DVD-ROM drives, including double-sided disks. On some systems, the user has a choice of either CD-ROM or DVD-ROM as the installation media device.

Rewritable optical disks use a similar laser-based technique to store information. However, CD-ROM drives can only read information, rewritable optical disks can read and write information on an optical disk. The pSeries options at this time are a 4.7 GB DVD-RAM drive, which can be mounted internally in a media bay, and the 3995 C-Series Optical Library Dataserver, an external device. Rewritable optical disks can be used for information or program distribution just as CD-ROMs. Although they are inherently slower than magnetic disk drives, they are specialized applications, as large databases of images that were originally created by hand and scanned into storage (as opposed to being in digital form), in which they may be used for working data.
