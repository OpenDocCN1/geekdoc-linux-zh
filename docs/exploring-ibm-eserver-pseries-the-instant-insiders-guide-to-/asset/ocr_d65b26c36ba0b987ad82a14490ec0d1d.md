# Introduction xxvii

+   - What capacity is required? You must determine what your low
high marks are for memory, storage, and adapters. This is a
very difficult undertaking, but application providers and other
similar customers can help.

+   - What performance is required? Consider floating-point or inte-
gner (for database and traditional business applications)?

+   - What future growth is anticipated? If your business is growing
or you foresee the use of a particular application increasing in
the near term, it may be better to invest in a system that can
grow with you. If growth will not be needed, then you can likely
save cost by purchasing a smaller machine fully configured.

+   - Are your business workloads likely to change rapidly, requiring
immediate upgrade of processing power in order to respond to
customer demands adequately? If so, you should consider sys-
tems that offer on demand features such as processor and memory
Capacity Upgrade on Demand.

+   - Finally, what price is acceptable and what priority does price
have? Price must be weighed against other factors. For example,
if a LAN adapter fails in your brokerage system and you conse-
quently lose $2,000 per minute while the system is being repaired,
an extra $1,000 for dual LAN adapters is a small price to pay.
