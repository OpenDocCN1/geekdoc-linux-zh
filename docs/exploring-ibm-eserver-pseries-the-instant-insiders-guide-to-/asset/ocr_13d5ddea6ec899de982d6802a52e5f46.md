The POWER architecture combines the concepts of the original RISC architecture with a sprinkling of more traditional concepts to create systems with optimum overall performance. The pSeries of servers, the third generation of RISC products, was introduced using the POWER3 and PowerPC architectures. The latest RISC generation—POWER4—was introduced in October 2001 on the pSeries 690 server, and this architecture has been followed by POWER4+TM technology on the most recent pSeries models.

The pSeries family is designed to address the computing needs of both high-performance computing (HPC) users and those who run more general commercial applications. High-performance computing applications can be found in business, government, and academic environments in areas as statistical analysis, geological analysis, molecular chemistry simulations, artificial intelligence, and life sciences. The engineering/scientific user may perform such tasks as designing mechanical devices on the screen (computer-aided design, or CAD), simulating physical stresses to test the strength of the design (computer-aided engineering, or CAE), and then automatically generating the programming necessary to manufacture the part with numerical control tools (computer-aided manufacturing, or CAM). Many HPC applications are best implemented on shared servers or clusters of servers, with each user having a PC as his or her workstation.

If three-dimensional (3D) graphics capability is required for the application (in the case of automotive or aircraft design), then an IBM IntelliStation POWER 275 or 265, the pSeries 630 Model 6E4, or one with large display and specialized input devices, as the IBM Spaceball, should be selected as that user's personal workstation. These workstations are usually networked to allow the user community to share programs, data, and peripheral equipment.

Another type of environment addressed by pSeries computers is of traditional commercial applications found in business, government, and academic organizations. In these environments, a single pSeries server or cluster of servers is shared by multiple users, each working at a personal computer (PC), which is connected to the server on a local area network (LAN). Applications in commercial environments cover a great range, from general accounting and order entry to the most advanced database management systems for supply chain management, data mining, and business-to-business transactions using the Internet.
