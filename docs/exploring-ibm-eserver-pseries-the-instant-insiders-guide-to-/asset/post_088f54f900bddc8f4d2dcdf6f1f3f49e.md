## Small Business—Bob’s Gearbox Company

Our hypothetical small business is a gearbox manufacturer named Bob’s Gearbox Company. Bob’s has a standard line of gearboxes and also accept orders for custom gearboxes. It is a private corporation (owned by Bob, of course) with 32 employees. Bob has been in business for five years and has experienced moderate growth. He currently conducts business by non-computer methods but finds himself needing to streamline operation as the business grows. Bob is especially concerned that his profits seem to be shrinking as basics. A study of Bob’s business shows two basic causes for this. First, salespeople commit to discounted pricing on a gearbox order to capture the business. The trouble is that Bob never knows what it actually costs him to produce a given gearbox. He uses standard cost estimates to price a customer’s order and hopes that his actual costs to build the gearbox are somewhere close.

The second basic problem uncovered in the study is that Bob’s inventory is not well managed. The production department is often hampered by not having the right parts and raw materials in inventory. This causes slips in the delivery of customer orders, hurting customer satisfaction and fueling heated arguments among the marketing manager, the production manager, and the materials manager. Finger pointing is commonplace. The study also showed that 25% of the inventory items in stock are obsolete and will never be used.

In this scenario, it is clear that Bob has outgrown his manual method of doing business and needs a better way to track the actual costs associated with manufacturing his products. This may uncover the fact that his salespeople sell gearboxes at or below cost. Bob also needs help tracking his inventory. He needs to know when critical parts are getting low and what parts are slowly moving. The deficiencies in Bob’s business can be addressed through the proper computer solution.

After looking through manufacturing trade journals and having formal discussions with others in the industry, Bob finds several prewritten industry-specific application programs that seem to fit his needs. After seeing demonstrations and detailed conversations with others using the application programs, Bob selects one of the packages written for UNIX operating systems, including AIX 5L. The selected package will allow Bob to track manufacturing costs more closely by letting him know more accurately the costs of the labor, outside purchases, and inventory that are used in making his products. Bob will gain better control over his inventory through improved procedures and the inventory-management function of this new application program. Bob also chooses to take advantage of the computer system to automate the general accounting functions of his business, such as payroll, accounts receivable, accounts payable, and general ledger. The order entry and invoicing functions of the application program will allow Bob to track orders and bill customers more quickly.

Based on the requirements of the chosen application program and his small number of users, Bob selected a pSeries 615 Model 6E3, shown in Figure 5.1, running the AIX 5L v5.2 operating system. The p615-6E3 was selected for its low cost, more ample power, and expandability as Bob's business grows. Bob decides to order a second 36.4 GB drive to augment the standard 36.4 GB of storage that comes with the p615-6E3. The total storage of 72.8 GB will be sufficient to hold the operating system software, application programs, and associated data. The 1 GB of main memory, expandable all to 16 GB, will meet his needs for. Since the selected manufacturing and accounting applications use a client/server design, Bob selects personal computers as workstations, connected on an Ethernet LAN using one of the integrated Ethernet adapters that is standard with the p615-6E3. One PC is for the inventory clerk, one is for the purchasing agent, two are for the accounting department, one is for the production department, and one is for Bob so that he can get the management he needs to make intelligent decisions. Bob will select priced PCs because the users will primarily interacting with the applications running on the pSeries 615 and he must control system costs. He will, of course, need an Ethernet adapter in each PC. As the future needs of his business grow, he can easily add more PCs on the LAN.

For now, Bob selects a printer attached to the parallel port of the p615-6E3. In the future, he can easily upgrade to a network printer attached to the Ethernet LAN. An IBM 4247 Multi-Form Printer is chosen to provide the various multipart form reports generated by the manufacturing application program at a rate of up to 700 characters per second. A 20/40 GB 4-mm Internal Tape Drive provides a way to back up data on the disk, ensuring that important business information will not accidentally be lost. This tape drive attaches to one of the standard internal Ultra3-SCSI ports.

A high-speed modem will be needed to connect the p615-6E3 to the Internet so that Bob can take advantage of the Documentation Library Service provided with AIX 5L to allow reading, navigating, and searching-online documentation. Although it is generally easier to view online than to use printed manuals, the Documentation Library Service allows the download of manuals in printable format if hard copy is needed. Internet access will provide many other business communications and information benefits, well. Bob will work with a local Internet Service Provider (ISP) to select the appropriate modem and software.

For businesses such as Bob's Gearbox Company, which has limited computer skills, the pSeries 615 offers some important serviceability advantages. The System attention light-emitting diode (LED) on the front of the system indicates if there is a potential or actual failure. There are other LEDs inside the system located near each replaceable unit to guide the component that needs attention, in a manner similar to that used in most copiers to update paper pans. All of the components of the p15 except the system planar and the operator panel can be replaced by the user, and the attention LEDs will help an untrained user, possibly with some telephone support from service personnel, to take corrective action.
