
## Diskette Storage

Diskettes provide a convenient way to transfer small amounts of information (up to 1.44 MB) between computer systems or to make backup copies of small data files. A 3.5-inch 1.44 MB disk drive is standard on pSeries systems. However, diskettes are declining in popularity, especially in the server market, where CD-ROM and DVD-ROM drives are standard, and where many systems are managed and software is installed over a LAN or the Internet.

## Internal Disk Drives

All the pSeries systems and most other pSeries and RS/6000 servers come with some amount of magnetic disk storage. The exceptions are the pSeries 660 Models 6H0, 6H1, and 6M1, which are designed to use external storage subsystems such as the IBM 2104 Expandable Storage Plus (Ultra3-SCS) or IBM 7133 Serial Disk System (SSA). However, even those servers offer optional internal boot disk capability. Chapter 1 provides information on the standard disk configurations and specifications. The disk options used with pSeries systems. The reason multiple feature numbers are used for ordering drives is that the physical packaging (disk carrier) of drive may vary depending on the pSeries server in which it is supported or there may be some technical differences among the disk technologies used. You might also refer back to Chapter 1 for the differences between SCSI types. Disk technology, in terms of both capacity and rotational speed, is changing rapidly. The smallest drive now offered on many pSeries systems is 36.4 GB, and 15,000 rpm drives are starting to replace the 10,000 rpm drives. It is anticipated that 18.2 GB disk drives will be withdrawn from marketing in 2003.

## External Disk Systems

External disk systems are units that contain multiple drives and attach to pSeries systems through a storage adapter, providing significant additional storage expansion capacity in rack drawer or tower packages. Both SCSI and SSA external units are available. These systems provide high-density rack storage solutions, as differentiated from the storage systems discussed under "Storage Area Networks" later in this chapter, which provide integrated storage control functionality like RAID.

## 2104 SCSI Expandable Storage Plus

The IBM 2104 Expandable Storage Plus (EXP Plus) provides flexible, scalable, and low-cost external SCSI storage for pSeries servers. The EXP Plus is available in two types of packages—the Model TU3 is a standalone tower; the Model DU3 is designed to be mounted in a 19-inch rack and offers optional 48-volt DC power for telecommunications customers. Each can hold up to 14 Ultra3-SCSI (160 Mbps) 10K rpm drives of 18.2 GB, 36.4 GB, 73.4 GB, or 146.8 GB, allowing a maximum capacity of 2.055 TB. The 2104 also supports 36.4 GB and 73.4 GB 15K rpm drives. Drives can be hot-plugged while the unit is operating. Redundant power is available as an option for each model.

The EXP Plus can attach to pSeries servers using a variety of SCSI adapters: SCSI-2 Fast and Wide, Ultra2SCSI, Ultra-3SCSI. For the highest performance and availability, the enclosures can be combined with the IBM PCI 4-channel SCSI adapter (#2498), enabling Ultra-3SCSI 160 Mbps performance and multiple RAID options. The EXP Plus can be attached to a second pSeries server by adding an optional SCSI port. Distances up to 20 meters are supported between enclosures and pSeries servers using Ultra-3SCSI adapters. High Availability Multiprocessing (HACMP) is supported for the EXP Plus when used with appropriate adapters in configurations.

## 7133 TotalStorage Serial Disk System

The 7133 TotalStorage Disk System employs IBM's Storage Architecture, which was discussed in Chapter 1. The 7133 comes in both rack-mounted (Model D40) and desktop (Model T40) configurations. The models are functionally similar and can be populated with up to 16 user-replaceable disk modules. The available 10K rpm disk modules are 18.2 GB, 36.4 GB, 72.8 GB, and 146.8 GB. Also available are 15K rpm disks in capacities of 36.4 GB and 72.8 GB. Maximum storage capacity per 7133 system is 2.3 TB (up to 14 TB per host). These systems provide redundant power and cooling units. In addition to their capacity and reliability, these provide outstanding performance. The 7133 subsystem connects to pSeries computers via 4-port adapters including the Advanced Adapter (#6230). An SSA Write Cache adapter (#6235) can be added to the Advanced adapter for even greater performance in single- or dual-host environments. Up to 16 adapters may be used within a single pSeries server, which would be housed in six 7133 units (each unit accommodates 16 drives).

7133 can have independent connections to different pSeries servers. This capability, along with mirroring support in AIX, makes high-availability pSeries configurations possible. That is, with proper application programming and configuring pSeries systems and AIX, a system can be configured to continue functioning in the face of a component failure. With the optional SSA Extender, 7133 systems separated by up to 10 km can be used for remote mirroring, providing protection of data from physical disasters such as fire or hurricane.

Overall, 7133 provides excellent reliability, performance, price/performance, capacity, and ease of use. It should be considered in any environment where moderate to large amounts of high-performance disks are required. The 7133's high number of I/O operations per second also makes it ideal for transaction processing environments in which a large number of requests are made for small blocks of data.

## 7204 External Disk Drive

Figure 2.2 shows an IBM 7204 External Disk Drive, which can be used with all models using either a single-ended Ultra-SCSI, Ultra2 SCSI Differential (low voltage differential) or LVDS adapter. Unlike the external disk systems previously described, the 7204 provides a single 18.2 GB or 36.4 GB disk drive attached to a SCSI I/O controller via a cable. For environments with stringent data requirements, the 7204 unit can be detached from the pSeries system and its electrical socket nightly to allow storage in a vault. The drives are shock-mounted to help prevent damage as they are moved.

## 7212 Model 102 TotalStorage Storage Device Enclosure

For situations in limited space, or when it is desirable to consolidate the media devices into a single location, the 7212-102 Storage Device Enclosure provides two media bays in one rack drawer that mounts in a 19-inch rack. It is supported on all pSeries systems and currently available RS/6000 systems, with the exception of the pSeries 635, 670, and 690, which use 24-inch-wide racks. There is a media drawer available for the p670 and p690, which is designed for a 24-inch wide rack.

7212-102 can accommodate any two of the following storage devices:

- Digital Data Storage 4-mm (DDS-4) tape drive
- 40H CD-ROM drive

## RAID Technology

RAID technology is used in many systems, especially those with large amounts of disk storage, to help improve reliability and data integrity. The acronym RAID originally stood for Redundant Arrays of Inexpensive Disks, since the technology was developed to provide improved data integrity for systems using inexpensive disk drives designed for the personal computer (PC) market, but now many people say that RAID stands for Redundant Arrays of Independent Disks, since the disks used in modern RAID arrays are state-of-the-art devices—not just the disks available. Whichever definition of the acronym you use, the important point is that RAID technology allows data to be written and stored on two or more drives, that in the event of a disk failure, data integrity can be maintained.
