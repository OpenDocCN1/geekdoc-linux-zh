Another contributor to eye fatigue commonly found in offices is improper lighting. The amount of glare by windows can be reduced by positioning the display at right angles to any window. Curtains or horizontal blinds can also be used to direct light away from the screen. Diffused office lighting will provide fewer “hot spots” and tend to provide the most uniform light and soften harsh shadows, or lower intensity bulbs or dimmer switches may be installed. To reduce the glare in the user’s environment, pSeries displays and terminals have antiglare screens.

## Workstation Comfort

The workstation furniture being used can also affect productivity. For this reason, attention should be given to the details of the user’s workstation, that is, the chair and desk and table to be used. A properly designed chair can reduce back problems and make the user more comfortable and productive. An improperly designed chair can lead to reduced alertness and shorter concentration spans. Users may not even realize they are uncomfortable as they unconsciously seek a more comfortable position.

Once the user is seated, his or her relationship with the computer display and keyboard will affect comfort and thus productivity, so the computer display should be positioned properly. The top of the computer display should be positioned at 10 degrees below eye level, the center of the display at 20 degrees below eye level and 14 to 22 inches away. A tilt/swivel stand under the terminal or display allows the user to adjust the display screen angle as desired. Users should avoid using bifocal lenses, which make them tilt their heads back when reading the screens. This can lead to discomfort in the back and shoulders.

The keyboard should also be in a comfortable position. pSeries systems and associated terminals all provide separate keyboards attached by a flexible cable, which allows the user to position the keyboard as desired. The keyboard height should allow the elbow to be bent at 90 degrees used during typing. Finally, provide sufficient desk space for documents used during the computer session.

## What About Noise?

Noise is not conducive to efficiency. Irregular noise is more distracting than constant noise. Unfortunately, irregular noise is commonplace in almost any busy environment. It results from nearby conversations, telephones, printers, copy machines, and many other things. If possible, isolate noise sources such as printers and copy machines by placing them in isolated areas or separate rooms. Noise can also be reduced by installing doors, carpets, and other sound-insulating materials.

## Security

In many environments, computer systems are the backbone of day-to-day operations. This makes the information stored on the computers an asset as valuable as cash. Like an organization's cash, computer information must be protected from loss or theft. Let's look at how this vital information can be protected.

## Loss Prevention

An ever-present hazard when dealing with information (with or without computers) is the possibility that the information will be lost. Loss can occur in many different ways. A computer system's breakdown, such as a disk failure, can result in lost information. Further, operator error can cause data to be accidentally corrupted, resulting in lost information. Finally, a disaster (such as a fire or flood) can result in a loss of vital information. For this reason, recovery from the loss of vital information must be addressed.

One way to deal with the losing of vital information is to have backup copies of computer information at regular intervals (for example, daily). Multiple backup copies should be made on a revolving basis and kept in a place safe from damage or loss. Three copies will allow at least one copy to be kept in safe storage at all times and provide for different levels of backup. In the event of an information loss, the computer system can be restored to the point at which the most recent backup copy was made. The AIX 5L operating system and the various tape and DVDRAM devices discussed in Chapter 2 are designed for these kinds of save/restore operations. Any changes to information after the point of the last backup will have to be re-created after the backup copy is used to restore the system. This may involve manually re-entering the transactions since the last backup. Mirroring, discussed in Chapter 3, can also be used to decrease the likelihood of losing valuable information.

## Theft Prevention

Theft prevention deals with protecting sensitive information from unauthorized disclosure. Security requirements vary widely from environment to environment and should be considered early in your planning.

pSeries systems provide various levels of security that help deter unauthorized access. Security is provided by the computer system itself along with the operating system. In fact, pSeries systems and the AIX 5L operating system have been designed to meet the stringent security requirements defined by the National Security Center Trusted Computer System Evaluation Criteria C2.

On the needs of the environment, various levels of security can be activated, from no security to detailed access control. A security officer is usually assigned to manage the security of the system.

For sensitive environments, some customers may consider restricting access to areas where pSeries systems or associated terminals are located. These site-planning considerations should be considered early in a computer automation project.

## Service

Although every effort has been made to make pSeries systems as reliable as possible, some computers will fail, and if a computer does have a problem, there must be a way of getting it fixed. All pSeries systems come with a warranty from IBM. For many systems, the standard offering is a 1-year warranty that provides free on-site repairs from IBM’s service division 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. In order to be competitive in the entry and midrange UNIX server markets, IBM has begun to offer on some models a 1-year warranty with “9 to 5, nextbusiness-day” service. Upgrading to “7 x 24” support is available for an additional charge on these systems. These alternate options are not available in every geography, so the local IBM Service Representative or IBM Partner should be consulted. The various terminals, storage systems, and printers associated with pSeries systems each have their own warranty terms and conditions.

After the warranty period, the customer becomes responsible for maintenance of the system. IBM and others offer service agreements that, for a fee, provide postwarranty on-site service just like that provided during the warranty, as well as less expensive options for other than 7x24 support. Various maintenance discounts are available, depending on the level of systems management procedures implemented in a specific situation.

If the system fails and there is no service contract, the user will have to pay for parts and labor, which can quickly become extremely expensive. For this reason, many businesses choose to keep their systems on maintenance after warranty as a type of insurance policy.

Many factors affect the choice of computer system for a particular use. These factors include application availability, total cost of ownership, ergonomics, and the availability of qualified support providers. Where a UNIX solution fits the bill, IBM eServer pSeries systems, the AIX 5L operating system and associated software products, and the support offerings available from IBM and IBM Business Partners deliver solutions that merit careful consideration.
