Along with this change, IBM's RS/6000 Division began a move from a line of specialized machines with custom-built components in fairly low volumes to a more generalized line of systems with common off-the-shelf components. There were advantages for both the user and IBM. IBM could design new systems and release them to the market more rapidly than before, and the use of common, high-volume parts, including some used in the personal computer line (power supplies, cables, disks, memory, etc.) allowed the user to continue to drop. Recent examples of implementing this open systems paradigm include Fibre Channel architecture and storage area networks (SANs), and support for applications requiring the Linux operating system. And, following the latest industry standard I/O architecture direction, recently announced pSeries servers and I/O drawers support hot-plug PCI-X adapters. To understand major differences among the various models, the following paragraphs provide a thumbnail sketch of the pSeries family and the RS/6000 models still available. Greater detail will be given in the next section.

The pSeries 610 is designed for small to medium-sized businesses and is available in two models. The pSeries 610 Model 6C1 is a rack-drawer server, and the pSeries 610 Model 6E1 comes in a desktop configuration. Both models are 1 to 2-way symmetric multiprocessing (SMP) servers using 333 MHz, 375 MHz, or 450 MHz POWER-3 processors. Larger companies may select the p610 for situations where many small systems must be deployed in separate locations, as in retail of stores.

The pSeries 615, a 1- or 2-way SMP server, introduces POWER4+ technology to the lower end of the pSeries family and is intended to replace the older p610 in the future. The pSeries 615 Model 6C3 is a rack-mount server, while the pSeries 615 Model 6E3 is a desktop unit. Both are powered by 2 GHz POWER4+ processors and provide extensive scalability for an entry system—up to eight disk drives and six PCI-X slots. Like the p610, this server is designed for small to medium-sized companies and for distributed systems in large corporations.

The pSeries 620, a midrange desktop server with up to three processors running at either 600 MHz or 750 MHz and lots of expansion capability, can satisfy the total computing requirements of many small or even medium-sized companies. There are two p620 models, differing solely in the fact that the pSeries 620 Model 6F0 can be expanded only for four processors whereas the pSeries 620 Model 6F1 offers up to a 6-way SMP configuration. The reason for the distinction between Models 6F1 and 6F0 is that the maximum of four processors in the p620-6F0 qualifies it for workgroup pricing for popular software programs, such as Oracle and IBM DB2 databases, making the total system cost more attractive for many users.

The rack-mount pSeries 630 Model 6C4 and desktop pSeries 630 Model-E4 are 1-, 2-, and 4-way SMP servers with either 1.2 GHz or 1.45 GHz POWER4+ processors. The 1- and 2-way configurations provide attractive entry points for small businesses, with room to grow in processing power, memory, and I/O capacity. The p630-6C4 rack system meets NEBS Level 3 standards, which it is designed to withstand under rugged conditions of temperature, vibration, and the like. This versatile entry system also offers the flexibility of logical partitioning (LPAR—the acronym LPAR is used in this book to refer to "logical partitioning" as a technology or function to, which allows users to create four "virtual servers within a server."). The p630-6E4 is positioned as a replacement for the pSeries 640, and the p630-6E4 will replace the RS/6000 Model 270.

The pSeries 640 is a rack-mountable server designed especially for high-density server farms in e-commerce and Telco applications. In addition, the 1-4-way POWER3-II microprocessors used in the p640 provide the floating-point power needed for many HPC applications. The pSeries 640 meets NEBS Level 3 standards, making it suitable for Telco and harsh environments.

This 2-8-way rack-mount system offers 1.2 GHz or 1.45 GHz POWER4+ processors, LPAR support, and the capacity for attaching up to IBM 7311-D10 or 7311-D20 I/O expansion drawers. The multipich packaging technology of the POWER4 architecture enables configurations optimized for the technical and scientific markets. The p690 also introduced to the pSeries family logical partitioning, developed from IBM’s extensive mainframe systems experience. With up to 32-way SMP configurations of POWER4 processors running at 1.5 GHz or 1.7 GHz, with half a terabyte (512 GB) of memory capacity, the flagship of the pSeries family is the p690, a datacenter-class UNIX server announced in October 2001. It was the first pSeries server to use the POWER4 processor, and now it offers up to 32-way SMP configurations of POWER4 processors running at 1.5 GHz or 1.7 GHz, with half a terabyte (512 GB) of memory capacity. Logical partitioning, which is supported on most POWER4-based pSeries servers, is described in greater detail in “A Closer Look” later in this chapter.

The pSeries 660 has three models—660-H0, 660-H1, and 660-M1—all of which have rack-mounted CEC (Central Electronics) drawer containing processors, memory, and I/O, plus one or more I/O drawers containing PCI adapter slots, memory devices, and I/O ports. The pSeries 660 Models H0 and H1 are electronically equivalent to the p620-6F0 and p620-6F1. The p660-M1, which offers up to eight POWER4 processors at 750 MHz, provides outstanding performance in industry-standard benchmarks for transaction processing, Web serving, and Java application.

The pSeries 670 is a 4- and 16-way midrange server, positioned between the p650 and the p690. It features POWER4 processor chip modules (MCMs) running at 1.5 GHz, LPAR support, and up to 256 GB of memory, and can attach to three I/O expansion drawers. For the p670, at the top of the pSeries midrange line, IBM offers a model conversion to the p690 for customers whose growth requires such expansion.

The POWER4-based-cluster-optimized p655 fills a special niche in the pSeries family. It is a high-density configuration, up to 12 processors in a single 24-inch frame, making the p655 an excellent choice for AIX 5L applications. Logical partitioning, which is supported on most POWER4-based pSeries servers, is described in greater detail in “A Closer Look” later in this chapter.
