For businesses, which are the customers for pSeries systems, it is vital that timely, accurate information be available to decision makers and employees be able to share information rapidly. It is also vital that business communications be secure so that personal information about customers and employees is kept confidential and a company's assets are protected. As improvements are made in communications within an organization, it is very likely that improvements in productivity and effectiveness will follow. Ironically, as an organization grows, it becomes more important and at the same time more difficult to maintain efficient, secure, and accurate communications—the very thing that facilitated growth in the first place. Communications difficulties grow with the size of the organization.

Today’s businesses, universities, research facilities, and government agencies are quickly finding customers in communications unequaled in significance Bell invented the telephone. Communications can occur in several ways—between a server and the users at their personal computers (PCs), between two computers, or among a group of computers in a complex communications network. pSeries systems, together with AIX 5L and its extensions, provide the hardware features and software support needed for operation in almost any communications network.

# Distributed Computer Communications

In some cases, the computing needs of an organization can be satisfied by a very simple communications arrangement. Users work at PCs attached by a local area network (LAN) to a single pSeries server. For outside communications (e.g., to the Internet), the server is attached by an appropriate modem. Such an arrangement is adequate for small organizations in most computer users are at a single location. The needs of an organization require multiple systems, then a more complex communications network is required. For example, a retail chain may want a computer system at each retail location as well as a computer system at headquarters (Figure 4.1). As shown, all computer systems are joined through a communications network that allows them to easily move information (such as daily cash register receipts) from place to place as necessary. This is called a distributed computer network.
