Data moves around the loop in a single direction, and much like a shared bus, only one activity occurs on the loop at a single point in time. To transfer data, the initiating device must get control of the loop by arbitration, much like SCSI. FC-AL sends bursts of data at 100 Mbps around the loop.

In response to competition to SSA, the FC-AL community added a second, redundant loop to the FC-AL definition. This second loop attaches to the same devices as the first loop and, along with additional switching circuitry, eliminates the single point of failure that is inherent in a single loop. Also, performance improvements can be achieved with a second loop. However, because of the shared, arbitrated nature of FC-AL, questions remain about its ability to scale and perform as well as SSA in large, complex configurations. FC-AL disks are not used in pServers.

A Fibre Channel adapter capable of up to 2 Gbps operation is available for the pServers. It is used to make connections from host systems to external storage subsystems. As Fibre Channel continues to evolve over the next few years, it will allow multiple systems and multivendor to attach hardware to increasingly complex Fibre Channel networks and infrastructure known as fabric.

Just as SCSI had to evolve until it became a truly robust, multivendor architecture, Fibre Channel networks are going through similar "growing pains." Tremendous benefits can be achieved when complex processor and storage systems can be interconnected, managed, and operated in a robust fashion in SANs. This is the direction being followed by user requirements for continuous access to well-managed, protected storage continue to expand.

# Tape Storage

The next type of storage we will cover is magnetic tape, or simply tape. One primary use of tape storage is to keep a backup copy of the information stored on the computer's disks. In the event of a disk failure, the backup tape can be used to restore the lost information to a new disk unit. The low cost and high recording densities inherent in tape also make it ideal for archiving information to which immediate access is no longer needed. Tape can also be used for distributing programs or transferring information from one computer system to another.
