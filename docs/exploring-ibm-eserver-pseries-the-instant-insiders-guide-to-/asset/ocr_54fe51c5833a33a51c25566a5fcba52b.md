Their primary concerns are floating-point performance, the ability to cluster many servers in parallel to process large amounts of data, and graphics features/performance. HPC customers are typically interested either in large rack-mounted servers with extensive scalability or in high-performance deskside models for individual technical personnel. IBM refers to a computer which is packed-a small unit that sits upright on the floor below a desk (similar to the personal computer in your home) as a “deskside”. Deskside models that support three-dimensional (3D) graphics capability are called “workstations” and are used in areas such as aircraft and automotive design. As mentioned earlier, IBM’s UNIX workstations are now part of the Intellianium family of products.

The other primary camp within the UNIX marketplace is termed “commercial”. This group deals with banking, manufacturing, large database-oriented environments, and retail applications, to name a few. With the rapid growth in recent years of the Internet and e-commerce, commercial-sector requirements have expanded to include both core business applications and the infrastructure required for today’s online business world, such as Web serving, networking, and security. The primary concerns of this commercial group include database and transaction performance, reliability and availability features for near-continuous operations, communications, and compatibility with existing systems. Typically, commercial users select from the range of deskside and rack-mounted pSeries models, depending on the number of users, size of the enterprise, and data storage requirements. An increasingly important subset of the commercial market is composed of users Internet Service Providers (ISPs), Application Service Providers (ASPs), and telecommunications central offices. Customers often operate large “server farms”—groups of servers linked together and mounted in racks in the same room. These server farms require high-performance processing, high availability, high-density rack-mounted packaging (to maximize the use of floor space), and compliance with Network Equipment Building System (NEBS) standards for operation in rugged conditions.
