There are 74 employees, almost all of them currently have PCs, which provide tools for such tasks as market analysis, trend analysis, word processing, financial modeling, and so on. Some Art Department employees use Macintosh workstations. Such applications as accounting and employee records are minimally automated at this time, and data is maintained on the PCs assigned to individuals in the relevant departments.

Steve Johnson recently sponsored a company-wide study to find a way to address the business goals of increasing marketing effectiveness and reducing operating costs. This study quickly uncovered the fact that the campaign staff relies heavily on the overloaded Art Department to generate the complex proposals with marketing campaigns. The Art Department generated needed artwork on the PC or Macintosh workstations, and they often rely on outside subcontractors. They find themselves inundated with urgent requests for new artwork and changes to existing artwork. Even though the Art Department personnel work overtime, they are unable to keep up with the workload, and the only way they can share is by hard code.

Another situation confirmed by the study is that the market analysis being done by one PC seldom correlates with the market analysis done by another PC. The cause of the conflicting information turns out to be different versions of the area demographic information reside on the various PCs used in market analysis. Even though one person has responsibility for periodically updating the demographic information and distributing the updates, it eventually that different versions of the information emerge, making the market analysis inaccurate. Further, as market coverage grows, the demographic information is growing in size and is becoming impractical to distribute via diskettes. It appears that more sophisticated data management and analysis techniques will become necessary as the company grows.

The third major problem is that, although the company is now approaching 80 employees in size, it lacks centralized systems for e-mail, internal messaging, and applications as accounting, payroll, and human resources. With almost every employee having a PC (Intel-based or Mac), there is little control over software levels of common applications and, as a result, limited ability to share or reassign when required.

The project team decided to find a solution that would increase the output of the Art Department, centralize the area demographic reformation, and provide the messaging system needed. They concluded that they could increase the output of the Art Department personnel providing them with the ability to maintain a shared database of artwork and to enable collaboration among the employees in that department. Such a library of graphic images could be easily searched for current methods and could be modified and used more quickly than creating new images. To change an image, it could be recalled onto one of the personal workstations required. Collaboration software, which supports multiple users modifying the same file, will enable Art Department employees to share their work and will facilitate contributions from more than a single person to the artwork. These capabilities will increase the productivity of the desktop publishing capability already available at individual workstations, which should allow the Art Department to create most of its proposals in-house and reduce expenses for outside contractors. Compared with current methods, these tools will help increase quality, decrease the turn around, and reduce the costs associated with artwork.

The team also concluded that the simplest way to centralize the demographic information is to place the information in a database on a single computer system and use a LAN to provide all PC users with access to the information, ensuring that all are using the same current data for their market analysis. This will result in a more accurate market analysis and then more effective marketing efforts for clients. Similarly, an e-mail server application will be implemented to support all employees. As needs for individual departments are identified, applications can be installed.

After examining several database and messaging application programs, the IBM DB2 Universal Database (UDB) Workgroup Server Edition and the Lotus Notes/Domino 6 server program, along with the Lotus Notes 6 client packages for the employees' personal computers. The server selected is the pSeries630 Model 6EA, which offers plenty of processing power and storage capacity, and being a maximum 4-way SMP system, qualifies for workgroup pricing of the database software. Art Department employees will continue to use their existing PCs, most of which are Intel and a few of which are Macs. Using DB2 UDB Workgroup, a database of images will be built and, to the degree that the image formats are readable on both types of workstations, all Art Department users will be able to share their work. Other company-employees, who are not involved in the production of graphics reformation on the database, such as market demographics, accounting data, and human resources. All employees will use the Lotus Notes application for messaging within and outside the company, and LAN was selected because it is supported by all existing PCs. A shared network printer, such as the IBM B140, which print about 40 pages per minute, will be managed by the pSeries and be available to all employees. A desirable color plotter (e.g., Tektron) will be used by the Art Department for high-quality image copies. The automatic sheet feed will allow the plotter to print multiple drawings unattended.

The pSeries 630 will be used as a database, file, and printing server by all users on the network. The DB2 UDB Workgroup database will be the central repository for demographic information, which needed by many PC users performing market research, and for the graphics to be shared by users in the Art Department. The relational database application, such as DB2 UDB, allows files of various kinds to be indexed with multiple keywords so that users can search and sort. As a file server, the p630-E64 in effect becomes a giant PC disk shared by all PCs. As a print server, it will allow all PC users to document on either the Network Printer or the plotter just if these devices were attached to their PCs. These files will be stored in Windows or Mac format on the pSeries disk so that the PC users can use the data with the application programs they currently use. Johnson & Thornbury selected AIX 5L v5.2, which includes the Bonus Pack and Expansion Pack of Internet and other-p630-E64 with a 2-way 1.2 GHz POWER4+ processor card will provide adequate performance for the initial implementation phase and plenty of expansion capacity for Johnson & Thornbury in terms of performance, storage, and memory. A 20/40 GB 4-mm internal tape drive will be used to back up the information on the pSeries 630 disk daily.
