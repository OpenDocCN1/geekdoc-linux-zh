# pSeries and Your Environment 283

Space. Whether raised floor construction is needed or not, one must pay for the floor space required for the systems to be installed. Customers who use rack-mounted systems should consider the “footprint” of each system being compared. This simply means to evaluate the area required for the entire system (processor drawer, I/O drawers, storage subsystems, other devices). How many systems can be installed per 19-inch rack? One, or more? Does the system require more than one rack? Using this approach, one can calculate a figure of merit system density (systems per 19-inch rack footprint).

Utilities. In the area of facilities, an increasingly important aspect of total cost of ownership is utilities cost. Computer systems can require a lot of electricity—both to power the hardware and to run the air conditioning to compensate for the heat generated by that hardware. So, when selecting among different computer systems, compare the power (in kilowatts, or kw) for each system and the air conditioning load that amount of power consumption represents. All system components (server, storage, printers, etc.) must be considered in order to make a valid comparison.

Education/Training. The people who will be using the computer system may need training. The computer operators will need to understand how to manage the day-to-day operations of the computer. The users of the computer system will have to understand the application programs. It may also be necessary for a company to train its own programmers to write custom application programs. Although pSeries systems are designed for ease of use, some training will still be necessary. Many different types of training are available, as is discussed later in the chapter.

Computer-based classes allow a user to sit at a terminal and learn about various subjects are available for free IBM Education Services. Some courses are for the users; others for the systems administrator and any programmers who may use the system. In addition, pSeries systems provide access to the IBMLink network, which allows the users and the systems administrator to electronically research technical issues, please service calls, and so on. The services provided by IBMLink are available seven days a week around the clock.

Communications-Line Costs. If the computer system will be attached to remote workstations, to other remote computers, or to the Internet (very likely), there will be communications costs. Many different communications services are available today, and these should be considered in your justification.
