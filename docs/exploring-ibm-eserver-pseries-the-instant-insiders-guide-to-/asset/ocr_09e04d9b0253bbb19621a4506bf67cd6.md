**Competitive Advantage.** The items just discussed, which contribute to reducing costs, improving quality, and improving customer service, all work to improve the effectiveness of an organization. The flexibility provided by a computer system can also help the competitiveness of a business by allowing it to respond more quickly to changing market demands. Change is inevitable, and it represents opportunity for the flexible and doom for the inflexible.

This list of general benefits is in no way complete. Each organization will be able to add to the list based on its current situation and objectives. Once benefits have been identified, however, the job is still not done. The benefits should be quantified in dollars and cents where possible to help focus on the areas with the largest payoff. However, quantifying benefits can be difficult and subject to judgment. Unlike the price of a computer, which can be looked up in a catalog, benefits must be calculated based on expected results. For example, if it is estimated inventory can be reduced by 10% by installing an inventory management application program, one would multiply 10% of inventory value the carrying costs to determine the annual benefit. Other areas that are more difficult to quantify include increased employee productivity, increased sales (resulting from improved customer service), or lower employee turnover (resulting from improved working conditions and pride). That these benefits (and others like them) are difficult to quantify doesn't make them any less valuable, but they are often overlooked.

# Lease or Buy?

After evaluating all the software and hardware alternatives and doing the cost/benefit analysis, a company must then decide on the acquisition method to be used. Several methods are commonly used to acquire a computer system. The most obvious alternative is to simply pay cash for the computer system, called an outright purchase. This is usually the least expensive way to acquire a computer system, but it has a direct impact on the organization's cash flow and capital position. The next acquisition alternative is to finance the purchase over a period of time, just as one would a new home. In this case, there is simply a down payment (of 10%, for example) and a loan for the balance.
