Along with this change, IBM's RS/6000 Division began a move from a line of specialized machines with custom-built components in fairly low volumes to a more generalized line of systems with common off-the-shelf components. There were advantages for both the user and IBM. IBM could design new systems and release them to the market more rapidly than before, and the use of common, high-volume parts, including some used in the personal computer line (power supplies, cables, disks, memory, etc.) allowed the user to continue to drop. Recent examples of implementing this open systems paradigm include Fibre Channel architecture and storage area networks (SANs), and support for applications requiring the Linux operating system. And, following the latest industry standard I/O architecture direction, recently announced pSeries servers and I/O drawers support hot-plug PCI-X adapters. To understand major differences among the various models, the following paragraphs provide a thumbnail sketch of the pSeries family and the RS/6000 models still available. Greater detail will be given in the next section.

The pSeries610 is designed for small to medium-sized businesses and is available in two models. The pSeries 610 Model 6C1 is a rack-drawer server, and the pSeries 610 Model 6E1 comes in a desktop configuration. Both models are 1 to 2-way symmetric multiprocessing (SMP) servers using 333 MHz, 375 MHz, or 450 MHz POWER-3 processors. Larger companies may select the p610 for situations where many small systems must be deployed in separate locations, as in retail of stores.

The pSeries 615, a 1- or 2-way SMP server, introduces POWER4+ technology to the lower end of the pSeries family and is intended to replace the older p610 in the future. The pSeries 615 Model 6C3 is a rack-mount server, while the pSeries 615 Model 6E3 is a desktop unit. Both are powered by 2 GHz POWER4+ processors and provide extensive scalability for an entry system—up to eight disk drives and six PCI-X slots. Like the p610, this server is designed for small to medium-sized companies and for distributed systems in large corporations.

The pSeries 620, a midrange desktop server with up to three processors running at either 600 MHz or 750 MHz and lots of expansion capability, can satisfy the total computing requirements of many small or even medium-sized companies.
