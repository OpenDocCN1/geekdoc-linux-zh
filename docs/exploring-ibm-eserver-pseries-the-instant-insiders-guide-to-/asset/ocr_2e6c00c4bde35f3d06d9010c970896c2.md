Beginning in 1991, a university student in Finland, Linus Torvalds, developed an operating system based on UNIX called Linux. The current-featured Linux Kernel Version 2.4 was released in January 2001. Although not certified as a UNIX operating system (are IBM’s AIX 5L or Sun Micros’ Solaris), Linux is a low-cost alternative operating system that is growing in popularity and has been ported to many configurations, including Intel-based PCs and some pSeries systems. Linux is developed under the GNU General Public License, and its source code is available free of charge. Several companies offer Linux distributions and associated support services.

“GNU” is just an acronym meaning “GUs' Not Unix.” The GNU Project, launched in 1984, is a voluntary organization dedicated to the sharing of free software. Not necessarily “free” in terms of price (a company may charge for distributing the software), “free” in three senses—freedom to copy the source code and give away to someone else, freedom to change the software by having full access to the source code, and freedom to distribute an improved version of the software.
