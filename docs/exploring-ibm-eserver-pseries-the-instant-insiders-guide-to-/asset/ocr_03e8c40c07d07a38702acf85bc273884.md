For these reas, the choice between pSeries and zSeries systems is not always clear and will depend heavily on the details of a situation. However, here are some general things to consider.

The zSeries spans the computational power range that overlaps with high-end pSeries models and extends to very large mainframe computing systems. To maintain a zSeries computer, a highly trained programmer, called a systems programmer, is needed, because the operating systems used by zSeries computers are both powerful and complex. Experienced personnel are needed to install and manage these operating systems. Although zSeries computers can easily be managed by systems programming personnel at a central site, the expertise must be available at some level. pSeries systems and the AIX 5L operating system, on the other hand, require a systems operator and a programming staff only if custom application software is developed and supported.

Another way of comparing a zSeries computer with a group of pSeries systems connected via a LAN brings us to the discussion of centralized versus decentralized computing. A single system being shared by a community of users is considered a centralized computer environment where the significant processing and all auxiliary storage are resident in a single computer. One advantage of the decentralized approach is easier system management because the computer operators, systems programmers, and programming staff have complete control over the computer system. Important practices such as nightly tape backup of disk storage are enforced. The decentralized approach is represented by multiple systems located throughout a building, throughout a campus, or around the world, connected via a LAN and/or telecommunications links. Advantages of this approach are physical separation and greater independence of operations at each location while retaining the ability to share data. There are good and bad points to both the centralized and decentralized approaches. zSeries systems, being larger than most pSeries models, offer less granular choices for decentralization.

Using Cluster 1600 technologies, pSeries systems can be clustered to help achieve the best of the decentralized and centralized models. Users can have separate systems for a distributed client/server environment, while management of the cluster is centralized in a single point of control, the cluster management server.
