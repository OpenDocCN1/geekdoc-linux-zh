The SCSI standards provide three alternative, incompatible, electrical bus interface configurations: single-ended, differential, and a new, lower power version of differential called low-voltage differential, or LVD.

High-performance disk units and efficient SCSI interface implementations can provide high information transfer rates between the disk units and the SCSI controller. However, the information transferred must flow between the pSeries system and the SCSI controller. Efficient transfer between the pSeries and the SCSI controller is just as important as disk seek times and SCSI bus rates. Finally, main memory in pSeries systems acts like a giant disk cache because of the implementation of single-level storage (discussed earlier in this chapter). In the spirit of the pSeries system's instruction and data cache is a temporary holding area that contains information likely to be requested by the active program. When the requested information is in the disk cache, delays associated with a traditional I/O access are eliminated, dramatically improving system performance. Single-level storage reduces the need for a disk cache on the SCSI I/O controller, reducing costs and eliminating I/O delays associated with such a disk cache. Chapter 2 will describe the optional SCSI adapters and I/O devices used with pSeries systems.

# Serial Storage Architecture Disk Storage

In 1995, IBM announced its second generation of serial disks. The architecture was dubbed simply enough Serial Storage Architecture (SSA). Serial Storage Architecture has cost and power requirements that are similar to those of SCSI, but does not require SCSI's discrete terminators or address switches. It supports up to 96 devices per adapter, whereas SCSI supports only 15, and allows 25 meters between devices on the loop. This gives much more flexibility than the 25-meter total bus length supported by SCSI. Fiber-optic extenders support device distances up to 10 km. The pSeries attaches to disk subsystems supporting SSA, such as the 7133 disk drawer or deskside.
