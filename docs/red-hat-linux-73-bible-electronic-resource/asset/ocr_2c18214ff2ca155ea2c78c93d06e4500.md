> CAUTION: After you open your server to public Internet traffic, attempts to break into your system are sure to occur. Most are likely to come from automated scripts, sent from infected computers on the Internet. Because the latest version of Red Hat Linux comes with good security features built in, many known attacks are likely to bounce off harmlessly (after logging appropriate messages). Permitting incoming connections, however, opens up your computer to vulnerabilities that aren’t there with an outgoing-only Internet connection. I strongly recommend that you use protection and monitoring tools, as I describe in Chapter 14, to protect your public servers.

# Determining Goals for your Server

Before you open up to the Internet that server sitting in your back office, stop for a moment and think about your goals. Here are a few questions that you may want to ask yourself:

+   - How critical is this server for supporting your business?
+   - How much traffic do you expect on your server?
+   - Do you need to support the server 24 hours per day, 365 days per year?
+   - Do you know enough to maintain and secure your server?
+   - Can you get the connection speed that you require at a reasonable cost for your location?
+   - Do you need special equipment to keep the server running, such as an uninterruptible power supply equipment?

What I’m getting at here is that maintaining a public server requires some resources. If the server is critical to your business and under high demand, you may not have the time or expertise to support this server yourself. Similarly, if no high-speed Internet access is available to your location, even a well-configured server may not have the bandwidth to support your clients.

# Using a hosting service

Tons of places are ready and willing to host your Web content. The number gets a bit smaller, however, if you need to find a place that can help you maintain your Red Hat Linux server. In that case, you want to find a company that offers dedicated hosting (that is, gives you control of an entire server) or permits you to co-locate your server on its premises. In either case, you want to make sure that your server:

+   - Is physically secure.
+   - Can access enough Internet bandwidth.
+   - Has someone there if the server goes down.
