Interestingly enough, the same thing happens with a Windows-based notebook from the same manufacturer: The docking station for that computer (a slightly different model) doesn’t have a functional USB port, but plugging the camera directly into the computer’s USB port works fine.

In gtkam, select Camera | Add Camera to open the Select Camera dialog (Figure 2). The Select Camera dialog gives you two options. You can select your camera from a pre-populated list, or have gtkam try to detect your camera itself.

The list that gtkam provides is extensive—several hundred models at least—so I figured I’d just grab my version if I could find it. Not surprisingly, my camera—a Canon Powershot S400—was in the list, but when I tried to manually select it, gtkam told me it couldn’t find that model physically attached to the computer. Mmmm, bummer, bummer. My heart sank, of course. However, when I asked gtkam to detect my camera, it found the physical device and identified it as a Canon Digital IXUS 400 model, as shown in **Figure 3**. Go figure.

After clicking the Apply button in the Select Camera dialog, the model shows up in the camera list, shown in **Figure 4**.

As soon as the camera is identified, gtkam will look for pictures on the camera and download the thumbnail images. Drill down through the treeview control as shown in **Figure 5** to see the thumbnails.
