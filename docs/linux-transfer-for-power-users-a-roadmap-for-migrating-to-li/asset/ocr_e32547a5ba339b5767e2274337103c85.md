If you’re searching the Web, careful consideration of keywords will help a lot. Vague, generic keywords like “Linux” and “GNOME problems” will generate huge numbers of hits. If you can provide specific text that may be unique to the situation you’re working on, you have a much better chance of finding a smaller number of hits that are directly related to the problem you’re trying to solve.

For example, suppose you’ve received a specific error, such as “device driver conflict: no dma available” in response to an operation you’re trying. If you search on “Linux device driver problem,” you’ll get oodles of hits. If you search on the exact error message—using quotes around the message text—you’re much more likely to find hits that are applicable, because they’ll have at least something to do with that exact error message. Of course, you may end up with a bunch of posts from other mailing lists or electronic forums that are all asking the same question (to which no one has received an answer), but more likely, you’ll also find the associated answer, or at least suggestions on dealing with it.

Another great site that is getting more and more traffic is the Unofficial FAQ (Frequently Asked Questions) site at http://fedora.artoo.net/faq/.

# Interactive help

A number of electronic forums and mailing lists are dedicated to Linux, including the Fedora Core project’s own mailing lists and associated archives.
