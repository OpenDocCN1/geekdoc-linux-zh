Finally, what if the program you want to use isn’t found in the Known Applications treeview? In that case, you’ll have to specify the program itself on disk, using the controls located above the Known Applications treeview in Figure 21. # Migration of data from Windows to Linux

This whole chapter has covered various topics relating to data, because, as I said, it’s your data. I haven’t addressed one topic yet—a subtle, yet important one. What about your existing data? After all, as a power user, you’re not going to fire up a Linux computer and just start fresh. You’ve got loads and loads of data already (“out there”—how do you get that data “into Linux,” for lack of a better phrase?).

The good news is that, generally, you don’t have to do a thing!

There are two levels of working with your existing Windows data on a Linux computer. The first is getting the files themselves onto or accessible by your Linux computer. The second is actually opening or working with your files once they’re accessible—that means using Linux programs to open your “Windows” data.
