The second thing you immediately notice is the summary information at the bottom of the screen: 23 items—14 files (213 MB total)—9 directories. That's one feature of PoPDesk on Windows had, that I missed in Naulus.

The next thing I want to point out is a terrifically elegant mechanism that gives Konqueror a number of different faces with just a mouse click. The icons in the column to the left of the Navigation Panel (they're called “tabs”) allow you to display different views.

Clicking on the second tab (I'll come back to the first tab in the stack shortly) displays your bookmarks in a treeview, as shown in Figure 11.

Remember how I said that Konqueror was a combination Web browser and file manager? Clicking on a bookmark in the left pane will open the Web page in the right pane. In Figure 11, I've navigated to the bookmark for the Web page for the Milwaukee Linux Users Group.

The third tab is your browser history; I didn't include a screen shot because I don't want to give away the list of all the Heather Locklear, Space Ghost and KISS shrine sites I regularly visit. Sorry.

The fourth tab in the list, the brown folder with a little house in front of it, is your home folder, as shown in Figure 10.

The fifth tab is the Sidebar Media Player, which I don't bother with, in favor of XMMS (see Chapter 10, “Multimedia: Audio and Video/). The sixth tab is the network view, and shows a variety of KDE-oriented Web and FTP sites.

The seventh tab, the red folder, displays the entire file system, starting with the “/” folder, as shown in Figure 5 earlier in this chapter. And, finally, the last tab displays the services running on your computer, as shown in Figure 12.
