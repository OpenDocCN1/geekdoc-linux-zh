Once the images are downloaded from the camera, you can choose to delete them from the camera by clicking File | Delete Photos, but I never use this function. Rather, I first disconnect the camera from the computer. Then I open up GQVView and make sure the photos are safely saved. Once I’m satisfied that I’ve downloaded my photos properly, I delete them from the camera using the camera’s own delete functionality. This may be unnecessary, but I figure it’s just too easy to make a mistake and accidentally delete photos from the camera that for some reason didn’t get downloaded successfully.

# ## Capturing screen shots

A common task for many power users is copying something you see on your computer screen so that you can manipulate it, such as placing it in a document or on a Web page. This operation is called capturing screen shots, and you can do it a couple of ways.

The easiest way is to simply use one of two key combinations to grab the contents of a single window or the entire screen.

Pressing the <PrtScn> key on your keyboard (it’s usually in a group next to the ScrLk and Pause keys) will cause an hourglass to appear briefly, and then a dialog asking what you want to do with the resulting image, as shown in Figure 12.

You can save the image (it’s currently just in the computer’s memory) to a file on disk or directly to your desktop. The default file name is, cleverly, “Screenshot”, and the default file format is PNG (Portable Network Graphics). You can navigate to another location on your hard disk if you like.

Sometimes you don’t want a “screen shot” per se; rather, you want a “window shot”—an image of just one window on your screen. Press Alt-PrtScn to capture the active window, as shown in Figure 13.
