### Summary

The mechanisms for updating Fedora Core are possibly one of its best-kept secrets. I’ve heard Windows users claim superiority despite the security holes constantly found in that operating system—because “Windows is easy to update; just click ‘Windows Update’ and that’s that!”

Fedora Core’s mechanism is actually better, both because updates require rebooting only if the kernel is modified, and because the source of updates is distributed across many servers on the Internet. If you take the time to configure up2date for your location, updating couldn’t be simpler!

## Chapter 4 Getting Help

Maybe someday in the future we’ll have honest-to-goodness self-healing computers that can fix themselves. Until then, we humans will have to get involved when problems occur. As the master of your computer, that job falls on your shoulders, and, generally, you’re not going to be able to do it yourself. Fedora Core provides several mechanisms for getting help: online help files, Web sites, and the mailing lists and associated archives. Taking advantage of these resources can be daunting for a Windows user who is not used to having a set of community resources to rely on. In this chapter, using live examples, I discuss how to use these resources when you run into a problem.

Back in the ‘60s, there were two kinds of folks who drove cars: those who could perform some minor maintenance on their own set of wheels, and those who stood by the side of the road helplessly, waiting for someone else to change a flat tire or put water in the radiator.

You’re a power user. You aren’t going to idly sit by the first time you run into trouble, saying, “Oh my! Helpless me!” You’re going to dig in and fix the damn thing yourself. But in order to do so, you need to know where to look for a helping hand.

There are two basic sources of help: static files such as online help, FAQs, Web sites and the like, and interactive forums and mailing lists.

### Static help

Software’s Achilles’ heel has long been documentation, and open-source projects are no different. Fortunately, there’s a cadre of open-source volunteers whose mission is supporting and enhancing the documentation through additional resources. There are several sources for static help files, beginning with the documentation that is installed with the product and ending with the endless multitude of sites on the Web.

### Online help

It may surprise you, but Fedora Core comes with a series of online documents for various components of the GNOME interface. Unfortunately, the help files are uneven, incomplete, and generally not comprehensively useful. Of course, it may also surprise you that Windows has a similar feature, for the little good that their help does one, either.

I hate to jam on help files, because the folks who wrote them spent a lot of time at a difficult and thankless job that is often last on anyone’s list of Important Things To Do. Still, being blunt in this respect is important, so that you don’t develop unrealistic expectations.

You can access the GNOME help by clicking the Red Hat button in the panel and selecting Help. Doing so will bring forward the main help screen, as shown in Figure 1.

## The Linux Documentation Project

The Linux Documentation Project (also known as TLDP) was started in the mid-’90s when a number of folks in the Linux community realized that the lack of good documentation was a serious hindrance to the increased acceptance of Linux.

The contributors at TLDP (well into the hundreds) have produced a huge amount of material over the years, and more is still being added on a regular basis. Visit them at http://www.tldp.org/ and be prepared to spend some time looking through the riches available. As a new power user, you may find that some of the help files are beyond you. Some authors assume a moderate level of Linux familiarity, and will say things like “then you can grep for the string in question.” Because you’re a new Linux user, that won’t be helpful now, but don’t be dismayed—you’ll be surprised at how quickly some of what seems to be Greek becomes understandable in short order.

## Fedora Core Web sites

There are a huge number of Linux-oriented Web sites. Simply doing a search via Google or another search engine will often generate more hits than you can process in a practical manner. Indeed, searching on “Linux tutorials” generated nearly 3 million hits. Because Fedora Core is a popular Linux distribution and adheres to standard Linux practices in most places, you don’t have to limit your searches to Fedora Core.

A number of popular Linux Web sites have tutorials and HOWTOS of various sorts. Here is a sampling of some sites that contain lots of additional information:

- http://www.linuxgazette.com/
- http://www.linux-tutorial.info/
- http://www.linux.org/
- http://www.linuxplanet.com/
- http://www.linuxbeginner.org/

If you’re searching the Web, careful consideration of keywords will help a lot. Vague, generic keywords like “Linux” and “GNOME problems” will generate huge numbers of hits. If you can provide specific text that may be unique to the situation you’re working on, you have a much better chance of finding a smaller number of hits that are directly related to the problem you’re trying to solve.

For example, suppose you’ve received a specific error, such as “device driver conflict: no dma available” in response to an operation you’re trying. If you search on “Linux device driver problem,” you’ll get oodles of hits. If you search on the exact error message—using quotes around the message text—you’re much more likely to find hits that are applicable, because they’ll have at least something to do with that exact error message. Of course, you may end up with a bunch of posts from other mailing lists or electronic forums that are all asking the same question (to which no one has received an answer), but more likely, you’ll also find the associated answer, or at least suggestions on dealing with it.

Another great site that is getting more and more traffic is the Unofficial FAQ (Frequently Asked Questions) site at http://fedora.artoo.net/faq/.
