As you’re starting to see, The GIMP is tremendously powerful, but its interface causes it to be one of those classic open-source tools that one either loves or hates; actually, many people love it and hate it at the same time.

Many people open The GIMP, struggle with it for a bit and give up, completely frustrated. In this section, I’ll show you how to get started with The GIMP.

# The GIMP interface

Load The GIMP by selecting Main Menu | Graphics | The GIMP. One of the two main GIMP windows (the Toolbox) appears, as shown in **Figure 20**.

The other main GIMP window (Image) appears once you have an image to work with. Select File | Open and navigate to the image you’re interested in. You’ll then see the image in the Image window, shown in **Figure 21**.

The Image window is where the action occurs—it’s where you’ll do most of your work. Figure 21 shows a screen shot of my desktop while I was writing this chapter; OpenOffice.org Writer takes up most of the left side, while The GIMP’s Toolbox window is in the lower right, and a partially obscured window of a picture in GQView is in the upper right.

Even as you’re just getting started with The GIMP, you’ll occasionally use two other windows: Color Selection and Brush Selection, as shown in **Figure 22**.

Open the Color Selection window by clicking on either of the large, colored rectangles in the lower left corner of the Toolbox window. Open the Brush Selection window by clicking the round dot in the lower right area of the Toolbox window. Clicking the colored square to the right of the dot will open the Pattern Selection window, and clicking the rectangle under the Brush Selection dot or the Patterns square will open the Gradient Selection window, shown in **Figure 23**.

The last primary piece of The GIMP’s interface is the Image window’s menu. With the Image window open, right-click anywhere in the image to display a context menu, shown in **Figure 24**.
