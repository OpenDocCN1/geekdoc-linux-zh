As with Python, Ruby, Lisp, and all of the other languages that give you an interactive shell to play with, you can try out snippets of Bash programs interactively in the shell, before you put them in a script. Indeed, scripts often grow from just one short chain of commands, with features added until the original short script is buried in other detail.

However, if you have a clear idea for a longer program, then it's well worth planning in advance, laying out the design with comments, then adding in code snippets – testing as you go – until you have a functioning piece of code. Do this, and even if you take out the comments, your script should be logical and easy to read.

Before we get on to planning, we're going to look at some of those practical bits, in the form of making your scripts interactive, taking in more flow control logic along the way. We'll introduce some simple ways of producing menu choice – which will work across distros – and some validation of input, to protect your script from error-prone or malicious users.

Finally, we fabluse Bash's weak dynamic typing to help lay an Easter Egg.
