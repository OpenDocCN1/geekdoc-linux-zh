## What is OpenELEC?

The media centre operating system available on Raspberry Pi. What makes it so special?

**What is OpenELEC, then?**
OpenELEC is an operating system, although it's based on Linux, so it's technically a Linux distribution that can be used in HTPCs and home theatres.

**What is a HTPC?**
It stands for home theatre PC, otherwise known as a media PC. For use in home theatres as we said, or a lounge for basically anyone else.

**Right, what do they do then?**
Well, the easiest answer is play media on your TV. Media such as music or video or what have you. More specifically, they usually present you with a nice interface to browse your available media on, either stored locally on a hard drive, on USB storage or over the network on another computer. It then plays that media with all the features of a full PC video player, such as VLC.

**What's the benefit of doing this?**
Well, you can then watch this stuff on your TV rather than at your computer. Some people find it more comfortable and it can be a bit more social.

**Oh, that makes sense. What exactly is the significance of OpenELEC in all of this then?**
OpenELEC is one of the premier ways of getting the Kodi software onto a device, such as a computer connected to your TV.

**What is Kodi?**
The new name for XBMC, which used to be the Xbox Media Centre a lifetime ago when hacking an original Xbox was a thing that people did. It's HTPC software and very popular at that, with an easy-to-use interface and a database used by some other popular HTPC software.

**Can I get Kodi anywhere else?**
Yep, it's available on a lot of Linux distros and other operating systems, and there are a handful of dedicated ones that run Kodi for HTPCs.

**Okay, so why is OpenELEC important?**
OpenELEC has ties to the Kodi development team – in fact some of the Kodi devs also work on OpenELEC. It also supports a huge range of different devices, including your average PC towers and laptops, to Apple TVs and other TV hardware along with many other things too.

### The Raspberry Pi has become extremely popular as a HTPC due to its size

**I'm guessing that the Raspberry Pi is included in this as well then?**
Absolutely, in fact they make a big deal out of whenever an OpenELEC release does something cool on the Raspberry Pi – the Raspberry Pi has become extremely popular as a HTPC due to its size and power consumption. You can even attach it to the back of some TVs.

**How can I get it for Pi?**
It's available standalone or on the N00BS installer for Pi. For a full breakdown how to get it and how to use it properly, you can check our tutorial on pages 66 and 67 of this magazine.

## Tabbed terminal emulators

If you've never looked beyond Gnome-terminal or Konsole, it's time to find out what you've been missing

### Sakura

A lightweight terminal emulator based on VTE (as used by GNOME and Xfce's terminals) and the Gtk libraries, with enough features to make it useful including tabs, contextual menus and some colour options. However, it has a more modern feel to it than Mxvxt and has full support for UTF-8.

Download: bit.ly/1AFT3KT

### Mxvxt

Mxvxt is a tabbed version of the light, flexible rxvt. It's been years since the last release, but as many people still use mxvxt as their daily terminal emulator, we'll look at it as a mature and stable product rather than a neglected one. As the lightest weight choice, it still offers plenty of useful features and configurability.

Download: bit.ly/1KWtZTB

### Terminator

Not to be confused with GNOME Terminator. Phil Norman wrote this in around a month during 2004. Terminator had had some advanced features added since then and it is now truly cross-platform. It's popular in some quarters, despite the common confusion with GNOME's same-name project.

Download: bit.ly/1FaWfNn

### Roxterm

Roxterm is written for the ROX Desktop environment, originally as a lower resource-using clone of Gnome-terminal, but the feature count has gradually crept up over time. Despite this it is still clean, quick, quite user-friendly and integrates well with GNOME, which is an advantage.

Download: bit.ly/1D5ymHs
