# Why the static?

What's the difference, right? Well, an *Internet Protocol address* (IP number) is a string of numbers that identifies a given computer on the Internet. Because most ISPs have many more customers than they have IP numbers to give out, the numbers are assigned *dynamically*. That is, when you log on, your computer gets the next available number; when you log off, someone else gets it. A *static IP number* means that you

have a specific string of numbers that identifies your computer all the time, whether or not you're logged on. This isn't necessary for most users, who do just fine with a *dynamic IP*. If you think you need a static IP, look into getting a cable modem or more advanced connection services like DSL or ISDN, which usually assign static IP numbers because the connection is always on.
