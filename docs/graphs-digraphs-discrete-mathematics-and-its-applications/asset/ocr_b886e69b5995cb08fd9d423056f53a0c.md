# Corollary 18.6 No graph with a bridge has a nowhere-zero k-flow for any integer $k \geq 2$.

The nowhere-zero $k$-flows of bridgeless planar graphs will be of particular interest to us because of their connection with region colorings of these graphs. Suppose that $D$ is an orientation of a bridgeless plane graph $G$ possessing a $k$-region coloring $c$ for some integer $k \geq 2$. Thus, for each region $R$ of $G$, we may assume that the color $c(R)$ is one of the colors $1,2, \cdots, k$. If $w$ is an edge of $G$ belonging to the boundaries of two regions $R_{1}$ and $R_{2}$ of $G$ such that $e=(u, v)$ is an arc of $D$, then define
$$\phi(e)=c\left(R_{1}\right)-c\left(R_{2}\right)$$
where $R_{1}$ is the region that lies to the right of $e$ and $R_{2}$ is the region that lies to the left of $e$ as indicated in Figure 18.3.

For bridgeless plane graphs, $k$-region colorability and the existence of nowhere-zero $k$-flows for an integer $k \geq 2$ are equivalent.
