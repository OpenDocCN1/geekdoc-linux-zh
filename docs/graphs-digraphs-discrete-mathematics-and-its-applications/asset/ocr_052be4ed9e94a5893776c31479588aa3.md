Next, we show that no triangle can contain exactly two vertices of degree 7 in H. Suppose the triangle T_1, say, contains exactly two such vertices, namely u and v. Then u is the initial vertex of six paths to six vertices outside of T_1. Necessarily, these six paths must contain all six vertices in T_2 and T_5. However, this is true of the vertex v as well. This implies that the six vertices of T_2 and T_3 are the remaining vertices of degree 7 in H. Therefore, there are two vertices of v_2 and v_5 in T_5 and two vertices u_2 and v_2 in T_2 such that the interior vertices of the u_5 - v_2 path, v_5 - v_2 path, v_5 - u_2 path and v_5 - v_2 path contain only vertices of T_2 and T_3. Since these four paths must be internally disjoint and since T_3 (and T_4) contains only three vertices, this is impossible.

Therefore, no triangle T_i (1 ≤ i ≤ 5) can contain exactly one or exactly two vertices of degree 7 in H. Thus, no triangle T_i (1 ≤ i ≤ 5) can contain exactly three vertices of degree 7 in H either. Thus, as claimed, G does not contain a subdivision of K_8. Hence, the graph G is a counterexample to Hajós' Conjecture for k = 8.

Let k be an integer such that k ≥ 9 and consider the graph F = G \vee K_{k-8}. Then \chi(F) = k. We claim that F does not contain a subdivision of K_k, for suppose that H is such a subgraph in F. Delete all vertices of K_{k-8} that belong to H, arriving at a subgraph H' of G. This says that H' contains a subdivision of K_8, which is impossible. Hence, Hajós' Conjecture is false for all integer k ≥ 8. As we noted, Catlin showed that this conjecture is false for every integer k ≥ 7 (see Exercise 9).

Recall that a graph G is perfect if \chi(H) = \omega(H) for every induced subgraph H of G. Furthermore, for a graph G and a vertex v of G, the replication graph R_v(G) of G is that graph obtained from G by adding a new vertex v' to G and joining v' to every vertex in the closed neighbourhood N[v] of v. We saw in Theorem 15.9 that if G is perfect, then R_v(G) is perfect for every v \in V(G). Carsten Thomassen [235] showed that there is a connection between perfect graphs and Hajós' Conjecture.
