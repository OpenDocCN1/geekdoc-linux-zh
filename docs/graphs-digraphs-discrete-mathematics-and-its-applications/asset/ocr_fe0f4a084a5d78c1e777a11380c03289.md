So, if R were surrounded by four or fewer neighboring regions, Kempe's proof involved using chains of regions whose colors alternate between two colors and then interchanging these colors, if appropriate, to arrive at a coloring of the regions of M (minus R) with four colors so that the neighboring regions of R used at most three of these colors and thereby left a color available for R. In fact, these chains of regions became known as Kempe chains.
