# **Kuratowski's Theorem**

A graph $H$ is a **subdivision** of a graph $G$ if either $H \cong G$ or $H$ can be obtained from $G$ by inserting vertices of degree 2 into some, all or none of the edges of $G$. Thus, for the graph $G$ of Figure 10.9, all of the graphs $H_1$, $H_2$ and $H_3$ are subdivisions of $G$. Indeed, $H_3$ is also a subdivision of $H_2$.

Certainly, a subdivision $H$ of a graph $G$ is planar if and only if $G$ is planar. Therefore, $K_5$ and $K_{3,3}$ are nonplanar as any subdivision of $K_5$ or $K_{3,3}$. This provides a necessary condition for a graph to be planar.

**Theorem 10.17** *A graph $G$ is planar only if $G$ contains no subgraph that is a subdivision of $K_5$ or $K_{3,3}$.*
