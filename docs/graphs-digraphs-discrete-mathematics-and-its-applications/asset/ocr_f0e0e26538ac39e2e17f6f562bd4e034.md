If $s : d_{1}, d_{2}, \ldots, d_{n}$ is the degree sequence of a tree of order $n \geq 2$, then, necessarily, $\sum_{i=1}^{n} d_{i} = 2n - 2$. Here too, more can be said.

# **Theorem 3.15** *A sequence $s : d_{1}, d_{2}, \ldots, d_{n}$ of $n \geq 2$ positive integers is the degree sequence of a tree of order $n$ if and only if*

$$\sum_{i=1}^{n} d_{i} = 2n - 2.$$

# **Proof.**

First, let $T$ be a tree of order $n$ and size $m$ with degree sequence $s : d_{1}, d_{2}, \ldots, d_{n}$. Then, as observed,

$$\sum_{i=1}^{n} d_{i} = 2m = 2(n - 1) = 2n - 2.$$

We verify the converse by induction on $n$. When $n = 2$, the only sequence of two positive integers whose sum is $2n - 2 = 2$ is 1,1, which is the degree sequence of the tree $K_{2}$.\

Assume for a given integer $n \geq 3$ that any sequence of $n - 1$ positive integers whose sum is $2(n - 2) = 2n - 4$ is the degree sequence of a tree of order $n - 1$. Now let $d_{1}, d_{2}, \ldots, d_{n}$ be a sequence of $n$ positive integers whose sum is $2n - 2$. We show that this is the degree sequence of a tree of order $n$. Suppose that $d_{1} \geq d_{2} \geq \cdots \geq d_{n}$. Since each term is positive and $\sum_{i=1}^{n} d_{i} = 2n - 2$, it follows that at least two terms in the sequence must be 1, and so $d_{n-1} = d_{n} = 1$. Furthermore, $2 \leq d_{1} \leq n - 1$. Hence, $d_{1} - 1, d_{2}, \ldots, d_{n-1}$ is a sequence of $n - 1$ positive integers whose sum is $2(n - 1) - 2 = 2n - 4$. By the induction hypothesis, there is a tree $T'$ of order $n - 1$ with $V(T') = \{v_{1}, v_{2}, \ldots, v_{n-1}\}$ such that $\text{deg}_{T'}\ v_{1} = d_{1} - 1$ and $\text{deg}_{T'}\ v_{i} = d_{i}$ for $2 \leq i \leq n - 1$. Let $T$ be the tree of order $n$ obtained from $T'$ by adding a new vertex $v_{n}$ and joining it to $v_{1}$. The tree $T$ then has the degree sequence $d_{1}, d_{2}, \ldots, d_{n}$.
