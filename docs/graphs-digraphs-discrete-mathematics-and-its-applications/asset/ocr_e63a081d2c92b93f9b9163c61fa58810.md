For a given even integer n ≥ 4, assume that all graphs H of even order less than n and satisfying k₀(H − S) ≤ |S| for every proper subset S of V(H) contain a 1-factor. Now, let G be a graph of order n satisfying k₀(G − S) ≤ |S| for every proper subset S of V(G). As we saw above, every component of G has even order. We show that G has a 1-factor.

For a vertex v of G that is not a cut-vertex and R = {v}, it follows that k₀(G − R) = |R| = 1. Hence, there are nonempty proper subsets T of V(G) for which k₀(G − T) = |T|. Among all such sets T, let S be one of maximum cardinality. Suppose that k₀(G − S) = |S| = k ≥ 1 and let G₁, G₂, . . . , Gₖ be the odd components of G − S.

We claim that k(G − S) = k, that is, G₁, G₂, . . . , Gₖ are the only components of G − S. Assume, to the contrary, that G − S has an even component G₀. Let v₀ be a vertex of G₀ that is not a cut-vertex of G₀. Let S₀ = S ∪ {v₀}. Since G₀ has even order,

k₀(G − S₀) ≥ k₀(G − S) + 1 = k + 1.

On the other hand, k₀(G − S₀) ≤ |S₀| = k + 1. Therefore,

k₀(G − S₀) = |S₀| = k + 1,

which is impossible. Thus, as claimed, G₁, G₂, . . . , Gₖ are the only components of G − S.

For each integer i (1 ≤ i ≤ k), let Sᵢ denote the set of those vertices in S adjacent to at least one vertex of Gᵢ. Since G has only even components, each set Sᵢ is nonempty.

We claim, for each integer ℓ with 1 ≤ ℓ ≤ k, that the union of any ℓ of the sets S₁, S₂, . . . , Sₖ contains at least ℓ vertices. Assume, to the contrary, that this is not the case. Then there is an integer j such that the union S′ of j of the sets S₁, S₂, . . . , Sₖ has fewer than j elements. Suppose that S₁, S₂, . . . , Sⱼ have this property. Thus,

S′ = S₁ ∪ S₂ ∪ . . . ∪ Sⱼ and |S′| < j.

Then G₁, G₂, . . . , Gⱼ are at least some of the components of G − S′ and so

k₀(G − S′) ≥ j > |S′|,

which contradicts the hypothesis. Thus, as claimed, for each integer ℓ with 1 ≤ ℓ ≤ k, the union of any ℓ of the sets S₁, S₂, . . . , Sₖ contains at least ℓ vertices.
