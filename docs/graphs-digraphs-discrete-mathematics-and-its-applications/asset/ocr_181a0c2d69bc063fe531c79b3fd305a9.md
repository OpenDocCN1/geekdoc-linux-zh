# Crossing Numbers of Complete Bipartite Graphs

Kazimierz Zarankiewicz [262] thought he had proved that

$$\operatorname{cr}(K_{s,t}) = \begin{bmatrix} s & \\ \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} \frac{s-1}{2} & \\ \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} \frac{t}{2} & \\ \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} \frac{t-1}{2} & \\ \end{bmatrix} \tag{11.4}$$

but, in actuality, he had only verified that the right-hand expression of (11.4) is an upper bound for $\operatorname{cr}(K_{s,t})$. As it turned out, both Paul C. Kainen and Gerhard Ringel found flaws in Zarankiewicz’s argument. Hence, (11.4) remains only a conjecture. The best general result on crossing numbers of complete bipartite graphs is the following, due to the combined work of Daniel Kleitman [144] and Douglas Woodall [260].

**Theorem 11.4** *If* s *and* t *are positive integers with* s ≤ t *and either* (1) s ≤ 6 *or* (2) s = 7 *and* t ≤ 10, *then*

$$\operatorname{cr}(K_{s,t}) = \begin{bmatrix} s & \\ \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} \frac{s-1}{2} & \\ \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} \frac{t}{2} & \\ \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} \frac{t-1}{2} & \\ \end{bmatrix} \text{.}$$

It follows, therefore, from Theorem 11.4 that

$$\operatorname{cr}(K_{3,t}) = \begin{bmatrix} t & \\ \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} \frac{t-1}{2} & \\ \end{bmatrix} \text{,} \quad \quad \operatorname{cr}(K_{4,t}) = 2 \begin{bmatrix} \frac{t}{2} & \\ \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} \frac{t-1}{2} & \\ \end{bmatrix} \text{,}$$

$$\operatorname{cr}(K_{5,t}) = 4 \begin{bmatrix} \frac{t}{2} & \\ \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} \frac{t-1}{2} & \\ \end{bmatrix} \text{ and } \operatorname{cr}(K_{6,t}) = 6 \begin{bmatrix} \frac{t}{2} & \\ \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} \frac{t-1}{2} & \\ \end{bmatrix}$$

for all t. For example, cr(K_{3,3}) = 1, cr(K_{4,4}) = 4, cr(K_{5,5}) = 16, cr(K_{6,6}) = 36 and cr(K_{7,7}) = 81. A drawing of K_{4,4} with four crossings is shown in Figure 11.3.
