Two other classes of graphs that are often encountered are the paths and cycles. For an integer n ≥ 1, the path Pn is a graph of order n and size n −1 whose vertices can be labeled by v1, v2, . . . , vn and whose edges are viv i+1 for i = 1, 2, . . . , n − 1. For an integer n ≥ 3, the cycle Cn is a graph of order n and size n whose vertices can be labeled by v1, v2, . . . , vn and whose edges are v1vn and vivi+1 for i = 1, 2, . . . , n − 1. The cycle Cn is also referred to as an n-cycle and the 3-cycle is also called a triangle. The paths and cycles of order 5 or less are shown in Figure 1.6. Observe that P1 = K1, P2 = K2 and C3 = K3.

# 1.2 The Degree of a Vertex

The degree of a vertex v in a graph G is the number of vertices in G that are adjacent to v. Thus, the degree of v is the number of vertices in its neighborhood N(v). Equivalently, the degree of v is the number of edges incident with v. The degree of a vertex v is denoted by degG v or, more simply, by deg v if the graph G under discussion is clear. Hence, deg v = |N(v)|. A vertex of degree 0 is referred to as an isolated vertex and a vertex of degree 1 is an end-vertex or a leaf. An edge incident with an end-vertex is called a pendant edge. The largest degree among the vertices of G is called the maximum degree of G and is denoted by Δ(G). The minimum degree of G is denoted by δ(G). (The symbols Δ and δ are the upper case and lower case Greek letter delta, respectively.) Thus, if v is a vertex of a graph G of order n, then

0 ≤ δ(G) ≤ deg v ≤ Δ(G) ≤ n − 1.
