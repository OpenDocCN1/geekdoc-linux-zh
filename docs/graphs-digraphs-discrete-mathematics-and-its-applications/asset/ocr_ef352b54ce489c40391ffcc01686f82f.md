If $D$ is an orientation of a graph $G$ with a flow $\phi$ and $a$ is an integer, then $a\phi$ is also a flow on $D$. In fact, if $\phi$ is a $k$-flow and $a \geq 1$, then $a\phi$ is an $ak$-flow. Indeed, if $\phi$ is a nowhere-zero $k$-flow, then $a\phi$ is a nowhere-zero $ak$-flow. More generally, we have the following (see Exercise 3).

Let $G$ be a graph with a nowhere-zero $k$-flow for some integer $k \geq 2$ and let $\phi$ be a nowhere-zero $k$-flow defined on some orientation $D$ of $G$. Then $\sigma^+(v_i;\phi) = \sigma^+(v_i;\phi)$ for every vertex $v$ of $D$. Let $S = \{v_1, v_2, \cdots, v_t\}$ be a proper subset of $V(G)$ and let $T = V(G) - S$. Define
$$\sigma^+(S;\phi) = \sum_{(u,v) \in [S,T]} \phi(u,v)$$
and
$$\sigma^-(S;\phi) = \sum_{(v,u) \in [T,S]} \phi(v,u).$$
Since $\phi$ is a flow on $D$,
$$\sum_{i=1}^{t}\sigma^+(v_i;\phi) = \sum_{i=1}^{t}\sigma^-(v_i;\phi).$$
For every arc $(v_a,v_b)$ with $v_a,v_b \in S$, the flow value $\phi(v_a,v_b)$ occurs in both the left and the right sums in (18.2).
