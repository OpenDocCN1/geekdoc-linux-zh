By Markov\u2019s inequality in (21.8),

$P[X \geq n/2] \leq \frac{E[X]}{n/2}.$

Thus, for $n$ sufficiently large, $P[X \geq n/2] < 1/2$.

Let $t = \lceil 3(\ln n)/p \rceil$. The probability that a given $t$-element subset of $V(G) = \{v_1,v_2,\ldots,v_n\}$ is independent is $(1-p)^{\binom{t}{2}}$. Since there are $\binom{n}{t}$ such sets, it follows that

$P[\alpha(G) \geq t] \leq \binom{n}{t} (1-p)^{\binom{t}{2}}.$

However, $1-p < e^{-p}$ (by 21.1) and so

$P[\alpha(G) \geq t] \leq \binom{n}{t} e^{-p\binom{t}{2}} < \left(ne^{-p(t-1)/2}\right)^t.$

Since

$t = \left\lceil \frac{3\ln n}{p} \right\rceil = \left\lceil \frac{3\ln n}{n^{\Theta-1}} \right\rceil = \lceil 3(\ln n) n^{1-\Theta} \rceil$

and $1-\Theta > 0$, it follows that $\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty} t = \infty$. Therefore, $\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty} \left(\frac{1}{e}\right)^{p(t-1)/2} = 0$. Consequently, by a generalization of (21.2), we have $\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty} n e^{-p(t-1)/2} = 0$ and it follows that we can choose $n$ such that $P[\alpha(G) \geq t] < \frac{1}{2}$ and $P[X \geq n/2] < \frac{1}{2}$. Thus, there is a graph $G \in S$ with fewer than $n/2$ cycles of length at most $\ell$ and $\alpha(G) < t$. Since

$t = \lceil 3(\ln n)/p \rceil = \lceil 3(\ln n) n^{1-\Theta} \rceil$

and $\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty} \frac{\ln n}{n^\Theta} = 0$ for a positive real number $\alpha$ (see (21.3)), we may assume that $n$ is sufficiently large to ensure that $t < \frac{n}{2k}$.

Remove one vertex from each cycle of $G$ of length at most $\ell$ and denote the resulting graph by $G_1$. Then $G_1$ has girth greater than $\ell$, that is, $g(G_1) > \ell$. Furthermore,

$\alpha(G_1) \leq \alpha(G) < \frac{n}{2k}.$

Moreover, by Theorem 14.9,

$\chi(G_1) \geq \frac{|V(G_1)|}{\alpha(G_1)} \geq \frac{n/2}{n/(2k)} = k.$

Finally, we remove vertices from $G_1$, if necessary, to produce a graph $G_2$ with $g(G_2) > \ell$ and $\chi(G_2) = k$. $\blacksquare$
