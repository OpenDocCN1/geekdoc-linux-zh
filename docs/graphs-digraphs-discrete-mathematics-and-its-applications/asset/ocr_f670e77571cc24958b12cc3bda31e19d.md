Therefore, $$\sum_{i=3}^{n} i r_{i} = n + 2c = n + 2 \sum_{i=3}^{n} r_{i} - 2$$

and so
$$\sum_{i=3}^{n}(i - 2)r_{i} = n - 2.$$

Similarly,
$$\sum_{i=3}^{n}(i - 2)r_{i}' = n - 2.$$

Therefore, $$\sum_{i=3}^{n}(i - 2)(r_{i} - r_{i}') = 0. \tag*{$\blacksquare$}$$

The following observations are quite useful in applying Theorem 10.27. Let *G* be a plane graph with Hamiltonian cycle *C*. Furthermore, suppose that an edge *e* of *G* is on the boundary of two regions *R*$_{1}$ and *R*$_{2}$ of *G*. If *e* is an edge of *C*, then one of *R*$_{1}$ and *R*$_{2}$ is in the interior of *C* and the other is in the exterior of *C*. If, on the other hand, *e* is not an edge of *C*, then *R*$_{1}$ and *R*$_{2}$ are either both in the interior of *C* or both in the exterior of *C*.
