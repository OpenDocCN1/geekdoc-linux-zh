# Theorem 3.13  If $T$ is a tree of order $n$ and size $m$, then $m = n - 1$.

Proof.  We proceed by induction on the order of a tree. There is only one tree of order 1, namely $K_1$, and it has no edges. Thus, the basis step of the induction is established. Assume that the size of every tree of order $n - 1 \ge 1$ is $n - 2$ and let $T$ be a tree of order $n$ and size $m$. By Corollary 3.12, $T$ has at least two leaves. Let $v$ be one of them. As we observed, $T - v$ is a tree of order $n - 1$. By the induction hypothesis, the size of $T - v$ is $n - 2$. Thus, the size of $T$ is $m = (n - 2) + 1 = n - 1.$

Suppose that a tree $T$ of order $n \ge 3$, size $m$ and maximum degree $\Delta(T) = \Delta$ has $n_i$ vertices of degree $i$ ($1 \le i \le \Delta$). Then

```
$$ \sum_{v \in V(T)} \text{deg} \, v = \sum_{i=1}^{\Delta} i n_i = 2m = 2n - 2 = 2 \sum_{i=1}^{\Delta} n_i - 2. $$

```
