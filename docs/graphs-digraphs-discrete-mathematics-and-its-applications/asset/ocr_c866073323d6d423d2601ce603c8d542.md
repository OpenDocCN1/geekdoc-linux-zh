The fact that the hypothesis of Theorem 13.16 requires the graph in question to be an Eulerian complete graph is critical to its conclusion. For example, the noncomplete Eulerian graph $G$ in Figure 13.12 has size $15 = \binom{6}{2}$ but the sizes of cycles in cycle decompositions of $G$ cannot be specified. In fact, there are only two cycle decompositions of $G$, namely $\{C_5, C_{10}\}$ and $\{C_3, C_3, C_3, C_3, C_3\}$.

The mathematicians who are responsible for Theorems 13.15 and 13.16 also obtained corresponding theorems for complete graphs of even order (see [6, 213, 39]), resulting in the following.

> Theorem 13.17 For every $t$ integers $k_1, k_2, \dots, k_t$ and even integer $n \geq 4$ such that $3 \leq k_i \leq n$ for each $i$ $(1 \leq i \leq t)$ and $\sum_{i=1}^t k_i = \binom{n}{2} - \frac{n}{2} = \frac{n^2-2n}{2}$, the Complete graph $K_n$ can be decomposed into a 1-factor and the cycles $C_{k_1}, C_{k_2}, \dots, C_{k_t}$.
