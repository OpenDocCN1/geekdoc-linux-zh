# Vertex-Transitive Graphs

A graph that contains a single orbit is called **vertex-transitive**. Thus, a graph $G$ is vertex-transitive if and only if for every two vertices $u$ and $v$ of $G$, there exists an automorphism $\phi$ of $G$ such that $\phi(u) = v$. Necessarily then, every vertex-transitive graph is regular. The graphs $K_n$ $(n \geq 1)$, $C_n$ $(n \geq 3)$ and $K_{r,r}$ $(r \geq 1)$ are all vertex-transitive. Also, the regular graphs $G_1 = C_5 \Box K_2$ and $G_2 = K_{2,2,2}$, shown in Figure 9.7, are vertex-transitive. The regular graphs $G_3$ and $G_4$ in Figure 9.7 are not vertex-transitive, however (see Exercise 11).
