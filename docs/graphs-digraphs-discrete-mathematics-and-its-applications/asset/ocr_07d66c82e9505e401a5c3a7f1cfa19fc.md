If $T$ is a Hamiltonian tournament, then, of course, every vertex of $T$ lies on every Hamiltonian cycle of $T$. Actually, every vertex of $T$ lies on a triangle of $T$ as well.

**Theorem 7.27** *Every vertex in a nontrivial strong tournament belongs to a triangle.*

**Proof.** Let $v$ be a vertex in a nontrivial strong tournament $T$. By Theorem 7.26, $T$ is Hamiltonian. Thus, $T$ contains a Hamiltonian cycle $\langle v = v_1, v_2, \ldots, v_n, v_1 \rangle$. Since $v$ is adjacent to $v_2$ and adjacent from $v_n$, there is a vertex $v_i$ with $2 \leq i < n$ such that $\langle v, v_i \rangle$ and $\langle v_{i+1}, v \rangle$ are arcs of $T$. Thus, $\langle v, v_i, v_{i+1}, v \rangle$ is a triangle of $T$ containing $v$.
