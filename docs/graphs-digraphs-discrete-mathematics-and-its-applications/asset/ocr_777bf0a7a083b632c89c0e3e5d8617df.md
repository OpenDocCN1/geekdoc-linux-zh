# **Theorem 15.6** Let $G$ be a graph obtained by identifying two complete sub-graphs of the same order in two graphs $G_1$ and $G_2$. Then $G$ is chordal if and only if $G_1$ and $G_2$ are chordal.

**Proof.** We have already noted that if $G_1$ and $G_2$ are two chordal graphs containing complete subgraphs $H_1$ and $H_2$, respectively, of the same order, then the graph $G$ obtained by identifying the vertices of $H_1$ with the vertices of $H_2$ is also chordal. On the other hand, if $G_1$, say, were not chordal, then it would contain a cycle $C$ of length 4 or more having no chords. However then, $C$ would also be a cycle in $G$ having no chords.

We have now observed that every graph obtained by identifying two complete subgraphs of the same order in two chordal graphs is also chordal. These are not only sufficient conditions for a graph to be chordal, they are necessary conditions as well. The following characterization of chordal graphs is due to András Hajnal and János Súranyí [115] and Gabriel Dirac [73].
