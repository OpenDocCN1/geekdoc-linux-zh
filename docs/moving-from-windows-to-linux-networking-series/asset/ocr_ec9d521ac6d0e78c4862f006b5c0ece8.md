Inserting a chart, which you can do in Microsoft Excel, is a very common task in any spreadsheet tools. A spreadsheet is an excellent way to organize data and perform calculations. However, most people respond best to visual cues such as charts. If you select Insert and then choose Chart, you will see a screen, shown in **Figure 9.16**, that prompts you to choose what cells to include in your chart. You can also select which rows and columns you want to use as labels for your chart.

![](bbox=[0.05, 0.13, 0.65, 0.34])

**Figure 9.16:** The Chart Wizard step one.

The next step, shown in **Figure 9.17**, enables you to select the various settings for your chart. You can select from bar charts, pie charts, line graphs, and 3D charts. There are a number of options at your disposal. You also can choose whether to display your data in rows or columns. For our purposes we will choose columns and a bar chart, then press Next.

![](bbox=[0.05, 0.45, 0.65, 0.68])

**Figure 9.17:** The Chart Wizard step two.

The next screen enables you to choose whether you want horizontal (X) and/or vertical (Y) grid lines. You then move to the next screen, shown in **Figure 9.18**, where you can enter a title for your chart and titles for the X and Y axes.
