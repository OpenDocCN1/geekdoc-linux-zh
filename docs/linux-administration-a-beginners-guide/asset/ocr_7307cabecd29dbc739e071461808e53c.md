# The NT Boot Process

![](bbox=[0.05, 0.22, 0.95, 0.7])

The NT and Linux boot processes do strike parallels between each other. If you understand the reasoning behind one, you can understand the reasoning behind the other: They both start by reading the MBR, and they both end by providing login: prompts for the user. Noteworthy points of similarity are the NTLDR and LIL O. They look different and act different, but in the end they do the exact same thing. NTDETECT, NTKSRNL, and HAL are three components that are all done in the Linux kernel. Although SMSS and init take very different paths, their goal is one and the same: getting the necessary service started.
