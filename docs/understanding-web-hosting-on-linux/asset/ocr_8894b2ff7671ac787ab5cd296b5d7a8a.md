Today, it's not at all unusual to send pictures and other files that have ASCII values that are in the upper 128 characters. These binary files wouldn't be able to pass normally through the email system without MIME. Simply put, MIME maps the nonroutable characters in binary files into roughly two-byte strings that are routable. These pass flawlessly through email systems.

Most clients take care of the conversion for you automatically. If you attach a picture to one of your emails, the client will convert it, and the recipient's email client will convert it back. If you look at the raw data in your file, you'll see how this is done, assuming that you've ever received a MIME-encoded attachment (which you probably have).
