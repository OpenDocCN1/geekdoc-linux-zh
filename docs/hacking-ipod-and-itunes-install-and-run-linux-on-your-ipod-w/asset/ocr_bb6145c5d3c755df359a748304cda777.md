# Change the play count

Most properties of songs, such as the name, artist, and album, are set and rarely change. Others, like play count and last played date, are more dynamic. Play count increases by one every time a song is played, either in iTunes or on an iPod.

In iTunes 4.1, Apple added a feature that was happily welcomed by scripters: you could now change the play count property (officially known as "played count") from a script. So, if you want to fake the number of times a song has been played, you can do it with AppleScript. This can be useful for artificially increasing or decreasing the apparent popularity of a song in your library, which can affect how it appears in smart playlists.

Two scripts on Doug's AppleScripts for iTunes site are useful for fooling around with play count. New Play Count lets you enter a new value for the play count. Add or Subtract Play Count changes a track's play count by a specified value.
