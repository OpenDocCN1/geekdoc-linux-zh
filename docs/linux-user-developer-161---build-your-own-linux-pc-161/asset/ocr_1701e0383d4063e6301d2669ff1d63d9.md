# Review Audio editors

# In brief: compare and contrast our verdicts

# Audacity

Capturing sound

The only editor that captures PulseAudio sound from home-made without errors

Effects and post-processing

Lots of built-in effects and plugins. Extras can be manually added

Performance

Sleeds down on heavy files, but for everyday use

Multi-track and mastering

Fine-control options are brilliant for multi-track recording tasks

Overall

The most balanced and featured audio editor. Highly recommended

# Rezound

Some issues with creaking code. Works okay after tweaking PulseAudio configs

Basic set of effects. Very dated—like Nineties software

Very snappy to catch errors, but with large files, the interface tends to crash

Can switch between tracks with good tools, but cannot play multiple tracks

Very good after you but not stable or enough for us

# KWave

Works flawlessly with ALSA and decency with PulseAudio. Great capturing interface

Modest set of built-in effects, though for light lights on nature recordings

Sane defaults but lacks memory swapping.

Cannot save projects easily. Not suitable for mastering records

Decent record but a simple editor. Stable for general use, though

# Sweep

Poor and half-working audio capturing. Hasn't been updated in years so needs a workaround

Not focused on professional recording at all—too lightweight for advanced use

Fast as a genius! Impressive and responsive make up for others

Like Audacity, but not decentering. No multi-track feature but a decent playground

Great for recording through long files keeping the simplest of edits

# AND THE WINNER IS...

# Audacity

If we used a time machine and travel back de, the results could be quite different: Audacity was not mature at the time, while others of this test group were actively maintained and enjoyed bug fixes. But of 2016, only Audacity and KWave were alive and keeping the flame. Audacity is clearly the best choice and the only option for anyone who wants to do some creative work with multiple audio tracks. You can add music background to your podcast or audiobook, also add a section to outro revision, adjust voice and master all tracks into one during starting and lagging, Audacity runs out of system resource and lagging, but it works to only now

KWave performed really well and appreciated its clear straightforward interface. We can give a voice figure how to use the right input device and then play with the various settings. KWave is a simple recording tool, though without tools for mixing or mastering a composition, but it works right and is stable.
