# INTERVIEW VIKTOR PETTERSSON

## Digital signage with Screenly

![](img/a54b3b473771472dd2321ddda1014be1_11_0.png)

The disruptive power of the Raspberry Pi gave Viktor Peterson the opportunity to launch a low-cost solution to the problem of effectively driving multiple screens.

> **Why did you create Screenly?**
> It was around New Year's 2012. I stumbled into a position where we basically had to create a digital signage solution for a company that was acquired. Just before June, when the deal closed, and the first of January, roughly, we ended up having a very rough-around-the-edges kind of software. So that was really the very first version of Screenly to be, the POC [proof of concept] of which ended up being a very rough-around-the-edges kind of tools built in Python, bash, and alike of boxed-in Linux.

Fast-forward about four months or so the Raspberry Pi came along. We were running on regularity, the bill of material for each screen was about five hundred dollars. Then the Raspberry Pi came along and it was thirty-four bucks per unit. It was like, 'Wait a second - this device is so cheap, we could do this at a much cheaper price point?' So I opened OSE, an open source version of Screenly, posted it on the Reddit Forum and then put it on GitHub.

In this time-frame we basically built Screenly before the Raspberry Pi – and our goal was to see what one could do with it. None of them were really up to snuff. We looked at them all around and found something that could be relied upon in production. So we said, we could do something better than this anyways – just needed something simple. Within two weeks we had a prototype that worked.

We never..., we wrote this for our own needs so we didn't intend for it to be a big product, but it quickly exploded. We posted one post on the Raspberry Pi forum and it grew close to 130,000 views, to 700 replies. The funny thing is, it is more or less what we needed for Screenly. So I-far spent under five hundred bucks on marketing!

## Reseller Program

Screenly launched its Reseller Program to enable wholesale companies to sell subscriptions to their own customers or qualified customers signed up for Screenly using our subscription value. 'Just think about organically,' said Mike Video, 'it's been overwhelming to say-demand for Screenly that we could never have thought of ourselves. We have had partners who are vertical-specific, so they cater to local stores, restaurants, or other specific niches. In our case, it's been an amazing not only that, but a commission off top of Screenly.'

So we launched Screenly and we were overwhelmed by the amount of feedback and traction from it, and it really got to have the Raspberry Pi community is amazing. Just a month or two after we opened the open source version of Screenly we were contacted by a company in the US that had 10,000 ATM machines across the US. They said, 'This would be great for us too,' so we thought we should probably do a commercial version.

That particular deal did not pay out, but we started to get more and more questions from companies who wanted what's commercially viable - a managed service and not just an open source solution: 'How Screenly for our hotel and restaurant chain can come out to us.' So we started working on this and it worked out about eight months after we built our first prototype on a Raspberry Pi.

So the OS is a standalone solution, you install it on the Raspberry Pi and basically point your browser to the IP, and manage it from Screenly. It's a managed service, so you don't have to have a image that download and basically pair it when you turn it up the first time, you get a QR-screen pair that you want - and that's it! No keyboard or mouse or anything on the actual device. And there's central management through the web interface, where you manage all screens. Sign up for a month and we give you the package for free. We have these screens running around the world today.
