# Putting the parts together

Assembling a modern PC is an easy job, thanks to standardised components

Before you begin creating your new PC, take a moment to ready your work area. Try to build on a hard surface, as static electricity from carpets can play havoc with sensitive electronics. Check all the items you've purchased to make sure they're in working order. If you bend a pin in the processor socket on the motherboard: it is easily damaged; and at the factory, you are only arguing that it wasn't your fault when it comes to claiming your warranty.

Begin by placing the motherboard face-up on your surface, then lift the protective cover over the CPU socket's pins by unhooking the ZIF (Zero Insertion Force) lever. Leave this cover in place, as it will automatically pop up. Find the triangle which matches the one on the CPU socket, carefully place the CPU alignment by looking for the two notches on the upper and right sides of the CPU towards and locking the ZIF lever.

With the CPU in place, you can install the heatsink by applying a thin layer of thermal grease on the CPU. An easy way to do this is to place a small blob in the centre, then spread it outwards with a piece of card or plastic so that it covers the entire surface nicely (and, ideally, nearly!). This fills in the tiny gaps between the heatsink and the CPU, allowing for heat transfer more effectively. Then, install the LGA 1150/1151 heatsink and double-screws (02) before mounting the motherboard to these holes from beneath.

Install the LGA 1150/1151 heatsink over the heads (03) and fasten with the screws into the mounting bracket. Be careful not over-tightening the screws into the processor (04) down into the mounting bracket and over-tightening runs the risk of cracking the processor. Finally, attach the heatsink's fan with the supplied wire clips and connect its cable to the CPU Fan header on the motherboard.
