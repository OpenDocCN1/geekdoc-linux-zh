I have long been a fan of emulation technologies. They are one of the most taxing programs a computer can run. Managing the resource requirements of one operating system and running programs is enough of a challenge for most computers, but having to juggle multiple operating systems with layers upon layers of software all competing for CPU, memory, and disk resources truly separates the wheat from the chaff in PCs. And it represents a marvelous feat in programming wizardry to create a system that can manage such a feat.

There's also a bit of the "forbidden fruit" complex: "I shouldn't be able to run Microsoft Word! This is Linux! But here I am!"

Of course, there are a variety of professional reasons to applaud VMware. One of the biggest setbacks in software development and systems testing is a computer crash. But what if the "computer" is just a window that can be closed and re-loaded on a whim, with no risk to any other part of the PC? The applications for cross-platform development and technical support are tremendous, and in fact companies in these businesses are among VMware's best customers.

# The Inevitability of VMware

I felt sure that someone would eventually recognize the tremendous potential of virtual machines for Linux, and VMware, Inc. came through. Linux's growing popularity is running head-on with the seemingly immovable force of Windows, and some people simply can't afford to make a firm choice between them. In a world where Linux is gaining ground as a top-shelf OS, I'm confident that more people will turn to VMware to provide a bridge to other powerful operating systems and software markets.

This book is intended to be your guide through the process of configuring and using VMware as a virtual machine for a variety of popular operating systems. Although VMware has a logical graphical user interface (GUI) and relies on straightforward configuration tools, the benefits and limitations of the VMware environment are not always crystal clear.

# Novices, Beware!

Have you ever heard the saying, "Before you can truly know another, you must first know yourself?" It applies to would-be VMware users as well.

VMware is not a simple piece of software. To comfortably install and configure VMware, you should first be familiar with basic Linux conventions of installing software, manipulating files (copying and editing), and allocating free disk space. If you need additional guidance on these topics, you might want to see Red Hat Linux 6 Fast & Easy.
