Most arcade games do not use a screen larger than 640×480, but if you encounter some that do, you need to increase that value to 800×600 or possibly beyond. At the same time, some older games are 320×200, which is extremely small on a modern desktop and might be hard to see. If you are playing a game that is too tiny to make out on your monitor, set the scale variables to 1.5 or 2 so you have a larger field of play. (This can slow down the emulation somewhat. Many old games can still run at full speed on modern PCs, however, even when their screen size is doubled.)

# Using VICE Commodore 64 Emulator for Linux

Before there was the Amiga, there was the Commodore 64. Remembered primarily as a game computer, the C64 was arguably the most powerful of all the 8-bit computers, and it is the best-selling single computer architecture of all time. (Although there are many, many more PCs in the world, the C64's CPU, graphics and sound, and RAM remained essentially unchanged throughout its entire lifecycle.)

To recapture 8-bit glory on your Linux PC, all you need is VICE, the Versatile Commodore Emulator. It is “versatile” in that in addition to the 64, VICE includes emulators for nearly every 8-bit computer Commodore made in the 70s and 80s, including the Commodore VIC 20 and 128.
