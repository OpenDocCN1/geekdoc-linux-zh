# Internal Scripts

One element of the automation attitude is to be on the lookout for opportunities within every application you use. Scripting has become a pervasive theme, and almost all common applications have at least a rudimentary macro or scripting capability. IRC users know about bots, Web browsers typically expose at couple of scripting interfaces, all modern PPP clients are scriptable, and even such venerable tools as v1 and ftp have configuration, shortcut, and macro capabilities that enormously magnify productivity. If you use a tool regularly, take a few minutes to reread its presentation in this volume; chances are you'll come up with a way to make your work easier and more effective.

# Concluding Challenge for an Automator

In this chapter, you've learned various ways of using your computer so that it best serves you. You now know how to improve your script writing skills, and you're ready to apply new approaches to increase your efficiency. The next challenge in your automation career is: How do you take your skills to the next level?

As usual, the solution begins with attitude. You no longer need to pound at the keyboard to bludgeon technical tasks into submission; you can now operate in a more refined way and achieve correspondingly grand results. As an employee, you can be much more valuable than an average system administrator or programmer who reinvents the wheel every day. In your recreational or personal use of SuSE Linux, the computer can work for you—rather than the other around, as it might have been in the past. Your attitude needs to adjust to this new approach of improving your productivity. In your technical abilities, by attending conferences you can further promote your skills (allowing you to possibly negate a higher salary), or take the time in your daily computer work to do things right. You will find that some initial effort in the application of a scripting tool can result in a robust, long-term solution to the particular problem at hand. In any organization, it's easy to give attention to crises and reward those visibly coping with emergencies, ignoring the fact that the crisis could possibly have been eliminated if a simple script or cron job was instituted several months in advance. Take true leadership to plan ahead, organize work so emergencies don't happen, and use the techniques of automation presented in this chapter to achieve predictable and manageable results on schedule.
