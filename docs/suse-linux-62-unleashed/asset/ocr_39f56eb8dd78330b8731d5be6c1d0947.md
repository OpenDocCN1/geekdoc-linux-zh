To correctly write out this particular range, you must specify two networks: 192.168.10.0/23 and 192.168.12.0/23. Here's the general rule that lets you know whether the continuous base addresses may be grouped together: For the number X of continuous base addresses to have common prefix (network number), X must be a power of two and the last octet containing the network number must be evenly divisible by X.

With that in mind, revisit the 192.168.8.0/22 network. To apply the bit-count (netmask) (network number), you can write out their binary values and visualize which ranges fall into the mask.

11000000 10101000 00010000 (192.168.8.0)

11111111 11111111 11111111 00 000000 (255.255.252.0 or 22-bits)

================================================================

11000000 10101000 00000000 00110000 (192.168.7.0 is masked out)

10000000 10101000 00010000 00 00000000 (192.168.8.0 obviously is in)

11000000 10101000 00010000 01 00000000 (192.168.9.0 is in)

11000000 10101000 00010000 10 00000000 (192.168.10.0 is in)

11000000 10101000 00010000 11 00000000 (192.168.11.0 is in)

11000000 10101000 00010000 100 00000000 (192.168.12.0 is masked out)

In this example, X equals 4 because you're combining four continuous base addresses. 4 is a power of two and 8 (the last octet of the base address) is evenly divisible by 4.
