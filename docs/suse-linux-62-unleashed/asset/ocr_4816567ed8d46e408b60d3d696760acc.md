The recommendation is to buy the best video card and monitor that you can afford. It is tough to look at a 14-inch, 800 x 600-resolution monitor driven by a 2MB graphics card for more than a few hours a day. If you are serious about graphics, the first thing to buy is a good video card. The cards that are good for games also work very well for displaying graphics. If you buy one revision or model back from the top of the line or latest fancy card, you should be able to get one at a fair price. The second benefit of buying an established video card is that there will most likely be a driver available that has the bugs worked out and can take advantage of the card's features. If you buy the latest card, there is a chance that the video driver might not be optimized or that it will be an experimental video driver. There is nothing wrong with experimental drivers, as long as you understand and accept the ramifications of pushing the edge. If you want something that has withstood the test of time and quality, experimental drivers are not for you.

Monitor prices follow a pattern: If you bought a 15-inch monitor last year, 17-inch monitors are available this year at a similar price point. Monitors are like audio speakers in that monitors appeal to different people. There is usually no one “best” monitor for everyone. The best advice is to research monitors on the Internet and narrow your choices to a few. The next step is to visit a store with monitors on display and look at each one under similar light conditions. Some of the video distribution systems may not push the monitor to the maximum—try to find a couple of the top choices hooked up directly to a computer for additional clarity. The last step is to find a store or vendor that will let you your monitor in your home or business under the lighting conditions and types of graphics applications that you normally use.

It might be nice to have a 21-inch monitor for your workstation, realize that they cost a significant amount of money and take up a significant amount of desk space. That old card or small computer table might not work with a large monitor. Large monitors also tend to be very heavy and bulky. Flat-screen monitors have made significant strides in the past year; they are becoming affordable and are approaching the quality of monitors. An informal survey found that a 15-inch flat-screen monitor was around $800 and an 18-inch flat-screen sold for $2000 to $3000. A new 19-inch monitor was $300 to $400. Flat-screen monitors are expensive, but they’re smaller than glass monitors and their picture is better. In the next couple of years, flat-screen monitors will approach glass monitors in price and functionality.
