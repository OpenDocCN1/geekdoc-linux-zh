# SuSE System Administration
PART III

Data is important, valuable by both the time it took to create it and the uniqueness of the data. Therefore, you should take care not to lose that data.

Data can be lost several ways. The first is through carelessness. Many system administrators have restored data after being in the wrong directory and issuing an rm -fr command. The second way data can be lost is via hardware failure. Although newer drives are more reliable than older ones, they still fail, and data is lost. A third way data is lost through faulty software. Instead of performing an intended task, programmed programs can sometimes run amid and destroy data instead of manipulating it. These days, programs are often released before they are ready. If there is a bug, the people who developed the software put out a patch; the data—and the time it took to create it—are gone. Finally, the earth can just swallow entire buildings; there can be earthquakes, tornadoes, or volcanoes, hurricanes or aliens from outer space.

This chapter covers the qualities of a good backup and the process of selecting a good backup medium and a backup tool. Finally, backup strategies are considered, including incremental and full backups and when to perform each.

# Successful Backup Considerations

Backups can protect your investment in time and data, but only if you are successful in backing up and keeping the information; therefore, part of a successful backup procedure is a test strategy to spot-check backups. The easiest way to spot-check your backups is to perform a restore with them, which you should attempt before it is needed.

Backups can take many forms. I worked for a company that had six servers. Their backup method was to tar and save a copy of that on another server. In addition, they did tape backups of the servers, which included the online version of the backups. These tape backups were used for disaster recovery purposes and kept off site. This example shows couple different ways to perform backups—storing copied on other machines and storing copies on tape backup (and keeping the copies off). The combination of these methods provides a reliable way of doing backups, covering everything from the simple “Oops, I accidentally deleted my database” to “Godzilla stepped on our building, and we need the data back in less than two days!
