# ## Spreadsheet Applications

Some people view computers as nothing more than fancy calculators. In fact, it was not until the advent of early financial calculation software such as VisiCalc (first introduced for Apple Computer hardware) that people started taking personal computers seriously. Before this, "serious" financial calculations were always conducted on "Big Iron," or huge mainframe computers.

Today, Apple G4 users can snicker about comparisons between their computers' power compared to the size and cost of those big computers of even 10 years ago. Today's Macs routinely sport more than 1GB RAM and provide hard drive storage only dreamed of a decade ago.

The various clients and programs discussed here run the gamut from GPLd projects and open source packages, to the public domain. You are sure to find a program that can fit your needs. These programs rival and often exceed the capabilities of commercial spreadsheet programs that cost computer users hundreds of dollars only a few years ago.

# ### Using gnumeric

The *gnumeric* spreadsheet client requires an active X session, and is part of the GNOME software library distribution. This program, initially started as a simple table editor, has rapidly evolved into a capable spreadsheet client.
