# # Playing DVDs


Linux users on the PowerPC platform can now enjoy the use of a free digital video disc (DVD) viewer to watch many different types of DVD videos, including encrypted discs. Despite the best efforts of the movie industry, the DVD Control Association, and the Motion Picture Association of America, the decryption algorithm has now been reduced to six lines of C code and is available to anyone through the Internet.


The vlc client, available in source form, allows the playing of most types of DVDs on nearly every Linux platform, including the PowerPC. This free DVD player is the result of a project by students at the École Centrale in Paris, and supports network broadcasting of MPEG streams.


Several hardware and software solutions have been available for Intel-based PCs. After a long hiatus, IBM released its ThinkPad model T22 on April 1, 2001. It is installed with Caldera OpenLinux 2.4 and includes InterVideo’s LinDVD software DVD player. This represented the first instance of a commercial software DVD player for Linux (even though it is only for one model laptop and the Intel CPU platform). This, however, does little good for the rest of the Linux market.
