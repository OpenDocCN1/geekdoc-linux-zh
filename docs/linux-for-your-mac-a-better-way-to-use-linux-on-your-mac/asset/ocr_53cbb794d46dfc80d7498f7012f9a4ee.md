# ## BSD on the Mac

The Berkeley Software Distribution, or BSD UNIX, is a variant of the commer-cial UNIX tree. Following a series of lawsuits in the mid-1990s, the free portions (to-tally rewritten and copyright-free portions) of this operating system were released to the world as 4.4BSD-Lite. Following this release, developers started working on various ports of this version of UNIX to run on Intel-based PCs.

# ### Types

After nearly 10 years, and nearly 30 years after the birth of UNIX, there are now three main variants of BSD available royalty and license-free, which include FreeBSD, NetBSD, and the ultra-secure OpenBSD.

One version that is available for use on Apple Power Macs is from http://www.openbsd.org. You can download a current development version, or *snapshots*, from ftp://ftp.openbsd.org/pub/OpenBSD/snapshots/powerpc/. This version has been in development since 1996, and is actively supported and constantly revised. Ac-cording to the OpenBSD project, the current version supports the Power Mac G4, Power Mac Dual G4, Power Mac G4 Cube, iMac Rev A, B, C, DV, and DV+, along with the FireWire PowerBook G3, and PowerBook (Titanium) G4. Sup-port for additional machines may be available by the time you read this.
