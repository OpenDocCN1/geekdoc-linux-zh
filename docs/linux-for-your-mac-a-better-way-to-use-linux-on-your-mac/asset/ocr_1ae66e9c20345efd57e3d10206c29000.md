If you use your credit card to purchase items on the Internet, make sure the purchase is through an encrypted browser session. Netscape provides versions of Navigator that support good encryption, and these versions should be used (and in some cases, are required for certain online transactions). Never allow your credit card to be stored on a remote vendor server. If someone breaks into the vendor, your card number could be stolen. Always carefully examine your credit card purchase, or consider getting a card to be used only for online purchases.

If you want a measure of personal privacy, never use your real e-mail address in Usenet postings. There are SPAM providers out there with software robots that harvest e-mail addresses from online postings and Web sites. If you continually receive unwanted electronic mail, save the mail and complain to your State Attorney General. You can also consider filing in small claims court against the SPAM agent if your local or state law allows.

Be courteous when visiting FTP sites and use a mirror site, or a site that contains a mirror image of the original if the use of a mirror site is suggested. Swamping an FTP site to get at the latest release of a game or application can quickly tax the resources of the remote host.

# ## Troubleshooting

Problems in getting connected to the Internet are generally related to proper set up and configuration of Linux. Make sure that your modem is working and recognized by Linux before attempting to connect. Try using a terminal application, such as minicom, to dial out and test your modem. Diagnosing problems with DSL connections is a bit trickier, but chances are very good that if your connection works using another operating system, then it will work with Linux too.

Not all internal modems on Apple PowerPC computers work with Linux. Some models, such as the PowerBook 3400 or the Apple Cube, may never be supported. The workaround is to try to use an external modem with a serial or USB-to-serial adapter. Realize that various hardware support will improve with each new version of the Linux kernel and as each PPC Linux distribution matures. What is not supported today may be supported tomorrow. Legacy hardware has a good chance of being supported; however, the latest and greatest on the market may not be supported right away. A pleasant surprise is the Apple TiBook, which works out of the box with LinuxPPC 2000 Q4, even though that Linux distribution was available months before Apple released the TiBook.
