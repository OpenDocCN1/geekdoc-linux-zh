This chapter covers a variety of multimedia topics, including the use of multimedia applications and how to create multimedia projects using PowerPC Linux and X. As Linux has matured, support has appeared and improved for a variety of hardware and software projects. This includes better support for USB cameras and scanners, sound mixers, playing audio CDs, ripping audio CDs into .mp3 files, burning CDs on CD-R or CD-RW media, and playing DVD movies.

In this chapter you will learn how to get started with the GIMP, set up a USB scanner, use GIMP scanner plug-ins, use a digital camera client named gPhoto, convert graphics, use audio mixers, play sounds, rip .mp3 files, and play DVD video. That is a lot for one chapter, but you will at least get enough information to get started. Linux and X have a great deal to offer the creative artist, musician, or technician—the rest will be up to you!

Using multimedia clients with X and Linux can require additional system and video memory, and in some instances specialized video hardware and software. If you plan to use multimedia clients with Linux and X, your computer's graphics system should support resolutions of at least 1024x768 and millions of colors (with 16bpp). It should support a minimum of thousands of colors. Some tasks, such as converting an audio stream into digital form, can tax your CPU. Other tasks, such as playing DVD movies, can require a fast PowerPC processor, extra memory, and greater color depths.

# Graphics Editors

Graphics editors have been a part of X since its first public release. Every XFree86 distribution of X includes Davor Matic’s legacy monochrome bitmap editor client, along with a healthy selection of sophisticated clients supporting nearly 100 different graphics file formats. Graphics are an integral part of the X desktop, which uses icons and background images. You will create, edit, or convert graphics as a normal part of conducting business while using X. This is particularly true if you have a scanner, use pictures in documents, or send and receive faxes.

# The GIMP

The GIMP is a feature-rich and capable graphics client for X, and has been ported to many different platforms and Linux distributions, including PowerPC Linux. If you are an Adobe PhotoShop fan, you will appreciate the numerous filters, scripts, and other tools supported by the GIMP.

The GIMP is distributed freely and comes with source code, which allows developers and artists to contribute improvements to this interactive image editor.
