You are asked to select your keyboard. Press Enter or click OK to continue, then choose your type of keyboard. You are asked to choose your mouse. Again, press Enter or click OK to continue and choose your mouse.

# Configuring Partitions and Installation

Next you'll be asked about drive partitioning. After selecting the designated drive, click the Edit button. You'll then be presented with a partition map of the selected drive. If the current partitioning is OK, then continue the install. Otherwise, you'll need to either delete or add partitions to create the minimal HFS boot, / (root), and swap partitions. Adding or deleting partitions will cause the new partitions to be formatted. Existing partitions remain untouched.

You'll then be asked to choose the mount points for your partitions. Click on each partition and type in its Linux mount point (such as /mnt, /boot, /, or /home). Note that you do not assign a mount point for your swap partition! After confirming the mount points, you'll then proceed to package selection.

You'll have your choice of a base, home/office, development workstation, Internet server, LAN/WAN server, or Everything installations. A base install is not recommended, as you won't be able to use X. Choose an installation that suits your needs. The installations range from 500MB to 2GB for everything. Select an installation, then press Enter or click the OK button.

The installer will read a database of software packages, choose the appropriate software and support libraries, and copy the packages to your hard drive. Go take a coffee break, but keep an eye on the Overall Progress indicator on the screen.

# Saving Settings

After Linux and the distribution software are installed, you'll be offered the opportunity to save your previous settings. This can be handy, especially if you've never installed Linux before, and need to return to the install. When finished, press Enter, or click OK.

You are asked if you want to configure your LAN. If you say Yes, you are asked if you want to assign an Internet Protocol address. You can assign a static IP (used every time you boot). If you do not want to network your computer, highlight No and press Enter. You can always add a networking interface later on. You'll also be asked to enter your domain name (such as home.org), along with a hostname (such as "stinky") for your computer. If you know the IP addresses of your LAN's nameserver, enter those addresses as well.
