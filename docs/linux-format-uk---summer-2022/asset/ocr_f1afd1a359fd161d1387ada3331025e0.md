# 1. The most recent evidence—Occam’s razor: A new approach to Occam’s razor in the context of model inference

![](bbox=[0.65, 0.16, 0.98, 0.36])

To expand on the notes to the main discussion of Occam’s razor, we’ve included a brief summary of our approach and how it differs from traditional uses of the razor. This is not a definitive guide, but rather an explanation of why we felt the need to revisit the concept. We have made several simplifying assumptions: (1) the model is a tree structure; (2) each node has a probability distribution over its children; and (3) the root node is known. Under these conditions, we can derive a closed-form expression for the marginal likelihood of the data given the model. This expression is similar to the one derived by Rissanen in his work on stochastic complexity, but it is simpler and more intuitive. Our approach can be seen as a generalization of the traditional Bayesian model selection criterion, where the prior distribution over the models is determined by the structure of the tree. This allows us to avoid the arbitrary choice of prior that is often required in traditional Bayesian model selection. We believe that our approach provides a more principled way to apply Occam’s razor in the context of model inference.
