## HISTORY

### The complete history of Unix commits

Want to feel old? A project on GitHub gathers together over 50 years of Unix commits for your delectation.

![](img/f20a68d91e56325c44e9a1405ca1923f_5_0.png)

It's the question of how often I should change the password on my Unix account every 30 days, but I've been wondering how long it's been since I last changed it. The first commit of any program, including Unix, is always an empty commit. Or so it seemed in the early days of Git. But a new project on GitHub has changed that. [https://github.com/jg1977/unix-commits](https://github.com/jg1977/unix-commits) is a repository containing the full history of Unix commits, from the very first one in 1969 to the most recent one today. It includes not only the code, but also the commit messages, which provide a fascinating look at how Unix evolved over the decades.

The project's creator, Jørgen Schaefer, has been working on it for years. He started by digging through the archives of various Unix systems, including AT&T Unix, BSD, and Linux. He then used tools like [git-filter-branch](https://git-scm.com/docs/git-filter-branch) to combine the histories into a single repository. The result is a whopping 1,500 commits, spanning from 1969 to 2022.

It's not just the code that's interesting; the commit messages are a goldmine of historical trivia. You'll see early debates about whether to use C or assembly language, as well as the evolution of file system designs. And, of course, there are the famous "bug fixes" that turned out to be fundamental changes to the operating system.

![](img/f20a68d91e56325c44e9a1405ca1923f_5_1.png)

Of course, no history of Unix would be complete without a discussion of the famous "Thou shalt not fork" edict. And that brings us to one of the more colorful entries in the repository: a commit message from 1984 that reads, "Forking is not a crime, but it is a sin." This was a reference to the early days of Unix, when system administrators would "fork" the operating system to create new, customized versions. The message was a reminder that while forking was a necessary part of the Unix ecosystem, it should be done with care and responsibility.

But perhaps the most fascinating aspect of the project is the sheer volume of code. With over 1,500 commits, the repository contains the entire history of Unix, from the original kernel to the modern systems we use today. It's a testament to the power of open source, and the value of preserving history.

From the first few lines of code to the latest security patches, you can see how Unix has grown and changed over the years. ### OPINION

#### CRITICAL THINKING

Katie Gompels

For the first five months this year, I didn’t read a single news article. It was my decision to break away from my daily news consumption and learn to think for myself.

### We lack all of the context in our daily lives

I know many people who are highly involved in their fields of study and/or work but haven’t ever heard of the ‘S’ in STEM. Equally, I know a lot of people in the social sciences who can’t tell a transistor from a diode or have no idea what, say, a ‘quantum computer’ is. This is a shame because the more we know, the more we can contribute and the better we can make informed decisions in our lives.

We lack critical-thinking skills too, and that saddles us with the danger of our own biases. For example, if your views on the environment are not backed up by hard data, that could lead to bad policy decisions. Likewise, if you are not aware of the implications of some particular medical decision, that could also lead to a poor outcome.

We lack empathy too. It’s a pity that we can’t feel the plight of those in other parts of the world. How can we help if we don’t understand their needs? Empathy is a key ingredient in critical thinking. We need to learn how to see the world from others’ perspectives.
