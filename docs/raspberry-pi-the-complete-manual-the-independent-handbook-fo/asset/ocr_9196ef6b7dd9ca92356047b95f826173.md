# Making your iBeacon smart

**11** You can take what you’ve done with the iBeacon so far even further with some smart interaction between your smartphone and Raspberry Pi, but where should you start? Rather than going headfirst into a DIY task, it pays to take your time finding the right app for your smartphone — one that will enable you to launch specific apps based on the presence of a iBeacon.
The tools we’ve looked at above are simply for detection and measurement, and the data they reveal is useful for developing trigger apps. But how do you launch an app when your phone is near a iBeacon?

# What a iBeacon can’t do

**13** As things stand, for better or worse, you’re not in a Phillip K Dick novel. This means that for the time being (at least), iBeacon technology cannot launch a web page on your phone without a third party tool (such as those mentioned above).
It is not beyond the realm of possibility that such a feature might be integrated into iOS or Android in the foreseeable future, a situation that might see an advert displayed on your phone as you pass an iBeacon, perhaps including a discount for shopping at the advertising store.

# iBeacon Trigger Apps

**12** Whether you’re using iOS or Android, you’ll find a trigger app on the corresponding app store that can be set up to launch an app when your smartphone comes into proximity with your iBeacon. Android users should take a look at NFC Beam, available free from Google Play and intended for devices equipped with Bluetooth 4.0 hardware and running Android 4.3 or later. Meanwhile, iOS users should take a look at Beacon, available for a pittance (£1.59) in the App Store. This app has support for iFTTT, Philips Hue, HomeKit and Lux lights integration.

# Choosing iBeacon vs NFC

**14** As you may have gathered by now, there are some similarities in use for an iBeacon and NFC, especially when it comes to smart home applications.
So which one should we use for your project?
Adept producers an NFC breakout board to add NFC functionality to the Raspberry Pi, which can prove very useful for any project that requires contact between an NFC tag and a piece of hardware — we grabbed ours from ModMyPi. And this is really where the distinction lies. If your project requires physical contact, then you should use NFC. Otherwise, the iBeacon solution should be completely adequate.
