# Secure the Software

Failure to keep software updated and fix well-known bugs is the leading cause of network-based break-ins. Most break-ins are not the work of sophisticated security crackers. Experts may be the first to discover a vulnerability, but they rarely waste their time exploiting the vulnerability to break into every small business connected to the Internet. Most break-ins are the work of unsophisticated computer users called "script kiddies". They attack scripts from places like rootshell.com and insecure.org, and let the scripts do the work. The scripts look for and exploit well-known vulnerabilities to give the kiddie unauthorized access to the target systems. The scripts can be configured to scan thousands of systems for vulnerabilities. Tracking the vulnerabilities exploited by the scripts and closing these holes as they appear are important components of improving the security of your server.

# Tracking Vulnerabilities

To secure a system, you need to know its vulnerabilities. Your goal should be to stay well informed about Linux and Sendmail vulnerabilities as the vandals are. Frankly, you won't be able to. You have a life and responsibilities, so the vandals who have nothing better to do will get ahead of you and may compromise your system. Defite the difficulty, you should do your best to keep up to date about security problems.

There are several sources of information about known security vulnerabilities.

+   - General information about security vulnerabilities is available from the bugtraq list at http://bug-girl1.com/bugtraq
- Good sites for security advisories are www.10pt.com and www.cert.org.
- A good site for Linux software updates and security hole announcements are www.freshmeat.com.
- The www.sendmail.org site provides information on Sendmail vulnerabilities.

You should also check the Web site maintained by your Linux distribution vendor for security announcements. Additionally, I recommend going to the SANS Institute site, www.sans.org, and signing up for the Security Alert Consensus, which is listed under the Security Digets. It is easy to pick the Linux-specific items out of the report, and the reports you keep updated on current vulnerabilities.

In addition to visiting the sites that report bug and security problems, visit the Web sites that provide attack scripts. Two such sites are www.rootshell.com and www.insecure.org. Make sure that your system is not vulnerable to the old attacks and evaluate the new scripts as they are released to understand the vulnerabilities they exploit. In providing scripts, these sites provide information about what is currently going on in the network security world.
