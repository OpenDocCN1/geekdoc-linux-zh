# Peripherals

Assuming that Linux will work properly with your PC, will it also operate with peripherals such as printers and modems? This very much depends on the particular peripheral you are using, and the more common the device, the better the chance of it being supported by Linux. You can not find specific support for a printer or other peripheral in a standard Linux distribution. Most Linux distributions include a Hewlett-Packard LaserJet printer. Using a mode driver or emulator, a Hewlett-Packard LaserJet printer can be configured to work with many other printers. There are many different types of LaserJet printers, and even within the same type there are many different variations. This is the most common type of printer you will find in many offices, and as such, it is the most important type of printer to support. However, the quality of the printing produced by a LaserJet printer is quite variable, depending on the particular model used. Some models produce very high quality output, while others are not so good. If you have a LaserJet printer, you should find that it works with Linux without any problems.

If you do not have a LaserJet printer, you will probably find that you can not use it with Linux, at least not without some modifications. There are many different types of printers, and each one has its own unique features. This makes it difficult to create a universal driver that will work with all of them. The best solution is to use a postscript driver. This is a universal driver that can work with many different types of printers. However, it is not always available, and even when it is, it may not work with all printers. If you have a printer that is not a LaserJet, you will need to look for a driver that is specifically designed for your printer. You may be able to find such a driver on the internet, or you may need to write your own. Writing a printer driver is not a simple task, and it is not something that most people will want to do. However, if you have the skills and the desire, it is a very rewarding project.

# Graphics card

The same is true of some graphics cards. There tends to be a lag between a new video card being announced and a driver appearing for the card. This has a two-fold effect. First, new cards may be slow to get support. Second, old cards, with well-established drivers, are slow to get support. For this reason it is important to keep your graphics card as up to date as possible. In other words, do not go in for the latest and most bleeding-edge graphics card, but instead go for one that is well-established and has a solid driver base.

# Monitor

A standard VGA monitor having a resolution of 640 by 480 pixels is the minimum. However, you should aim to get a monitor that supports 800 by 600 pixels, or even 1024 by 768 pixels. If you have a 19 inch monitor, you can get 1280 by 1024 pixels. The higher the resolution, the more text and graphics you can display on the screen. This is important if you are doing a lot of work with spreadsheets, or if you are working with large images. A higher resolution monitor will allow you to see more detail, and it will make it easier to read small text.

In practice I have found this type of thing to be a little awkward, and where possible I would prefer to have the screen at a higher resolution of 800 by 600, or 1024 by 768 pixels. Even if it results in a rather small menu text, etc., or the text being small, I would still recommend using the monitor at one of these higher resolutions.
