If you answer `n`, those nuggets of unknown data remain detached from the filesystem. The filesystem remains dirty until you fix them by some other means.

# **Trusting `fsc (8)`**

If `fsc (8)` can't figure out where a file goes . . . can you? If not, you really have no choice but to trust `fsc(8)` to recover your system or restore from backup.

A full `fsc(8)` run inspects every block, inode, and superblock, and identifies every inconsistency. It asks you to type y or `n` to approve or reject every single correction. Any change you reject you must fix yourself, through some other means. You might spend hours at the console typing , y, y, y.

So I'll ask again: if `fsc(8)` can't fix a problem, can you?

If you can't, consider `fsc -y`. The `-y` flag tells `fsc(8)` to reassemble these files as best it can, without prompting you. It assumes you answer all its questions “yes,” even the really dangerous ones. Using `-y` automatically triggers `-r`, which tells `fsc(8)` to retry cleaning each filesystem until it succeeds or it's had 10 consecutive failures. It's cure or kill. You *do* have backups, right?
