+   Use the principle of least privilege (PoLP) when giving access to an auditor.

+   Avoid gaining external access to your systems for audit purposes. Instead, request that the auditor performs the audit on-site to reduce risks.

+   Make sure you check with your data retention policy to ensure that all required logs are available for the audit.

Also, make sure you check with your HR and legal department to ensure that the external auditor signs all documentation, to reduce the risk of exfiltration of data (such as signing a non-disclosure agreement (NDA), confidentiality clauses, and so on).

# As an auditor

Auditors have a huge responsibility because the results of their findings may have legal, monetary, or professional consequences. Therefore, as an auditor you must adhere not only to high ethical standards but also to technical standards, to ensure that the data gathered is not tampered with. Here are the best practices:

+   Adhere to best practices when gathering evidence. A good example is to hash the files before extracting them from the source. That way, you can demonstrate that the data you have was not altered.

+   If you foresee any conflict of interest—for example, a family relation with the CISO of the company being audited—then speak before you start to avoid the audit questions questioned.

+   Sometimes, you may find errors that can be fixed with a simple click on a checkbox, but as an auditor your job is to report and not fix, do not fall into the temptation to apply quick fixes.

+   Remember that your access may be monitored, so do not abuse your access. Something as simple as accessing a folder or files that were not strictly necessary as part of your audit may result in fines or legal action against you and your company, so remember: with great power comes great responsibility.
