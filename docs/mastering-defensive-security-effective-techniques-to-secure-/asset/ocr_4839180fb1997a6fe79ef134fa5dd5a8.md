# The structure of a good CMM

A good CMM must be modular so that the company implementing it can add or remove sections without impacting the final results—for example, not all companies have developers, so asking a company without developers to fill in those sections just to complete the model doesn't make any sense.

# Domains and controls

When implementing the CMM, the IT person in charge of it (let's say an analyst) will assess a plurality of items called controls.

To make the implementation (including the analysis of the results) easier, the controls should be logically grouped with other related controls. These groups are called domains.

As seen in the following, a good CMM evaluates other areas that have a direct impact on your cybersecurity strategy—for example:

# Maturity levels

Now that you have the controls sorted by domain, it's time to evaluate them. The evaluation should be done using a scale that clearly defines each level. The most common evaluation scales are 3-level, 5-level, and +7-level scale.

A 3-level scale may be very simplistic and gives you very little room for rating and improvement, that is not the best option for you.

On the other hand, a +7-level scale may be too complex to evaluate, analyze, and report, and since one of the goals of a CMM is to be simple to implement, then this is not a good option either.

However, a 5-level scale is considered the best standard because it allows you to accurately rate a control or domain without unnecessary complications.
