
This is especially relevant if your company runs a transactional website (such as an e-commerce website) because an attacker may attack your database and create or modify discount codes, and by the time you discover the issue, your merchandise may already be sold and delivered for a fraction of the original price.

What makes these attacks more dangerous is that the attacker will not just apply this discount to their purchase but everyone. Therefore, you may find 1,000 orders discounted by 99%, making it harder for you to identify who performed the attack.

The most famous hacks to banks were caused because the integrity of the data was compromised.

Therefore, due to the impact of these kinds of attacks, companies must proactively and constantly invest time and resources to prevent them.

## Availability

These attacks aim to disrupt the availability of a given system, network, or resource. Except for online stores, these are the less dangerous type of attack; however, such attacks are also the most common type of attack. In fact, the majority of attacks performed by hacktivist groups aim to disrupt availability.

## Denial-of-service attack

The favorite favorite attack employed by hacktists, the Distributed Denial-of-Service (DDoS) attack, is very interesting because it affects you in two ways, as an attacker and as a target. As mentioned earlier, the impact of these kinds of attacks depends on the nature of business; however, your infrastructure can be used by an attacker to launch a Botnet-based attack on another company and will have serious implications for your company regardless of the type.

> **Botnets**
> A Botnet is a network of infected devices that are remotely controlled by an attacker (normally using a command and control server) to perform a plurality of tasks without the consent and knowledge of the owner. The controlled or infected machines are normally called zombies and, as mentioned, they will perform background tasks as DDoS attacks, sending spam, and mining cryptocurrency (Bitcoins).

One interesting variant of these attacks is the SYN flood attack. This attack is very clever and it is based on the TCP three-way handshake. But wait, what is a TCP three-way handshake?

## A Refresher on Defensive Concepts

Let me do an analogy to explain it: Imagine that you (the client) need a cab (the server), so you decided to call the cab (SYN). When the cab arrives, it informs you that it is at the gate (SYN-ACK) and waits for your confirmation to pass (ACK). Now, imagine that you never confirm and keep calling more cabs. Eventually, your driveway will be full of cabs, preventing the arrival of any other car to your house.

## Teardrop attack

The teardrop attack leverages an old vulnerability in which the system tries to reassemble fragmented packets, but since they were corrupted, the system crashes (by taking the CPU to 100%). As mentioned, this is an old vulnerability and an up-to-date system should not be affected. However, there is a new version called FragmentSmack, which affects current OSES, including Windows 10 and Linux distributions (see CVE-2018-5391). The good thing is that there are already patches for both.

> **Important note**
> As a walkaround in Windows systems, you can disable packet reassembly as follows:
> Netsh int ipv4 set global reassemblylimit=0
> Netsh int ipv6 set global reassemblylimit=0

There are other types of DDoS attacks, including the Ping of Death and Smurf Attacks (using ICMP packets), but these are old attacks that you should already be familiar with, so I am not going to waste your time on them.
