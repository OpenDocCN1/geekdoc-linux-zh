# Keeping Files Secure

Anyone at all familiar with computers and their use knows that occasionally files just simply get lost. There is a long list of reasons why this might happen: accidental deletes, hardware failures, pilot error, software bugs, termites, cockroaches, and mysterious malicious alien hackers to name a few. But there is some relief. Tools are built into the security system that can help you deal with trouble. These tools can be used to protect your files from all the threats we just listed and many more.

# Planning for Fire and Flood

The first consideration for any disaster management and recovery plan is to decide what you can do to prevent every possible realistic threat to your system. Obviously, there are some broad differences between systems and situations that require special consideration. It is a truism that the more secure you make something, the more inconvenient it becomes to use. With that in mind, you can see that a very important part of this planning exercise is determining just how far you, and your users, will go in tolerating the inconveniences imposed by security measures taken to ensure what you want.

Plan for the worst: it might happen anyway. When you are deciding what to do about backups, you might try imagining what you would do if the building burned down overnight. Picture yourself having to get the business back up and running without anything but the data you backed up and stuck in the fireproof safe the night before. Think about it that way and see whether that doesn't stimulate some good ideas for you.
