# Locking Up the System

Certainly one of the more obvious means of securing your equipment and data is just to lock them up in a location to which only you have the key. This might seem like an oversimplification of the problem, but in reality, maintaining the physical security of your equipment is very important. The investment in computers and peripherals can be quite significant for a home office or small business. Furthermore, this doesn't take into account the cost of the software or the value of your data.

Where and how the computer will be used are the determining factors in deciding how it should be protected. This might vary so much that it runs the gamut from doing nothing for a game computer you have at home, to permanently super-gluing the computer you make available for customer use right to the desk it's sitting on. You will have to decide where you fit. After you do that, you can determine what you need to do to physically secure your equipment in a way that meets the needs of your particular situation.

# Creating Backup and Disaster Plans

No one wants to turn on the computer in the morning only to find corrupted programs and missing data. It could be an uncomfortable situation if you find the big report for the big boss (you know, the report that you were so happy about because you had just gotten it finished on time) all scrambled into gibberish or just plain missing. And, it could get worse.

Since the advent of the computer age, computer users have searched for some protection from “The Case of the Missing File.” No one appreciates this kind of surprise. What you could do to recover from a disastrous loss like this would depend entirely on what assets you had put safely aside that could be brought back to restore (to whatever extent) what was lost.
