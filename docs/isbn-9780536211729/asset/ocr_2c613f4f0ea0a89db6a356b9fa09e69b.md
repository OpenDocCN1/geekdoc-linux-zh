The solution to forged mail is to recognize mail headers and News headers can be spoofed easily and to not trust them. Use PGP-style signed and encrypted e-mail, verifying that the recipient has the sender's nonspoofed PGP-style public key and that the sender has the recipient's. It is important to educate your users about this.

# 3.2.5 Where Is All That Spam Coming From?

It seems like every fool looking for a get-rich-quick scheme has discovered spam. Spam is to e-mail what junk mail is to paper mail. Simply, it is e-mail sent to people without with the sender. The vast majority of recipients have absolutely no desire to receive the mail. Their companies and ISPs certainly do not want their resources wasted on.

> The term spam comes from a Monty Python television skit in a group of Viking warriors starting chanting 'spam, spam, spam' so loudly that they drown out all other conversations, similarly to the problem of unsolicited commercial e-mail (junk e-mail) seeming to drown out legitimate e-mail.

Although many manner of capitalizations of the term are seen, I will follow the example of the U.S. Congressional Record, March 25, 1999, page S 3511 in which Sen. Frank Murkowski (R-AK) introduces Senate Bill S 759, the Inbox Privacy Act of 1999.

Spammers get their lists of e-mail addresses of people to annoy in a variety of ways. The following are some of the ways:

+   * Scan Usenet (Network) news group postings and harvest the posters' e-mail addresses.

+   * Run Web 'bots that scan Web pages for e-mail addresses by recognizing text around 'e' characters.

+   * Generate lists of domain names (via Web 'bots and other means) and proceed from there.
