Edit foo2's last line if you are not paid enough to be paged every hour in the middle of the night and weekend. The unedited last line will look like

```
00 * * * /usr/local/etc/logcheck.sh
```

The second space-separated field is a list of what hours the command should be run. 9-17 for anyone who can leave this behind at 5 P.M.. For an additional check at 8 P.M., use 9-17,20. The fifth field specifies the days when you script should be run, with 1-5 indicating during the week. For those who want additional checks on the week-ends, duplicate the line and edit each one appropriately. The resulting Logcheck lines output by crontab -1 then might look like

```
00 9-17,20 * 1-5 /usr/local/etc/logcheck.sh
00 10,14,18,22 * 6,0 /usr/local/etc/logcheck.sh
```

PortSentry is Psionic's Intrusion Detection System (IDS) that also can act as an Adaptive Firewall. It is an excellent product that I have used with great success for several years. Though it is not GPL, individuals and companies may install it for internal use. It allows you to monitor a user-configurable list of TCP and UDP ports for attempted access by possible crackers. When a cracker knocks one of these monitored ports, a variety of actions can be taken, including notifying you of what system touched what port and locking system out of your network temporarily or permanently.

It is capable of detecting stealth attacks, such as the common cracker technique of sending the initial SYN packet, waiting for a SYN-ACK to indicate that a process is listening on the port, and never sending the third packet of the initial TCP three-way handshake, the ACK packet. As in physics, two objects may not occupy the same space. Therefore, you will not want PortSentry listening on a port where a “real” server will be bound to that same system.
