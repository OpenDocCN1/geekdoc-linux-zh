# # PROTECT YOUR PRIVACY

The government wants your data, but they don't understand tech. Facebook and Google want your data – and they do. **Jonni Bidwell** tries to disappoint them all.

![](bbox=[0.45, 0.12, 0.98, 0.52])

A s our lives increasingly take place online, traditional definitions of ‘privacy’ seem to be fast eroding. Instead of ‘what you do in private shouldn’t be anyone else’s business’ (unless you’re hurting someone), perhaps we should now think in terms of ‘suitably decorated government officials can access your data at any time, but don’t worry because they’ll only do that to prevent crimes’.

The UK’s disastrous Investigatory Powers Act, passed in 2016, goes some way to enshrining this dark pattern. ISPs are now obliged to keep a one-year record of everyone’s online activities, and they regularly interfere with DNS queries to enforce content blocks or satisfy other nebulous agendas. Most websites use encryption now (which frustrates efforts to eavesdrop on your browsing), but think about all the information you voluntarily hand over to social media titans and search engines, both of which we could reasonably

describe as ‘advertising platforms’, on a daily basis. We tell them where we are, what we had for breakfast, our political and moral ideologies, whether we prefer dogs or cats. *(cats! –Ed)* All this information can be used to profile you. Even if you stopped using social media – which comes with its own downsides – just using the web can, thanks to browser fingerprinting and dodgy extensions, allow these advertising platforms to track your activities across sites.

The Android-iOS duopoly further erodes traditional notions of privacy, since now your texts and calls (or at least the metadata about them) can all be correlated, via your AppID or Google account, with your activity on the interwebs. And let’s not forget the GPS module in your phone that gives these titans up-to-the-second data about your location. There’s no need to go full foil-hat (or ‘Tux tank’) to take back your privacy. A few simple tweaks to your online habits can go a long way, letting you communicate privately and keeping the snoop at bay.
