Fortunately, this particular hoax did not cause any actual damage to the infected machine. However, it did dissuade victims from using a valuable and efficient service, online tax return processing, thus causing a great deal of inconvenience for the victim. You should remember this when we discuss information warfare in Chapter 14, “Physical Security and Disaster Recovery.” This e-mail hoax was clearly designed to erode confidence in a government service.

# The W32.Torch Hoax

This hoax, like most others, causes no direct harm but can become a huge annoyance—the Internet equivalent of a prank call hoax. Unlike the dbmgre exe, it does not encourage you to delete files from your system. However it does induce a fair amount of concern in recipients. In this hoax message is stating the following:

# NEW VIRUS DESTROYS HARDWARE

A new virus found recently is capable of burning the CPU of some computers and even causing damage to the motherboard. Yes, it’s true, this virus damages the hardware of your computer. The virus, called w32.torch, uses the winbind w83781d chip, present in most modern motherboards, which is responsible for controlling the speed of the CPU and fans. The infection takes place using the well-known Microsoft DCOM net-trap Vulnerability, and when installed, the virus spreads to other computers in the local network using this method. Reverse-engineering the virus, we find no evidence of code other than that responsible for the CPU burnout. The virus turns off the high temperature detection in the BIOS (already disabled by default) and then decreases the speed of the fans, leading the system to a deadly increase of the internal temperature. If you feel your computer is becoming “quiet,” it’s better to check it because it may be stopping, and you may have only a few minutes left to disconnect it. The virus contains the text “Moscow Dominates - out/27/03” which is shown in the tray of some machines based on their IP address, perhaps indicating Soviet origin. There is no payload to activate on this date, but it may be an indication that something is supposed to happen on this day. Some antivirus already detect, check if yours is up to date.

To date, there have not been any viruses that directly damage hardware.

# Ransomware

It is impossible in modern times to discuss malware and not discuss ransomware. While many people first began discussing ransomware with the advent of CryptoLocker in 2103, ransomware has been around a lot longer than that. The first known ransomware was the 1989 PC Cyborg Trojan, which only encrypted files with a weak symmetric cipher. In early 2017 the WannaCry ransomware spread, starting in health care systems in the United Kingdom. It attacked unpatched Windows systems. This reiterated the need for patching, discussed in Chapter 8.

The Bad rabbit virus spread in late 2017. This virus is ransomware. It began attacking in Russia and Ukraine, but quickly spread around the world.
