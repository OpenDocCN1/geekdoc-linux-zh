# Virus Variations

Sometimes, some intrepidid programmer with malicious intent receives a copy of a virus (perhaps his or own machine becomes infected) and decides to reverse-engineer it. Many viruses are in the form of a script attached to an e-mail. This means that unlike traditional compiled programs, their source code is readily readable and alter. The programmer in question simply takes the original virus code, introduces some change, and then-releases the variant. The people who are most frequently caught creating viruses are the developers of variants who simply lack the skill of the original virus writer and are therefore easily caught.

> FYI: The Economic Impact of Viruses

Getting an exact accounting of the damage caused by Flame, FakeAV, MacDefender, or any other virus is impossible. However, if one considers the hours of IT professionals spend working on cleaning up a given virus, the worldwide cost of any virus is in the millions of dollars. The amount of money spent trying to defend against such viruses through antivirus software, hiring consultants, and purchasing books like this one is included, the amount of all viruses can easily reach billions of dollars. In fact, one study showed that in 2007 the economic damages from viruses exceeded $14 billion.

For this many security experts recommend that governments enact stricter penalties for virus creators. Federal law enforcement agencies taking a more active role in investigating these crimes would be helpful. For example, some, private companies such as Microsoft have begun offering substantial bounties for information leading to the arrest of virus creators, a very positive step.
