The reality is that many people who call themselves hackers are less knowledgeable than they think. Systems protected by even moderate precautions have a low probability of being compromised by a hacker of this skill level.

This does not mean that skilled hackers do not exist. They most certainly do. However, people with the skill to compromise relatively secure systems must use rather time-consuming and tedious techniques to breach system security. These hackers must also weigh the costs and benefits of any hacking mission. Skilled hackers target systems that have a high benefit, either financially or ideologically. If a system is not perceived as having benefit, a skilled hacker is less likely to expend the resources to compromise it. Burglars are one analogy: highly skilled burglars exist; however, they seek high-value targets. The thief who targets small businesses and has limited skills. The same is true of hackers.

> FYI: Skilled Versus Unskilled Hackers

Skilled hackers usually target highly attractive sites. Attractive sites offer valuable information or publicity. Military computers—even simple web servers with no classified information—offer a great deal of publicity. Banks, on the other hand, generally have very valuable information. Novice hackers usually start with a low value and, consequently, often less secure system. A college web server would be a good example. Although novice hackers' skills are not as well developed, their numbers are greater. Also, monetary gains are not the only factor that make a system attractive to a skilled hacker. If a hacker objects to an organization's ideological stance (for example, if an organization sells large sport utility vehicles that the feels is poor environmental policy), then they target their system.

Both extremes regarding the dangers to computer systems are inaccurate. It is certainly true that people who have both the comprehension of computer systems and the skill to compromise the security of many, if not most. However, it is also true that many who call themselves hackers are not as skilled as they claim. They have ascertained a few buzzwords from the Internet and are convinced of their own digital supremacy, but they are not able to express any real concerns to even a moderately secure system.

You might think that being on the side of caution, or extreme diligence, would be the appropriate approach. In reality, you do not need to take either view. You should take a realistic view of security and formulate practical strategies for defense. Every organization and IT department have finite resources: You only have so much time and money. If squander part of those resources guarding against unrealistic threats, then you might not have adequate left for more practical projects. Therefore, a realistic approach to network security is the only practical approach.
