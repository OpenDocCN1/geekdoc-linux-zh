# Port Scanner for Unix: SATAN

This is an old tool, one that has been quite popular for years with Unix administrators (as well as hackers) SATAN. This tool is not diabolical supernatural entity, but rather an acronym for Security Administrator Tool for Analyzing Networks. It can be downloaded for free from any number of websites. Many of these sites are listed at http://linux.softpea.com/progDownload/SATAN-Download-23306.html. This tool is strictly for Unix and does not work in Windows.

SATAN was created by Dan Farmer, author of COPS (Computer Oracle and Password System), and Wiese Verma, from the Eindhoven University of Technology in the Netherlands. It was originally released on April 5, 1995. It should be noted that SATAN, as well as many other probing tools, were originally used by hackers to find out about a target system. Over time, the more creative administrators began to use these tools for their own purposes. Clearly, if you wish to protect your system against intruders, it can be quite helpful to try the same tools that intruders use.

The user can enter either a single machine or an domain of machines to attack. There are three classes of attacks:

+   * Light: A light attack simply reports what hosts are available and Remote Procedure Call services hosts are running.

+   * Normal: A normal attack probes the targets by establishing various connections including telnet, FTP, WWW, wiper, and SMTP. These are used to discover what operating system the host is running and what vulnerabilities may be available.

+   * Heavy: A heavy attack includes everything that a normal attack does with the search for several other known vulnerabilities, such as anonymous FTP directories or trusted hosts.

The history of this particular product is quite illuminating. It began with the work of two scientists, Dan Farmer of Microsystems and Wiese Verma of Eindhoven University of Technology. They together published a paper titled "Improving the Quality of Your Site by Breaking Into It" (http://www.ucs.ed.ac.uk/home/rah/Security/admin_guide_to_cr.pdf). This is a very old paper. The technology in it may no longer be relevant, but the concept is. In it, they discussed using hacking techniques to break into your own system and discover its security flaws.
