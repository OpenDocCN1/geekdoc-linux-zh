Finally, COBIT includes maturity models. Maturity models examine any process from the point of view of how developed that process is. Essentially, each security process is first assessed to determine how mature that process is. Maturity is defined as how that is performing against objectives. Over time, the security process is evaluated to determine if it is maturing and improving. As an example, a policy regulating passwords might initially be developed based on generic guidelines. Then later, the policy could be revised in events within the organization, published standards, or increasing the understanding of the security personnel. This process would then be said to be maturing.

# ISO Standards

The International Organization for Standardization creates standards for a wide range of topics. There are hundreds of such standards, and it would be impossible to cover them in a single chapter of a single book. In fact each standard should be the subject of a book itself, or at least a few chapters. Some of the most important standards for network security are listed here:

* ISO/IEC 27005: Risk Management
* ISO/IEC 27006: Accredited Certification Standard
