Three factors are considered (attractiveness, information content, and security devices). Each of those factors is given a numeric designation between 1 and 10. # Hacking Terminology

Let’s begin by examining hacker terminology. Note that this terminology is not precise, and that many definitions can be debated. No “official” hacker vocabulary exists. The terms evolve through their use by the hacker community. beginning this examination by defining hacker, a term used in movies and news broadcasts, would be prudent. Most people use it to describe any person who breaks into a computer system. However, security professionals and hackers use this term differently. In the hacking community a hacker is an expert on a particular system or systems who want to learn more about the system. Hackers feel that looking at a system's flaws is the best way to learn about it.

For example, someone well-versed in the Linux operating system who works to understand that system by its weaknesses and flaws would be a hacker. However, this often mean seen whether a flaw can be exploited to gain access to a system. The “exploiting” part of the process is where hackers differentiate themselves into three groups:

- White hackers, upon finding vulnerability in a system, will report the vulnerability to the vendor of that system. For example, if they were to discover some flaw in Red Hat Linux, they would e-mail the Red Hat company (probably anonymously) and explain what the flaw is and how it was exploited.
- Black hackers are the people depicted in the media (e.g., movies and news). After they gain access to a system, their goal is to cause some type of harm. They might steal data, erase files, or delete websites. Black hackers are sometimes referred to as crackers.
- Gray hackers are typically law-abiding citizens, but in some cases venture into illegal activities. They might do so for a variety of reasons. Commonly, gray hackers conduct illegal activities for reasons they feel is ethical, such as hacking into a system belonging to a corporation that hacker feels is engaged in unethical activities. This term is not found in many textbooks, but is a commonly used term in the hacking community

Regardless of how hackers view themselves, intruding on any system without permission is illegal. This means that, technically speaking, all hackers, regardless of the color of the metaphor they wear, are in violation of the law. Many people feel that white hackers actually perform a service by finding flaws and informing vendors before they are exploited by less ethically individuals.

The various shades of hackers are the beginning of learning hacker terminology. Recall that if so, what is the term for someone who calls herself a hacker but lacks expertise? The most common term for an inexperienced hacker is script kiddie. The name derives from the fact that the Internet is full of utilities and scripts that one can download to perform hacking tasks. Whoever downloads these tools without really understanding the target system would be considered a script kiddie. A significant number of the people who call themselves hackers are, in reality, merely script kiddies.

This discussion brings us to some specific types of hackers. A cracker is someone whose goal is to compromise a system's security for purposes other than to learn the system. No difference exists between a black hacker and a cracker. Both terms refer to a person who breaks through a system's security and intrudes on that system without permission from the appropriate parties, with some malicious intent.

This is another specialized of hacker—the ethical hacker or sneaker (an older term, not often used days), a person who legally hacks/cracks a system in order to assess security deficiencies. Robert Redford, Dan Aykroyd, and Sydney Poite starred in a movie about this subject, Sneakers. Consultants who perform work of this type, and you can even find firms that specialize in this activity as more companies solicit these services to assess their vulnerabilities. Today, these are usually called penetration testers (or simply pen testers). and the profession has matured since the first edition of this book.

A word of caution for readers considering or becoming a pen tester: Any person hired to assess the vulnerabilities of a system must be technically proficient and morally sound. This means that a criminal background check should be done before engaging his/her services. You would certainly not hire a convicted burglar as your watchman. Neither would you consider someone with any criminal background, especially in computer crimes, as a penetration tester/ethical hacker. Some people argue that a convicted hacker/cracker has the best qualifications to assess your system’s vulnerabilities. This is not the case, for several reasons:

- You can find security professionals who know and understand hackers but have never committed any crime. You can get the skills to assess your system without using a consultant with a demonstrated lack of integrity.
- If you argue the argument that hiring convicted hackers means hiring talented people to logical conclusion, you could surmise the person in question is not as good as a hacker he would like to think, because he was caused.
- Most importantly, giving a person with a criminal background to your systems is comparable to hiring a person with multiple DWI convictions as your driver. In both cases you are inviting problems and, perhaps, assuming civil and criminal liabilities.

A review of a penetration tester’s qualifications is also recommended. Just as some falsely claim to be highly skilled hackers, there are those who will falsely claim to be skilled pen testers. An unqualified pen tester might pronounce your sound when in fact it was a lack of skill that prevented him from successfully breaching your security.
