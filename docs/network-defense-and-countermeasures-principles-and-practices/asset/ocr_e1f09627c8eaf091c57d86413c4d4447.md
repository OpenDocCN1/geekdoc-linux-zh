# CHAPTER 9 Defending Against Virus Attacks

# Antivirus Policies and Procedures

Examining how virus attacks spread and looking at specific attacks offers a clear picture of the dangers posed by viruses. We have also taken a look at how virus scanners work, including examining some commercial antivirus products. However, antivirus scanners are not the only facet of protecting yourself against viruses. In fact there are situations where a virus scanner is simply not enough. You will need policies and procedures to complete your antivirus strategy. Policies and procedures are simply written rules that dictate certain actions administrators and end users should take and other activities they should avoid. The following is a brief examination of appropriate antivirus policies. Policies will be discussed in greater detail in Chapter 11.

+   - Always use a virus scanner. McAfee, Norton, AVG, and Kaspersky are the four most widely known and used virus scanners. However, we have examined other solutions. It costs only $30 to a year to keep your virus updated. It can cost much more to not do it.

+   - If you are not sure about an attachment, do not open it. If you have specifically requested a file from someone, opening an attachment from that person is probably safe. However, unexpected attachments are always cause for concern.

+   - Consider exchanging a code with friends and colleagues. Tell them to use the code word if they wish to send you an attachment. Without the code word, do not open any attachment.

+   - Do not believe "security alerts" you are sent. Microsoft does not send out patches in this manner. Go check its website regularly, as well as one of the antivirus websites previously mentioned.

+   - Be skeptical of any e-mail you are sent. Keeping an e-mail to official traffic will help reduce your danger. Jokes, flash movies, and so simply should not be sent on a company e-system.

+   - Do not download files from the Internet. If you need a file downloaded, the IT department should do that, carefully scan the file, and then forward it. Now clearly many people will choose to download files, so this admonition is an ideal that is unlikely to be realized. If you feel compelled to download you should follow two simple rules:

+   1. Only download from well-known, reputable sites.

+   2. Download to a machine that is not the network first. Then you can scan that system for viruses. In fact, if you request your IT department download something for you, this is likely to be the process they use.

These policies will not make a system 100 % virus proof, but they will go a long way in protecting it. Feel free to expand upon them any way you see fit.
