# Understanding Security Terminology

When and why would someone give permission to another party to hack/crack a system? The most common reason is to assess the system’s vulnerabilities. This is another specialized of hacker—the ethical hacker or sneaker (an older term, not often used days), a person who legally hacks/cracks a system in order to assess security deficiencies. Robert Redford, Dan Aykroyd, and Sydney Poite starred in a movie about this subject, Sneakers. Consultants who perform work of this type, and you can even find firms that specialize in this activity as more companies solicit these services to assess their vulnerabilities. Today, these are usually called penetration testers (or simply pen testers). and the profession has matured since the first edition of this book.

A word of caution for readers considering or becoming a pen tester: Any person hired to assess the vulnerabilities of a system must be technically proficient and morally sound. This means that a criminal background check should be done before engaging his/her services. You would certainly not hire a convicted burglar as your watchman. Neither would you consider someone with any criminal background, especially in computer crimes, as a penetration tester/ethical hacker. Some people argue that a convicted hacker/cracker has the best qualifications to assess your system’s vulnerabilities. This is not the case, for several reasons:

+   * You can find security professionals who know and understand hackers but have never committed any crime. You can get the skills to assess your system without using a consultant with a demonstrated lack of integrity.
* If you argue the argument that hiring convicted hackers means hiring talented people to logical conclusion, you could surmise the person in question is not as good as a hacker he would like to think, because he was caused.
* Most importantly, giving a person with a criminal background to your systems is comparable to hiring a person with multiple DWI convictions as your driver. In both cases you are inviting problems and, perhaps, assuming civil and criminal liabilities.

A review of a penetration tester’s qualifications is also recommended. Just as some falsely claim to be highly skilled hackers, there are those who will falsely claim to be skilled pen testers. An unqualified pen tester might pronounce your sound when in fact it was a lack of skill that prevented him from successfully breaching your security. Chapter 12 covers the basics of assessing a target system as well as the necessary qualifications of any consultant hired for this purpose.

Another specialized branch of hacking involves hacking telephone systems. This sub-field of hacking is referred to as phaking. The New Hackers Dictionary accurately defines phaking as the action of using mischievous and mostly illegal ways in order to not pay for some of telecommunications bill, order, transfer, or other service" (Raymond, 2003). Phaking requires a significant knowledge of telecommunications, and many phakers have some professional experience working for a phone company or other telecommunications business.
