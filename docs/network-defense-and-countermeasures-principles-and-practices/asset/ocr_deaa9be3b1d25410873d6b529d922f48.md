Also keep in mind that a few jurisdictions have passed laws requiring that in order to extract the evidence investigator must be either a law enforcement officer or a licensed private investigator. This law is controversial, given private investigator training and licensing normally does not include forensics training. You should check with specifics in your state. Many of those states will allow you to forensically examine computers if you have the permission of the owner. So, this will not prohibit you from forensically examining computers in your company. However, forensics has become an integral part of response to network intrusions, so much so that the field is often called Digital Forensics Incident Response (DFIR).

# # General Forensics Guidelines

You should follow some general guidelines in any forensic examination. You want to have as little impact on the evidence as possible, which means you want to examine it and not alter it. You want to have a clear document trail for everything that is done. Of course, you also want to secure your evidence.

# # EU Evidence Gathering

The Council of Europe Convention on Cybercrime, also called the Budapest Convention on Cybercrime or simply Budapest Convention, refers to electronic evidence as evidence that can be collected in electronic form of a criminal offense.

The Council of Europe's Electronic Evidence Guide is a basic guide for police officers, prosecutors, and judges.

The EU also has principles that establish a basis for all dealings with electronic evidence:

+   - **Principle 1: Data Integrity**: You must ensure that the data is valid and has not been corrupted.
- **Principle 2: Audit Trail**: Similar to the concept of custody, you must be able to account for the evidence. That includes its location as well as what was done with it.
- **Principle 3: Specialist Support**: As needed, utilize specialists. For example, if you are a skilled forensic examiner but have limited experience with a Macintosh computer, get a Mac specialist who needs to examine a Mac.
- **Principle 4: Appropriate Training**: All forensic examiners and analysts should be fully trained and always expanding their knowledge base.
- **Principle 5: Legality**: Make certain all evidence is collected and handled in a manner consistent with all applicable laws.

If you don't work within the European Union, these guidelines can be quite useful. Yes, they are rather broad, but they provide guidance as to how to properly conduct a forensic examination.
