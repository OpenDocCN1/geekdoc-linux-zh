# Other Port Scanners

There are many more port scanners and security tools available on the Internet, a few of which are listed here:

+   - Like Active Ports, Fport reports all TCP/IP and UDP ports and map them to the owning application. Additionally, it maps those to running processes. Fport can be used to quickly identify unknown ports and their associated applications. This product is available at https://www.macaffee.com/us/downloads/free-tools/fport.aspx.

+   - TCPView is a Windows program that will show you listings of all TCP and UDP endpoints on your system, including the remote address and the state of TCP connections. TCPView provides a conveniently presented subset of the NetStat program. TCPView is available free of charge at https://docs.microsoft.com/en-us/internals/downloads/tcpview.

+   - SuperScan is a port scanner originating from Foundation Inc, now distributed by McAfee. It is available as a free download at https://sectools.org/tool/superscan/. This particular scanner gives its report in HTML format. What is most interesting about SuperScan is the wide variety of tools also available at that same website, including tools that scan for any number of very vulnerabilities. Exploring this website is well worth your time.

The specific port scanner you use is often more of a personal preference than anything else. The best approach is to use three or four separate scanners to ensure that you are checking all the possible vulnerabilities. Using more than three or four scanners provides limited incremental benefits and can be very time consuming. I would definitely recommend that Cerberus be one of the scanners you use. You may also wish to fully test your password with some of the password crackers we mentioned in Chapter 6, "Encryption Fundamentals," to ensure that your passwords cannot be easily cracked.

More security-savvy network administrators will use these tools on their servers, just to check security. Full-time security professionals should try to stay ahead of the hacking community, and may even use the same tools as hackers. This is a proactive and important step for a network administrator to take.
