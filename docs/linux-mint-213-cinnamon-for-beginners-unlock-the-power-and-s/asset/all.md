INTRODUCTION

Welcome to the Linux Mint 21.3 Cinnamon operating system tutorial!

If you have ever considered before, to give the Linux operating system a go, on your computer, you have been thinking about something very good and useful!

If you are someone, who has been using the Micro$oft Windows operating system, for some time, perhaps for years, and have decided to swap to Linux, you are going in the right direction. Why? Because using Linux on your computer, instead of Windows, has many benefits like:

●       Linux is a free of charge to download and use

●       Linux is a stable and secure operating system

● You can do on Linux all those most common computer tasks like, browsing the Internet, watching YouTube videos, listening to music, playing games, watching your own videos, manipulating and browsing photos, typing and printing text documents, Email, and much more…

● Linux Mint comes with more than 60 000 free applications, waiting for you to download them and use them on your computer.

● Linux Mint is one of the most popular and most used Linux operating system in the world.

● Linux is nearly free from viruses, so you will not be worrying about a virus on you computer, and you do not even need to install an antivirus application, on your computer. The author of this tutorial has been using Linux for many, many years, various Linux versions, and he never needed to install an antivirus application, on his computer, and during all those years, he never had any virus, on his Linux computers, as much as he is aware.

● Linux Mint is based on the Ubuntu Linux, another most popular operating system in the world, which is used, in businesses and at home.

Linux Mint is easy to use, even if you are a beginner of Linux, especially, if you have already been using, the Micro$oft Windows operating system.

INSTALLATION PART 1

Making a bootable flash drive. You may be wondering, what is a bootable USB flash drive? Well, it is an USB empty disk, which needs to be with about 4 GB of empty space on it, which we will use, to download from the Internet Linux Mint 21.3 Cinnamon, and install that downloaded Linux Mint 21.3 Cinnamon file, on the USB flash drive, so that we can latter, plug that bootable USB flash drive with the downloaded Linux Mint 21.3 Cinnamon on it, and install Linux Mint 21.3 Cinnamon, from that USB flash drive, on the computer, which we have prepared already, to install Linux Mint 21.3 Cinnamon on that computer.

Do not worry, if you think it is difficult to do that! If you follow all the steps here, which I describe in this tutorial, then you will be able to download Linux Mint 21.3 Cinnamon and install it on your computer, in no time!

Refer to Part 2 of the Installation process, which is described, latter on, in this tutorial….

INSTALLATION PART 2

MAKING BOOTABLE DISK

First I will show you, how to create, the bootable USB flash drive, with Linux Mint 21.3 Cinnamon on it. You need to make a bootable USB flash drive, and by installing the Linux Mint 21.3 Cinnamon ISO file on it. Once that is done, you can plug that bootable USB flash drive into the USB port of your computer, restart your computer, and the Linux Mint 21.3 Cinnamon, will start loading. Once it has loaded to the Desktop, there is an installation icon on the Desktop, which if you click on it, will start installing the Linux Mint 21.3 Cinnamon, from the bootable USB flash drive, on to your computer. But now, let us make the bootable USB flash drive first, by following the steps bellow.

● You will be using a free tool, called ‘BalenaEtcher’. Just visit the following website: [https://www.balena.io/etcher](https://www.balena.io/etcher) and download the free program, on your computer. You do not even have to install the ‘BalenaEtcher’, once you download it, on your computer, from the Internet. If you are using a Windows or a Linux computer, on which you will download the ‘BalenaEtcher’ free program, all you need to do, in order to use it, is to click on the ‘BalenaEtcher’ program you have downloaded, and in a few seconds, it will be opened and ready to use.

● Here is a screenshot, of how ‘BalenaEtcher’ program looks like, once it is opened. Look at the following image bellow.

![](image_rsrc27J.jpg)

● As you can see from the image above, it has only three easy steps, in order to make a bootable USB flash drive, by using the ‘BalenaEtcher’ program tool. Once you have downloaded the Linux Mint 21.3 Cinnamon, from the Linux Mint 21.3 Cinnamon official website, on your computer, as an ISO file, all you have to do, is to follow the three easy steps, in the ‘BalenaEtcher’ program, as you see above on the image. Do not worry what that ‘ISO’ file means! It is just the extension of the Linux Mint 21.3 Cinnamon file, which you will download, from the Linux Mint official website.

● The first step as you can see in the blue color, on the ‘BalenaEtcher’ program image above, which says ‘Flash from file’ is the blue button, which you click on, in order to select, the Linux Mint 21.3 Cinnamon operating system file, which you have downloaded, from the Internet, already. Once you do that, you continue to the second step, in making the bootable USB flash drive.

● The second step is, to select the USB flash drive, which you are using, to make it a bootable USB flash drive with Linux Mint 21.3 Cinnamon on it. So, just click on the ‘Select target’, in the ‘BalenaEtcher’ program, you have already opened, and then, chose the USB flash drive, which you are going to make bootable, from the options.

● The next step is, to click on the ‘Flash’ button, in the opened ‘BalenaEtcher’ program. Let it ‘cook on slow fire’, for a few minutes, or in other words, let it do what it is supposed to do, for a few minutes, and voila, you have made a bootable USB flash drive, with the Linux Mint 21.3 Cinnamon operating system on it. Now, that bootable USB flash drive, with Linux Mint 21.3 Cinnamon on it, is what is called a live operating system, meaning, that bootable USB flash drive, with the Linux Mint 21.3 Cinnamon on it, can be plugged into any computer, and you can run the Linux Mint 21.3 Cinnamon from the USB flash drive, and use it, like any other operating system which is already installed on any computer. It is like Windows operating system, which is on any computer, ready to be used, for the various tasks you desire. The only difference is, that the Linux Mint 21.3 Cinnamon operating system, which is on the bootable USB flash drive, which you have made, is only run live, from that bootable USB flash drive, and is NOT yet installed, on any computer, even not yet on your computer, on which can run it live. It runs live on the computer where the bootable USB flash drive is plugged into, and is not installed yet, on the computer. You just switch on the computer, with the bootable USB flash disk, already plugged into the computer, and the computer will start Linux Mint 21.3 Cinnamon, from that bootable USB flash drive, in a live mode, even if you have already Microsoft Windows, installed on that computer. Do not worry, running the Linux Mint 21.3 Cinnamon live, from the bootable USB flash drive, will NOT affect, change or do, anything on any other operating system, you have on that same computer, like for example, Windows existing on that computer already. Everything you do with that bootable USB flash drive, with Linux Mint 21.3 Cinnamon on it, is done in that flash drive only. And of course, you can at any time, install the Linux Mint 21.3 Cinnamon from the bootable USB flash drive, on the computer, any time you chose to do so.

Once you click on the website link bellow, your computer will start to download the Linux Mint 21.3 Cinnamon ISO installation file, on your computer. ![](image_rsrc27K.jpg)

![](image_rsrc27M.jpg)

●       Chose your preferred keyboard layout, and click on the Continue button.

![](image_rsrc27N.jpg)

![](image_rsrc27P.jpg)

USING LINUX MINT 21.3 CINNAMON

MAIN MENU

●       Let us start with showing you, how to use the Main Menu.

● Click on the Linux Mint logo icon, located at the bottom left corner of the screen, in order to open the Main Menu.

![](image_rsrc27V.jpg)

● When you want to shut down the computer, restart the computer, or just put the computer to sleep, click on the Quit red shortcut icon, located in the Favorites, in the Main Menu, at the left side of the open Main Menu.

![](image_rsrc27W.jpg)

● From the open window, chose what you want to do, to restart your computer, to suspend your computer, or to shut down your computer, by clicking on one of the buttons.

![](image_rsrc27X.jpg)

● If you just want to logout from your desktop, click on the Logout shortcut icon, located in the Favorites section, of the Main Menu, at the left side.

![](image_rsrc27Y.jpg)

● But let us say, you do not want to switch off your computer, you just want to lock the desktop screen.

● Click on the Lock screen shortcut icon, located in the Favorites section, of the Main Menu, at the left side.

![](image_rsrc27Z.jpg)

● The files and folders on your computer, can be accessed, by opening the files manager called Files.

● To open the Files file manager, click on the yellow Files shortcut folder icon, located in the Favorites section, of the open Main Menu, on the left side.

![](image_rsrc280.jpg)

● The System Settings, can be accessed, by clicking on the System Settings shortcut icon, located in the Favorites section, of the Main Menu, at the left side of the Main Menu.

![](image_rsrc281.jpg)

● When you just want to browse the Internet, click on the Firefox browser shortcut icon, located at the top of the Favorites section, in the Main Menu.

![](image_rsrc282.jpg)

● The applications categories shortcuts, are placed on the left side, of the Main Menu. The applications sub-categories, are placed in the sub-menu, on the right side of the Main Menu.

● For example, click on the Main Menu to open it. Once opened, click on the Internet applications category, in the Main Menu. In the sub-menu on the right side, of the Main Menu, you can see all those Internet applications, which are under the Internet applications category.

![](image_rsrc283.jpg)

● If you want to see all the installed on your computer applications, click to open the Main Menu.

● On the top of the Main Menu, click on the All Applications icon. On the right side, of the Main Menu, you will see all the installed applications, in the sub-menu. Just scroll down in the sub-menu, to see all of them.

![](image_rsrc284.jpg)

● Now, there is a folder category shortcut, in the Main Menu, called Places. When you click on that folder looking icon, in the Main Menu, you will see in the sub-menu, shortcuts to the various places on your computer, like the Documents folder, the Music folder, Pictures folder, the Home folder, and other places.

● Open the Main Menu, and click on the Places category shortcut, and look at the various places on your computer, in the sub-menu.

![](image_rsrc285.jpg)

● In this step, we will look into how to place an application shortcut, on the Panel. Remember the Panel is that bar, at the bottom of your desktop, on which are places various applications shortcut, the Task Bar, and others.

● Let us say for example, that you want to place a shortcut, of your Email application, called Thunderbird Mail, on the Panel.

●       Click on the Main Menu to open it.

●       Click on the Internet applications category, in the Main Menu.

● In the sub-menu, right click with the right mouse button, on the Thunderbird Mail application shortcut.

●       From the drop down menu, click on the Add to panel option.

![](image_rsrc286.jpg)![](image_rsrc287.jpg)

● Now, if you look at the left side, of the Panel, you will the Thunderbird Mail shortcut, which is ready to be clicked, and to open the Thunderbird Mail application.

![](image_rsrc288.jpg)

● There are a few grouped together, applications shortcuts, on the left side of the Panel. If you want to remove those grouped together applications shortcuts from the Panel, right click with the right mouse button, over those grouped applications icons shortcuts.

● From the popup menu, click on the Remove ‘Grouped window list’ option. That will remove all those grouped together applications icons, from the Panel.

![](image_rsrc289.jpg)

● You now know how to add an application shortcut, to the Panel. But what if you want to add an application shortcut, to the Desktop? This step will show you to do, exactly that.

●       Click on the Main Menu to open it.

●       Click on the Internet applications category.

● Right click with the right mouse button, over the Thunderbird Mail icon, in the sub-menu of all the Internet applications.

●       Click on the Add to desktop option, in the drop down menu.

![](image_rsrc28A.jpg)

● Now you can see, that the Thunderbird Mail shortcut icon, has been added to the Desktop.

![](image_rsrc28B.jpg)

● You can remove the Thunderbird Mail shortcut, from the Desktop, by right clicking, with the right mouse button, over the icon, and then, from the drop down menu, click on the Move to Trash option.

![](image_rsrc28C.jpg)

● And of course, you can add the Thunderbird Mail shortcut icon, to the Favorites.

●       To do that, click on the Main Menu, to open it.

●       Click on the Internet applications category.

● In the sub-menu, right click on the Thunderbird Mail icon, with the right mouse button.

●       From the drop down menu, click on the Add to Favorites option.

![](image_rsrc28D.jpg)

● Now the Thunderbird Mail shortcut icon, has been added, to the Favorites section, of the Main Menu.

![](image_rsrc28E.jpg)

● When you want to remove the Thunderbird Mail shortcut icon, from the Favorites section, just right click with the right mouse button, over the Thunderbird Mail icon, in the sub-menu, of the Internet applications category, and then, from the drop down menu, click on the Remove from Favorites option.

![](image_rsrc28F.jpg)

PANEL

● It is good for you, if you are beginner of Linux, to know how to properly use the Panel. Of course, it is good for you to know how to use every part of the Linux operating system, but I emphasize the Panel, because like the Desktop, of which the Panel is a part of, it is what you will use most of the time, when you use your Linux Mint 21.3 Cinnamon computer.

● If for example you right click with the right mouse button, over the folder icon, in the grouped icons on the left side of the Panel, which is the icon for the File Manager, you will see a menu popup.

● From that popup menu, you can access various places on your computer, access various recent files, or just configure some settings.

![](image_rsrc28M.jpg)

● You can get a preview of an open application window, by just placing the mouse pointer, over one of the grouped icons on the left side of the Panel. Do not click on the icon, just place the mouse pointer over it, and you will see a little preview window, popup. From that little preview window popup, you can also close that application, by clicking on the Close red button, in the preview window.

![](image_rsrc28N.jpg)![](image_rsrc28P.jpg)

● When you right click with the right mouse button, over an empty space on the Panel, you will see a menu popup.

● From that popup menu, you can move the Panel, on another place on the Desktop, you can remove the Panel, and you can add some Applets to the Panel. Also, you can configure the Panel, or add a second Panel to the Desktop.

● Only make sure that first, you click on the Panel edit mode button, in that popup menu, so that you can do all those things, mentioned above, with the Panel.

![](image_rsrc28R.jpg)

● The red lines you see, on the edges of the screen, are the places on the Desktop, where you can move the Panel to, by dragging with with the mouse pointer, after you have enabled the Edit Mode for the Panel. You can move the Panel, by clicking on the Move option, in the popup menu, mentioned in the previous step.

![](image_rsrc28S.jpg)

● In the following image bellow, you can see that the Panel, has been moved to the top edge of the Desktop screen.

![](image_rsrc28T.jpg)

● To see the time and dates, just click on the time icon, shown in the bottom right side of the Panel.

![](image_rsrc28U.jpg)

● The Volume can be changed, by clicking on the Volume icon, located at the right side of the Panel.

![](image_rsrc28V.jpg)

● The screen brightness, can be adjusted, just by clicking on the Power icon, located on the right side of the Panel. From that Power icon, you can also access the Power Settings.

![](image_rsrc28W.jpg)

● When you want to pin an application shortcut icon, to the Panel, once you open that application, you can see that open application icon, displayed on the Panel. The icon is displayed, but is not pinned to the Panel yet.

● To pin that open application shortcut icon, to the Panel, just right click with the right mouse button, over that application shortcut icon, and then, from the popup menu, click on the Pin to Panel option. An example is shown bellow, of the Firefox browser being open, and its shortcut icon, being pinned, to the Panel.

![](image_rsrc28X.jpg)

● But there will be times, when you will want to unpin, an application shortcut icon, from the Panel. To do so, right click on the already pinned application shortcut icon, on the Panel, with the right mouse button, and then, from the popup menu, click on the Unpin from Panel option.

![](image_rsrc28Y.jpg)

● There is a little space on the very right side edge of the Panel, which if clicked, will hide all opened applications window, from the Desktop, so you can see the Desktop. If you click on that space again, on the right edge of the Panel, all those hidden windows, will be visible again.
