# # Setting up Your Modem

We're going to take some time out at the very beginning to set up your modem. In this day and age, it's almost impossible to navigate your way through the computer world without some use of the Internet. And to get on the Internet, you need to use a modem. The modem is one of the most-used devices on the system, and one of the most basic devices that you and anyone else who uses your computer will want to use. That's why we're going to tackle setting it up first.

# ## Compatibility

As with most devices, you must first make sure that your modem is compatible with Linux. To do this, you should check Red Hat's web site and look at the compatibility list. You can assume that your modem does **not** work if you have a combination modem/sound card (such as IBM's MWave) or a modem specifically designed to work with Windows (most winmodems). Modems that require extra software to enable compression options probably won't work either.

Again, you should see if you can find a patch for your modem from your vendor or from a site like Sourceforge. If this fails, you should invest in a Linux-compatible modem. These are generally quite inexpensive—you should be able to buy a 33.6 kbps modem for under forty dollars.

# ## Using Modemtool

To set up your modem, we're going to access the modemtool command from the control panel. Open the control panel as directed above and then click on the icon that has a telephone on it.

The window that comes up will look like the picture to the left of these paragraphs.

The ports are labeled as TTY50, TTY51, TTY52, TTY53 for COM1, COM2, COM3, and COM4, respectively. You should select the port that matches the one on your modem. Then, click on okay to complete the setup.
