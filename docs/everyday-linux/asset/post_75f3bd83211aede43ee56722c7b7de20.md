## Spell Checking, Find and Replace, Cut and Paste

There are several ways that you can check spelling in a document. You can wait until you are through writing a document and then click “tools,” “spellcheck,” and “check.” When StarWriter checks a document like this it will scan through the entire document and then bring up a spellcheck dialog window when it finds an error.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_215_0.png)

The dialog window will show the word in question, and its suggestions (if it has any) for the correct spelling. On the right are buttons which allow you to "ignore," "ignore all," "replace," and "always replace." Ignore all will ignore all occurrences it finds of that particular word in that document. Replace all will replace all occurrences it finds of the misspelled word. You also have the choice to add a word to the dictionary.

The other way to spellcheck is to do it as you type. By clicking "tools," "spellcheck," and then "autospellcheck," you can see a misspelled word as soon as you type it. When you misspell a word, Star Office will place a squiggly red line underneath the word. You can then click on the word with the right mouse button and see suggestions for correct spelling, ignore all, or to add the word to the dictionary.

Find and replace can be used to replace a word which is not misspelled with another word. Suppose you have written a personal letter to someone and then realize that you have used the wrong name and typed "Burgers" instead of "Burgess." If you have used the name multiple times, it can be very time consuming to go back through the document and hunt and replace the names. You will find it much easier to choose "edit" and then "find and replace."

In the find and replace window, simply type in the word you want it to find and then the word you want to replace it with.

You might also want to move blocks of text around and this also is quite easy. You can select the text by clicking the left mouse button at the beginning and dragging the mouse to the end of the text, or you can use the shift key with the arrow keys. Place the cursor at the beginning of the text you want to select. Hold the shift key down and then copy entire lines by using the arrow down or up keys, or copy a character at a time by using the right and left arrow keys. The text you select will be highlighted.

Now that the text is selected you may click “edit” and then “cut” or “copy.” Use cut if you want to move the text from where it is and paste it somewhere else. Use copy if you want to copy the text to another location. After doing that, place the cursor where you want to put the text and then click “edit” and “paste.”

## Adding an Image to Your Document

You can add images and clipart to any StarWriter document very easily. Place the cursor where you would like the image to be and then click “insert,” then “graphics,” and then “from file.”

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_218_0.png)

**Figure 12-7**

*Inserting a Graphic.*

You will see a preview of the graphics you click on, and once the image is loaded into your document you can resize it or move it around to just where you want it.

> **Tip**
>
> Remember that adding larger images will add to the size of the document. Adding two or three images can make a huge bulky document.

## Printing Your Document

We have already covered how to set up your printer in Star Office. When you want to print a document, simply click on the printer icon at the top of the screen. You can also click “file” and “print.”

If you want to review your document before printing, click “file” and then “page preview.” StarWriter will show you a view of the entire page as it will look.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_220_0.png)

*Page Preview.*

The print will likely be too small for you to see, but you will be able to see how many pages your document is and get an idea of how it will look printed.

## Importing and Exporting Files

StarWriter can import a wide range of documents including MS Word 95/97/2000, Excel, Lotus 1-2-3, Mac text, DOS text and much more. You should have no problem loading documents from a Microsoft operating system. Occasionally there can be some formatting recognition problems, but most of the time it works fine.

To import a different format, click “file” and “open.” Usually StarWriter will recognize the format and use the correct filter to import. Sometimes it will, however, ask you for which filter to use. StarWriter will then pop up a list of filters from which you choose the correct document type.

Likewise, StarWriter can export documents in a wide variety of formats. Use the “save as” feature and then choose the format you wish to save it as in the “file type” box. StarWriter makes it very easy to exchange documents with people using different operating systems and word processors.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_221_0.png)

> If you need to give someone a file and you are in doubt as to what file types they can handle, you can always save as plain text (.txt). Just about any editor can open these files. Unfortunately, any formatting or images will be lost as plain text, but the text will be readable.

## Using Auto Pilot to Import and Export Multiple MS Documents

If you have a large number of MS Word documents, and/or templates which you would like to have imported and then exported as StarWriter (.sdw) documents and templates, there is an auto pilot which can do this automatically.

Choose “file,” “auto pilot” and then “Microsoft Import.”

The first screen that comes up just explains what this auto pilot does. Keep in mind, this does not delete any files, it just adds a copy of an MS Word document exported as a StarWriter document. You end up with two copies of the same document, one remaining an MS Word document and the other .sdw format. When you are ready, click “continue.”

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_222_0.png)

**Figure 12-11**

*Using Auto Pilot to Import a Document.*

Here you are asked whether Auto Pilot should get both MS Word templates and Word documents. It also asks you to tell it which directory it should look in. Click on the little box to the right to browse and select a directory. On the left, you will also be asked whether it should include subdirectories. At the bottom of the window you are asked where Auto Pilot should put the exported documents. Again, you can click on the little box on the right to browse directories. When you are finished, click “continue.”
