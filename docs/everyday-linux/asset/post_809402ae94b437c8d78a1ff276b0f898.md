# Foreword

Linux is everywhere! Linux is everywhere! In today's computer trade press every other story seems to be about Linux surging into yet another niche in the computing world—Internet servers, network appliances, embedded systems, graphics workstations, cellphones, and even (in one recent IBM announcement) wristwatches. These days Linux seems to be ubiquitous, from handhelds to distributed supercomputers.

There is just one exception, so far, to the otherwise inexorable trend: the ordinary end-user desktop. People who track these things estimate that Linux is at about 4% to 5% of the market on desktop computers as of late 2000 — comparable to the Macintosh's market share but pretty small potatoes compared to Microsoft's 91% monopoly. And that's sad, because Linux has huge advantages over Windows, starting with its price (which is low or free), and it's rapidly getting into more serious features like the utter banishment of the Blue Screen of Death.

Conventional wisdom has it that Linux is doomed to a niche role on the desktop because it's too difficult for Aunt Tillie to run. But the days when Linux was really more complex to administer than a Windows machine are long past. In the last three years, the open-source community has made enormous strides in simplifying installation and normal housekeeping and presenting it through graphical user interface—to the point where it's really quite a bit easier over time to maintain a Linux box than a Windows machine, whether you're an expert techie or not.

The real problem is that most of the ordinary people who could benefit from Linux don't know how easy it is. And that's the problem Ethan Metsger and Kathy Miles have set out to solve. With gentle introductions and step-by-step explanations, they demystify Linux and build the reader's confidence.

Help stamp out bad software in your lifetime! You can use this book to escape the Microsoft monopoly's predatory clutch, and turn your computer more to being an enjoyable tool and less of an irritation. Enjoy! And spread the word to your friends.

*Eric S. Raymond*

# What Is Linux?

Linux is an *operating system*. An operating system is the software that runs your computer and controls how various elements of it act. For instance, an operating system recognizes that you can pass data between your computer and monitor or printer, and allows the programs on your computer to do so.

Most personal computers today use the Windows operating system, created and maintained by Microsoft Corporation. The next contender is the Macintosh OS, or MacOS, though it owns only a small portion of the market share.

More recently, with the advent of better marketing by Apple Computers, the evidence brought against Microsoft indicating actions that violate United States anti-trust laws, and the activities of the Open Source community, Microsoft has experienced more competition, much of which is vested in the form of the Linux operating system.

Most Windows users I'm acquainted with are frustrated with using Windows—its programs are mammoth, often expensive, and they frequently crash. It is an industry expectation that each version of Microsoft will have enough bugs to warrant a "second edition" or some other program to patch the holes in the system. And with recent e-mail viruses targeting Windows programs like Microsoft Outlook, users are more aware than ever that security is not Windows' strong point.

Enter Linux.
