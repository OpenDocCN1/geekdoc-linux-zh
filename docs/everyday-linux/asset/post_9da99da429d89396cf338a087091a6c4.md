## Gnibbles

Gnibbles is an entertaining game involving a wandering worm. The object of the game is to eat up the diamonds that appear on the screen without crashing into the sides of the box surrounding your worm. As play goes on, the worm gets longer, making it a new challenge not to crash into yourself as well as the box sides. You use the arrow keys to move your worm about the board.

As levels increase, so do the number of diamonds and obstacles you must avoid.

There are several skill levels, ranging from “Nibbles Newbie” to “Finger Twitching Good.”

## Gnome Stones

Gnome Stones is just like an old favorite video game, Boulderdash. The game involves moving about caves and other areas, and collecting the diamonds and avoiding the boulders. If you tunnel directly under a boulder, it will fall on you and kill you. You also must be careful to move quickly when tunneling near a stack of boulders as they will fall on you.

You move around in Gnome Stones by using the arrow keys. You have a limited amount of time to get all the diamonds, and then you must find the exit and move on to the next level.

## Gnotravex

Gnotravex is a game of skill where you are given a board of nine spaces and nine blocks with four numbers on each. You must place all the blocks in the nine spaces so that all the numbers match up on all sides. With only nine spaces it isn't too difficult, but the game allows for up to 36 blocks! Try that one!

## GTali

Gnome Tali is a GNOME version of the popular board game Yahtzee. It is more or less like poker with dice. The game comes with full instructions found by clicking "help."

The game default is for one human to play against three computer characters. You may change the defaults to have additional human players or fewer computer characters.

## XBoing II

If you are good at ping pong, you will probably like Xboing. This game provides you with rows of targets which you must hit and destroy with the ball you can bounce off your paddle. Use the mouse to initially release the ball, and then bounce it off the paddle by moving the mouse back and forth. You may also fire shots at the targets by clicking either right or left mouse button.

## XJewel

XJewel is a game where blocks with three colors each are dropped one at a time. The blocks will stack up and the objective is to clear space by lining up three or more of the same colors in a row or diagonally. Blank blocks will remove all of whatever color they land on. Move the blocks sideways with the arrow keys. Use the down arrow key to drop a block quickly. The arrow up key will rotate the colors on the block.

## Sokoban

Sokoban is a cute game of strategy where the player has to move red rubies from one area to an end point. The rubies may only be pushed forward and only if the space directly in front of it is vacant.

The easiest way to move around the play area is to use the arrow keys. To undo a move, click the right mouse button.

There are multiple levels to the game. You must complete one level to move to the next.

## Mahjongg

The goal of Mahjongg is to remove all the tiles from the board. This is accomplished by using the mouse and clicking on matching tiles that are available for play.

Tiles available for play are on the outside edges and do not have tiles on top of them.

Mahjongg allows multiple undo moves.

There is another Mahjongg game found under the main games menu, but we liked this one's animation better!

## Minesweeper

The goal of Minesweeper is to uncover all the squares without clicking on a bomb. There are ten bombs hidden under the squares which you need to flag. To uncover a square, click on the left mouse button. To flag a bomb use the right mouse button.

Under some squares you will find a number. The number will tell you how many bombs are touching that square. You must deduce where the bombs are. For example, if you uncover a square with a number one, it means that it touches one bomb. This may mean above, below, sideways or diagonally.

Some squares have no number, and when you click on that square, it will turn over all other unnumbered squares near it.

## Patience

For those who like cards, Patience offers the classic Klondike as well as eight other solitaire games. There are some options, with rules for play and card decorations.

There isn’t much for directions on playing the various games, so you must know beforehand how to play.

## Reversi

Reversi is a game of strategy. You play against the computer in this game. The board comes up with two red and two blue balls. The goal is to change the opponents’ balls to your color. This is accomplished by using one of your balls to jump over the opponents’ balls which changes the opponent’s balls, to your color.

Reversi comes with full documentation. Click on “help” and then “contents.” You will find rules of the game as well as some strategy hints.

## Other Games

As Linux grows in popularity, more and more games will become available. There are additional games which are available now, some for free and some commercially. One additional free game we thought worth mentioning was xjig.

## Xjig

If you like jigsaw puzzles you will fall in love with xjig. Xjig takes a .gif image and creates a jigsaw puzzle out of it. You may specify the number of pieces and even create a double-sided puzzle if you like!

Xjig does not come with Red Hat, but is available as an RPM package from the Red Hat website. You can download it and install it with GnorPM. Instructions for using GnorPM and installing new programs are in the “Maintaining Your System” chapter.

Xjig unfortunately uses a command-line interface, but you can work around this by creating an applet in GNOME. Instructions for this are available in the GNOME chapter.

The commands used for xjig are available in the man pages. From a terminal, type “man xjig.” You must do this after you have installed the program. We use an example of creating a medium-sized puzzle.

This was the original image. We created the puzzle by going to a command line and typing this command:

```
xjig -file puzzle.gif -side n -w 16 -h 26
```

The result was a jigsaw puzzle like the one in Figure 20-18. Of course, the color original looked much better!
