# Adding a Printer to Star Office

You will most likely want to print documents in Star Office, so you will need to install a printer. Even if you have already installed a printer on Red Hat, you will still need to add your printer to Star Office.

From the main Star Office desktop, double-click on Printer Setup icon.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_204_1.png)

You will see a list of existing printer drivers in the lower part of the window. Scroll down through the list and see if your printer is there. If you find your printer, double-click on it and it will appear at the top portion of the window under “installed printers.” There is an existing choice of “generic printer.” Click once on your printer in the upper window and then click “test page.”

If you do not find your printer in the existing printer drivers, just try the generic printer as it usually works.

Click once on the printer you want to use as your default printer and then click “default.”

You can also click “configure” and set default values for your printer such as paper size, margins and paper orientation.

# Star Office Auto Pilots

You can get a good start to using Star Office by using the auto pilots. These will guide you through creating a document, webpage or other project. To access the auto pilots, click "file" and then "auto pilot." Another menu will allow you to choose document, webpage, spreadsheet, etc. As an example, we will choose to create an agenda from the auto pilot.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_206_0.png)

**Figure 11-7**
Using Auto Pilot to Create an Agenda

The auto pilot begins by asking you some basic layout questions about your agenda. When you have answered them, click "next."

Auto pilot will continue asking you questions about your agenda. Keep clicking "next" until you have all the information you want in your agenda. When you are through, click "create." Your agenda will be created and opened in Star Office. You can then fill in additional information and finally save your document.

# Configuring Options

Clicking “tools” and then “options” will bring up the options dialog window. We won’t go into configuring all of them, but rather the few more important ones.

First, you might want to give Star Office some information about yourself, as it uses this information as defaults in some templates. Pull up the options dialog window and click “general” and then “user data.” Fill in the areas on the right with your personal and professional information.

Tell Star Office where you want to keep your projects folders. By default, Star Office will keep them in the desktop’s “Work” folder, but you may keep them anywhere you like. To specify a location, in the options dialog window, click “general” and then “paths.”

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_209_0.png)

# **Figure 11-11**
*Star Office Options.*

In the default window in the right, click on “my documents.” Now click on “edit” below and you will be able to type in a path. I simply use a documents directory in my home directory, so my path would be /home/kmiles/documents. Note that I had to first create the “documents” directory. Once you type in the path where your work will be saved, click “default.” This will make Star Office always look in this folder when you want to save something. You can also change the paths of other functions.

Another option you might want to configure would be mail if you want Star Office to send and retrieve mail.

To configure mail, pull up the options dialog window and choose “internet” and then “mail/news.” You need an Internet service provider in order to have email. You will also need to know what the incoming and outgoing mail servers are for your provider. Ask the help desk of your provider if you are not sure what this is. The only other thing you need to supply is your username and password. If you use Internet news groups, you might also want to specify your news server in this box.

# Creating, Opening, Formatting and Saving a New Document

StarWriter is the word processing part of Star Office Suite. To begin a new document, click “file,” “new” and “text.” A blank page will appear on your screen.

You may also open an existing StarWriter document by clicking “file” and “open.”

You can move around your directories and choose the file you want to open.

When you have either a new document or an opened one, you may begin typing or editing. There are formatting buttons at the top of the window which will help you to choose font, font size, text alignment and more. You may also right-click on the window with the mouse and a pop-up window will appear with choices for formatting page, character, etc.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_213_0.png)

Formatting character is probably the operation you will do most often. Choosing this will bring up a window that will allow you to choose the font, font size, and characteristics such as underline, bold, italics, and more.

**Tip**

If you've already typed some text and want to make it bold type or change the font type, you can do so by selecting the text with the mouse or shift+arrow keys and then changing font, font size, colour, or font attributes (bold, italics, etc.).

When you save your document for the first time, StarWriter will use the "save as" feature. Click "file" and then "save as."
