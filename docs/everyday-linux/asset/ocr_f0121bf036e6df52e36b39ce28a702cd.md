# ## Account Configuration

The next step is to configure your account and the root user account. You will first have to specify a root user password. The length of the password must be greater than five characters for it to be valid, and you will have to type it in twice.

You must then add an account for yourself. (Well, you actually don't have to, but it'll save you the hassle later on.) Near the middle of the screen is a form for you to fill out; simply supply a username, a full name, and your password, and the installation program will add the account for you. You should pick a username that is composed of your name; outlandish names or pseudonyms for mainstream Internet purposes are usually looked down upon. Suggestions would be a first initial and your last name, your three initials, or your first name and a last initial.

Make sure to write down the root password so you won't forget it. If you forget the password, you won't be able to do any administrative tasks on the system (such as adding additional users, mounting drives, and so on) until you reset it in single-user mode. Once you're sure that you know the root password, you should make sure that you destroy the paper that you wrote it down on. The root user is completely unrestricted and can cause irreparable damage to your system, so you don't want the password falling into the wrong hands.
