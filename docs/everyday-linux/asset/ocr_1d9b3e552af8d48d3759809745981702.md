When a drive is *user-mountable*, every user can mount and unmount the drive. Most of the time, only drives that make use of removable media, such as floppy disk drives, CD-ROM drives, or Zip and Jazx drives, are user-mountable. To make a hard drive user-mountable would allow any user to make the main files of the system unusable—it could crash the computer.

# To Make a Drive User-Mountable

This is the process that you’ll take to make a drive user-mountable.

This time, we look for a category on the right called “File Systems.” A subtopic to “File Systems” is called “Access Local Volume.” Click on “Access Local Volume,” and a list will appear on the right, showing all of your drives and partitions; the floppy drive and CD-ROM are also listed, as are Zip drives and others you may have installed.

First we’re going to make the floppy drive user-mountable. This is one of the more commonly used drives, and it also makes use of removable media. This gives us reason to make it user-mountable. What happens if it’s not user-mountable is that your users will have to ask you to mount the drive every time they need to read the contents of a floppy disk.

The device name of your floppy drive is /dev/fd0. This is how the system knows your floppy drive. Click on the line where you see that device name. A new dialogue box appears on the right; this is known as the volume specification page.

We’re interested in the “Options” tab. The first couple of tabs allow you to configure the floppy drive for use; since it’s already configured for use, we don’t want to change any of those settings. Click on the options tab and the options list appears; from this list, you should find “User mountable.” Click it, and then click on the Accept button. If you wish to make other drives available to your users, repeat this process for the other devices in the list (e.g., a CD-ROM, a CD writing device, a Zip drive, and so on). Make sure that you do *not* make hard drives user-mountable.
