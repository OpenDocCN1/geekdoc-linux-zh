## StarCalc

StarCalc is the spreadsheet portion of Star Office. Spreadsheets are useful for storing and organizing information. There are a number of templates which you can use for creating household budgets, car financing, loan and mortgage calculations, cash journals and even stocks manager. StarCalc can do more than just make spreadsheets however; it can graph the results, make calendars and more.

### Spreadsheets

#### Creating and Saving Spreadsheets

To begin a new spreadsheet, click “file,” “new” and “spreadsheet.”

The empty rectangular areas are called cells. Click on one of the cells to begin entering data. To move across the row, use the tab key. To move to the cell below, use the enter key. You may also use the arrow keys to move around the spreadsheet.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_227_0.png)

**Figure 13-1**
StarCalc Spreadsheet

When you are finished with your spreadsheet and want to save it, click “file” and then “save.” The first time you save a spreadsheet, StarCalc will use the “save as” window. By default, StarCalc saves spreadsheets in its native format (.sdc). If you want to export your spreadsheet in another format so that you might view it in another spreadsheet program, use “save as” and choose from the document types window.

StarCalc will both save and open Excel spreadsheets.

#### Formatting Cells, Rows and Columns

You may format an individual cell, or partial or entire rows or columns of cells. Format can include type numbers (percent, currency, scientific, etc.) font, borders, and more.

To format a single cell, just click on the cell and then, from the menu at the top, click on “format,” then “cell.” When you are through formatting, press “ok.” This will affect only the cell which you had clicked on. If you add data to the cell and then hit enter, the next cell will revert back to whatever the previous formatting was.

To format multiple cells, simply click on the first cell and then, while holding down the mouse button, drag over the cells you want to format. Then click “format,” and “cells.” You can also select the entire row or column for formatting by clicking on either the row number or column letter.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_229_0.png)

**Figure 13-3**

#### Totaling Columns of Numbers

To total the cells in a column, simply click on the bottom cell of the column and then click the sum button. The sum button is the button which has $\Sigma$ as a symbol. Once you do that, two new buttons will appear in its place. One is a green check mark and the other a red “x.” If you want to have the sum of that column, click on the check mark, otherwise click on the red x to exit back to data entry.

To total several columns, just click on the bottom cell of the first column and then drag it across the other columns. Then just click the sum button. You can also total rows in the same manner as you do columns.

#### Making a Chart from Your Spreadsheet Data

Charts often make it easier to see what's going on with figures, and StarCalc makes it possible for you to graph your data in a chart that can be placed right on your spreadsheet.

First, select the columns and rows you would like to make a chart from.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_230_0.png)

**Figure 13-4** *Selecting Rows or Columns.*

Once they are selected, click on the insert object button on the vertical toolbar. This is the toolbar which runs up and down to the left of your spreadsheet. When you do this, the cursor turns into a plus sign with a tiny chart attached to it. Choose a blank area on your spreadsheet, and then click and drag the cursor over the area where you want your chart to be. This will determine the initial size of your chart. When you release the mouse button, the auto format chart window will pop up.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_231_0.png)

**Figure 13-5**
Adding a Chart.

There are four parts to the auto format chart operation. You will be asked to choose what type of chart you would like to create and some specifics about it. There is a preview window on the left which will show you how your data will look.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_231_1.png)

**Figure 13-6**
Formatting the Chart.

When you are finished formatting your chart, click "create" on the last window. Once you do this your chart will be created and appear on your spreadsheet where you may resize and position it.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_232_0.png)

When you have your chart positioned where you want it, right-click and then choose "anchor." Then click on the chart to anchor it to the spreadsheet.

#### Creating a Calendar in StarCalc

You can use StarCalc to create a monthly or yearly calendar which you can then customize or add events which you want to remember. To begin creating your calendar click "file," then "new," and "from template."

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_233_0.png)

When the “new from template” window pops up, click on “miscellaneous” in the box on the left and then choose “month/year” calendar on the right. Click “more” to see a preview, and click “ok” to begin creating your calendar.

After clicking “ok” to the “new from template” window, a new window will come up called “StarOffice Calendar.”

You can choose either year or month. If you choose month, you will need to specify which month. To enter in events you want to remember, click “personal data.”

Using this window, you can enter the event (a birthday, or some other special event you want to remember). Enter the day and month of the event and then click “insert.” You may enter as many events as you like. When you are done, click “ok.” StarCalc will create your calendar.

You will notice that the calendar is divided up into rows and columns just like a spreadsheet. You can move around the calendar just as you do with a spreadsheet, using arrow keys to add events as needed.

#### Creating a Database Using StarBase

StarBase is the portion of StarOffice used to create and manage databases. Databases are useful for handling addresses, inventories and any other type of list. Once you have a database of information, you can search through it easily as well as sort it in many different ways.

Your database is created by using database tables. The tables look similar to the tables used in StarCalc worksheets but there are some differences in formatting and arrangement.

##### Database Formats

There are quite a few different format types for databases. If you are just creating a database for your personal use, you could choose any type. However, if you are going to be sharing the data, you will need to choose a format compatible with everyone who will be using it.

One of the most widely used formats is dBase, most often used in the DOS/Windows environment. The ODBC format uses the SQL language and is the type used by Oracle, Sybase, Informix and other large databases. Text format is a last resort when there are no other options.

##### Creating a New Database

It's a good idea to put your database in a directory all its own, especially if you might create more than one database. Multiple databases in one directory can easily lead to tables getting intermingled and other problems.

You can create a new directory using your directory explorer on your Red Hat desktop. Click "file" and then "new," and then choose "directory."

A new window will pop-up and you can then type in the name of the new directory.
