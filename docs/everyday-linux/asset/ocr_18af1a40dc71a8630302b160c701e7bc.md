You can make a lot of different initialization strings, so you should play with the ones you have to see how they work.

The communication settings also reflect a sort of “login script” that you will need to log in. This is set up in the form of “expect/send” statements. Your computer will expect a certain statement, such as “login:” and send a certain state- ment, such as your user name. It will do the same for your password. You should use the expect/send statements to compose a “login script” of sorts if your ISP requires that you use one.

You should also make sure that all users can activate and deactivate this con- nection, unless you intend to allow only root to use it.

**Networking**—You can leave these settings at default, unless instructed oth- erwise by your network administrator or ISP. They simply control a few network settings.

**PAP Authentication**—If you need to use PAP Authentication, you should talk to your network administrator or ISP to find out what settings you should use in this situation. PAP stands for *Password Authentication Protocol*.

# Done

When you’re finished making the configuration changes, click on the button labeled “Done”. A new window will ask you if you want to save this configura- tion, and you should click Yes. For good measure, click “Save” at the bottom of your Network Configurator window.

You must then activate your connection in order to use it.

# Activating Your PPP Connection

To activate your PPP connection, highlight the device you just created (proba- bly ppp0). Then click on the “activate” button at the bottom of the window. Your modem will immediately dial the number that you specified in the setup.

Once you’ve connected, you won’t really know if your PPP connection is functioning properly because nothing happens. To check that you are connect- ed correctly, we will run a simple test. Click on the terminal icon on your panel. When the window comes up, type yahoo.com. If data begins appearing on your screen, you are successfully connected. To exit the program, hit CTRL-C.
