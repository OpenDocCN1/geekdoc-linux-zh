# # Foreword

Linux is everywhere! Linux is everywhere! In today's computer trade press every other story seems to be about Linux surging into yet another niche in the computing world—Internet servers, network appliances, embedded systems, graphics workstations, cellphones, and even (in one recent IBM announcement) wristwatches. These days Linux seems to be ubiquitous, from handhelds to distributed supercomputers.

There is just one exception, so far, to the otherwise inexorable trend: the ordinary end-user desktop. People who track these things estimate that Linux is at about 4% to 5% of the market on desktop computers as of late 2000 — comparable to the Macintosh's market share but pretty small potatoes compared to Microsoft's 91% monopoly. And that's sad, because Linux has huge advantages over Windows, starting with its price (which is low or free), and it's rapidly getting into more serious features like the utter banishment of the Blue Screen of Death.
