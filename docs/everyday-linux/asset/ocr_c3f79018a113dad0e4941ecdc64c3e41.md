Enlightenment is the most popular, mostly due to its adaptability and ease of use.

We'll look at configuring Enlightenment later in this chapter. Among the items we'll look at are various window themes, desktop backgrounds, how your windows will act, some special effects, sound, and shortcut options that bind some mouse clicks to the keyboard.

You should not confuse window managers and desktop systems. A desktop system manages your *desktop environment*. A window manager controls how the windows look and act when you interact with them.

# ## Personalizing GNOME

Now that you're using your own account, it's time to add your own personal touches. In this section, we describe customizing GNOME so that you can use different themes, backgrounds, or window managers.

The first step to customizing GNOME is to click on the toolbox on the bottom panel. This is the GNOME configuration utility, also known as the control center.

When the control center pops up, you will notice a nice long list of options on the right side. At this point we're concerned with the category labeled “Desktop.” There are four options here: “Background,” “Screensavers,” “Theme Selector,” and “Window Managers.”

# ### Background

To configure the background, simply click on the background option. You will be able to use either a solid or gradient colored background, or you can browse for pictures on your computer. GNOME can display backgrounds from almost every image type, so you won't have to convert your files to use them for backgrounds.

Once you have finished customizing your background, hit the “Try” button. It will apply your changes so you can see if you like them or not. If you don't, press “Revert” and they'll be restored to normal. Otherwise, hit “Apply” to keep the changes and click “Accept.”
