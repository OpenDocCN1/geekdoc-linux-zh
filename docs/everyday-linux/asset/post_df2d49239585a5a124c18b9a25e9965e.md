## CHAPTER 16: Scheduling and Productivity

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_300_0.png)

In this chapter we will cover some various miscellaneous programs which can add to productivity. They are assorted utilities for calculating, notetaking, scheduling and so forth. We think you will find them useful.

### GNOME Calculator and Kcalc

GNOME provides a calculator with a good variety of functions on it. To open it click the GNOME foot, and then from the utilities menu click “simple calculator.” One advantage of the GNOME calculator is that you have the option under “file” to “copy.” This copies whatever is in the display window. You can then paste the value into an editor and print.

There is another calculator under the KDE menus in utilities. We liked this one better because you can configure a few things, but both calculators work very well.

To configure Kcalc, just click on the Kcalc button to the left of the display screen.

### Taking Notes with KJots

You will find KJots under the utilities in the main KDE menu.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_302_0.png)

Figure 16-3  
KJots.

You can save your notes in a series of topics, called books by the program. Click “file” and “new book” to begin using KJots. Another little window will appear and you can give your book a name. You might want to use things like groceries, computers, meetings, phone calls, etc. as your topics. Each time you create a new book you must click “file” and “save book.”

When you want to jot down a note to one of these topics, simply click on “books” and choose the topic you want. At the bottom type in a subject, and in the main window type in whatever notes you want.

To add a new page to the book, just click “edit” and “new page.” You will stay in the same book, but will be able to type in a new subject and notes.

You can use KJots to keep track of all sorts of things, and just think of the paper you will save!

### GNOME Calendar

GNOME provides an excellent way to keep track of daily, weekly and monthly events. Choose “calendar” from the GNOME applications menu.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_303_0.png)

The calendar comes up with a day view. You can see the hour blocks on the left. You can add specific events during the day by clicking on the hour block and then typing in the event. To get an hourly calendar for a different day, simply double click on whatever day you want from the calendar on the right.

You can add “to do” lists for any day by selecting that day and then clicking on the “add” button under the “to do” window at the bottom right. These will show up when you look at the weekly or monthly calendars.

Display weekly, monthly or yearly calendars by clicking on the tabs above the small calendar. If you want to add an appointment while looking at one of these displays, simply click on the appropriate day and then left-click to drop down a menu with “add appointment.”

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_304_0.png)

**Figure 16-5**  
*Adding an Appointment.*

Fill out the appointment information and click “ok.” This information will now be added to the other calendars, including the hourly calendar for the day of that appointment.

When looking at the calendar, you may scroll forward and backwards in dates by clicking either the “prev” or “next” buttons. To return to today’s date, click “today.”

### Notes with KNotes

KNotes can be found in the utilities submenu of the KDE menu. KNotes are a sort of cross between virtual stick-on notes and an alarm clock.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_305_0.png)

**Figure 16-6**  
*KNotes.*

The first thing you will likely want to do with your note is give it a date and time stamp. This can be accomplished by right-clicking on the note and selecting “insert date.”

Type in whatever text you want to and then give your note a name.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_305_1.png)

**Figure 16-7**  
*Adding a Date to your Note.*

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_306_0.png)

Figure 16-8  
Renaming the Note.

Do this by right-clicking on the note and choosing “operations,” and “rename.” A box will pop up and you can type in a name for your note.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_306_1.png)

Under “operations” and “options” you can choose a different color for your note, as well as the font.

#### Mailing Your Note

One of the ingenious things you can do with KNotes is e-mail it to yourself or someone else. To e-mail your note, click on "operations" and then "mail note."

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_307_0.png)

Figure 16-9 Mailing the Note.

A window will pop up and ask you for an e-mail address and also a subject for your note. If you have already named your note, the name will be the default subject of the e-mail.

#### Setting an Alarm

If you really want to make sure you don't forget something, you can set an alarm on your note which will go off at a specific time to remind you. To set the alarm, right-click on your note and choose "operations" and "alarm."

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_308_0.png)

**Figure 16-10**  
*Setting an Alarm.*

You can use the arrows to set the date and time for the alarm to go off.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_308_1.png)

The alarm will go by your system clock, so be sure that your system clock is set correctly!

At the time of the alarm, your computer bell will beep three times.

#### Adding and Deleting Notes

When you start up KNotes, whatever relevant notes you have created will appear on the screen. To create a new note, right-click on a note and select “operations” and “new note.”

To delete a note, right-click on it and select “operations” and “delete note.” A window will pop up and ask you if you are sure you want to delete the note. If you're sure, click “ok.”

### GNOME Address Book

The GNOME Address Book is very much like a virtual Rolodex. It will keep track of addressees, phone numbers and much more, and it will even sort it for you. You will find the Address Book under the GNOME applications menu.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_309_0.png)

Figure 16-11  
GNOME Address Book.

#### Adding an Entry

To add an entry to your address book, click on "add."

You can add a good deal of information about each entry. There are tabs which go across the "add entry" window to add information for address, phone, network, geographical (which would tell you which time zone the person was in), and you can even add some notes about the entry.

When you are finished, click "ok." Note that you can add phone numbers and addresses to separate lists.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_310_0.png)

*Figure 16-12*  
*Adding Entry*

#### Making Changes to Existing Cards

After you have created entries to your address book, you can go back and edit an entry if needed or update information. To do so, click on “modify.” The same window used to create an entry will pop up. You can click on the tabs and edit whatever information you need to. To have the changes take effect immediately, click “apply.”

#### Saving Your Address Book

When you are through adding or modifying entries to your address book, you will need to save it. You can click on “save.” and you will be prompted for a name and location. You should save your address book in your home directory, rather than a more public directory.

#### Finding a Card

We’ve all suffered the inability to remember a name or phone number. GNOME Address Book will allow you to search for a string of information. You can search for part of a name, series of numbers, street name, etc. To begin a search, click on the “find” button.

Fill in the information you want to search for and click “ok.”
