- *Distorts*—these filters create a variety of special effects on image.
- *Emboss*—does what its name implies, makes the image appear embossed into a piece of tin.
- *Ripple*—makes image appear as a reflection in rippled water.
- *Waves*—are similar to ripple but more like a reflection with the ripples emanating from the center of the image.
- *Edge-Detect*—a series of three filters, all of which do basically the same thing, detect edges in an image. This filter can be useful when used with other filters.
- *Enhance*—contains two very useful filters.

- *Destripe*—removes stripes which tend to appear in scanned images.
- *Sharpen*—useful for sharpening scanned images.
- *Glass Effects*—two filters which are fun to play with.
- *Apply Lens*—makes center of image appear as in a convex lens.
- *Glass Tile*—makes image appear as if seen through glass tiles.
- *Light Effects*—my personal favorites!

- *FlareFx*—creates a flare on your image. You are able to choose the exact position you want your flare to be. You may not choose color. Best used with layers.
- *Sparkle*—finds the brightest points in an image and creates a starburst effect.
- *Supernova*—similar to flare except the Flare has spikes. You may also specify size, color, location and number of spikes.

- *Map*—these filters either tile an image in some manner or use one image as a source to change another.
- *Bump Map*—adds depth to an image. You need to choose some texture image and then map this to your other image.
- *Illusion*—divides image into a series of "spikes," creating an interesting illusion.
- *Make Seamless*—uses the center part of the image surrounded by a seamless array of the rest of the image.
- *Map to Object*—maps image to a plane or sphere with options for orientation, lighting and more.
- *Noise*—three filters which are variations on a theme of adding "noise" to an image. Basically they blur the image by varying degrees while also giving it a grainy effect.
- *Render*—a variety of filters which will achieve some interesting as well as useful effects.
- *Gfig*—almost a program in itself and currently a bit difficult to use, but future versions of GIMP will improve on Gfig's usability. Gfig is used for drawing circles, curves, and outlines. For now, it is almost easier to use xpaint if you have to draw shapes and then save the image and open it with GIMP if you want to do further operations in the image.

## **Script-Fu Menu**

Script-Fus are scripts installed in GIMP which perform various operations. They are especially useful when creating logos which would otherwise take many steps to create. GIMP comes with an assortment of Script-Fus and there are a myriad of others which you may download and install from gimp.org.

### **Alchemy**

- *Clothify*—produces an effect similar to apply canvas.

### **Animations**

- *Rippling*—makes image look as if it has ripples through it.

### **Décor**

- *Fuzzy Border*—gives image a fuzzy border.
- *Old Photo*—one of my favorite effects in GIMP. This script takes an image and transforms it into a brownish antique photo.
- *Coffee Stain*—produces a very real-looking coffee stain on an image.

### **Modify**

- *Add Border*—adds a border. You may specify color and size.
- *Shadow*—takes image and adds shadow behind it, giving the image a 3D effect. There are two variations to this; drop shadow and perspective.

### **Render**

- *Line Nova*—turns the image into lines shooting from the center of the image.

## **Dialogues Menu**

## **Creating a New Image**

To create a new image click “file” and then “new.” You will be prompted to specify image size and what color you would like the background to be. You may choose foreground or background colors, white or transparent. You may also choose whether you want the new image to be RGB (full color) or grayscale. The default is RGB.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_344_0.png)

## **Opening an Existing Image**

To open an existing image, click “file” and then “open.” The Load Image window will appear.

Move around your directories in the left window and choose the specific file on the right. At this time GIMP does not have “preview image” options.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_345_0.png)

*Opening an Image.*

## **Saving an Image**

The first thing you need to do when you want to save an image is decide what format you want to save your image in. This has a lot to do with what type of image you have. By understanding the image formats mentioned at the beginning of the Chapter Seventeen, you can get an idea of what format you should use.

Once you know what format you want to save your image as, you can do whatever you need to do to prepare it for being saved. This might be nothing, and you can just right-click for the menu and select "file" and "save as."

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_346_0.png)

**Figure 18-36**
*Saving a File.*

You can type in a file name and choose the extension (file format) from the "determine file type" box. You could also choose "determine type by extension," however, if your image is not in the correct format, the save will fail.

An example of getting an image prepared for saving would be an image you wanted to save as a .gif. This file format requires the image to be indexed. To index the colors of your image, simply choose “indexed” from the image menu. You may then save the image as a .gif.

## **Cropping an Image**

Very often it is desirable to keep certain areas of an image and remove others. The crop tool is the easiest way to do this. Click on the crop icon from the main tool menu. Put the cursor where you want to begin cropping and then left-click and drag the mouse over the area of the image you want to keep.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_347_0.png)

Figure 18-37
Cropping an Image.

For example, in this image of my cat, I wanted to have just her face in the image. I dragged the crop tool around just the area I wanted to keep. To do the actual crop, there will be a small crop dialogue box in which you may click "crop" or "close" to cancel the crop.

## **Adjusting Brightness and Contrast**

This is another common operation. Often an image is too dark and we want to lighten it up. To adjust brightness or contrast, the image must be in RGB format.

To put your image in RGB format, simply right click and then choose "Image," "RGB."

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_348_0.png)

To adjust brightness or contrast, right-click over the image and then choose "Image," then "Colors," then "Brightness-Contrast."

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_348_1.png)

**Figure 18-38** Brightness/Contrast Controls.

As you move the slide bar to increase or decrease brightness or contrast, you will see the effect on the image itself. If you are completely dissatisfied with results and want to start over, simply click "cancel." If you are satisfied with the results, click "ok."
