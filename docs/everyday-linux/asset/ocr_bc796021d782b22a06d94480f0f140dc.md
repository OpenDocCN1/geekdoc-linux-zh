# ## Creating Partitions

The next order of business is the creation of partitions. In my early days of computing, mentioning partitions was scary stuff, and it still can be today. To combat whatever tremors may be knocking in your knees, we're going to distill partitioning into its conceptual components. We're also going to explain why in the world you'd want to mess around with them.

This will likely take up some space in the book—if you're not interested or already know enough about partitions to satisfy yourself, simply skip ahead.

# ### What Is a Partition?

It's often helpful to think of computer terms by their physical equivalents. A partition in a room, for instance, is a barrier between one side and the other. It might separate two classrooms in a pinch or provide us with two smaller rooms instead of one really large one.

In the computer sense, this concept is the same. A hard data partition is a magnetic barrier that separates data on one side of the hard drive from data on the other side. Partitioning is usually done to organize files and manage space. A 40-GB hard drive is like owning a farm—there's a lot of acreage, but it's pretty tough to manage. By partitioning up a hard drive, you reduce it into manageable pieces. The real estate equivalent would be building subdivisions.

That, in a nutshell, is a partition and its use.
