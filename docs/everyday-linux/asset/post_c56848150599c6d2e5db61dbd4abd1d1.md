## Changing the Main Menu

The main menu comes preset with several different applications built into it. However, over the course of your use of Linux, you will likely download or install many other applications. It is obviously somewhat tedious to click on the main menu, then “run program” every time you want to use them, so we want to be able to add them to the main menu (or perhaps to the panel itself). Doing so is very simple. Click on the main menu, and go to the submenu labeled “Settings.” There is a program listed called “Menu Editor.” Click it. Using the menu editor is fairly simple. There are essentially two kinds of menus available—the system menu and the user menu. Unless you are logged in as root, you will not be able to modify the system menu. (Modifications to the system menu affect all users.)

You will notice several buttons at the top of the screen which will allow you to add a submenu and launchers to your menu. Simply click their respective buttons and fill in the pertinent information. The information is exactly the same as the information you would supply for adding launchers or directories to your desktop, and the format is similar.

After you finish filling in the information, click the save button at the bottom of the form. This is very important, since the new submenus and applications are not automatically added when you close the program. You must save them first.

## Adding (or Removing) More Panels

If you so desire, you can add more panels. There are two types of panels: *edge panels* and *corner panels*. Edge panels will take up the entire edge of your screen; corner panels will take up more space as you add more applets or launchers to the panel.

To create a new panel, right-click on one of your existing panels. Click on “add new panel” and select either a corner panel or an edge panel. It will automatically orient itself on your screen, and it will have nothing in it. We’ll learn how to put stuff there momentarily.

If you find that the new panel is inconvenient or has lost its use, simply right-click that panel and find “remove this panel.” Click that, and it’s history. Unfortunately, you cannot recover panels, so be careful when removing them. It’s usually not a whole lot of work to put them back together, but it can be annoying. Initially, you cannot remove the first panel, since one must be running at all times. Once you add a panel, however, the default panel can be deleted. It’s not a good idea to delete it.

### Adding and Removing Applets

*Applets* are simply “small applications” which run directly on the panel. You can use applets to monitor system usage, the time of day, or to provide eye candy and other pointless wastes of system resources and your time. (Helps writer’s block, though.)

To add an applet, right-click on the panel you want to add it to. Find the “add applet” submenu and mouse over it. Four or five different other submenus will appear. You can add a variety of applets from these menus. Experiment with them and decide which you need and which you don’t.

To remove any applet, simply right-click the applet and click “remove applet” from the submenu.

A personal preference is the “Life” applet, which plays the game of life (not the board game, but the colonization game) right on panel. It can provide hours of entertainment in addition to becoming a convenient distraction when you have better things to be doing (such as writing a book, for instance).

### Adding Launcher Applications to Your Panel

To add a launcher to your panel, right-click the panel. Then click on “add launcher.” The same screen will appear that came up when we added a launcher to the desktop; you can follow the exact same steps to fill in the information as provided above in “Adding Desktop Applications.”

Remove a launcher the same way you would remove an applet; right-click the launcher and click “remove launcher.”

## Adding Drawers to Your Panel

If you have a number of applets or launchers on your panel that go together, you can create a *drawer* for them to go in. To create a drawer, you simply right-click the panel and click "add drawer."

A new screen will appear which asks for information about the drawer. You should fill in the fields appropriately, since all drawers have the same default icon. If you don't provide a name or comment for the drawer, when you mouse over it, nothing will appear and you may not know what's contained in it.

To "open" a drawer, click on it. A submenu of sorts will appear with all of the applets or launchers in the drawer. At the end of the drawer is an arrow; you can "close" the drawer by clicking on this arrow or on the drawer itself.

Drawers, like panels, have properties you can change. They are similar to the properties of a panel, so we'll look at them at the same time. To change the properties of a drawer, right-click the drawer icon and click on "properties." You can remove a drawer by right-clicking on it, too.

## Changing the Properties of Your Panel

Panels have a variety of properties associated with them. We'll look at a couple here. To change the properties of a panel, click on the panel. Near the bottom of the submenu is an option called "this panel properties." Click on it (make sure that you don't change the global properties yet).

Your panel can operate in a number of ways. At the end of your panel are two arrows. These are called *hidebutton arrows*. When you click them, the panel will disappear, leaving an arrow behind that you can click on that will allow you to expand the panel again. However, these arrows take up space, and when you maximize windows, they will not cover the arrows.

If this poses a problem, you can remove the check in "enable hidebutton arrows." You will still be able to collapse your panel, but there will no longer be arrows available. Instead, you'll simply have a couple of buttons on either end, thinner than the arrows. (So they'll take up less space.)

You can also have your panel "hide" when your mouse pointer is moved off of it. This will create more space on your desktop.

You may also want to change the orientation of your panel. To do this, you can either modify the position from the properties or use your middle mouse button. (If you chose to emulate a three-button mouse in your installation, you should click on the left and right mouse buttons simultaneously instead.) To move it with the mouse, click on the panel with the middle mouse button and move it to the location you desire. Otherwise, modify the properties and click on the location you want.

When you're done, press the "apply" button and then click "OK."

## Modifying Global Panel Properties

You should have noticed when you right-clicked your panel that there was an option to modify global panel properties. These are properties that apply to all panels, regardless of their position, type, or make up.

Most of these are self-explanatory and likely tangential to your understanding of how to use GNOME. If you want to further customize your panels, however, you should use the options here. You will be able to modify how your icons look when depressed, how much space a hidden panel will consume on the screen, and other such properties.

## Changing Drawer Properties

Changing drawer properties is very simple. The properties are almost exactly the same as panel properties. If you right-click a drawer and then click on "properties," a menu will appear with all sorts of fun goodies to play with.

You'll notice the same information that you input when you created the drawer; you can change this if you like. You can modify the hide/button arrows for your drawer as well.
