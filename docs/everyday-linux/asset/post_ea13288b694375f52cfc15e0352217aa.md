### Playing Audio and Video Files with Linux

It seems incredible how much computers have changed in the past decade. They have changed from a simple command line interface to multimedia entertainment systems. Computing power now allows for playing all sorts of video and audio. All you need to know is which program plays the file type you want to see or hear.

There are a number of programs available for Linux, but, as with other topics, we’ve chosen the programs which come with a boxed Red Hat install. You will likely find many other programs on the net which you might like to download and try.

#### Playing MP3 Formats

##### XMMS (MP3 Player)

MP3s are growing in popularity each day. It is a format which reproduces music in CD quality. In fact this format may be copied to CDs. XMMS will play MP3s and the audio portion of Mpeg movies.

316 Everyday Linux

![img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_351_0.png](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_351_0.png)

To open a file in XMMS, click on the up arrow with a bar underneath in the lower left row of buttons. A window will pop up which will allow you to move around directories and find your music files. Once you have selected a song and clicked “OK,” that window will close and the song will begin to play automatically.

> Playing MP3s can be CPU intensive. You may notice that while playing an MP3, and opening another program (or doing some operation which takes CPU power) that the music breaks up. This will likely be noticed most on slower machines and not noticed at all on fast ones.

#### Playing Compact Disks

There are numerous programs which will allow the user to play compact disks, but we have selected the more stable and intuitive ones.

##### CD Player

Place your CD in the tray and start CD player from the GNOME “multimedia” menu. The controls of the program are not labeled but are intuitive. Their functions also appear as a small label when you hold your mouse over a button. An alternative controls menu will pop up if you click your right mouse button. (Make sure you are not over the actual control buttons!)

#### Playing .wav Files

##### Media Player

This little program can be found by clicking "KDE Menus" and then "multimedia" and selecting "media player." Media player will play only .wav files.

Because they are large uncompressed files, usually only sound clips are saved as wav files.

![img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_353_1.png](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_353_1.png)

**Figure 19-5**
Media Player Wav player.

The controls for media player are quite intuitive and there are pop-up labels when you hold the mouse over a button. You will notice a few buttons (such as “eject media”) are grayed out. This is likely preparation for adding additional features such as playing CDs.

As with most programs which run on Linux, you may open as many windows of media player as you like.

#### Playing Midi Files

##### Kmid

Mids are popular tunes and often played on websites. KMid can be found under “KDE menus” by selecting “multimedia” and then “Midi/Karaoke player.”

The controls for KMid are intuitive, and there is also a menu bar across the top of the screen. KMid allows you to set up collections of your favorite tunes. You could, for example, put a group of holiday mids into a holiday collection, while keeping your favorite everyday mids in another collection. This saves a lot of opening of directories!

KMid comes complete with instructions for playing songs and creating collections. It also has a “tips and tricks” section that is useful and informative.

#### Sound Accessories

##### KMix—Sound Mixer

There are numerous sound mixers available for Linux. We have chosen the one that has full features and also comes with documentation.

KMix is a small but full-featured sound mixer for your computer's multimedia programs. This will allow you to increase or reduce volume, bass, treble and more. It will also allow you to set recording levels if you are working with a microphone.

There is a global volume control which will affect all sound-related programs. There are also separate volume controls for CD players, microphone, speaker and line-in.

Where applicable, there are separate right and left (for speakers) controls. Generally, you will not need to make adjustments to the mixer for everyday use, but there are times when you will want to have such controls available.

#### Video on Linux

##### AKtion! The Video Player

At first glance this program might not look so impressive, but it is a real workhorse and handles numerous video formats. AKtion can be found by choosing the “KDE menus” and then “multimedia” and selecting “AKtion.”

AKtion will handle five types of video format. The two most common are Quicktime Movie (.mov) and MPEG (.mpg). These are the two you will most likely be using, but AKtion will also handle .avi, .fli, and .fle formats.

The control buttons of AKtion have pop-up labels when you hold the mouse over a button. Although there is good documentation, you will likely find all you need to do is open a video file and it will play automatically. You may also resize AKtion to be larger, but you will lose quality.

### Games on Linux

Red Hat comes with a number of games installed on it. We've covered some of the more popular ones. Some are controlled by mouse and others by the arrow keys. Many you have doubtless seen before on other operating systems.

There are also a number of commercial games which you can purchase for Linux, including the ever popular Quake. Loki Software makes a number of good games for Linux and we have endeavored to give an overview of what they have to offer also.

#### Main Games Menu

These games are available from the main GNOME menu under games.

##### Same Gnome

Same GNOME is a rather easy but cute game, best suited for children though it seems to hold some amusements for adults. There are three types of balls and the object is to get a high score for eliminating balls. A single mouse click will eliminate all balls of the same type that touch either horizontally or vertically.

![img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_359_0.png](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_359_0.png)

Figure 20-1  *Sane GNOME*

The more balls eliminated with a click, the higher the score you get. Eliminating all the balls will get you a 1000 bonus points.

To help you know how many balls will be eliminated by a mouse click, the affected balls animate when you hold the mouse over one of them.

There are several “themes” of balls you can use to play the game. The illustration shows a screenshot of the planet theme. There are even some instructions for creating your own themes.

##### Aisle Riot

Aisle Riot is a literal riot of solitaire games. The default is Klondike, but there are an additional thirty-one variations of solitaire including Canfield, Clock, Scorpion, Zebra, *Auld Lang Syne*, and more.

![img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_360_0.png](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_360_0.png)

Figure 20-2
Aisle Riot.

There is an extensive help area with instructions and strategy for each card game.

There are a few options which the user can define, such as card decorations and font. Aisle Riot will allow the user to set up large fonts for those who might be visually impaired.

##### Chess

For the chess buff, there is a chess game in Linux. Documentation of rules to play the game is scant, so you should know the basic chess moves.
