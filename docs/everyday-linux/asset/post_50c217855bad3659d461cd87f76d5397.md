
## Creating a Presentation Without a Template

If you would like to start a presentation without using Auto Pilot you can do so by way of StarDraw. Either double-click on the "new drawing" icon on the Star Office desktop, or click "file," "new," and then "drawing." When you see StarDraw you will find it familiar, as it is used by Auto Pilot when creating a presentation.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_260_0.png)

**Figure 14-9**  
*StarDraw window.*

We will go through the construction of several slides. We'll cover the basic tools that come with StarDraw. When you are working on a presentation you have an additional menu screen on your Star Desktop.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_261_0.png)

**Figure 14-10**  
*Presentation Menu.*

This menu allows you to create additional slides or to select a layout of existing slides. If this toolbar is not visible, click on "view" and then "toolbars" and select "presentation." There is also an icon for making this menu appear and disappear. It is on the right hand side of the Star Desktop.

### The Left Toolbar

First, take a close look at the toolbar on the left side of the screen. These tools will allow you to do things like draw boxes, circles, cones, and lines and to set alignment and other effects. Each of the tools has its own set of options which you can view by holding the Shift key down and then clicking on the tool icon. For instance, looking at the options for the cube tool, you will see that you may also choose a sphere, pyramid, cylinder, torus, and more. When you see the option you want, simply click on it.

#### Zoom Ratio

There are times when it is helpful to get a closer look at an area in your drawing, or to get a view of the entire page. StarDraw provides numerous zoom views and several ways to achieve them.

On the left toolbar, near the top, is an icon of a magnifying glass. Holding the shift key and clicking will show you the zoom options. Holding your mouse over each individual one will tell you how much the tool will zoom in or out. Choose one of these options, click on it, and then click on your image (pick the specific area if you are zooming in).

The other zoom option is on the lower part of the StarDraw window. You'll see the percentage of zoom displayed. The default is 27%. If you double-click on that percentage, a zoom options window will pop up.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_262_0.png)

**Figure 14-11**  
*Zoom Options.*

You’ll be able to choose between 50% and 200%, page width, or you may choose variable and set your own.

#### Adding Text

It is likely you will need to add text to your drawing or presentation. To do so, click on the text icon from the left toolbar.

> **TIP**
>
> The options for text are “plain text,” “text in a frame,” and “callouts.”
>
> The “callouts” is the ever famous “text in a bubble” used by cartoonists.

Select which font you want to use by clicking “format” and “character.” You may choose a font, its size, and some other attributes. When you are through, click where on the image you want to type text.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_263_0.png)

A dotted rectangle will appear and you may type in your text. When you are through, click somewhere outside of the dotted rectangle and the text will be anchored to the page.

While the dotted box is surrounding your text, you may change other attributes. If you want to center your text, click on the alignment icon and select center. If you want to add color, just click on a color box at the bottom of the window.

Any time you want to go back and change something about the text, it is possible to do so. Just click over the text and the dotted box will reappear and you may then make changes. To delete the text, first click to make the dotted box appear and then hit the backspace key.

### Adding an Image to the Slide

To add an image to your slide, click “insert,” “graphics,” and “from file.” A window will pop up and allow you to navigate around directories and choose the image you wish to add. Then click “open,” and the image will appear on your slide.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_264_0.png)

**Figure 14-13** Adding an Image.

You will see a dotted box around your image. To move the entire image around, simply place your mouse inside the box. The cursor will turn into a small crossed arrow. You can now move the image to where you want it on the screen. To resize the image vertically, click on one of the vertical dots and drag the mouse to the size you want. To alter size horizontally, click and drag on a dot on the top or bottom of the image. To maintain the ratio, use a dot on the corner of the image.

### Arranging Things: Placing an Image Behind Text

If you've already typed some text and then decide you want to place an image behind it, you can do so quite easily. Click on the text to make the dotted box appear. Now, from the top menu, click “insert,” “graphic,” and “from file.” An “insert graphics” window will appear.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_265_0.png)

**Figure 14-14**  
*Inserting a Graphic.*

You may move around directories and choose a graphic. There is a small preview window on the right side of this window. If you cannot see it, widen your Star Office main window. Choose an image and click “Open.”

The image will appear over the text (hiding it) on your slide.

To get the image to go behind the text, hold the shift and click on the arrange icon. Choose “send to back” and the graphic will be moved behind the text.

#### Drawing a Box

Suppose we want to draw a box around the title of our slide. We would want a box with just an outline, rather than a solid color. From the left toolbar, shift and click on the rectangle icon. Select the outlined box. You will now see some different controls appear at the top of the screen, beneath the main toolbar.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_267_0.png)

**Figure 14-17**  
*Box Controls.*

The icon on the left side which looks like a calligraphy pen tip is for line style. Click on it and you may choose from various line types such as solid, dotted, dashed, or others.

The icon to the right of the line style icon is two arrows. Clicking on this will allow you to choose from various arrows or endpoints of lines. You won’t need this for drawing a box, but if you were drawing lines you might want to use this.

You may also choose line style from the pop down window to the right of the arrow style icon.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_268_0.png)

**Figure 14-18**  
*Line Style.*

To the right of this is the line width window. Use the tiny arrows on the right to increase or decrease line width.

Next on the right is the line color window. Clicking on this will generate a pop-down menu which will allow you to choose a color. To the right of this is the fill color window. This is only used if you are choosing a solid rectangle.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_268_1.png)

**Figure 14-19**  
*The Fill tool.*

You may choose invisible fill, pattern, gradient, or color.

![](img/92fb6d8ad19de3b3470ba95c7b71b82e_269_0.png)

**Figure 14-20**  
*Using Gradients*

When you do choose gradient hatching or bitmap, click the window to the right and you will see a selection to choose from. If you choose one of these, you will need to use the “arrange” icon to then move the fill to the back of the text so that your text will show.

### Adding a Background to Your Slide

You might want to add a color or some other background to your slide. Click on “format,” and “page.”

The window which pops up has two tabs: “page” and “background.” Click on the background tab. You may choose between color, hatching, gradient, invisible, or bitmap.
