# # Installing Packages

The first step that the installer will take is to format the Linux partitions you specified beforehand (see "Selecting Partitions for Format"). Then it will install the packages you selected in the "Selecting Components" dialogue.

This can take quite a long time, especially if your hard drive is large or you're installing a lot of different packages, so it might be a good time to get a bite to eat or otherwise take a break. This is the next-to-last step in the installation, but after it's done we'll have a little more work to do to get you up and running.

# # Creating a Book Disk

When the installation of all of the packages is complete, you will need to take out a floppy disk and label it (perhaps "Red Hat Linux Boot Disk" or something equally descriptive). Put it into the floppy drive and then click Next. Red Hat will format the floppy disk and place all of the necessary information on it to boot into Linux.

Once this is done, you can remove the disk from the drive (unless you're booting to Linux solely from a boot disk).

# # You're FINISHED!

The installation program will then reboot your computer. Make sure that you remove the Red Hat Installation CD from the CD-ROM drive if you booted from the CD to install the software, otherwise the installation will begin again. (Don't worry—if it does, just eject the CD and restart your computer.)

You will then have the singular delight of watching your computer boot into a completely new operating system. We're going to spend the next several chapters getting your computer completely set up for use with Linux and acclimating you to the wide variety of applications available for use with your system.

Usually, at this point we suggest you take a small break, unless your brain isn't fried by staring at a computer screen during the installation process. Learning the basics of Linux and GNOME can take a little while, and customizing everything to your whim isn't always an instantaneous process. But it is a lot of fun.
