# ## Troubleshooting Your Modem

There are invariably some modems that have problems with Linux. These are usu-ally compatibility problems, though some are due to the default settings that Linux
associates with the modem (using ATZ as an initialization string, for instance).

The subject of modem troubleshooting would require another book entirely,
however, we can leave you with a few pointers.

If you're using an external modem, make sure that it is on. Sounds stupid,
yes, but a lot of problems can be caused this way. If you have lights on the front
of your modem, consult your modem manual to see what they mean. There are
some that should be lit when the modem is working properly. Your manual will
be able to tell you which.

Your modem manual may also supply you with some standard initialization
strings to try in case ATZ doesn't do the job. Every modem will respond to
AT&F, which resets the modem to factory defaults (hence the "F"). However,
that may not be enough for your modem to work; extra settings may need some
tweaking. Your modem manufacturer can usually supply you with two or three
strings tested to work with most operating systems and applications.

Your modem manual will also have most of the “AT-commands” that you can
use with your modem, and it will explain the meaning of each. More adventur-
ous users can invent their own initialization strings to see if they'll work.
