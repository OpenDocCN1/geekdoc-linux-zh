# Using Masks

+   1. Open your image and add a new white layer.

+   2. Add a layer mask, but this time in black so you can see through it to the image.

+   3. Activate the' selection tool and select an area in a unique shape surrounding the head in the image, as in Figure 10.11.

+   4. Right-click on the image window to bring up the menu, and choose Select, Invert.

+   5. Set the foreground color in the toolbox to a very light grey. Activate the Paintbrush tool, choose a large brush, and paint the grey in the newly selected area around the tiger's head. Deactivate the selection. As illustrated in Figure 10.12, there's another kind of photo edge easily mastered with the use of masks.

Just for fun, let's apply one final step to this last mask to really make it special.

+   1. To bring back the selection, you can either select Edit, Undo or right-click on the mask thumbnail in the Layer dialog window and click Mask to Selection. You will see the marquee reappear around the tiger's head.

+   2. Right-click on the thumbnail and choose Apply Layer Mask. When the options box pops up, choose Apply also. This will attach the mask to the layer, but you will still see the selection. Once again, right-click on the image and choose Select, Invert.

+   3. Right-click again on the image and select Script-Fu, Shadows, Drop Shadow. When the options window pops up, set the blur radius to 30, opacity to 100, and make sure the Allow Resizing option is unchecked before applying. Deactivate the selection, and see what a difference the drop shadow has made to your photo edge—check out Figure 10.13.
