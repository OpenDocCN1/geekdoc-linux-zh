# Working on Selections

# Antialasing Is Your Friend

Antialasing is an effect used to produce a smooth-edged selection by filling in pixels just along the edge of the selection with similar colors, thereby softly blending it in with adjacent colors. If Antialasing is not used, a selection might tend to appear jagged around the edges, especially along curves. This is a condition commonly known among graphic artists the jaggers. By using the Antialasing option, you'll end up with a much softer and smoother appearance to your selection edges, as seen in Figure 5.3 (which has been magnified somewhat to enable you to see the difference).

# Feather Effects

The Feathering option produces an even smoother transition between the selection and the surrounding area. It works by fading the pixels into the background along the edge by a chosen amount, called the feather radius. You control the feather radius by a slider in the Tool Options window. The higher you set the slider (0–100), the farther the pixels of your selection will feather out and blend in. This is an invaluable effect, particularly working with photo manipulations.
