This painting is screaming for mountains, so add a few of those too.

To form the mountains, you'll need a simple custom brush with a flat tip, similar to a flat brush used in oil painting. To make this brush, open a new, very small image (around 10). Draw a straight line horizontally across the center of the image. Right-click, and select Script-Fu, Selection, To Brush (in the description option, name it something as flat brush or line brush). If you check in the Brush dialog box, you'll find the new brush waiting for you.

Take the new brush, reduce the opacity a little, and use quick downward strokes to put in the basic shapes of mountains. By having the opacity reduced, you'll see that overlapping the strokes will automatically gain cliffs and ridges, as in Figure 18.7. You might want to add few lighter colored strokes to highlight one side of the mountains as the sun were shining on them.

Now that you have a start on the mountains, you'll need a tranquil lake in front of them. Use the Rectangle tool to select the mountains and a large part of the sky. Copy and paste it into a new transparent layer. Use the Flip tool to perform a vertical flip on this new layer and place it so that the foot of the mountains meet on both sides to form a reflection. Use a 180 degree motion blur on the reflection layer and reduce the opacity a little to give a more realistic effect, as in Figure 18.8.
