At this point, your image is only lacking one very important thing. On a clear and star-filled night as the one depicted here, the sky would surely be reflecting in that tranquil water!

To form the reflection, first activate the Rectangle Select tool and select the top portion of the image, all the way down to where the water line begins. Right-click on the selection and choose Edit, Copy Visible. This will copy the selected portion of all visible layers. Click the selection to unselect as it is now saved in the Clipboard and ready to paste. Add a new layer and name it reflection.

What you will have to do now is flip the layer containing the copy of moon and stars and move the resulting reflection to its rightful place in water. Do this by double-clicking the Flip tool, selecting Vertical in the options box, and clicking anywhere on the layer to flip it. To be able to see through this reflection layer, lower the opacity to 20.

# Summary

By learning the basics of layering during this last hour, you have discovered the key to unlock one of the most important powers an image-editing program can possess.

The capability to combine and yet independently edit portions of images will unquestionably ensure that you gain a new and distinct form of mastery over your creations.

# Q&A

- How many layers can I have in a single image?
- Unlimited. The GIMP will allow you to add as many layers as you want. However, layering does require a good amount of memory from your computer, and the bigger the images, the more memory it takes. As long as you are fortunate enough to have a nice large amount of RAM, the sky is the limit!

- Can I view just one layer at a time?
- Yes. The eye symbol in the Layers & Channels dialog window enables quick-and-viewing of one or number of layers at a time. Just click on the Eye icon to add or remove a layer from view. By Shift-clicking on the eye, you can hide all but that layer.

## Blend Modes Quick Guide

| Mode | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| Normal | All pixels on the selected layer cover the lower layers. |
| Dissolve | This mode also requires opacity setting; creates a speckled effect. |
| Multiply | This works by multiplying color values. Makes images darker. |
| Screen | This option produces lighter colors by multiplying the inverse of the lower layers. |
| Overlay | This combines multiply and screen modes. Lower color values get darker, higher values get lighter. |

## Hands-on layering

- Difference: Compares the color values of all layers and subtracts any values.
- Addition: Blends the color values of all layers.
- Subtraction: Subtracts the selected layer's color from the combined color.
- Darken Only: Makes a comparison of color values all layers and picks the darkest pixels.
- Lighten Only: Works the same as Darken Only, except this one chooses the lightest pixels.
- Hue: Applies the hue of the selected layer to all layers.
- Saturation: Applies the saturation (color intensity) of the selected layer to all layers.
- Color: Applies the combined hue and saturation of the selected layer to all layers, yet will not affect the luminance.
- Value: Known as luminance or luminosity, this blends the brightness of the selected layer with the hue and saturation.
- Behind: Only available in the Brush dialog box; allows painting on transparent areas without affecting other areas.
