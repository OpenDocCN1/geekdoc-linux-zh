## Transformation Tools

A transformation is anything that alters the perspective of the image, whether it is by size, position, rotation, mirroring, or plain distortion.

You have a good image to begin with, but something just isn’t right with it. Perhaps you could position it a little better, rotate it, or get a closer look at it. That’s what the Transformation tools are for (see Figure 3.4). These tools are great at altering the perspective, size, position, ratio, or orientation of an image, as in Figure 3.5.

## Artistic Tools

The Artistic tools do exactly what their name implies—they enable you to draw, paint, erase, and brush your images with colors and patterns. The tools used to do these resemble the tools you find in any artist's tool collection—pencils, brushes, erasers, airbrushes, and so forth (see Figure 3.6). And they work in much the same way as the real things (see Figure 3.7).

The last three Artistic tools are unique, and worth mentioning here. The Airbrush works just like a can of spray paint, thickening the paint longer you use it in one place or enabling a thin spray of color to be applied with a sweeping motion. The Clone tool will paint with an exact duplicate of any image or pattern (which will come in handy for photo retouching). And the Convolve tool works like a paintbrush, but will blur or sharpen an image instead of painting it with color.

## The Color Tools

The Color tools, as you've probably guessed, have to do with color, and the way it is applied to your images. With these tools, shown in Figure 3.8, you can choose and fill selections with color in ways that would be difficult to using physical art equipment. But thanks to the wonders of technology, changing and altering colors is as simple as moving a mouse. We will go ahead and touch on some of their aspects here, as they will be vastly useful when working with the other tools.

## FIGURE 3.8

## Color Select Tool

The Color Select tool, also known as the Eyedropper, can select any color in your image. By clicking on the Tool icon, your cursor will be transformed into a little crosshair, at which point you can pick up a color from any pixel in your image (see Figure 3.9). With a mouse click, this will change the selected color in the toolbox, either foreground or background, to the same color as the pixel located under your cursor. This is a handy tool for color matching, which is very important for doing things as print and Web graphics.

## FIGURE 3.11

The Fill Option dialog box enables you to use the tool in many ways.

- Opacity: Refers to the see-through quality of the color or pattern used as a fill. You can adjust this incrementally to let more or less of the background through.
- Fill threshold: Approximates the area to fill by looking at the surrounding pixels and determining if they are close to the one and deciding whether not to fill them well. So, for example, a setting of 1 would mean only the color specifically will be filled. The maximum setting of 255 will fill the whole image or selection regardless of color.
- Fill type: Lets you select what you want to fill your selection with, a color or a pattern. You can select any color using GIMP’s Color Selector dialog box by double-clicking the Foreground color on the toolbox.

Patterns are accessible from File, Dialogs, Patterns, in the toolbox. Patterns are interesting, repetitive designs that can be used to fill a selection or background to add texture. You can select a pattern in the dialog box, and fill an image with that pattern to create interesting effects. Using and creating your patterns will be discussed at length in Chapter 4, "Using Brushes and Patterns."

## The Foreground/Background Color Selector

As long as you're covering the topic of color, you should go ahead and learn how to use it tool, shown in Figure 3.13, because it is a vitally important one.

## FIGURE 3.13

The Foreground/Background Color Selector is primarily what you will use to deal with color when working in the GIMP.

![](img/0183f053b347a6407a2381be231d0345_57_0.png)

The foreground color is used by your tools—Paintbrush, Pencil, Airbrush, Bucket Fill, and Gradient (the first color). The background color is used by the Eraser tool when cutting a selection from an image, and acts as the endpoint of gradients.

You can access the GIMP Color Selector dialog box by double-clicking on either the foreground or background color icons in the box (see Figure 3.14). Now you can select any color available by either dragging the cursor through the spectrum and color boxes, or by specifying any value with the sliders and text boxes. The sliders control Hue, Saturation, Value, Red, Green, and Blue. By clicking the Close button, you have made your active color selected color, which you can now use with any tool.

## FIGURE 3.14

The Color Selection dialog box lets you pick any color to use with your tools.

![](img/0183f053b347a6407a2381be231d0345_57_1.png)

The little arrows on the Foreground/Background Color Selector tool allow you to swap the foreground and background colors. The little black-and-white boxes in the lower-left corner bring the colors back to the default black and white.

## Text Tool

The GIMP Text tool is very useful, especially in regards to creating graphics for the Web. It enables you to insert type anywhere into your image. And because text is treated essentially as a selection, you can insert it into anything as you would a selection—fill it with patterns, gradients, or alter it any number of ways (see Figure 3.15).
