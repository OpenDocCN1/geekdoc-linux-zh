# Hour 20

If you need to get a closer or removed view of what you've just drawn, you can use the slider next to the Scale to Image option in the Paint tab to zoom in or out (You need to uncheck the option first). You can then recheck the Scale to Image button to restore the original view.

After you your shape, you can paint it onto your canvas. Gfig uses the standard GIMP brushes in order to do this. Go to the Brushes tab and select the brush you want to use. (Click the Paint button to draw the shape using the FG color and brush you selected. Make sure to save your shape in the Object list if you want to use it).

There are a few more options to consider when painting your image. In the Paint tab (shown in Figure 20.3), you can set the tone for the way which Gfig paints your image. Using the drop-down menu determines what sort of drawing you will have. Brush uses the standard brush method to outline your shape; selection+Fill will paint your selection according to the Select tab options, which resemble the Fill options. Furthermore, the Draw Option will let you paint your image on one of three canvases: Original paints the object directly onto your image, New paints onto a new layer, and Multiple paints each part of your object (having unique control points) onto a separate layer.
