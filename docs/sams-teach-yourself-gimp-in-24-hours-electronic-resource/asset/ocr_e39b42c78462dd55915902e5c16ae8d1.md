Besides being easy to make, textured backgrounds are good to use because you really only need a small piece of texture that can then be tiled (repeated over and over with seamless edges) as the background. This helps keep your site's total file size down.

Create a quick and simple texture here to illustrate the effect.

First, open a new canvas in a small size, around 100 × 100, and fill with a background color.

Now choose a darker shade of the same color, open the Brush dialog box and choose one of the texture brushes. Activate the Airbrush tool and spray the texture evenly over the entire image, as in Figure 15.2.

# FIGURE 15.2

Because this will be tiled, it doesn't need to be very big.

Ok, it doesn't look like much yet, but there are a lot of ways to go with it. You can get really good textures with filters as Canvas and GIMPression. In Figure 15.3, the motion blur and bump map were applied to achieve something like a stucco texture. All that is left is to turn it into a seamless tile by selecting Filters, Map, Make Seamless.

> The seamless patterns supplied in the GIMP Pattern dialog box can be a great resource for ready-made background textures and ideas.
When you've made a seamless background texture you like, you might want to save a copy of it as (See the "Saving Patterns" section in Hour 4, "Using Brushes and Patterns in Depth" to refresh your memory). That way, you can use it later with the Bucket Fill tool to make other matching graphics.
