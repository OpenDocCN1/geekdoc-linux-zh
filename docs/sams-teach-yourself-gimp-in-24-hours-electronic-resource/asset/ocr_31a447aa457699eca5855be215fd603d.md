Filters are truly a blessing. They make short out of otherwise tedious tasks, and they’re
fun. The only thing you need to be leery of the overuse of any cool new techniques.
Truly, in practice, less is more, and an effect used sparingly has more impact.
There have been too many times that I have been guilty of wearing out new tricks, and it
is even easier to observe in other people’s work. A Web site that would be slick, if only it
wer’t for the fact that there are five lenses on every page would be a prime
example.

# Q&A

+   - I try to apply a certain plug-in, but it's ghosted out in the menu.
- Well, if you've made sure that your image is in RGB color depth, you might want
to check for layering. Some plug-ins won't work with layers, while others require
them. The presence of another open image, or a selection, can also be an issue. It
it depends on the filter itself.
- How do I know which .bfs I can use with the User Filters plug-in?

+   To my knowledge, only Filter Factory filters work. Any Photoshop filters that
require the presence of dynamic libraries such as msvc.dll and the like will not
work under GIMP, which is to say any filter that behaves like a separate
application will not work. That’s what GIMP plug-ins are for.

# Exercise

Tinker with the filters I did not get to in this hour and see if you can finds for
them (and there are, many of them). Traditional logic would compel you to use the filters
you know for purposes you need, sometimes playing with filters just to see what
do can be an enlightening experience. You may come up with effects that you never
thought of.

Try the Lighting Effects plug-in in on some of those old filters lying around. A simple
photo cube and light source is enough to impress the heck out of your friends and family,
and it's really not all that difficult to do. (But don't tell them that.)
