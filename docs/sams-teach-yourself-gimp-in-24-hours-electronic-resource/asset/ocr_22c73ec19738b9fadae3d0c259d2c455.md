# Animations

Animations are one of the more entertaining aspects of working with digital graphics. Creating an image that appears to move is a little eye candy that can enhance any project. (For instance, what would the Netscape/Mozilla browser be like without that little animated lizard?)

You probably have noticed what impact a simple movement can have on a Web site. A scrolling banner or small, animated button can make quite a difference and enhance the site considerably without a lot of effort. (Wilber's eyes on gimp.org or the title on opencontent.org are good examples of animation used well.) Sure, it's frivolous, but people like things add a little life to something that is static and motionless.

Likewise, a Web page with way too many animated GIFs can be distracting and can look downright goofy, and thus detract from the entire experience. It is obvious that animation itself well, if used, can enhance or break a site altogether. (I'm sure you have run across sites built by newbies who got carried away with animated GIF fever. It is sometimes difficult to avoid the temptation to overuse a new technique.) Therefore, it is easy to see how much impact these animations can have.
