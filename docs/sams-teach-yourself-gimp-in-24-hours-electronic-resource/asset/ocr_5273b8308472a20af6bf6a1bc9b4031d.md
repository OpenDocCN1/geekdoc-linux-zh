Now you will attempt to give it some depth. Imagine there is a light source just above and to the left of the circle. It would shine on the area of the circle it was closest to and the other side of the circle would appear darker because it wasn't in the path of the light.

To achieve this look, you will rely on the Gradient tool. Because you don't want to change the color or color the pattern, but want to add depth, you need a gradient that goes from transparent (letting the pattern show where the light is hitting the object) to black (forming the shadowy area). This is a good opportunity to make a custom gradient. Bring up the gradient editor by selecting File, Dialogs, Gradient Editor, and clicking the New Gradient button in the upper-right corner. This brings up a box where you can give a gradient name. (I've named mine trans-to-black.)

Now, going back to the gradient box, right-click in the area where the gradient colors appear and other options will pop up. This is where you set the colors you want in the gradient. In the top of the options, you'll see it says Left End Points Color. This means the color that will begin at the left side of the gradient. Because you can place gradients in any direction you want by merely dragging the cursor in the different directions, it doesn't really matter which color you choose to go on the left or right. Directly under End Points Color is the option called Load From. Clicking that will bring up a window where you can choose a color to insert into the left end point. Because you are making transparent to black, choose transparent, which is RGBA (0.000, 0.000, 0.000, 0.000) and appears. Now right-click again to bring up the same options, this time go to Right End Points Color and choose your. Your custom gradient is now set in the gradient editor. You can leave it in the editor for future projects.
