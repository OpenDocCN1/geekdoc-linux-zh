## Perspective Shadow

The Perspective Shadow is like the Drop Shadow but with a touch more sophistication. It enables you to create a shadow with a much more realistic 3D perspective from any angle.

From the same menu as the Drop Shadow, the Perspective Shadow dialog gives you some important options. The most important of these is Angle, which is to the direction of the shadow itself (see Figure 21.12). You can effectively change the direction of the shadow by means of this control. You can alter the opacity, color, length, and horizon distance as well. As before, you will need a separate, layered selection, and then you can execute the script. Your shadow should appear.

When dealing with shadows, borders, glows, and other such effects, I find it handy to render the effect onto its own layer. Instead of trying to find the perfect luminance from within the plug-in or script itself, I will make the effect at full darkness (lightness), and then adjust the effect depth by moving the Opacity slider for that layer in the GIMP layer dialog box. That way, I can avoid guessing the perfect shade beforehand, and therefore avoid redoing the effect if I find it too light or too dark for my liking.

## Add Bevel

Under the right-click menu, select Script-Fu, Decor, and you will find a few scripts that add an aesthetic touch to your image, and therefore act as effective filters. One of my personal favorites is Add Bevel. This by far surpasses doing bevel by hand—even if it limits your options a little—and is indeed a fantastic time-saver. Again, as before, you will need a selection, preferably on a separate layer before you execute the script. The script simply creates a bump map and renders your object with a grooved 3D edge. And, of course, this effect is most useful on text headers in combination with a drop shadow (see Figure 21.13).

Sometimes it's useful to adjust the brightness and contrast of a freshly beveled layer in order to add a little more depth.

## Old Photo

Last but not least, here's a neat script that can come in handy from time to time. Old Photo uses a few subtle distortion techniques to replicate that old time look. Why spend so much time and energy making those old photos look better when you can make them worse? Believe it or not, this effect can have some surprisingly pleasing results.
