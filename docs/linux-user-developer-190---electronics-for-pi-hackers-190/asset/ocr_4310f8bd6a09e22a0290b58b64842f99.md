Stripboard and printed circuit boards provide a means of building a more permanent circuit than breadboards

Breadboards are great for educational use, but for a more permanent project, there are better alternatives. That's why stripboard and PCB construction

Stripboards, often referred to by the trademark Verboard, are similar to breadboards. They have an array of holes on a 0.1-inch pitch which allows you to solder from the back that comes to be made. There are important differences, though. First, components aren't grabbed when their leads are put through the holes, they have to be soldered into the copper

strips. Second, the copper strips are much longer than breadboards. For this reason, being in stripboard and soldering is necessary to make breaks in the copper strips where connections are not required.

In addition, you'll have to make additional connections by using thin copper wire that runs right angles to the copper strips. In simple-to-medium complexity circuits, it's usually feasible to work out the wire links, and the breaks in the copper strips.

You could do this using a pencil and a graph paper, so that everything you have to work out before you get your head around this, software design tools will also be available (discussed on p26).

Stripboard construction is suitable for circuits you want to keep when you don't need to build lots of them. If you anticipate making examples of a particular circuit, especially if you want to make your circuit so compact and build it, a printed circuit board is a better solution. Printed circuit boards (PCBs) are used pretty much exclusively in commercial electronics. The motherboard in your PC is an example of a PCB. +   1 Bend component leads With axial leads, such as resistors, will need to be bent at right-angles at the correct position, just through the holes in the stripboard. Hold the lead using small pins, close to the component body.

+   2 Fit components to board Place all the components in their correct positions on the non-side of the board, with the leads passing through. Use a piece of foam over the components to help them in place while you turn the board over.

+   3 Solder the components While holding the foam in place, turn the board over so the component leads is uppermost, and place it on your work bench. Make a solder joint between each component lead and the copper track.
