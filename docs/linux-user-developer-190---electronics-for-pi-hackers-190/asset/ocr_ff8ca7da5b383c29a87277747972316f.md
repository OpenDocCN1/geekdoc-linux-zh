# LED interfacing

An LED lights when a positive voltage is connected to its anode and negative rather to its cathode. We can control this, connecting an LED's cathode to the negative supply (0V) on the GPIO and its anode to an I/O pin configured as output.

To determine the value of the resistor, you'll need to look at the LED's datasheet, voltage and operating current. As an example, if the supply is 5V and the LED is 2V, then the resistor needs to drop 3V. Now, according to Ohm's law (V=I*R), if I is 20mA, we would then pick the highest standard value of 150Ω. As an example, you could connect the LED's cathode to the positive supply and the GPIO pin. It will then be turned on by writing 1 to the I/O pin.

# Switch interfacing

The original components, of the type we've looked at, have leads that pass through holes in the board. These are much easier to use when switching circuits by hand, but the alternative, which is common in commercial circuits, is surface-mounting of components. Surface-mounted resistors, capacitors and transistors are usually blocks with no leads; while integrated circuits do have leads, they don't pass through holes on the board. They're hard to build by hand.
