# Stream to Twitch with a Pi

Enable non-conventional uses for streaming with one of the most popular single-board computers

On a hackathon a year ago, we decided to build a 24/7 life-streaming device. Among other things, we learned how to create a Twitch-streaming device from a Raspberry Pi - and now can you! With high-res cameras and affordable hardware and hardware-accelerated HD video encoding, connections to now live video-streaming blew up. People stream real-life events, tutorials, and the most weird things - like eating at McDonald's - there are new purposes for being invented every day, and if you find a new and interesting subject, stream it.

Talking of viewers, they are one important aspect of modern livestreaming; with Twitch, you can chat with them and respond in real time, as opposed to traditional one-way live streams. Even if interacting with people isn't your goal, the chat still has a lot of ideas to help you. From remote-controlled robots to USB cameras,

You, too, can grab a webcam and stream. First and foremost, you can share your experiences with others - be it gaming or whatever you want to talk about. However, you don't have to get involved in the stream

With a streaming platform like Twitch, you can do it this and more. There are also technical reasons for using a platform like Twitch such as others enable you to stream in a way that you yourself cannot - particularly when you are assisting others in a project or having a large audience of viewers. For that, they need to stream or re-stream your video to everybody watching.

Compare the approach where each viewer connects to your computer to Twitch, this reduces your internet usage (drastically) when your stream is viewed by hundreds or thousands of people. As a consequence, this improves latency even though stream has to another server first, that can handle all the viewers better than your home router. Don't forget that
