The only other thing you'll need is component identification. If you buy components in bags with labels, you'll really need to be able to read them. With transistors and diodes, for example, part numbers will be printed on them.

Resistors and capacitors are different. Resistors use coloured bands to identify their value—see the instructions for how to interpret these bands. Several schemes use marking values and language—like 47J. Another common scheme uses three figures, with resistor colour bands, and four digits and markings in this case. A marking of 103, would yield 10,000pF (10 picofarads), which equals 10nF.

As identifying components, you will also need to identify their terminals. Resistors and capacitors can be connected either way, except for polarized capacitors where you need to differentiate the positive from the negative lead. A plus or minus sign is printed on the capacitor, and with electrolytic capacitors, a construction in the body identifies the positive side.

In the case of diodes, a band printed on the body identifies the cathode, and anode. Transistors have a flat part on the body, so much you'll have to consult the datasheet to identify the base, collector, and emitter.
