## Resistors

A conductor such as the copper inside a wire, allows an electric current to flow, whereas an insulator, such as the plastic coating on wire, impedes electronic current. A resistor resists between a conductor and an insulator in that, but not perfectly. The degree to which it resists the flow of electric current depends on its resistance, which is measured in ohms (symbol Ω).

In the realm of digital as opposed to analogue circuits, a capacitor may be thought of as a component that stores electrical charge. The charge of a capacitor is measured in farads (symbol F).

Values you might range from picofarad (pF), through nanofarad (nF) to microfarad (µF). Like resistors, values will normally be taken from the E12 series, and so voltage they can withstand, but usually a small voltage rating of 10V is adequate. For some applications, it might not specify the method of construction. Note that high-value capacitors are referred to as polarised (divided) electrolytic and tantalum according to their construction); positive and negative lead must be connected in the circuit the correct way. ## Transistors

Transistors are components that allow the flow of electric current in one circuit to be controlled by current flowing in another circuit. They can therefore be thought of as electronic switches. However, because the switched circuit can carry a higher current than the switching circuit, they can also be described as amplifiers. Transistors also allow a high voltage to be switched by a low voltage.

There are several types of transistor but the most common in simple interface circuits, and the type we will discuss is the bipolar transistor. In fact there are two types of bipolar transistor, which differ in the polarity of the switched current. These are NPN and PNP transistors. Resistors and capacitors have two terminals, whereas transistors have three called the emitter, base and collector. The circuit allows that by applying voltage to the base, turns on the transistor, which in turn allows a current to flow through the circuit between emitter and collector.

Unlike capacitors, which are defined by their characteristics, they are usually specified by a particular number such as BC544 or 2N907. The part numbers are much less meaningless than the datas will show any given transistor's characteristics.

## Diodes

Diodes are twofold components that allow electrical current to flow in one direction only. For this reason they have a positive terminal (the anode) and a negative terminal (the cathode), like capacitors, they cannot be connected the right way around. Their characteristics cannot be summed up as in fact they are defined by the part number such as 1N401. Their defining characteristics can be looked up in the datasheet, the main ones being forward and reverse voltage. You only need to look up such a diode if you are designing a circuit from scratch.

## Relays

Relays perform a simple function to switch one circuit with another. Some components are available in several form factors. They contain a coil, magnetic field attracts one. When current is applied to the coil, magnetic field attracts one, making a circuit open or closing another with high current.

## LEDs

Light emitting diodes (LEDs) operate to generate light. They have an anode and cathode and must be connected the right way. Their characteristics can be looked up in the datasheet.

## Switches

There are several types of switches, including:
- Toggle switches
- Rotary switches
- Momentary switches (push buttons)

Momentary switches turn on when pressed and immediately off when released.
