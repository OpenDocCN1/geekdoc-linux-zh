# Dell sticks with the 'If it ait broke, don't fix it' formula for another generation of XPS

You can opt for FHD resolution screen rather than 4K if you want... but why would you?

At first glance you could be forgiven for thinking the new XPS 13 9370 Developer Edition, is actually the last-generation XPS 13.

This has consistently remained the top of range small laptop, focusing mostly on keeping the internals bang up to date while still being incredibly thin.

The new laptop carries over a number of design elements from the 9360, shrinking in size and eschewing USB-A ports in favor of USB-C. The lid is tweaked, but the overall design remains one that successfully combines both an underrated business attitude and a 'Look at me!' wow factor.

The drop in size and weight represents a 24 per reduction in volume and it's a noticeable improvement. This is partially achievable due to a reduction in the size of the battery, which drops from 60wh to 52wh, although not due to increased efficiency. Aside from the loss of the USB-A ports, other significant space-saving changes have made it really code here.

When laptops get smaller, often one of the things to suffer is the keyboard. Frankly, that's not the case here. The keyboard mechanism remains just as good. We were somewhat disappointed that the Windows keys remain on the Linux edition - but apparently that's due to Microsoft's licensing terms.
