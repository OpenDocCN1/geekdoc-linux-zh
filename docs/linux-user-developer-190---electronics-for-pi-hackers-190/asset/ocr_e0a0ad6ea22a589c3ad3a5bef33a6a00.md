Distributions with a focus on security offering different levels of protection, from ultra-secure options to slightly tweaked versions of the main popular distros. User awareness of security is growing.

As time progresses, Linux continues to make and become desirable for all types of users who prefer to use the open OS as their daily driver. From powering millions of servers on the internet to touching virtually everybody daily, even if they don't rely on it. Its popularity continues to provide a rich variety of alternatives for all types of users, a promise for the next generation of Linux.
One growing trend within Linux distros is to instead offer a jack-of-trades experience based on a specific use case. Linux's roots are all powerful, with a wide range of users—from enthusiasts, developers, artists, and educators to just a few. But general-purpose Linux is now more accessible and powerful than ever before. This makes it easy to get started, and ensures that the vision of free Linux as something other than Windows or MacOS no longer feels second-class.

In addition to code, we are already seeing advancements in the realm of package distribution which mean for those who are new to Linux, installing and uninstalling applications will be a much easier improvement in this area very well. The same hardware-virtualisation technology is driving improvements in personal security — as well as users are increasingly conscious of the safety of the data.
