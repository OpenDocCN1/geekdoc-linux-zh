(The latter is the function of the mks utility, so the two programs, dformat and mks, must work in tandem to prepare the floppy disk for use.) The low-level format checks the recording surface more thoroughly than the high-level format does. The command-line entry to format a floppy follows this usage, where the -n option disables the verification performed after the format. The filesystem is usually indicated as /dev/fd0 for drive A (the first floppy drive) and /dev/fd1 for drive B (the second floppy drive):

Syntax
`dformat [-n] [filesystem]`

Note: The default format type is for 1.44 megabyte (high-density) floppies. The filesystem may also be indicated as /dev/fd0H1440 for a high-density floppy on drive A and /dev/fd1H1440 for a high-density floppy on drive B.
