# XXXVI Introduction

The company was founded on September 2, 1992. The founders were Roland Dyroff, Thomas Fehr, Burchard Steinbild, and Hubert Mantel, all in their mid-twenties at the time. Three of the founders were still at University studying mathematics: Thomas Fehr had already graduated and was working as a software engineer. The original intention was that the company would do consulting work and software development for clients; according to Hubert Mantel's account, this did not work out well as work was in short supply, and after a while the group had decided to Initially the company distributed a Linux called SLS (Soft Landing Systems). After they switched to Slackware, producing a German-language version in cooperation with Slackware's founder. Patrick Volkering.

According to the recollections of Bob Bauer (one of the earliest SUSE employees), the SUSE people decided rather than fixing fixing in Slackware before shipping their translated and enhanced version, it would be better to develop their own distribution. They also felt the lack of a good installation and configuration tool in Slackware. The result was that SUSE took Florian Roche's जिक्र as a starting point and began to develop YaST. (Florian joined the SUSE team.)

The first suse distribution was released in May 1996 and was numbered 4.2 (an intentional reference to the use of the number 42 in *The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy* by Douglas Adams).

At that time all early versions of Red Hat (and Red Hat clones) were ubiquitous in the United States, SUSE Linux gained popularity in Europe worldwide with the establishment of offices in the United States (1997) and in the United Kingdom (1999).

SUSE never attempted an IPO, although there were rumors that this would happen on one stage. The company went through a number of rounds of funding from venture capital and industry sources. Over-optimism and too rapid expansion led to a point in 2001 when the company was forced to downsize significantly. After that time, stricter financial discipline, the release of the enterprise versions, and the growing uptick of Linux by business put the company on a sound footing. With the takeover by Novell in 2003, the investors recovered their investment, while the market's average became clear during the dramatic and stayed rise since the announcement.

Originally SUSE provided one product (simply known as S.u.SE. Linux), which was released three times a year and was available for the x86 platform only. The current SUSE Professional is the direct descendant of this, and the current version number of 10.0 is one of a series that goes back to the original 4.2.

In 2000, the SUSE offering was split into Professional and Personal versions, and versions for other hardware platforms (Alpha, Sparc, and PPC) were released.
