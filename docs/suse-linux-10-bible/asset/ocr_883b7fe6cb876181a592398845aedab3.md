# The Limits of Shell Scripting

A large amount of the basic infrastructure of the SUSE system rests on shell scripts. In particular, the whole init and runlevel system depends on a large number of interdependent shell scripts. These are also read from /etc/sysconfig/ and elsewhere. They may have heavy use of basic standard commands as awk, sed, and grep work very efficiently.

However, as a user or system administrator, you may not necessarily consider that shell scripts are the easiest or most elegant way to execute some logic to get done. In general, shell scripts are best for the kinds of things they commonly do: relatively small scripts working at a low level. For more complex tasks, people often turn to one of the dedicated scripting languages. In practice, this means either Perl or Python (or that just Ruby). If you are interested in creating dynamic web content, look also at the PHP inline scripting languages as well as CGI scripts written in either Perl or Python, and take your choice.
