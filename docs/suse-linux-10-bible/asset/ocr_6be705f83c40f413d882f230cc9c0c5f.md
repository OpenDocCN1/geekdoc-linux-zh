# Foreword

My Brothers and Sisters,

Should we accept that this book is indeed a Bible? Let us use a dictionary to see what the definition of a Bible is.

The first definition is marked obsolete, and just means "a book." I think that anyone would agree that this tome is a Bible by that definition.

The second definition was "The Book by way of eminence . . accepted as divine authority and origin." Well, Linux has been known for its gurus who hand out snippets of sage advice. Sometimes sage advice is in many books, and beginners are often told RTFM (The Freaking Manual). Easy for the guru to say, when there are so many manuals. HOWTOs, and other pieces of information scattered about, how do you put it all together?

Therefore, a Bible is necessary. Hopefully it carries pertination to your religion, or in this case distribution. If the Bible tries to cover the pertination in every religion, the reader may become lost. That is why this SUSE Linux 10 Bible, and not some other brand of Linux.

Some people say Linux and Free Open Source (FOSS) are a religion, and that the people that support it are religious zealous. I don't think that is true, for the people I know in Linux are multifaceted. But when it comes to programming, we believe that Linux and FOSS offer education, government, and business the most flexible, powerful, and lowest-cost solution. All you have to reach out and accept that.

Like any good thing you have to practice, study it, and really understand what is being said to you. You also have to apply it to your life. Look for ways in your life that this software can help you, organize or your life by your better.

Many people think that Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) is simply made up of the cost of the hardware, software, and services of the solution you pick. How naive. TCO is also made up of the cost of not picking a better solution, one that is flexible enough for you to solve 99 percent of your problems instead of 80 percent of your problems. How much does it cost you not to be able to solve 19 to 20 percent?
