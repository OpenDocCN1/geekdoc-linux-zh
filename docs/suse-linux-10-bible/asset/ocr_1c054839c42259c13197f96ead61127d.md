Assuming that your modem is supported, YaST asks you for the necessary information to set up the modem and dial-up connection. In the first screen of the setup you are asked for a dial prefix if necessary (a prefix you might need to get an outside line, for example) and to choose between tone dialing and pulse dialing (tone dialing will be the correct choice unless your telephone exchange is antiquated). The next screen asks you for your country and offers a small selection of configured providers (ISPs). This means that the access is already known to the system for these providers. One or two have gone further and enable to use a preconfigured username and password to sign up with them, or to get anonymous access with payment collected through your phone charges.

If you already have an account with an ISP that is not listed, you you need to press the New button and add the details of the provider's name, the access number, and your username and password.

# ISDN and ADSL Connections

SUSE has very good support for internal Integrated Services Digital Network (ISDN) cards, and these can also be set up in the installation. In most cases, the card will be automatically set up, and you just have to provide the specific information given to you by your ISP.

Asymmetric digital subscriber line (ADSL) providers sometimes offer a particular hardware device to connect. These are USB devices. Unfortunately, there are a large number of different types and not all of them work with Linux. There are different standards in different countries and as a result, getting these devices to work on Linux has always been something of a problem. If YaST detects such a device during the installation, it attempts to set it up, but there are many cases in which USB ADSL devices fail to work with SUSE Linux.
