Filesystems and end cylinders are discussed in more detail in Chapter 3.

Start and end cylinders are often new concepts to new Linux users who are used to data sizes being defined in megabytes. Your partition in human readable format, such as MB and GB. The start cylinder, as this is the first partition on the disk, 0 (is the usable space on the disk), and the end cylinder is what we need to change. It is usually custom to select a swap size that is 1.5 times the amount of RAM in the system, but this is subject to conjecture. A reasonable swap size should be considered based on the workload of the machine you will use; modern PC systems have at least 512MB, so it is safe to use the standard 1.5 times physical memory. To specify you want the swap partition to use 750MB, +750M in the End cylinder entry box. The + signifies that you need to add space, the M is the unit of space needed, and the M specifies the amount of data is expressed in megabytes. You can also specify G for gigabytes, which will be using in the following example of creating a root partition.

After entering the size of your new swap partition, click OK to proceed.
