Setting up terminals and modems has always been a complicated task in UNIX. When you encounter problems, pinpointing the cause can be difficult. For example, not being able to access the modem could mean an incorrect serial port setting, a cable configuration problem, or an incorrect initialization string on the modem. This is one of those areas where experience and practice can make all the difference in the world. In the early years of UNIX, nearly all peripherals were connected to the serial port. Now, many devices—such as printers and terminals—are connected via the network. Chapter 13, "Networking," will describe how to set up your network and introduce you to some networking fundamentals.
