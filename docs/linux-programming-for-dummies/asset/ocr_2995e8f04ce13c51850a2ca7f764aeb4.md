You use the `-c` switch to include a copy e-mail address, as shown in the following example:

```
mail bob@mycomputer -c mary@mycomputer < memo
```

In this example, mary and bob both receive a copy of the memo e-mail message.

# Inserting a blind-copy address

The *blind copy* is a feature of the **mail** utility that enables you to send a copy of the e-mail to someone without the other recipients knowing who else is receiving the e-mail.

Blind copies often come in handy if the e-mail involves an internal political struggle, where covering your . . . well, you know . . . is important. You can send the e-mail without tipping your hand to your adversary by using the blind-copy feature.

The switch `-b` is the switch that you use to create a blind copy, as shown in the following example:

```
mail bob@mycomputer -b boss@mycomputer -c mary@mycomputer < memo
```

In this example, the **mail** utility sends the memo to bob and a copy to mary. Bob can see Mary's e-mail address in the e-mail, so he knows that Mary's receiving a copy of memo. The boss also receives a copy, but neither Bob nor Mary sees the boss' e-mail address in the e-mail. On the other hand, the boss sees all the e-mail addresses of everyone who's receiving the memo.

You accomplish the task of sending blind copies to many addresses in a similar fashion. Say that, instead of sending Sandy, Joanne, and Ann a standard copy, I want to send them a blind copy. The following command shows how I do it:

```
mail bob@mycomputer -b sandy@mycomputer joanne@mycomputer<br>ann@mycomputer < memo
```
