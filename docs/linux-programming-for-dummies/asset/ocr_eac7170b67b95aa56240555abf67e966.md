# The inside story of redirection

Your computer needs to know where to get information. The nerds call this standard *in* (short for input). Where does your computer get data? Linus Torvalds answered that question when he made Linux. His choice was the keyboard.

How about the *screen*? (Brilliant.) This standard is known as *error*.

Your computer also needs to know where to show information. Computer folks call this standard *out* (short for output). And where does your computer display information? Linus Torvalds decided to show it on-screen.

You can change how information flows in your computer by using the redirection signs: the less-than ($\u003c$) and greater-than ($\u003e$) signs, as follows:

+   - The $\u003c$ sign tells the computer to get information from a file.
+   - The $\u003e$ sign tells the computer to send information to a file.
+   - The string $2\u003e$ redirects all error messages to a file.

Your computer needs to know one more thing: If something goes wrong, where do you want it to send the error message?
