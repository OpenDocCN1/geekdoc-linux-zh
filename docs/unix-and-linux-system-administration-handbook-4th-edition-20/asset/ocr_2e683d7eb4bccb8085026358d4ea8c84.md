# 20.2 THE ANATOMY OF A MAIL MESSAGE

A mail message has three distinct parts:

+   - The envelope
- The headers
- The body of the message

The envelope determines when the message will be delivered or, if, to whom it be returned. The envelope is invisible to users and is not part of the message itself; it’s used internally by the MTA.

The envelope addresses generally agree with the From and To lines of the header and headers may not agree if the message was sent to a mailing list or was generated by a spammer who is trying to conceal his identity.

The headers are a collection of property/value pairs as specified in RFC5322. They record all kind of information about the message, such as the date and time, the agents through which it passed on its journey, and who is to and from. The headers are a bona fide part of the mail message, but users often hide the less interesting ones when displaying messages for the user.

The body of the message is the content to be sent. It usually consists of plain text, although that often represents a mail-safe encoding of various types of binary content.

# Reading mail headers

Dissecting mail headers and using them to locate problems within the mail systems is essential sysadmin skill. Many users hide the headers, but there is usually a way to see them, if you have to use an editor on the message store. Below are most of the headers (with occasional truncations indicated by ...) a typical nonstop message. We remove another half of headers that Gmail uses as part of its spam filtering.
