# Policy enforcement

Technical measures can never protect against these attacks because they target your site's most vulnerable weakness: its users. Your best defense is a well-educated user base. To a first approximation, no legitimate email or web site will

+   - Suggest that you have won a prize
- Request that "verify" account information or passwords
- Ask you to force a piece of email
- Ask you to install software you have not explicitly searched for
- Inform you of a virus or other security problem

Users who have a basic understanding of these dangers are more likely to make sensible choices when a pop-up window claims they have won a free Macbook.

# Policy enforcement

Log files may prove to beyond a shadow of a person X did bad thing Y, but to a court it is just hearsay evidence. Protect yourself with written policies. Log files sometimes include time stamps, which are useful but not necessarily admissible unless your computer is running the Network Time Protocol (NTP) to keep your clock synced to a reference standard.

You may need a security policy in order to prosecute someone for misuse. It should include a statement such as: "Unauthorized use of computing systems may involve not only transgression of organizational policy but also a violation of state and federal laws. Unauthorized use is a crime and may involve criminal and civil penalties; it will be prosecuted to the full extent of the law.”

We advise you to display a splash screen that advises users of your snooping. You might say something like: "Activity may be monitored in the event of a real or suspected security incident."

You may want to ensure that users see the notification at least once by including it in the startup files you give to users. If you use the SSH to log in (and you should), you can configure `/etc/ssh/sshd_config` so that SSH allows the splash screen.

Be sure to specify that by the act of using their accounts, users acknowledge your written policy. Explain where users get copies of policy documents and post keys on an appropriate web page. Also include the specific penalty for noncompliance (deletion of the account, etc.). It is more important that you demonstrate a good faith effort to notify users of their responsibilities than that the notifications precisely correct in a legal sense.

In addition to the splash screen approach, a good idea is to have users sign a policy agreement before they are given access to your systems. This acceptable agreement should be crafted in conjunction with your legal department. If you don't have signed agreements from current employees, make a sweep to collect them, then signing the agreement a standard part of the induction process for new hires.
