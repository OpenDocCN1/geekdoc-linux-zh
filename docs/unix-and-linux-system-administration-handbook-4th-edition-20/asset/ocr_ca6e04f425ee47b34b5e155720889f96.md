UPSs need maintenance, too. This function is probably outside the scope of your sysadmin duties, you are responsible for ensuring that it is performed.

If you have a generator, contract with a local company that can deliver fuel for the generator when you need. Keep enough fuel on hand to power your systems during an extended outage, but remember that fuel eventually goes bad. Gasoline starts to turn in as little as one month. Even when treated with a stabilizer additive, gasoline should not be stored for more than a year. Diesel is more chemically stable than gasoline but can support the growth of algae, anyway. Consider an algal additive for diesel that will be held for an extended period.

Most outages are of short duration, but plan for two hours of battery life so that you have time to shut down machines properly in the event of a longer interruption. Most UPS use a USB port or Ethernet interface that you can use to initiate a graceful shutdown of noncritical machines after a defined period without power.

Take advantage of power outages to get any five-minute upgrades that you have already tested but not yet deployed. You are down anyway, so people expect to be inconvenienced. In some shops, an five minutes during a power outage is easier to accept than a scheduled downtime with a week’s notice. If you have machines that you suspect not are in use, leave them off unless someone complains. It might not be until weeks later—or never—the “missing” machine is noticed.

Cooling systems have a notification system that you can call if the temperature gets too hot. Tune the value of “high” so that you have time to get in before machines start to fry after the cooling systems page; use 72 degrees instead of 90, but some of us in the mountains 45 minutes away (summer, indeterminate in winter). Keep a couple of mechanical or battery-operated thermometers in the machine room—losing power means that you lose all those nifty electronic indicators that normally tell you the temperature.

If you co-locate equipment at a remote site, ask the hosting site’s backup power facilities before you sign a contract. Verify that the generator is real and tested regularly. Ask to be present at the next generator test; whether or not you get to see an actual test, you’re likely to get useful information.

# Internet connection redundancy

ISPs are occasionally swallowed as part of a merger. Such mergers demolish many companies’ carefully laid plans for maintaining redundant connections to the Internet. A post-merger ISP often consolidates circuits belonging to the independent companies. Customers formerly had independent paths to the Internet may now have both connections running through single conduit and once again be at the mercy of a single backbone cut.
