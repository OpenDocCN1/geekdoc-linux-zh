# 20.7 EMAIL CONFIGURATION

The heart of each email system is its MTA, or mail transport agent. sendmail was the original UNIX MTA, written by Eric Allman while he was a graduate student at UC Berkeley many years ago. Since then, a host of other MTAs have been developed. Some of them are commercial products and some are open source implementations. In chapter we cover three open source mail transport agents: sendmail. Postfix by Wietse Venema of IBM Research, and by Philip Hazel of the University of Cambridge.

After the top-level design of the mail system, configuration of the MTA is the next big sysadmon. Frankly, the default or sample configurations that ship with MTAs are often very close to the average site needs. You don't have to start from scratch when configuring your MTA.

SecuritySpace(securityspace.com) does a monthly survey to determine the market share of the various MTAs. In their December 2009 survey, 1.7 million out of 2 million MTAs reported, and 95,000 replied with a banner that identified the MTA software in use. Table 20.4 shows these results, as well as the SecuritySpace results for 2007 and 2001 values from a different survey.

The trend is clearly away from sendmail and toward Exim and Postfix, with Microsoft gaining market share and leveling off. By among our example systems, the UNIX variants all ship sendmail. Ubuntu is moving from Postfix to Exim, SUSE ships Postfix, and Red Hat includes all three defaults to sendmail.
