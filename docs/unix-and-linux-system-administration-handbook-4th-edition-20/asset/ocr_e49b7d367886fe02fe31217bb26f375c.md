# Greylisting/DCC

Greylisting is a scheme in which you configure your mail server to defer all connections from unrecognized IP addresses, for 15 minutes to an hour. The server rejects the mail with a “try again” message. Real MTAs sending users’ email will wait and then try again; spammers will move on their lists and won't retry.

Greylisting has been implemented for hot MTAs; it is especially effective as a spam-fighting tool DCC (the Distributed Checksum Clearinghouses; from rhyholite.com/dcc) that detects the “bulkiness” of a message by a fuzzy checksum and seeing how other mail servers have seen that same checksum. It is not real a spam detector per se, but a bulk detector. If you whitelist the bulk email you expect to receive (such as mailing lists you belong to), then the remaining detections of unsolicited bulk email, which is pretty much the definition of spam.

DCC can do greylisting as well; it is used as a miller and greylist or reject inline during an SMTP session. Because DCC does not do pattern matching as SpamAssassin-type tools do, it is not fooled by spammers who add randomness to their messages in an attempt to foil the pattern matchers.

The effectiveness of greylisting has declined (from more than 97% to below 90%) as spammers have begun to take it seriously and spruced up their SMTP implementations. However, it is still effective when used with blacklists because the automated maintainers often manage to spamming sites onto the blacklist before the retry period has elapsed.

# SpamAssassin

SpamAssassin (spamassassin.apache.org) is an open source Perl module written by Habeeb Dihu and maintained by Ian Lustman. It is a good product of identifying spam. It can be invoked through a miller and is used in lots of antispam products.

SpamAssassin uses a variety of ad hoc rules to identify spam. The rules used to be updated frequently, but they seem to be less actively maintained these days.
