Name servers are associated with zones, not domains. You can determine a given name (such as booklab.astrust.com) identifies a subdomain rather than by checking DNS. Subdomains have name server (NS) records associated with them.

Domain names originally had to be made up of letters, numbers, and dashes, with each component (label) being at most 63 characters long and an entire FDN being less than 256 characters. FDNs are not sensitive, but they are usually written in lowercase letters. Domain names were liberalized by RFC2181.

The ongoing internationalization of domain names is forcing changes to these to allow lower FDNs. Characters in non-roman alphabets are represented through an encoding called Punycode, in spirit to Unicode but with different implementation details.

There are two types of top-level domains: country code domains (ccTLDs) and generic top level domains (gTLDs). ICANN, the Internet Corporation for Assigned Names and Numbers, accords various agencies to parts of its shared registry for registering names in the gTLDs such as com, net, and org. As of this writing, you have like 1,000 choices for a registrar and 21 gTLDs in to register. Check icann.org for the definitive list. ICANN is in the process of creating more gTLDs.

To register for a ccTLD name, check the IANA (Internet Assigned Numbers Authority) page iana.org/cctld to find the registry in charge of a particular country's registration.

# Registering a second-level domain name

To obtain a second-level domain name, you must apply to a registrar for the appropriate top-level domain. To complete the domain registration process, you must choose a name that is not already taken and identify a technical contact person, an administrative contact person, and at least two hosts that will be name servers for your domain. Fees vary among registrars, but these days they are generally inexpensive.

# Creating your own subdomains

The procedure for creating a subdomain is similar to creating a second-level domain, except that the central authority is now local (or more accurately, within your own organization). Specifically, the steps are as follows:
