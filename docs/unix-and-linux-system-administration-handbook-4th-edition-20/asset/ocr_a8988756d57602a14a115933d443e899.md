On systems that support passwords of arbitrary length, you can use the phrase as the password (it then becomes a “passphrase”). Or, you can reduce the phrase to a shorter password by recording only the second letter of each word or by some similar transformation. Password security is increased enormously if you include numbers, punctuation marks, and capital letters, and some systems require this.

If your site has hundreds of computers, you should have hundreds of root passwords? It depends on your environment and risk tolerance, but probably not. A good rule of thumb is that machines are clones (e.g., desktop workstations) should have the same root password. Servers should have unique passwords. In particular, every major piece of network and routing infrastructure should be separately protected.

Make sure you have accurate records that tell you machines are sharing a root password. It’s also important that you have a structured way to change root passwords on the machines that share them. Left-behinds are a security risk and an administrative headache.

Change the root password

+   - At least every three months or so
- Every time someone who knows the password leaves your site
- Whenever you think security may have been compromised

It’s often said that passwords “should never be written down,” but it’s perhaps more accurate to say that they should never be accessible to the wrong people. Root passwords and other important passwords probably should be written down or stored in a cryptographic vault so that there is room for administrators to get to them in the event of an emergency. # Logging in to the root account

Since root is just another user, you can log directly to the root account and work will upon the system. However, this turns out to be a bad idea. With it leaves no record of what operations were performed as root. That’s bad when you realize that you broke something last night at 3:00 a.m. and can’t remember what you changed; it’s even worse when an access was unauthorized and you are trying to figure out what an intruder has done to your system. Another disadvantage is that the log-in-as-root scenario leaves no record of who was really doing the thing. If several people have access to the root account, you won’t be able tell who used it and when.
