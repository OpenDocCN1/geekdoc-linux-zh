# 21.3 SMOKEPING: GATHERING STATISTICS OVER TIME

As mentioned earlier, even a healthy network occasionally drops a packet. On the other hand, networks should not drop packets regularly, even at a low rate, because the impact on users can be disproportionately severe. Because high-level protocols often function in the presence of packet loss, you might never notice dropped packets unless you're actively monitoring for them.

For this purpose, you may want to check SmokePing, an open source tool by Tobias Oetiker that tracks of network latencies. SmokePing sends ping packets to a target host at regular intervals. It shows the history of each monitor linked through a web front and can send alerts when goes amiss. You can get a copy from smoketier.com/smokeping.

Exhibit A a SmokePing graph. The vertical axis is the round trip of pings, and the horizontal axis is time (weeks). The black line from which the gray spikes up indicates the median round trip time; the spikes themselves are the transit times of individual packets. Since the gray in this graph appears only below the median line, the great majority of packets must be traveling at close to the median speed, with just a few being delayed. This is a typical finding.
