Here is some information to gather as you start your green IT assessment:

+   **Equipment survey** – everything, including servers, laptops, workstations, monitors, printers, storage devices, network gear, backup devices, UPSs, and cooling units. Capture the location, model number, "size" (in units appropriate to the specific equipment), and each age of item.

It's helpful to have power consumption data for each item well. Rated power consumption can be misleading—better to measure a device's actual energy use with a Kill A Watt meter, which costs around $20. For devices that have both active and sleep states (e.g., printers), you may want to record average energy use over a one-day or one-week period.

+   **Accounting for consumables** – power, toner, storage media

+   **Organizational metrics** – including gross revenue, number of employees, number of physical locations, total facility energy consumption, IT equipment consumption (in data centers), data center energy consumption, total IT capital cost, total IT operations cost, and total facilities for data centers.

Once you've collected this baseline data, identify three targets for optimization. These targets should be tied to your organization's overall strategy for success and growth, and if achieved, they should also demonstrate progress toward becoming a green IT shop. We can't tell you what targets will work best for your environment, but here are some examples:

+   * Data center energy consumption per dollar of gross revenue
* Number of employees per physical server
* Sheets of paper used per employee per month
* Average energy consumption of an employee's workspace equipment
* Average life of a laptop computer
* Data center energy use as a proportion of total facility use
