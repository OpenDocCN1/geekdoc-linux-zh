The University of California at Berkeley (berkeley.edu) uses anycast IP addresses to replicate caching servers. # 17.7 WHAT'S NEW IN DNS

One of the newest developments in the DNS world is the use of DNS records to authenticate and verify the integrity of email messages. This system, called DomainKeys Identified Mail (DKIM), helps expose phishing (e.g., mail that appears to come from your bank and asks you to “verify” your account information). DKIM also detects attackers who forge the sender's address.

In the DKIM system, originating email servers sign outbound messages with a cryptographic private key. The corresponding public key is published as a DNS TXT record. Email receivers can verify the integrity and origin of a message by looking up the DKIM (public) key of the message's ostensible origin and checking against the message's signatures.

The DNS system does not require a change to your DNS software, but it does require the cooperation of your outgoing email server (to sign messages) and incoming email server or mail reader (to verify the signatures). From DNS point of view, only the configuration and data files need change to support a new subdomain called _domainkey.

Even better, a construct called the Author Domain Signing Practice (ADSP) declaration allows a site to say whether it signs all, or none of its outgoing email for each DNS zone. Receiving sites can use this policy statement to decide how to treat unsigned messages and messages whose signatures cannot be verified.

For example, a bank that generates several categories of email (e.g., marketing messages, account statements, and wire transfer instructions) could create a subdomain for each function and institute policies for each. Readers can ignore missing or mismatched signatures on advertisements or reject messages that are out to be secure.

This mechanism for expressing policy is similar to the Sender Policy Framework (SPF) system, which defines a way for organizations to publish the identities of their valid mail servers in DNS so that spammers who try to forge the From address can recognize and have them rejected.
