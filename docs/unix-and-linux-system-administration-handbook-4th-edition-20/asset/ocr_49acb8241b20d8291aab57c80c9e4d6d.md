# Miltering: mail filtering

The developers of sendmail created an API that lets third-party programs filter the headers and content of mail messages as they are being processed by the MTA. These "milters" are used for spam fighting, virus detection, statistical analysis, encryption, and host of other purposes. Milters are fully supported by the sendmail and Postfix MTAs; Exim uses filters and ACLs instead. For a catalog of available milters, complete with user ratings, license information, and statistics on downloads and updates.

MTAs invoke milters on incoming messages while they are still connected to the sending site. The MTA can recognize the profile of a virus or spam message and report back to the MTA, discard the message, create log entries, or take whatever other action you feel is appropriate. Milters have access to metadata and message content.

Miltering is potentially a powerful tool for fighting spam and viruses for violating users' privacy. A tough situation evolves when managers want to know exactly what proprietary information is leaving the organization by email, while employees feel that their email should be private. Make sure employee agreements are explicit about any kind of scanning you intend to do.

# SPF and Sender ID

The best way to fight spam is to stop it at its source. This sounds simple and easy, but in reality it is an impossible challenge. The structure of the Internet makes it difficult to track the real source of a message and to verify its authenticity. The community needs a sure-fire way to verify that the entity sending an email is really who or what it claims to be.
