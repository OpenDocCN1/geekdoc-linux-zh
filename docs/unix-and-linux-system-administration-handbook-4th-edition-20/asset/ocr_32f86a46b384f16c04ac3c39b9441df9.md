BSDI fueled explosive growth in the early Internet as it became the operating system of choice for early Internet service providers (ISPs).

In an effort to recapture the genie that had escaped from its bottle in 1973, AT&T filed a lawsuit against BSDI and the Regents of the University of California in 1992, alleging code copying and trade of secrets. It took AT&T's lawyers over two years to identify the offending code. When all was said and done, the lawsuit was settled with three files (out of more 18,000) removed from the BSD code base.

Unfortunately, this two-year period of uncertainty had a devastating effect on the entire UNIX world, BSD and non-BSD versions alike. Many companies ship to Microsoft Windows, fearful that they would end up at the mercy of AT&T as it hugged its child to near-death. By the time dust cleared, BSDI and the CSRG were mortally wounded. The BSD era was coming to an end.

Meanwhile, Linus Torvald, a Helsinki college student, had been playing with Minix and began writing his own UNIX clone. By 1992, a variety of Linux distributions (including SuSE and Yggdras Linux) had emerged. 1994 saw the establishment of Red Hat and Linux Pro.

Multiple factors have contributed to the phenomenal success of Linux. The strong community support enjoyed by the system and its vast catalog of software from the GNU archive Linux made quite a powerhouse. It works well in production environments, and some argue that you can build a more reliable and performant system on Linux than you can on any other operating system. It is also interesting to consider that part of Linux's success may relate to the golden opportunity created for it by AT&T' action against BSDI and Berkeley. That ill-timed lawsuit scared into the hearts of UNIX advocates right at the dawn of e-commerce and the start of the Internet bubble.

But who cares? What remained constant through all these changes was the need for system administrators. A UNIX system administrator's skill set is directly applicable to Linux, and most system administrators guided their users gracefully through the turbulent seas of the 1990s. That's another important characteristic of a good system administrator: calm during a storm.
