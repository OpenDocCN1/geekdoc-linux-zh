amavisd-new communicates with the MTA through a local domain or TCP socket. It can filter in-line (when used as a milter, before the MTA has accepted a message) or after a message has been accepted before it is delivered to the recipients.

Why use another piece of software when your MTA can do its own scanning with milters and like? One answer is that it's convenient to keep the configuration of all your filters in one place. It is also likely to be easier to respond to a new attack or to include a new tool if all your filtering is coordinated through one interface. Another benefit is that the scanner can run on a separate machine and thereby distribute some of the load of accepting and processing messages on a busy server. A good compromise is to do easy, quick checks in your MTA and to hand the more expensive checks to a tool like amavismd.

amavisd can interface to any antispam and antivirus scanning packages. It is quite powerful but has a couple of disadvantages:

+   - The documentation is a bit scattered, and it's not clear what is current and what is old and no longer true.

+   - The configuration is complicated. There are lots of parameters and subtly different variants of those parameters.

How amavisd works

amavisd stands between the MTA that holds the message to be vetted and the software that will actually do the checking. amavisd listens for connections from the MTA on TCP port 10,024, speaks SMTP or LMTP to receive messages and returns its answers to the MTA on port 10,025. It can also use a local domain socket if it is running on the same machine as the MTA.
