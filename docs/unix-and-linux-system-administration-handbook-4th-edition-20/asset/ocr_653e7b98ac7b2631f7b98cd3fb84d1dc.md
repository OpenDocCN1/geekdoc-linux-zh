The DNS system is defined by a series of RFCs, Several implementations exist, varying in functionality, focus, and adherence to the RFCs. Table 17.1 shows the major players. The market shares shown in Table 17.1 are measured with respect to Internet-facing naming servers, not internal servers.

This chapter includes general information about DNS and the sysadmon chores associated with the BIND, NSD, and Unbound name server implementations. Examples are drawn from BIND 9.7, NSD 3.2.4, and Unbound 1.4.1.

You might ask why we waste space on NSD and Unbound when their market is so small, especially in a chapter that is already so long. Three reasons:

+   - First, to deploy a truly robust DNS environment, should not have all servers running the same software. A successful attack on your site's DNS service essentially takes your site off the Internet. Diversity of software, hardware, and network connectivity are the keys to surviving the Darwinian pressure of the Internet. Add geographical location and systems to your diversity pile and you will be in shape.

+   - The second reason is performance: NSD and Unbound are significantly faster than BIND.

+   - Finally, of all the name server implementations, only BIND and NSD/Unbound implement DNSSEC, the security extensions to DNS. DNSSEC is better tested and more robust in the NSD/Unbound implementations than BIND.
