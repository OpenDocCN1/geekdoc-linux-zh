### Your team of Linux experts..

- Jon Masters is a Linux kernel hacker who has been working on Linux for some 19 years, since he first attended university at the age of 13. Jon lives in Cambridge, Massachusetts, and works for a large enterprise Linux vendor. You can find his brilliant Kernel Column on pages 16-17 this month.
- Richard Smedley has been helping businesses and community organisations of all sizes to move to GNU/Linux since the 1990s, and doesn't expect those he works with to share his love of Emacs. Well, maybe a little. This month he dives deep into Wine to show us how we can run essential Windows software natively (p.28).
- Liam Fraser is the creator of the RaspberryPiTutorials YouTube series and volunteers as a Linux server administrator for the Raspberry Pi Foundation. Liam combines his love of electronics and music in this issue, explaining how to map audio to an LED strip for real-time visualisation (p.62).
- Mihalis Tsoukalos is a UNIX system administrator with expertise in programming, databases and maths. He has been using Linux since 1993. Mihalis has been busy this month, creating some excellent guides to systems programming (p.20), R (p.36), D3.js (p.40), as well as this month's Coding Column (p.11).
- Dan Aldred is a Raspberry Pi Certified Educator and a Lead School leader for CAS. He is passionate about teaching and hacking projects, and uses projects like this to engage the students that he teaches. Turn to page 78 to find his simple guide to setting up Twilio with your Raspberry Pi, so you can send an SMS from the terminal.
- Gareth Halfacree is our resident news reporter and brings us the latest developments from all over the open source world, starting over on page 6. Welcome to the latest issue of Linux User & Developer, the UK and America's favourite Linux and open source magazine.

This month we're focusing on the developers among you: starting with a big guide to systems programming in C that we're going to follow with a run of tutorials over the coming months (page 20), we have some excellent guides that will get you started with C# programming, Docker containers, automation and security on AWS, and a pair of tutorials on R and D3.js to help you manipulate and visualise data. We'll be revisiting some of these before the year is out, so stay tuned.

With Windows 10 raising all sorts of privacy concerns, it was also time to complement the guides to virtualisation and multibooting that we ran in previous issues with a third option for giving you access to software that doesn't play nicely on Linux: Wine. Using this and PlayOnLinux, we show you how to run things such as Photoshop and your PC games on your favourite distro (page 28), so you can wash your hands of Windows altogether and help your friends do the same.

As for the Ras Pi section, we've got something really special for you. Remember the LED strip you used a couple months back? Well, we're turning it into a music visualiser (page 62). Plus, we show you how to text and tweet from your Pi – enjoy!

Gavin Thomas, Editor
