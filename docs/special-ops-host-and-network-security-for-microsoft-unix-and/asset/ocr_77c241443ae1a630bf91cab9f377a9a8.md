A risk assessment considers this scenario with a multitude of different variables, and defines a risk number to each item. This can be quite a daunting task to perform on your own, but there are tools developed to help you perform this. Here is a list of four products that can help you conduct your risk assessment (note that there are a multitude of products available):

+   Operationally Critical Threat, Analysis, and Vulnerability Evaluations (OCTAVE) A process document that provides an extended risk assessment format (www.cert.org/octave).

+   GAO Information Security Risk Assessment Case studies of organizations that implemented risk assessment programs (www.gao.gov/special_pubs/ai99139.pdf).

+   RiskWatch Software created that asks a series of questions to help individuals perform a risk assessment. Also includes modules for review against the ISO17799 standard (www.riskwatch.com).

+   Consultative, Objective and Bi-functional Risk Analysis (COBRA) Another risk assessment software program. Also includes questions that map against the ISO17799 (www.security-risk-analysis.com/index.htm).

> Simply running a vulnerability scan does not perform a risk analysis. However, most risk assessments include requirements for a vulnerability scan. Vulnerability scanners are covered in Chapter 3.

Let's take a closer look at OCTAVE as an example. It is a tool developed by the Software Engineering Institute (SEI) at Carnegie Mellon University. The goal of OCTAVE is to allow an organization to fully understand and manage the risks they face. OCTAVE usually has more components than most people want to throw at their risk assessment process, most because it was developed with large organizations in mind (over 300 employees). Due to demand for a simpler risk assessment, the SEI is creating OCTAVE-S, designed for smaller organizations. The OCTAVE-S is due in Q2 of 2003.
