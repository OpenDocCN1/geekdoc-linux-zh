# Summary

At initial glance, information security is a fairly straightforward field. When asked
by layman what I do for a living, and receiving blank stares when I reply “infor-
mation security,” I usually find myself explaining what I do to keep hackers
outout. But as we've discussed here, managing information security for an organiza-
t is not merely a technical position. As the beginning of the chapter,
"security" is the careful balancing of the integrity, privacy, ownership, and access-
bility of information. Effectively addressing all four of these requirements entails
a working knowledge of technology, business practices, corporate politics and
psychology.

The common element in all of these disciplines is the individual: the sys-
t administrator, the executive, the administrative professional, the vendor or
t partner. Despite all the statistics, case-studies and first-hand experiences, this
one common element that binds the elements of an effective security posture
is commonly regarded as not a core resource, but as the primary
obstacle. Although indeed steps must be taken to protect the organization from
t actions of these corporate entities, concentrating solely on this aspect of secu-
ity is in fact the issue at the heart of a reactive security stance. In order to trans-
to a truly proactive security posture, the individuals, every one of them, must
be part of the plan.

Actively engaging those who make use of your systems in protecting
t resources that they rely on to do their jobs distributes the overall load of
security management. Educating users on how to recognize security risks and
providing simple procedures for relaying their observations through have a greater impact on the potential for expensive security inci-
d than any amount of hardware, software, or re-architecting. Although then
years ago the number of people within an organization who had the capacity
cause far-reaching security incidents was very small, in today's distributed envi-
ment anyone with a networked device poses a potential risk. This shift in
t potential sources of a threat requires a change in the approaches used to
add them.
