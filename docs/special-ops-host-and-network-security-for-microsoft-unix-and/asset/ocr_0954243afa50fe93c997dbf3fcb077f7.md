Granted, this sort of event can be protected in most cases by the use of a password-protected screensaver, and physical security (such as locking the office on exit) further minimizes the risk. But you have ever misaddressed an e-mail, perhaps by clicking on the wrong line in the address book or neglecting to double-check what your e-mail client auto-resolved the Addresses to? It happened Cisco (a February 6, 2002 earnings statement was mistakenly distributed to a large number of Cisco employees the evening before public release, (http://newsroom.cisco.com/dls/hd_020602.html) and Kaiser-Permanente (“Sensitive Kaiser E-mails Go Astray,” August 10, 2000, Washington Post).

Data encryption, in transit and locally, can prevent such accidents as those who held proved keys. A step beyond password protection, encrypted data does not rely on the application to provide security, so not one byte-wise of the storage medium can ascertain the contents of encrypted messages or files.

A number of encryption applications and algorithms are available to address varying levels of concern, but perhaps the most popular data encryption tools are those built around Pretty Good Privacy (PGP) system, developed by Phil Zimmermann in the early 1990s. PGP has evolved over the years, from its initial freeware releases, to purchase and commercialization by Network Associates, Inc., who recently sold the rights to the software to the PGP Corporation Compatible versions of PGP are still available as freeware from the International PGP Home Page (www.pgpi.org). The commercial aspects of PGP lie in the usability enhancements and some additional functionality such as enterprise escrow keys and file system encryption.

PGP does take some getting used to, and unlike personal firewalls PGP is an active protection method. However, in my experience users tend to react positively to PGP and its capabilities; there tends to be a James Bond-esque quality to dealing with encrypted communications. For e-mail applications, PGP adoption is brought about more word-of-mouth, with users reminding one another... In the case of a misdirected financial report, PGP key selection forces the user to review the recipient list one more time, and if encrypted messages later find their way to individuals for whom the message was not intended, they will be unable to decrypt the contents.
