The question is, why weren't security policies in place if they could have helped prevent some of these incidents? Slowly, those in the industry have begun to realize the importance of policies and procedures as a mean to protect their information assets. The article Human Error May Be No.1 Threat to Online Security by Jaikumar Vijay at Computerworld reports on a story where VeriSign issued two digital certificates to an identical claiming to be a Microsoft employee. "The whole thing proves online security isn't about the technology," said Laura Lime, a vice president at Identrus LLC in New York, which was established by eight leading banks to develop standards for electronic identification verification for e-commerce."It is more about the operating processes and procedures rather than technology, that is crucial in preventing incidents such as these,"Rime said.

These examples demonstrate how employee's actions put cracks in the walls, and the best way to mitigate the crisis of cracks is with the proper policies—policies that are meaningful, understandable, and enforceable. The purpose of this chapter is to help you create a strategy and program to develop a system to create, deploy, administer and enforce policies.
