# Quantifying the Need for Security

One of the first things you can do to increase awareness is to attempt quantify the unknown elements of risk that cross your network on a daily basis. By simply monitoring current network traffic at certain checkpoints, you can get an understanding of what kind of data traverses your network, and with the trained eye afforded by resources as books this one, identify your exposure to current threats and begin to extrapolate susceptibility to future issues.

Depending on the resources available to you and your department, both fiscal and time-based, there are a number of approaches you can take to this step. Cataloging network usage can be fun too, for many geeks—you'll be amazed at some of the things you learn about how your servers and clients communicate. If your environment is such that you've already implemented intrusion detection, QoS (quality of service) or advanced traffic monitoring, feel free to skip ahead a paragraph or two. Right now we are going to offer some suggestions to the less fortunate administrators.

Get your hands on a midrange PC system, and build up an inexpensive traffic monitoring application using the Snort IDS (www.snort.org). Snort is an open source, multipatform intrusion detection system built around the libpcap capture library. Arrange to gain access to a spanning port at one of your internal network peering points. Make sure the system is working by triggering a few of the sensors, make sure there's enough disk space to capture a fair number of incidents, and leave the system alone for a couple of days.

Once you've got your improptu IDS system to know your network, take a look at the results. If you enabled a good number of capture rules, you will undoubtedly have a mighty collection of information about what kind of data is traversing the peering point you chose. Look for some of the obvious threats: SQL connections, NetBIOS traffic, and various attack signatures. If your data isn't, don't make the mistake of assuming that you're in the clear; many organizations with extensive IDS infrastructures can go days at a time without any sort of alert being generated. Just take what you can from the data you've gathered and put the system back online.

Regardless of whether you have data at your fingertips or if you need to deploy bare-bones solutions like the Snort system described here, your goal is to take this data and work it into a document expressing what kind of threats you perceive on your network.
