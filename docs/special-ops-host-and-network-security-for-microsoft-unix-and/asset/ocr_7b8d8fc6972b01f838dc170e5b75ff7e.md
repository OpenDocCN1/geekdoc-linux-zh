# Secure Your Equipment and OS

It should be something that doesn't need saying, but I'm going to reiterate a truism at this point. It is absolutely of paramount importance that your systems be secured against unauthorized access. Everything that is discussed throughout the book has to do with security. Yet, as I visited or I probe various networks and work sites I find that the equipment is not physically secured at all. Often, the doors are standing open and unlocked, cages are unsecured, or there is simply open air above and around the equipment that I can see. I've found network access points in hallways that were live on networks, and on unbelievable lackadaical attitudes that permit us to say to someone “I’m from IT, and we will be working on this equipment for a while and be allowed to do whatever I wish in the system with no challenge or questions raised at all. I've often wondered if people are as casual with their belongings that they perceive to be some-thing as they are with the equipment and information that they are supposedly trying to protect. So, here are a few necessities securing the OS:

+   - Secure the equipment from outside contact—that means from above, from below, from any side.

+   - Lock the doors, cages, and cabinets.

+   - Restrict access to equipment and network access points—enforce and follow the restrictions.

+   - Train your users about appropriate access to said devices and storage facilities.

Now that that has been discussed, take a look through the next few sections and think about how you can sufficiently secure your systems against intentional or unintentional damage.
