# Foreword

In the fast-paced, caffeine-powered, and sometimes reckless world of computer security, the security analogy of a “hard crunchy outside and soft inside,” a staple of the security community today, is ironically apropos as we spend millions to protect and fortify the outside perimeter network and spend a dime to address internal threats. However, as convenient it may be to leave internal systems free from controls, it is a disaster once someone “bites” through to unprotected inside; consider the potential damage (whether intentional or not) that could be generated by employees or partners who legitimate access to the center. Lackadaical attention to the soft and chewy inside could compromise your security at any time. The authors of *Special Ops: Host and Network Security for Microsoft, UNIX, and Oracle* immerse you in the intricacy of intranet security and the “soft chewy inside” frequently neglected in today’s security order. You will find the critical pieces to securing your vital systems from attackers (both friend and foe) and a near complete picture to understand your internal security risk.

The task of securing the inside of your organization is daunting and viable; so many systems, many vulnerabilities, so little time. You must manage a myriad of system frailties and control the day-to-day cyber mayhem. You may feel that to allocate your IT security resources to the battles that matter most, you cannot possibly do it all. At the end of the day, if your rights are not secure from the right measures, you might wonder what you are really accomplishing. Motion does not equal progress, and effort does not equal equality. Although you may be keeping everything under control in the short run, eventually some breach will test that control. Management does not care how many vulnerabilities exist, how difficult they are to fix, or how diversely controlled they are.
