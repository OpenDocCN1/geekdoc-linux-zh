All often, policies are written with the compliance requirements in mind. The author may forget that policies need to be read and followed by users. I can’t emphasize enough that policies need to be relevant to the end user and their needs.

# Make Policies Referable

What is the point of developing a policy if nobody is going to refer to it? Your policy may be a great literary work, and it may be well written and easy to understand, but if users don’t keep the information in their minds, they will forget what they have read. You need to use some strategy in your policy to keep that information in your readers minds, and one way to do that is to make them refer to it often. After, all your policies needs to be a living document, and part of being alive is it gets used often.

One way to encourage your readers to review the policy is to keep it close at hand. Your policies need to be easily accessible, either in print form, electronic form, or both. One of the best examples I’ve seen of keeping policies current was a client that presented them in four ways. First, they distributed hard copies to all the employees. Second, they all had the policies stored on their intranet, and also had multitudes of useful information.
