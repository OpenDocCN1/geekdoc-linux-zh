Let's use example: Say a new buffer overflow is discovered for the Apache Web server. Your vulnerability is the newly discovered buffer overflow. The threat is the ease with which an attacker can exploit the new vulnerability, such as if an exploit is active in the wild. Finally, the exposure is how much damage you will suffer if you were exploited. This is a server with client information, or a corporate honeypot set by network administrators to lure hackers away from the real critical systems with juicy names and listening ports? A properly configured honeypot will have no exposure if properly configured, because attackers could own it without giving access to any corporate assets.

You have probably seen other forms of risk assessment formulas, such as Annualized Loss Expectency (ALE) or Estimated Annual Cost (EAC). I believe the risk formula earlier is the best to use for all-around risk assessment, though individuals may need many.

At this point in your policy, you should have a scope defined for each policy you hope to implement, and you should have created a rough outline. You should have acquired management support and defined areas of responsibility. Management support is critical when performing a risk assessment, because you will be reviewing their most sensitive information. They may not feel comfortable telling you where their soft underbelly is located, which of their points are the most vulnerable. There could be any number of reasons why they may not be forthcoming with information, but in many ways you are like a doctor and they are the patient. You need to be honest and tell truthfully what problems and what vulnerabilities they are having. It doesn't hurt to have a manager on your side to emphasize the need for forthcoming information. This is why it is important to have identified areas of responsibility, so you know who you need to talk to each organization to get the answers you need. A little beforehand will help you from being bounced around staff members. It's much more powerful to come in with a manager and an organizational chart or job description, and know exactly who you need to talk to.
