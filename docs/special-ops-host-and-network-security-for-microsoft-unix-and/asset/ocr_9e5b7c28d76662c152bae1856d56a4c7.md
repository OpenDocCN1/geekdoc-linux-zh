# ## Testing Outbound Exposure

Outbound exposure is the capability of the server to initiate connections to hosts in other networks beyond the firewall or screening router; in other words, the outbound visibility from the server's perspective. Allowing critical business servers to establish connections for business can make a bad thing much worse. If an attacker is successful in penetrating your network perimeter and compromising a critical host, the last thing you want is to use your own host against you. Restricting outbound exposure can help mitigate this risk. When you restrict outbound exposure to traffic only, you limit the attacker's ability to connect to other systems and tools that might further his escapes into your network. This can be performed at the host and the network level. I would recommend both if possible, because an attacker may realize what is happening and disable the host level filters. This type of network filtering is called egress filtering, and can be used on Internet-facing networks as well as internal networks. The outbound exposure is shown in Figure 2.14.
