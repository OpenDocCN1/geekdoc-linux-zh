# Frequently Asked Questions

The following Frequently Asked Questions, answered by the authors of this book, are designed to measure your understanding of the concepts presented in this chapter and to assist you with real-life implementation of these concepts. To have your questions about this chapter answered by the author, browse to www.synergy.com/solutions and click on the “Ask the Author” form.

Q: Should I use WEP when it can be cracked?

A: Yes. WEP, MAC address filtering, and any other built-in security feature of your access point should be used. If you have the infrastructure to implement any of the 802.1lx or other products designed to provide security beyond WEP, you should pursue those as well. Even though some of these barriers can be broken, they make the attacker spend more time to get into your network-work, and unless they are adamant about gaining access to your network in particular, they will discourage the casual war driver.

Q: Can security measures I take for wireless clients?

A: In the locked-down wireless environments, the best way to gain access to the network beyond the access point is to attack the legitimate wireless clients directly. An access point may require 802.1lx authentication, use a VPN, and have MAC-level filtering, but if an attacker can compromise one of the real clients they can still gain access to the network using their credentials. Apply the same local system security policies to wireless clients as you would home Internet users logging into your VPN. Before you hand out the wireless cards, make sure the systems are fully patched, not running any unnecessary services, and have a personal firewall IDS installed.

Q: Can I detect people driving my network?

A: It depends on the tool. NetStummer and other products that incorporate nonmonitor AP detection flood the network with broadcast packets that are easily detected. Using a properly configured wireless sniffer of your own, such as AeroPeek NX or a wireless IDS, can help you detect NetStummer and other scanners using that method.
