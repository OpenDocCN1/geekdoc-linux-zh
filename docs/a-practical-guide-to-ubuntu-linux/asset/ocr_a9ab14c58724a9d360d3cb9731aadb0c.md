The Clock applet has an interesting Preferences window. Right-click the Clock applet (Figure 4-2, page 101) and select *Preferences*. The Gnome General tab of the Clock Preferences window. This tab enables you to customize the data and time displayed on the Top panel. The clock immediately reflects the changes you make in this window. Click the *Locations* tab and then add the *Add* button to enter the name of the city you are in or near to cause the Clock applet to display weather information.

Different objects display different Preferences windows. In contrast, objects that launch programs display Properties windows and do not have Preferences windows. Experiment with different Preferences and Properties windows to see what happens.

# MOUSE Preferences

The Preferences window (Figure 4-6, next page) enables you to change the characteristics of the mouse to suit your needs. To display this, select **Main menu: System❖Preferences❖Mouse** or give the command *gnome-mouse-preferences* from a terminal emulator or Run application (ALT+F). The Preferences window has two tabs: General and Accessibility (and a third, Touchpad, on a laptop).

Click the *General* tab. To change the orientation of the mouse buttons for a left-handed person, click the radio button labeled *Left-handed*. If you change the setup of the mouse buttons, remember to reinterpret the descriptions in this book accordingly. That is, when this book asks you to click the left button or does not specify a button to click, click the right button, and vice versa. See “Remapping Mouse Buttons” on page 274 for information on changing the orientation of the mouse buttons from the command line.
