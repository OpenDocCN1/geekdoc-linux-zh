Two of the additional special characters are the plus sign (+) and the question mark
?). They are similar to *, which matches zero or more occurrences of the previous
character. The plus sign matches one or more occurrences of the previous character,
where the question mark matches zero or one occurrence. You can use any one of
t special characters *, and ? following parentheses, using the special charac-
ter to apply to the string surrounded by the parentheses. Unlike the parentheses in
bracketed regular expressions, these parentheses are not quoted (Table A-7).

# Table A-7 Extended regular expressions

| Regular expression | Matches | Examples |
| /ab+c/ | a followed by one or more b's followed by a c | yabcw, abc57 |
| /ab?c/ | a followed by zero or one b followed by c | back, abcdef |
| /(ab)+c/ | One or more occurrences of the string ab followed by c | zabcd, ababc! |
| /(ab)?c/ | Zero or one occurrence of the string ab followed by c | xc, abc |

In full regular expressions, the vertical bar (|) special character is a Boolean OR
operator. Within vim, you must quote the vertical bar by preceding it with a back-
slash to make it special (|). A vertical bar between regular expressions causes a
match with strings that match the first expression, the second expression, or both.
You can use the vertical bar with parentheses to separate the rest of the regular
expression the two expressions that are being OReded (Table A-8).

# Table A-8 Full regular expressions

| Regular expression | Meaning | Examples |
| /ab|ac/ | Either ab or ac | ab, ac, abac (abac is two matches of the regular expression) |
| /^Exit|Quit/ | Lines that begin with Exit or Quit | Exit., Quit., No Exit |
| /(D|N)\. Jones/ | D. Jones or N. Jones | P.D. Jones, N. Jones |
