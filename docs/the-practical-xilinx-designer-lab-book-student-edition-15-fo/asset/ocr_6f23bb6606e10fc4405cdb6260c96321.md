The answer depends on the detailed electrical characteristics of T1 and T2, but for TTL, the answer is that a valid low logic level is any voltage between 0 V and 0.8 V, and a valid high logic level is any voltage between 2.0 V and 5.0 V. For CMOS logic, the voltage ranges are slightly different: 0V–1.0V and 3.5 V–5.0 V, respectively.

What happens if we do not connect the input to anything? In that case, the state of the circuit is unclear. The inputs to the transistors are not driven to any solid voltage and the transistors may be on or off. Thus, the output voltage could be anywhere between +5 V and ground. To prevent unconnected inputs from causing logic circuits to enter an indeterminate state, some ICs have pull-up resistors from every input to the +5 V supply (see Figure 6.2a).
