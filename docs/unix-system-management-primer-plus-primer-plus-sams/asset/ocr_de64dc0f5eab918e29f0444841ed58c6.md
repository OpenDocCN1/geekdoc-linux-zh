System administrators are in demand, but our jobs are more complex than ever before, receive a great deal more scrutiny, and demand higher accountability. Planning, coordination, and test- ing are now central to any system administrator's job; we can no longer simply jump into a project and hack it together in a couple of days. By reading this book, you have already real-ized this, and are ready to enhance your skills both yourself and your organiz-ion. The information in this book helps you to do just that.

# # How This Book Is Organized

Part I starts from scratch and introduces the planning process. Here you learn how to trans- a list of business goals into a fully functional system architecture, and how to make the right decisions in the process. You also learn how to move your systems into production and test them.

Part II focuses on the maintenance of your new systems, as a system is only as good as its tech-n support. Topics discussed in this part include support infrastructures, methods for moni-ng systems, patching and upgrade procedures, dealing with outages, and recovering from disasters such as a fire or crash.

Part III introduces concepts for tuning, sequencing, and automating your systems, as well as methods of planning for future expansion. This part of the book documents and tech-niques that you will find immensely useful on your systems. Many of these tools receive little attention in print, yet they are part of the standard system administrator's tool belt.

Topics in Part IV cover the human aspect of system administration. The topics covered in this part of the book also receive little coverage in typical system administration references, and they include communicating with your peers, managing your relationship with your users, and understanding standard policies and agreements common in today's information techn-ology environments.

# # For Your Attention

This book uses some special elements to draw specific types of information to your attention. Located throughout the book, you'll find Notes, Tips, and Cautions in graphic boxes. The information in these elements will help you better understand the issues and approaches- cussed in the surrounding text.

I've also included a number of Real-World Examples throughout the book. These short vignettes offer you a look at the "front-line" experiences of system administrators in a variety of business, ISP, and academic environments. By learning about the successes, chal-lenes, and mistakes other system administrators in the course of managing Unix systems, you gain some of the valuable insight you might receive if you were learning the job of system administration from the side of a seasoned veteran. You can build upon the in-formation you gain from these real-world system administration experiences to find your own creative solutions and best practices for keeping your Unix systems running smoothly and effectively.
