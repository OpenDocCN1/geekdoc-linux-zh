# Using Flexible Technical Support Structures

The three-tiered support model is considered the ideal situation for an organization because each level is isolated from the petty problems of the levels both above and below it. In a three-tiered system, for example, system administrators rarely rarely speak to the customers directly; that's the job of the help desk and, on occasion, the support engineers. And, since the help help desk in a three-tiered system doesn't dive into the deeper, more problematic issues, first-level support staff can dedicate their resources to managing trouble tickets and tending to the customers' needs.

Not all organizations, however, have the need or the resources to implement a three-tiered system. A large ISP may need a help desk with support engineers and senior engineers working behind them, but a small local ISP may only need a few help desk staff and the system administrators that maintain the shop scenes. In small organizations, all support staff need to wear many different hats.” The help desk may need to know a bit more about the underlying infrastructure to be effectively communicate problems to senior engineers, so help desk staff need to understand and involve themselves in the issues addressed by the engineers.

At the same time, the senior administrators need to be able to translate technical jargon into plain English for the help desk, and they might even need to talk to customers directly. Senior administrators in small support organizations can't entirely separate themselves from the duties and responsibilities of the help desk staff. Where organizations exist only online, communication with their customers takes place through email or Web Forms. In these organizations (especially in smaller shops), the engineers themselves may be answering the support mail.
