## Chapter 7

+ 1. A sandbox would mimic your production environment so that any tests you run in the sandbox will produce results comparable to those you should achieve on your production platforms.

+ 2. You should patch in single-user mode because there are fewer processes running that may interfere with or be affected by the patching process. If you are unable to bring the system down into single-user mode, you should choose a nonpeak time to apply the patches and kill all nonessential processes before you start.

+ 3. A shared library is a file containing code that is shared among different programs and. You must pay particular attention to shared libraries when upgrading an operating system, even a small change or incompatibility in an upgraded shared library can affect many programs that are critical to your system's functionality.

+ 4. To replace a disk on a server that does not support hot-swappable disks, you would have to shut down the server, remove the old disk, insert the new disk, power on the server, and (if the new disk is a different model than the old one) reconfigure your system to recognize the new disk. On a system that supports hot-swappable disks, you would simply remove the old disk and insert the new disk, letting the hardware and the operating system worry about the details of the disk-swapping process. (These are just generic procedures, however—always read your vendor's documentation before swapping.)

+ 5. A system administrator can determine the end users of a particular service using one or more of the following methods:

- Read the documentation for the service to get a feel for who would be using such a service.
- Extract the information from an account database, such as /etc/passwd for Unix shell users.
- Analyze the service's usage using log files or network traffic analyzers.
- Talk to actual users, sales people, account managers, or any other people who would have an idea of who is using a particular service.

## Chapter 8

+ 1. The best time to schedule a maintenance window is during the time of least usage for services the maintenance is affecting.

+ 2. Activity logs provide a history of previous problems and how they were handled. Perusing these logs before beginning work on an outage situation can save time by preventing you from making the same mistakes twice or even by providing the actual solutions or procedures to solve the problem.

+ 3. SLAs (service level agreements) specify the level of service a customer should expect from its provider, typically in terms of guaranteed uptime and response time. To avoid breaching a SLA for maximum downtime, you should monitor a service at an interval that is less than the specified maximum downtime for that particular service. This timing ensures that a system administrator will be notified of an error or failure before the maximum SLA for the service is exceeded.

+ 4. You should allocate time in your maintenance window to back out changes so that, if the upgrade or installation isn't successful, you can complete back-out procedures before the end of the window.

+ 5. Band-Aid solutions only cover the symptoms of a problem or add infrastructure to contain the problem. They do not solve the actual problem, and add complexity to your systems in the long run. Band-Aid solutions are appropriate in situations where you need a quick fix to a problem. However, Band-Aid solutions should be temporary; plan to follow any Band-Aid solution with measures that find and fix the source of your problem.

## Chapter 9

+ 1. Power outages are often accompanied by power surges and brownouts that can damage electronic equipment. Power surges often occur when power is restored after a power failure, and “dirty” power with fluctuating voltages can seriously damage equipment and threaten the safety of your data. Surge protection and backup, such as a UPS (uninterruptible power supply), are essential for protecting equipment from this kind of damage.

+ 2. A UPS provides battery backup in case of a power failure and it maintains a constant voltage level to protect hardware from "dirty power."

+ 3. A generator connected to your building's PDU allows you to seamlessly switch from utility power to generator power (and back) during a power outage.

+ 4. A hot site contains real-time copies of your data in addition to live hardware, you can seamlessly migrate operations to the hot site without having to move any data. A warm site contains only live hardware, you must first transfer up-to-date copies of your data to the site before you can migrate operations to the warm site.

+ 5. Disaster recovery planning keeps databases of all your resources, including employees, hardware, software, and vendors. If anything changes, the software updates its databases, and the change will automatically propagate to all of your procedures and documents. Otherwise you would have to make these changes manually, in potentially hundreds of documents.

+ 6. Distribute hard copies of your disaster recovery plan in multiple, hazard-proof locations—both in and away from your data center. You should also distribute both hard copies and electronic copies of the plan to all disaster recovery plan participants to take home with them.

## Chapter 10

+ 1. Fault tolerance guarantees 100% uptime by providing redundant hot-swappable components and software to support them. High availability provides automated mechanisms by which a service can remain available when individual components fail, but it does't guarantee 100% uptime; there may be short outages if components fail.

+ 2. Network services using the "least connections" method of load balancing should have long-lived (at least 5 seconds), nondirectional network connections. In these situations, the load on any system is directly related to the number of existing connections. Assigning a new connection to a system with the least number of connections guarantees that the new connection is processed by the system with the least number of connections.

+ 3. The most expensive form of RAID (redundant array of disks) is RAID-1, or mirroring. RAID-1 is expensive because each disk must be accompanied by at least one other disk. In contrast, RAID-4 and RAID-5 provide redundancy by storing parity data on one or more disks; the data itself is not duplicated and requires less disk hardware.

+ 4. The value of the missing bit is 1. Due to the transitive nature of the XOR operation, you can move various bits anywhere in an XOR equation. The missing bit (?) can be calculated as follows:

```
1 0 ? 0 1
1 XOR ? XOR 0 1 = 1
((1 XOR 0) XOR 1) XOR 0) XOR 1 = 1
((1 XOR 0) XOR 1) XOR 1 = ?
```

+ 5. You should distribute streaming media servers across multiple geographical locations (and time zones) in order to reduce bandwidth congestion on individual during periods of high usage.

+ 6. The Internet mail infrastructure uses queuing and redundant mail exchange (MX) records to provide what appears to be a highly available service that rarely fails.

## Chapter 11

+ 1. There was significant processing load on the machine in the past 15 minutes, but it has subsided within the past 5 minutes.

+ 2. User time is the time a processor spends executing code in user processes, while system time is the time spent executing kernel code.

+ 3. A multithed process contains multiple "threads of execution" in which different parts of the same process can execute simultaneously. Multithreaded processes are more efficient at executing small bits of code than forked processes (child processes spawned from a parent process); in addition, multithreading allows a single process to take advantage of parallel execution on multiprocessor systems.

+ 4. A CPU-bound process is a process whose performance bottleneck is the execution of code on a processor. An I/O-bound process is a process whose performance bottleneck is the reading and writing of data to or from the network.

+ 5. Placing a swap partition and a database partition on the same disk puts two very busy partitions on one disk. This would result in poor performance for those partitions and the disk as a whole if the database or swap were heavily used.

+ 6. When loaded into memory, a single copy of a shared library's code is used by all of the processes linked with that library.

+ 7. You should request burstable bandwidth so you can have a large amount of bandwidth for use only when you need it. Allowing for occasional spikes in traffic keeps your width solution economical without capping its capacity at a limit you must exceed only occasionally.
