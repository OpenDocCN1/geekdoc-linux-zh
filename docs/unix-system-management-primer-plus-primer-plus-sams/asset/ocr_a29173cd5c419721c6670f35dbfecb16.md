# Incident Response Procedures

Most sites on the Internet will eventually come under attack from other sites, receive a virus, or have to deal with a disgruntled employee trying to sabotage company data. Defining your responses to these incidents ahead of time will help you deal with them consistently and effectively—and minimize their damage to your system.

# Plan for Incident Response

Integrate your incident response procedures into your disaster recovery plans. You should consider a security breach a disaster, considering the amount of damage it can potentially cause.

The security administrator is responsible for executing the incident response procedures. Rather than handling everything on his or her own, this person should designate the responsibility for individual parts of the plan. The team should be made up of IT staff, facilities staff, and members of any other groups who have administrative access to electronic and building resources.

Security incidents take many forms, some of the more serious incidents include

+   - Compromised root account
- Denial-of-service attack
- Breach of physical security
- Attack on another site by your own users

Each of these incidents requires different responses, but in general most responses follow the steps:

+   1. Identify and stop all activities in breach of your security policy. This process—known as containment—minimizes damage while you investigate further.
2. Disable any local accounts used in the security breach.
3. Block all access to services used to gain entry to your systems. These services include network services as well as local programs, especially setuid programs.
4. Report all activities in breach of your policy to your legal department and the administrators of any remote sites involved.
5. Secure the services used to gain entry to your systems. This can be done with configuration changes, patches, or by removal of the services altogether.
6. Enable the services that you have secured and monitor them for any suspicious activity.

As you can see by reviewing the steps of this procedure, incident response entails more than simply stopping attacks in their tracks; it involves investigating and reporting the causes of security incidents, repairing their damage, and stopping breaches that enable the incidents to occur. These actions prevent history from repeating itself and causing you to suffer through similar incidents in the future.
