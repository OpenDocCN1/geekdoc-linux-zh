+   - Honor the legal protection afforded by copyrights and licenses for material you might
post or distribute electronically.

+   - Do not commit illegal acts using system resources.

Such "universal" responsibilities may be obvious to most people, but some responsibilities are
specific to particular businesses and might not be obvious to users. For example, systems at a
onprofit organization might require that users not use system resources for profit, as this
jeopardize the organization's nonprofit status. Health insurance companies might
require compliance with the HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act), in
that data must be kept private.

Users who are ignorant of all responsibilities could participate in behavior that leads to
severe legal ramifications for an organization. As the system administrator, you must ensure
that all users know and understand their responsibilities. Finding the most appropriate and
effective avenues for communicating policies and requiring users to verify that they have read
 understand the policies are essential steps in formulating user agreements for your system.

# # Security Policies

Since the event of the Internet, most organizations have placed an increasing priority on
security. You learned about implementing security measures in your system in Chapter 13,
"Implementing Security System." Security policies establish the goals and guidelines for your
organization's security in a formal document.

In addition to describing system and network security, these policies also define proper user
behavior, which is as important to overall system security as any system configuration.
The information in this section of the chapter provides a brief overview of drafting and implement-\ing security policy, but the topic is much too broad and complex to cover in a single chapter
this book. Several books provide excellent information on establishing system security and
crafting effective security policies (see Appendix A for a list of security references). You can
find more information in RFC 2196, Site Security Handbook.

# What Is an RFC?

RFC stands for "request for comment" and is the primary method for proposing standards in the
Internet community. You can find everything from a formal definition of the FTP protocol to security
handbooks in an RFC. A comprehensive archive of all RFCs is located at http://www.fqs.org.

# # Choosing the Site Security Administrator

Before establishing any response procedures, you should designate a site security administra-tor. This person is responsible for maintaining your security policy, implementing its require-ments, and monitoring compliance within your organization.
