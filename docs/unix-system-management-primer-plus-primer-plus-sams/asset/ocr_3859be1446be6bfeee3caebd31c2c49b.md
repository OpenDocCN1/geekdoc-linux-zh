# Keep a Hard Copy of Security Incident Procedures

As with disaster recovery plans, you should distribute and store hard copies of incident response procedures, in case a security breach renders your systems unusable.

# Involving Other People in Your Security Policy

Security is not just an information technology initiative; it requires the cooperation of the entire organization. RFC 2196 suggests that the following people be involved in the creation of security policies:

+   * Site security administrator
* IT staff
* Management from each group within the organization
* System administrators from each group within the organization
* Incident response team
* Executive management
* Legal counsel

One reason to include all of these in your policy decisions is to gain support from all departments in your organization. By involving multiple parts of your organization in the creation of its security policies, you quickly learn how each department utilizes the services available to them, and you are better able to identify potentially insecure practices. Legal counsel will verify the legality of your policies and provide the actual legal ramifications for policy violations.

# Enforcing Your Security Policy

A security policy must be properly enforced in order to effectively harden your organization's security. From applications, to password selection, needs to adhere to the policy, and it is the job of the site security administrator to ensure adherence.

The most important way to ensure your authority to enforce security policies is to gain the support of your organization's management and—if applicable—legal department. Because the security administrator is likely a member of a technology department, having the support of upper management can help to quickly resolve interpersonal disputes.
