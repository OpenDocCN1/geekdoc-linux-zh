# Maintaining Support Communication Etiquette

No discussion about communication is complete without touching on etiquette. As an experienced professional, you can find frustrating to repeatedly deal with people who do not speak the technical language as you speak. Your frustration can grow dramatically when you have to conduct communications at 3:00 a.m. from your bedroom phone, two or three nights a week.

Periods of high call volume are inevitable, and it's very easy to lose cool with the people who are calling you (usually the help desk shift). Regardless of your level of frustration with the situation, you will have to deal with every person in a professional manner. Help desk staff are out some common courtesy by doing jobs and calmly analyzing the problem you're presented with. Instead of yelling, “I can't believe you guys don't know how to deal with this,” you could take a deep breath, collect your thoughts, and say, “Okay, I'll take care of it and send you some documentation about this tomorrow morning.” Remaining calm and giving those who contact you will be a civil and helpful response—and your blood pressure.

# Support Tools

Software tools can help you and your organization manage your support infrastructure. Some are commercial, while other support tools are open source; prices range from free to tens of thousands of dollars. Some organizations prefer to use their own tools that better meet their specific needs.

Support tools provide a wide variety of functionality to help you manage, automate, and document your support processes. Some of the major functions you should look for in support software are as:

+   - Trouble ticket management
- Mail to trouble ticket gateway with auto-responder
- Customizable form fields for entering new tickets
- Trouble ticket summary reporting
- Escalation process automation
- Historical logs with search capabilities
