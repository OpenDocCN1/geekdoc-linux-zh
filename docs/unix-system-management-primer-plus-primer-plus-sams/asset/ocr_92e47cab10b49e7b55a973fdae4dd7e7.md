# Chapter 2 • DESIGNING THE DATA CENTER INFRASTRUCTURE

If you're still undecided about choosing a raised or fixed floor, your answers to the following questions may help you decide:

+   * **Is space at a premium?** Raised floors require ramps or stairs at the entrance to, and those represent unusable space. Even a relatively short 8-by-4-foot ramp can take over 30 square feet—space you'd have available for equipment in a fixed-floor room.

![](bbox=[0.139, 0.196, 0.203, 0.233])

If you are building your own data center and space is at a premium, consider building the ramp side of your server room. This trades potentially unused space in a hallway for valuable floor space in your data center.

+   *Have you already purchased racks?** A raised floor is most useful when cables are under the floor. Your racks need open or removable bottoms to accommodate this cabling. If you're stuck with cubicles that have closed bottoms, you won't get use out of a raised floor, because you'll still have to run cables above ground to run them into your cabinets.

+   **Is physical security a concern?** In a shared hosting facility, all hosted customers share the space under a raised floor. And its wide-open space, there is no way to organize or securely partition it. Cables from different companies can run over, between, and around each other, presenting a real opportunity for one company's cabling run to bring another customer's services. In a fixed-floor environment, the only physically shared spaces are the cable trays that run along the ceiling. While harder to access, narrow paths are more easily organized than the hundreds or thousands of square feet of open space under a floor.

# Rais Floor Hazards

Although raised floors hide cables so you don't trip over them, they present their own hazards. It is all too easy to trip over, misaligned, or missing tiles, which can be disastrous if you fall into a rack or drop expensive equipment that you are carrying.

In general, although raised floors do have benefits, unless you have a pressing need for one, it makes more sense to build or lease space that has a fixed floor. Fixed floors are cheaper, safer, and more flexible in the long run.

Standalone hardware is too large to fit into a rack and is designed to stand directly on the data center floor.
