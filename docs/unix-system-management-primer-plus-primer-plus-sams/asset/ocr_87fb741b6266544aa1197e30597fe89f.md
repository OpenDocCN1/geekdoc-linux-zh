# UNIX SYSTEM MANAGEMENT PRIMER PLUS

# Planning Software

Creating a viable disaster recovery plan can be overwhelming due to the number of variables that comprise these unpredictable events. Although it is possible to create a plan on your own, many Unix system administrators turn to outside companies for assistance with the process. many companies specialize in disaster recovery planning, and even provide complex software packages to help with plan generation. Two such applications are the following:

+   - Strohl LDRPS (Living Disaster Recovery Planning System)
  http://www.strohl.com/Software/LDRPS/default.asp
- Sungard® PreRecovery® Business Continuity Software
  http://recovery.sungard.com/recovery.cfm

The products from Strohl and Sungard are two of the most popular disaster recovery planning applications, and can be purchased independently of any disaster recovery consulting engagement with these companies. Their solutions are pricey, however, each costing over $20,000 for multiuser licenses. The cost of such software might become more manageable as part of an overall disaster recovery engagement with the vendor.

Disaster recovery planning software collects all of the information necessary for compiling the plan (as you've learned in this chapter), including resources, locations, personnel, contact information, dependencies, and even such mundane information as serial numbers. The software puts together a comprehensive plan for your business to recover from any type of disaster.

For example, you input into the software that your primary data center is destroyed; the software then locates all resources at that location, identifies who is responsible for each resource and compiles their contact information, and then puts together a plan for recovering services from that data center. If you instead specified that your office were destroyed, a completely different plan would have been generated. Generally you should have plans for each major disaster scenario, focusing on what is unavailable or what has been destroyed, as in the following examples:

+   - Data center destroyed
- Data center inaccessible for one week
- Equipment in data center destroyed, but data center still available
- Office destroyed
- Office inaccessible for one week

# Focus on High-Level Situations First

By planning for high-level disaster situations rather than individual disasters (such as a hurricane or flood), you can focus on the actual procedures rather than coming up with an infinite list of potential disasters. However, if there are procedures you can identify for certain situations (such as cleaning after a fire-suppression system has been activated), planning software gives you the flexibility to generate plans specific to these situations.
