# UNIX SYSTEM MANAGEMENT PRIMER PLUS

Most operating systems have built-in facilities for log rotation, but it's easy enough to write your own if the need arises. Red Linux rotates logs in /var/log on a weekly basis, renaming each file every week:

```
$ls -l messages* root 95505 Jan 12 15:14 messages
-rw-r--r-- 1 root 118162 Jan 06 04:02 messages.1
-rw-r--r-- 1 root 91290 Dec 30 04:02 messages.2
-rw-r--r-- 1 root 91736 Dec 23 04:02 messages.3
-rw-r--r-- 1 root 121928 Dec 16 04:02 messages.4
```

This rotation scheme is customizable. Red Hat Linux uses a program logrotate to automate the rotation of log files. You can find a detailed description of logrotate in Chapter 12.

OpenBSD employs a rotation scheme, but it also takes the liberty of compressing the files after rotating them. In addition to rotating files, you should also think about your log retention policy, or how long you want to keep each log on disk. As you can see from the preceding example, Red Linux has a four-week log retention policy; after four weeks, rotated logs are permanently removed from the system.

Retention is all available disk space; how much disk space are you willing to dedicate to old logs? Will the size of logs seriously interfere with the space available for new logs? Experiment and see what works for your systems.

# Log Rotation Procedures

syslog opens logs once and keeps them open for the life of the process. If you move or rename the log files, they will still remain open and will be written to. The proper way to rotate these is to rename the logs and restart the syslog daemon. Alternatively, you could stop the daemon, move the logs, and restart it, but you may lose some logs during the process.

# Archival Storage

When the oldest log in a rotation is deleted, it is gone forever. Logs are critical for problem diagnosis, performance monitoring, and may even have legal ramifications, so it is important to archive them to some kind of long-term storage. This storage is usually some tape backup, but archiving to CD-ROM is becoming popular because of its longer shelf life.

# Keep Archives Readily Available

No matter medium you use to store the logs, it should be readily available and convenient to access, as the logs may be needed at a moment's notice. A CD-ROM or tape library can make older archives available at the touch of a button.
