# Protect Your One-Time Passwords

Just because each password can only be used once is no reason to guard one-time passwords any less securely than regular passwords. Keep printed lists of passwords safe and away from the prying\eyes of others.

# Time-Based Passwords

An improvement over the one-time password scheme is the time-based password scheme.
Here, your password is based on the current time, which facilitates the use of a token that dis-
plays your current password, called a PIN in this context. This PIN is usually a random num-
baser on a seed shared with the authentication server. The functionality of this system
dends on the accuracy and synchronization of the clocks in the token and the authentica-
server as a common random number seed.

Most time-based authentication systems utilize two-factor authentication, requiring a separate
password as well as the PIN displayed on the token. These systems hold a distinct advantage
over one-time passwords, because you never have to go back to the server to get more pass-
—your current password is always displayed on your token.

One of the most recognized companies in field is RSA, and their SecurID system uses
cryptographic tokens for time-based two-factor authentication. You can read about this tech-
ology at http://www.rsasecurity.com/products/secured.

# Certificates

Digital certificates use public key encryption to provide authentication. If you recall from the
discussion of public key encryption, a private key can be used to privately “sign” a document—“nif the corresponding public key can decrypt the document, it is certain that it was encrypted
using the private key. A digital certificate in its simplest form is a document that is signed with
special key, usually belonging to a trusted third party called a certificate authority
(CA). When you send the certificate to a server trying to authenticate you, this entire public key infra-
structure, or PKI, depends on the proper behavior of certificate authorities and your trust in
PKI has been the subject of numerous papers, talks, and conferences since the mid-
1990s.

The problem with digital certificates is that they are just single documents, much like a pass-
word. If a third party manages to get hold of your certificate, they can potentially impersonate
you just as they can with your password. Digital certificates are only secure in a two-factor
authentication system where they are supplemented with a password or other authentication
mechanism.
