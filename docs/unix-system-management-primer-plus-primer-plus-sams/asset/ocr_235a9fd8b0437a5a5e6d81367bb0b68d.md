# Chapter 9 • PREPARING FOR DISASTER RECOVERY

The data center facility might not be the only secondary site you'll need following a disaster. Disasters that make office space unusable create problems beyond resulting from a few servers down. If people have no working space and basic office facilities, they can't keep the business running.

When offices are rendered unusable, the people working there must be relocated to another facility. Many companies that provide secondary sites for disaster recovery also have temporary office space available for customers. Temporary space is usually limited (especially in the case of widespread disasters, such as floods and hurricanes); available space usually is filled on a first-come, first-serve basis. A temporary facility must provide for all of the office functionality you specified in your plan, which may or may not include the following:

+   * Individual workspaces
* Desktop workstations
* Phones
* Operator/call network
* Private network
* Internet access
* Voicemail

# Plan Emergency Office Operations Wisely

Only small companies will be able to relocate their entire staff to a secondary location. Not only are workspaces a strain on an emergency partner's resources, they are extremely expensive options in your services contract. Your recovery plan should include a list of the minimum staff that must be relocated to keep the business running, as well as their backups in case someone is not available. Others can return to work after the disaster has ended or they can telecomm. In fact, telecommuting is an attractive option when reserved workspaces start to eat a large hole in your budget.

# Choosing a Recovery Partner

One facility is hard to maintain, unless two or three, so many businesses choose to partner with companies experienced in disaster recovery procedures. These service companies provide the recovery sites, infrastructure, and personnel to help you through the transition. Sungard® Recovery Services and IBMs Global Services division are just two big names in a pool of recovery service companies. Such companies can offer data centers, hardware, Internet connectivity, planning software, and professional consulting services to guide you through the emergency operations process.

Your recovery partner can also provide the hardware that you use during recovery. Companies usually offer hardware identical or at least comparable to your specifications, then tack a fee onto your bill to cover the costs of reserving the hardware for you.
