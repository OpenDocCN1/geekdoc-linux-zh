# Communicating with Users

System administrators communicate with users on their systems for a variety of reasons,
including the following:

+   * Announcing system maintenance
* Announcing new or removed features of a system
* Warning a user about his or her activities
* Discussing a problem in real time
* Posting disclaimers and policies
* Posting contact information

Unix provides a variety of ways to communicate with users. You can use active communication-methods that enable you to exchange written messages, broadcast messages to a group,
or open a real-time dialog with instant messaging. Unix also facilitates passive communication methods, such as issuing messages of the day and posting to newsgroups, Web
pages, and email.

Each communication technique described in this section is effective for given situations and purposes. Some are better more suitable for announce-ments and updates. By understanding all of the Unix communication tools available to you,
you'll be able to choose and use the techniques that are best suited to delivering your
unications to the right people, at the right time, and in the right way.

# Be Consistent When Communicating

You should be consistent in your use of communication methods; users will come to expect certain
types of messages in certain places and might overlook them if they are placed anywhere else. Also.
remember that your communications aren't effective if users do not receive them; whenever possible,
get the message to your user, rather than expecting your users to come looking for messages. This is
espec important for urgent messages like system maintenance notifications.

# Active Communication Methods

Active communication methods are those in which you send messages to users in real time
and sees the messages as they arrive. Active communication methods are essential for
sending urgent messages, such as those announcing system shutdowns and asking users to log
out immediately. Communicating these messages actively guarantees that the messages appear
immediately on each user's terminal.
