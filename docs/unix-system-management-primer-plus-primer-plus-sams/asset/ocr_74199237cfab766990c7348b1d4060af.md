Many causes of data loss can be prevented. Chapter 10, “Providing High Availability in Your Unix System,” describes many different methods, including RAID and network redundancy, that provide protection for your data on the back end. Chapter 13, “Implementing System Security,” provides some proactive approaches to monitor and defend against security breaches, which a malicious person or virus could unintentionally destroy your data. Unfortunately, human error is more difficult to manage; proper training and minimizing the use of root and other privileged accounts are the most effective methods for counteracting human error.

Depending on the nature of the data lost, a few employees could lose a couple of spreadsheets, or all of your customers lose months worth of email. It's up to you and your management to decide what constitutes a disaster and develop plans to recover the data or work without.

One of the most important things you can do to minimize loss of data during a disaster is to store backup tapes (or other backup media) offsite in a geographically separate location. This prevents your backup media from being destroyed along with all of your hardware and live data. The frequency at which you move this media offsite will determine how much data you can potentially lose in a site-wide disaster. For example, if you move tapes once per week, you are at risking at most one week's worth of data, and that is only if your data center burns to the ground seconds before the tapes are moved. Your tolerance for data loss should be determined during a business impact analysis, which is described later in this chapter.

# How Far Away Is Far Enough?

Determining the appropriate safe distance from your data center to store media offsite is an interest-dilemma. On one hand, you want your data as far away from any disaster involving your data center as possible. On the other hand, you want your data close enough that you can retrieve it in a reasonable amount of time during a disaster. In general, offsite media should be stored at least 50 miles away, but close enough that it can reach you within 4 hours, or the service time vendor has specified in your contract.

Many companies offer offsite storage of media, but the most well-known company in this field is Iron Mountain. Iron Mountain Mountain will pick up boxes of your media, store them in a secure facility, and return them to you on designated dates. The company will also return stored media to you within a specified amount of time following a disaster.
