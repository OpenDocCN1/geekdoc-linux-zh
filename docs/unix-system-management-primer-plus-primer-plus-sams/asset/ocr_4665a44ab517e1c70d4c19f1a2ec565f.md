# Make Plan Adjustments Every Six Months

Disaster recovery professionals suggest that you review your disaster recovery plan every six months for changes or inaccuracies, regardless of whether any major changes were made in your organization. Each department should examine its of the plan and report back changes that they need to be.

Regular updates to your recovery plan are a required part of your change management process, with no exceptions (you learn more about change management in Chapter 14, "Internal Communication") Keeping your disaster recovery plan up-to-date can be very easy if you use disaster recovery planning software; most of these applications automatically update the official plan when resources change on the back end. If technology is constantly changing, this feature alone may be reason to purchase planning software.

Without software, you must update the plan manually. Manual updating is usually quite manageable in smaller companies, but for larger companies, especially with multiple locations, it can become a major project; simple information such as contact information must be updated in multiple places, something planning software handles automatically. If updating requires constant attention, you should split the work among several people or even hire a consultant to do the work for you. In fact, most consulting companies will be more happy to return on a periodic basis to re-evaluate your plan; it makes money for them, and it benefits your organization as well because company is already familiar with your recovery plan and doesn't have to start from scratch

# Distribute Updated Plans

Don't forget to redistribute any plans that have been updated to the plan participants. A stale plan can bring even a simple recovery procedure to a grinding halt, especially if contact information is inaccurate.
