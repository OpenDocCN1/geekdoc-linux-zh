# Get Good Emergency Contact Information

After you've designated emergency staff responsibilities, you need to make sure you and the other participants of the plan can contact the staff when you need them. Here's the contact information you should compile:

+   * Work phone number
* Home address
* Home phone number
* Pager number and/or address
* Vacation schedules
* Direct manager
* Backup personnel (who else can perform the same task)
* Emergency contacts

Emergency staff assignments and contact information are part of your disaster recovery procedures; keep this information with your plan and update it along with the rest of the plan (you learn more about updates later in this chapter). Participants cannot be expected to carry around a disaster recovery plan with them at all times; however, at the very least you should make the contact list available separately from the overall plan so people can carry it around with them. One company went so far as to laminate a small wallet-sized card with contact information so it would be as convenient to access as a driver license or credit card.

# Identifying Critical Tasks

There is no master switch to flip that will transition your business into disaster recovery mode. You need to carefully plan the sequence of emergency procedure events that will get your operation through a disaster. Even the migration of a single mail server to another location can become a guessing game if the requirements for the move haven't been carefully researched and documented.

To eliminate any confusion or guessing on your part during a disaster, document the requirements of each critical task, including the following:

+   * Necessary technology resources
* Necessary human resources
* Time required for each step
* Locations for each activity
* Dependencies within the process
