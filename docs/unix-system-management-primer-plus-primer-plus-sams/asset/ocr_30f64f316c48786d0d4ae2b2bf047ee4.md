# Simplify Platform Management

The 2000 SAGE Salary Survey reports that system administrators manage an average of five different operating systems at their primary jobs. Managing many operating systems can be difficult—even when they're just different distributions of Linux. Choosing one operating system and sticking with it saves a lot of time, and headaches; you don't have to repeat processes, rewrite documentation, or mentor patches for each operating system you manage.

Choose your operating system carefully, because you can't go back and change your mind later, at least not without an enormous amount of effort. Here are some of the factors you should consider when choosing an operating system:

+   * **Application support** Choose an operating system that is supported by your application vendors, where you run into problems.
* **Your expertise** Go with your strengths and choose an operating system that takes advantage of the expertise you and your have developed over the years. If you're fluent in only a few operating systems, and none of these is the best choice, make sure you're prepared to upgrade your skills to manage the system you choose.
* **Vendor support** What kind of support does the operating system vendor provide? Is there 24/7 phone support where you can talk to an engineer? Can you get onsite support if necessary? How much will it cost?
* **Market share** Never underestimate the power of a massive user base! Best of the support comes from the end users; you can often find an answer more quickly on a newsgroup or mailing list than by calling a support line.
* **Features** You may choose an operating system because of one or more unique features. For example, a mail server needs support for efficiently searching directories hundreds of thousands of files may need a specialized file system that requires kernel modifications. In this case, Linux may be the most appropriate choice, as the source code is freely available and can be modified as you see fit. The BSD family of operating systems also falls into this category.

You can't ignore the importance of choosing one operating system for your operations. Using a single OS makes the daily care and feeding of your servers simple and easy. Good reasons exist, however, for using multiple operating systems.
