# Remain Calm

It is difficult to remain calm in highly outage situations, but you can't debug a problem and execute highly technical processes while you're running around like a chicken with its head cut off. As an old coworker once said, "You get ice water in your veins," meaning that as you experience outages and problems over the years, you become more and more calm even in the most dire of situations—your blood no longer boils at the mention of the word "outage."

The more panicked you are during an outage, the more likely you are to make a mistake, possibly worsening the situation. What's worse is panic is contagious—if you are running around your office or data center screaming, other members of your staff are likely to start doing the same.

Lead by example and stay calm analyze the problems you need to solve, and take things one step at a time. If other administrators are panicking, ask them politely to leave, as they only add to the problem at hand. Nontechnical coworkers are likely to stop and ask what's going on; it is very easy to get angry at them for bothering you during an outage, so simply ask them to go back to their desks and let you do your job. Even your managers need to be told this as well; you cannot possibly remedy a major outage with a manager looking over your shoulder, for example, reminding you how much money the outage is costing the company. Just ask everyone to leave you alone so you can fix the problem.
