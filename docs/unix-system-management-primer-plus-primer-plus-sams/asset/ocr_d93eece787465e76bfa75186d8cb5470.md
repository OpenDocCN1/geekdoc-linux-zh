# Should You Rotate Based on Size or Age?

Logs are generally rotated based on either their size or age. Which of these criteria is the better basis for your log rotation strategy depends on available disk space and your retention policy. If you are concerned about disk space, you should rotate your logs based on their size—that you may have a predictable amount of logs at all times. On the other hand, you may have a retention policy for calls on log archives of up to several months or years. In that case, you could rotate log files based on age and set the number of saved logs to a number that your retention policy—this would provide you with a consistent timeline of logs. Of course, nothing beats trial and error, over time, you will learn approximately how long it takes for a log to reach a certain size. Logs that do not grow much and are not rotated will be small, but they will also contain very stale data (often months or old), while logs that grow quickly often contain more data is convenient to work with. Make sure to consider these trends along with your disk space and retention policies when deciding your criteria for log rotation. Of course, you could implement a combination of these schemes, for example, you could rotate logs that are larger than 50MB or older than two months.
