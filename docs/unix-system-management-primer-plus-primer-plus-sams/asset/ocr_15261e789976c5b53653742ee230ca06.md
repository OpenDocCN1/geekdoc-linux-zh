Power cable management offers some special concerns. Cable management boxes can contain a number of different cable types—power, network, console, and fiber, to name a few. Power cables present the unique problem of electromagnetic interference. When you bundle power cables with other types of wiring, this interference can introduce unwanted current into other wires in the bundle, causing noise on serial cables and errors in Ethernet interfaces (optical are not susceptible to this interference). To avoid this interference, separate power cables from other types of cabling. You can use ties to keep power cables on one side of the rack, or you can use a separate cable management infrastructure just for power cables if you have enough available space in your racks.

# Securing Uninterruptible Power Supplies

The uninterruptible power supply (UPS) is one of the greatest inventions of the twentieth century—at least for system administrators! This simple device can save you from all sorts of data center evils, including power outages and "dirty" power (power with wide voltage variations or other noise that delicate computer circuitry is not designed to handle). Uninterruptible power supplies feed a steady, constant current to your hardware while charging a backup battery in case of power failure.

# A UPS Is a Must

There is no reason for not considering a UPS in your data center planning. Think of it as a cheap insurance policy for your expensive, business-critical hardware.

A UPS can be one of the following distinct types:

+   - A standby UPS feeds power to your hardware from the normal utility power source (wall-outlet power), switching over to battery only when the utility power source fails. The UPS charges the battery with wall-outlet power during use. Continuous UPS battery power is very clean, without voltage spikes or surges (built-in surge protection). Another benefit of a continuous UPS is that there is no need to switch to battery when wall power fails; battery power is already active, making power 100% unnoticeable to the hardware. A continuous UPS is best for data centers because it provides the cleanest power, while a standby UPS is perfect for desktop use.
