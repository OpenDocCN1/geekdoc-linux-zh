# Get Multiple Power Feeds from Your Power Company

If you are concerned about long power outages and your data center does not have a generator, you should look into getting additional power power feeds from the local power company, giving you two or more sources of power. Although multiple power feeds will not save you from a large-scale regional outage, it can save you from local incidents such as lightning hitting across the street.

# Out-of-Band Management for Maintenance and Repair

What happens if a server crashes at your data center 20 miles away and doesn't come back up? How do you fix a machine whose network interface was accidentally disabled? How do you handle this and other repair and maintenance problems without physically being at the data center? The answer is out-of-band management, a term that refers to any technology that allows you to manage a device from outside the network or other normal channels of communication.

On servers, out-of-band management is usually facilitated by consoles. For other hardware, like power strips, out-of-band management might mean accessing the equipment over the network instead of direct physical access.

# Using Consoles for Out-of-Band Unix Management

A console is the primary display of a server, router, switch, or any other piece of hardware with a user interface. It is usually the only way to access hardware when it is inaccessible via the network. Traditionally, consoles consist of keyboards, monitors, and mice, but most hardware today, especially hardware supporting Unix operating systems, supports serial consoles. Serial consoles are text-based interfaces that are served through a server's serial port.

Whether you use traditional or serial consoles, the problem is one of consolidation. How do you get access to all these consoles from one place? Can you choose any of devices to help resolve that problem? To provide central access to traditional consoles, you can use a KVM (keyboard, video, mouse) switch or a remote console.

A KVM switch provides a single keyboard, monitor, and mouse that can access the video consoles of many different machines. A KVM switch is great because it reduces the amount of hardware required to access a real live video console on every machine in an infrastructure. Physical buttons on the switch or controls on the keyboard allow you to switch the console to control any of the connected servers.
