# Raised Floors

Raised floors usually consist of a grid of individual 2- by 2-foot tiles resting on a sturdy framework supported underneath by a fixed floor. Raised floors have numerous advantages over fixed floors, but can be more expensive to build and maintain. The advantages of raised floors include the following:

+   - Flexible cabling options; you can run cabling under the floor into your racks, hiding 100% of your cabling from view (you learn more about racks in the next section).
- You can ventilate through the floor, forcing cold air into your racks.

The disadvantages of raised floors are as follows:

+   - Long cable runs are extremely difficult, requiring multiple tile pulls to access the cabling.
- Easy to catch one cable on another (including cables that do not belong to you) during pulls.
- More expensive to build, and tiles can be damaged, requiring periodic replacement.
- Requires a ramp or steps from the lower level entrance to the raised floor.
- Seams between tiles can make moving larger equipment across the floor.

# Choosing the Floor That Fits Your Needs

Which floor right for you? This is a decision you will have to make whether you are building your own data center or choosing a hosting facility. Raised floors are more common in older buildings where large mainframes were once located; they covered cooling pipes and power cabling. Unless you have one of these mainframes, a raised floor is probably not worth the money to build; smaller machines and large amounts of sensitive cabling do not lend themselves to the logistics of a raised floor. Raised floors are still available from many hosting facilities, who keep them for customers who need them or prefer them.

Fixed floors are much more affordable and do not require any extra infrastructure to build. In fact, it is quite easy to build a data center out of existing office space while keeping the floor; many organizations choose to do this in their own leased office space. The space can usually be converted back to office space just as easily, making a fixed floor a very flexible choice.
