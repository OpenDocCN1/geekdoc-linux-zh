# Software Support

Software support has limitations of its own. Like hardware support, most vendors offer software support as a 24/7 or 8/5 (normal business working hours) phone support service, depending upon how much you want to spend. Software engineers can hold your hand while diagnosing problems from disastrous crashes to simple upgrades.

Ask for the Help You Paid For
Don't be afraid to ask a software support engineer for help; you shouldn't hesitate to ask questions such as, "I'm uncomfortable upgrading to this new version without help; I don't want to lose my data." You're paying for help with these kinds of situations.

Software support can also include the right to receive patches and upgrades on CD-ROM or downloadable files from the Internet. Often, paying support customers receive these patches and upgrades before you're waiting on a new feature or bug fix. Vendors can also make development code available for their customers, either for beta testing or for emergency bug fixes that haven't been formally released yet. This benefit is invaluable, especially if you're the one who submitted the bug report in the first place.

# Vendor Liability

Pick up any support contract from your files, and you'll likely find a liability clause lurking in the details. No vendor will assume responsibility for damages caused by your use of its products or the vendor's support for them, at least not in a boilerplate contract. Your company's lawyers may have a problem with this, but most companies find it futile to battle with large vendors like Sun or Microsoft. Unfortunately, this means the responsibility of supporting your infrastructure ultimately rests on yours.

If management stresses that liability is a problem, stress to them that you may need expertise of staff to cover any lapses by the vendor. Remember, an experienced administrator may be able to solve a critical problem faster than it takes a support engineer to drive out to your site and make the proper diagnosis.
