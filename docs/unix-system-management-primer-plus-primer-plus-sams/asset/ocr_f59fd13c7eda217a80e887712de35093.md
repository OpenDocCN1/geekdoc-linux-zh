# UNIX SYSTEM MANAGEMENT PRIMER PLUS

# Limit Your Use of the talk Command

Use the talk command sparingly when you communicate with users. Otherwise you may find that users will start initiating conversations with you when they have a problem instead of using more appropriate support lines like your help desk.

# Passive Communication Methods

Passive communications are like postings on a bulletin board or a newspaper—you write a message and hope that users see it within a reasonable amount of time. This communication is less intrusive than the active communication methods you've just read about, and, therefore, less disruptive to users. However, passive communication delivers messages only if users know where to find them—and actually take the time to access that location and read your messages.

# Tell Users How to Reach You

Always provide the means for users to communicate back to you any problems or concerns they have with announcements you make. A simple way to provide this information is to add an email address or a help desk number to your announcements. Use an IT department email address rather than your own, though; you may want others to manage these emails in your absence.

# Posting Messages in a Banner

Unix displays banners when users first connect to a system; typically, the banners provide information about the system users connected to, including any warnings or usage guidelines. For example, when you Telnet to one of the login servers at the University of Michigan, the system displays the following banner before you can log in:

+   * This is the University of Michigan information environment.
* You must be authorized to use these resources. As an authorized user, by
* your use of these resources, you have implicitly agreed to abide by the
* highest standards of responsibility to your colleagues -- the --n* faculty, staff, and external users who share this environment. You are
* required to comply with all University policies, state, and federal laws
* concerning appropriate use of information technology. Non-compliance is
* considered a serious breach of community standards and may result in
* disciplinary or legal action.

You can use TCP Wrappers or /etc/issue to display a banner on a Unix system. Under Red Hat Linux, use /etc/issue.net to display a banner for Telnet sessions.
