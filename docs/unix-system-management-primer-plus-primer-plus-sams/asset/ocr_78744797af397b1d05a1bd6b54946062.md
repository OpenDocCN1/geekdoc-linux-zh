# A Custom Support Tool

A large national DSL provider needed a ticket management system that was completely integrated with its installation and activation processes, including escalations to and from local phone companies. No commercial or freely available tools could meet the provider’s needs, so the provider spent close to a year writing and perfecting their tool, which was perfectly integrated with their owning business processes. Many organizations have the time or resources to do this, but it can be worth the effort if cost is an issue or if none of the available tools provide enough functionality to meet your needs.

To better understand the variety of support tools and how they are used in practice, consider the support organization for two example companies: a large national ISP and a small market research company. By examining the support needs of these organizations, you can see how the tools they choose fit into each support system.

# Support Tools at a Large ISP

A large provider of dial-up services provides Internet access for 500,000 users. The average call volume is 75 calls per day, and the ranges from users forgetting their passwords to national service outages. A two-tiered support hierarchy is employed, consisting of the help desk on one end and the engineers, desktop support, developers, and account managers on the other end. This ISP has a huge user base to support, and investors to appease, so it has decided to use Remedy HelpDesk, a well-known commercial help desk system. The company Remedy will manage the hundreds of trouble tickets the ISP might have to deal with on a daily basis.

Remedy offers help desk software with multiple front ends, including Windows, Solaris, and browser-based management. The support staff can store trouble tickets in a database like Oracle for historical data and for quick references when working on other tickets. Various notification methods let people know when they have been assigned a ticket or when the status of a ticket has changed.

Remedy also offers a customizable interface where you can define things like ticket categories, groups of people to assign tickets to, and customer contact information. Figure 5.3 shows the interface for editing a ticket.

The help desk staff was ecstatic at the deployment of Remedy, as they now had a friendly face to enter, and update tickets. Remedy also provided a point-and-click escalation process that made life easier for everyone. IT staff automatically received email stating that they have been assigned a ticket, and the system paged them for high-priority tickets. Upper management appreciates the fact that Remedy can generate monthly activity reports that are useful for gauging the performance of the support staff and the IT department in general.
