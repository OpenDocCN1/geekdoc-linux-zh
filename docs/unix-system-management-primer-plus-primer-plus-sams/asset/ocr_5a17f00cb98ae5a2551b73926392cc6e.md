# UNIX SYSTEM MANAGEMENT PRIMER PLUS

Although turnover is inevitable, it's important to make each member of your help desk staff excited about his or her job. To keep your help desk employees involved in and contributing to the technical support process, provide them with ongoing training in the use of your products and services, as well as general technical training to help them better understand the inner workings of what they are supporting. The more your help desk knows, the better they are able to solve problems on your; that means they send fewer problems off to be lost in the caverns of multiple support levels.

Of course, with training comes the desire for advancement, and it is a certainty that you will lose well-trained help desk staff to higher levels of support within or outside your company. Although a promotion might cost you, it is not necessarily a bad thing; someone who has moved up the ranks through the years from help desk to system administrator to management has a unique—and valuable—insight into different aspects of your organization. This kind of promotion also helps with staff retention, because it shows that there is opportunity for advancement.

A common practice is to have periodic meetings between help desk staff, their management, and higher-level support such as system administrators, and even developers if they are involved in the support process. These meetings can be used to review reports and to raise any concerns that each party has. Ongoing communication between the multiple lines of support can improve the support process.

You should also provide your help desk staff with tools that empower them to investigate problems on their own, even if you have to write the tools yourself. Providing these tools not only gives the help desk an opportunity to actually solve user problems, but it reduces the number of issues that escalate to your group.

# Outsourcing Help Desk Functions

Due to the high turnover and headaches involved with help desk support, many companies choose to outsource it. Several companies provide help desk services, and outsourcing companies typically have a large pool of available resources to redicate to your company if the needs arises. Outsourcing your help desk functions may be more expensive than hiring part-time employees to handle the service, but it can eliminate the time and energy required to combat high employee turnover problems. If employee retention is becoming a problem for your organization, outsourcing might be your solution.

One major caveat with outsourcing is to make sure the staff you are providing are qualified to provide support for your product or service. Are they temps do they have actual technical experience or certifications to justify their hiring? You should also ask for the agency's references for organizations similar to your own. Any outsourcing agency should be able to provide you with this information, and if you are not satisfied with what you see, you should go with another agency.
