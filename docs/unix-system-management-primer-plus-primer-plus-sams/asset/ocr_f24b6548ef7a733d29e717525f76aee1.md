# Check the Details

Don't overlook the basics when assessing hardware support. If you're a 24/7 operation, it makes sense to have a 24/7 support contract so you can make nervous phone call to your vendor at 3:00 a.m.

+   - **Will it scale with your business?** Buying hardware that meets your requirements today may seem cost-effective, but that decision may end costing you money (not to mention time and effort) down the road. Plan for reasonable growth by purchasing hardware that you can easily scale and at a predictable rate. If you predict enough growth, can't afford to buy that four-CPU server with 4GB of RAM, that same server with one CPU and 1GB of RAM, leaving the other CPU and memory slots free for later. When you need to increase the capacity of your database server, you can simply add more CPUs and memory modules instead of replacing the entire server, and knowing which components you need ahead of time can help you predict the costs of these capacity increases

You learn more about the specifics of managing support and planning for capacity in later chapters of this book, but the information you've learned here gives you some type of the decisions you'll have to make when choosing hardware. The more thoroughly you plan, the better your hardware decisions will be—and the less chance you'll have to make multiple purchases. Plan thoroughly and accurately, in order to avoid having to make additional purchases.

Arranging for adequate vendor support of the system you're designing is an important part of the planning process (you learn about constructing and managing an internal support infrastructure in Chapter 5, "Support Administration"). You can't wait until the project is completed to think about support. As you've learned in the preceding section of your chapter, your plan should include an evaluation of contracts for both hardware and software.

# Hardware Support

Hardware support contracts come in many forms, but they must cover one universal concept: replacement parts. If a part fails, your hardware support contract must guarantee a replacement part to be delivered within a specified time. Individual contracts vary in the details of how parts are to be delivered and who is to install them—conditions accompanied by their own variable costs.
