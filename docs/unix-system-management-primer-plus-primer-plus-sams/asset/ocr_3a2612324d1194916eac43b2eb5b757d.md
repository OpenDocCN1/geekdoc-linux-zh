+   * Sprinklers A sprinkler system is the most common fire-suppression mechanism, but it can be a very dangerous choice for a data center. Dousing explosive equipment with water can damage it almost as severely as a fire can. Many install sprinkler systems because they work reliably, they're cheap, and they're easy to install and maintain. But this up-front cost-saving can cost a lot down the road: If fire strikes, you run the risk of damaging all of your hardware with the water used to put out the fire.

# # Limiting Noise

Excess noise won't damage equipment or cause it to work less effectively, but it can really bother the people who work in the data center. The number-one culprit in data center noise pollution is the HVAC system; simply put, large air conditioning units are noisy. You can reduce HVAC noise by locating the HVAC unit outside of the machine room and running the air through ducts or under a raised floor. Open racks also contribute to noise levels by leaving fans and spinning drives exposed. If this is a problem, consider using enclosed cabinets for your hardware.

> Controlling noise isn't just a "nicety" for the people working in the data center. A noisy data center can make phone conversations difficult. When data center workers are discussing a problem on the phone with support staff, you want to make sure that they can hear each other clearly to avoid any misunderstandings or mistakes.

# # Choosing Raised or Fixed Flooring

The floor underneath your system affects all other pieces of the network infrastructure. The floor physically supports the system hardware, just as a foundation supports a house. You can choose from two types of floors: fixed and raised. The flooring decision you make directly influences the choices you make down the road, so consider your choice carefully.

# # Fixed Floors

Fixed floors are the floors you are used to walking on every day—solid platforms that rest on building structures and don't move. The advantages of fixed floors are as follows:

+   * Stability; fixed floors don't move.
* Can bear very heavy loads.
* Low maintenance.
* Smooth surface makes it easy to move equipment.
