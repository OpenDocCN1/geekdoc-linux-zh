# ANSWERS TO QUESTIONS

# Chapter 1

- 1. The top-down approach to system design starts by specifying the high-level business goals of a project and using those to determine technical goals and solutions. Technical goals and solutions, in turn, determine what hardware and software must be in place in order to make the business goals a reality. This top-down approach dictates each step of the solution process is guided by the project's desired impact on the business.
- 2. It is difficult to switch operating systems after deployment every piece of software, including small utilities, large applications and databases, and your own maintenance scripts, depends on the operating system. Changing the operating system, even to move to a different version of the same operating system, requires installing, configuring, and testing each piece of software all again.
- 3. When you purchase servers, you can save money down the road by buying servers with a larger capacity than your system requires. You may not need (or be able to afford) a server with 4GB of RAM today, but that doesn't mean you won't need it next year. Buying larger, partially populated servers allows you to scale your hardware by adding the missing components, such as processors and memory modules. If you buy a server that can't grow with your business, you'll have to completely replace it or buy additional servers when it no longer meets your needs.

# Chapter 2

- 1. The advantage of using fiber cabling in a raised-floor environment is that you can store the fragile cables under the floor where nobody can accidentally step on them. The disadvantage is that the cables may be snagged on an object over the floor or entangled with other cables, causing problems when you have to move them.
- 2. Humidity can have dramatic effects on Unix system equipment. If the relative humidity is too high, condensation may occur when the temperature drops a degree or two; this moisture can quickly destroy equipment and lead to data loss. If the relative humidity is too low, static electricity can easily create dangerous sparks in the overly dry atmosphere. Static electricity can damage sensitive electronic equipment when you are performing maintenance.
