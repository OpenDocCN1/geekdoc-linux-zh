Of course, not all requests are reasonable. You might find, for example, that fixing the problem with that pesky mail server is more cost-effective than purchasing a new one. You discover that the mail server's network card is dropping packets, and you know that your support contract with the vendor allows you to request a replacement card for free. If you purchased a new server, as management requested, the company would have spent thousands of dollars unnecessarily.

As this example demonstrates, companies get the most effective use of their systems—system administrators—leaving the technical decisions with technical people. As system administrator, your job becomes that of gathering information about what users want the system to do or provide; then, you can determine the best way to meet the project's goals and requirements, based on those specifications. To do your job properly, you first need to help management express your business goals as accurately as possible; then, you must be capable of constructing a detailed technical plan for achieving these goals.

# Ask for Goals, Not Methods

Force your management to communicate their business goals, rather than their ideas for technical solutions. Devising and implementing those solutions is your job.

# Translating Business Goals into Technical Solutions

Business goals should dictate what the end result of a project should be, and how the new system will aid the company's business. Your job is to translate these business goals into technical solutions that will produce the results management is looking for.

To better understand that process, consider some typical business goals and how they translate into technical solutions for achieving those goals. For example, this business goal:

+   Recruit 5,000 additional users for our email service.

might translate into this technical solution:

+   1. Verify we can back up an additional 50GB of mail (assuming an average of 10MB per email user).
2. Order and install 50GB of disk space on our mail server.
3. Add the new space to the mail pool file system.
