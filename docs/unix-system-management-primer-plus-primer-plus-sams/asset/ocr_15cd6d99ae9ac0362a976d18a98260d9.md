# Generating a Disaster Recovery Plan

It's now time to take all of the information you've collected in the previous steps and create a detailed recovery plan you can use in any disaster, from a leaky roof to a tornado. Creating this plan can be a difficult endeavor, it requires you to take into account every type of disaster that you could possibly encounter and plan procedures that respond to each of them. Procedures for recovering from a fire are drastically different from recovering for data loss caused by human error, but you still need to make sure that you know what you are doing in each situation.

In a fire, for example, hardware, buildings and other valuable infrastructure can be lost; your plan must include provisions for procuring new hardware and network connectivity as well as relocating to another data center. Data loss, on the other hand, requires restoration from backups and possibly reloading of operating systems and software; your recovery plan for such a loss needn't include the replacement of any physical space or equipment.

You have several options for creating your plan. Most organizations choose one or more of the following paths:

+   - Create the plan in-house.
- Hire a consulting firm specializing in disaster recovery to assist with creating the plan.
- Purchase planning software that helps you organize and maintain the plan.

Smaller organizations with very straightforward business processes may be able to create the plan in-house by putting their heads together and constructing a document using Microsoft Word, for example. However, larger and more complex organizations will struggle with this approach without an experienced person to lead the project. These organizations should consider hiring consultants to analyze your business and technology and help create the plan.

Evaluate Disaster Recovery Consultants Carefully
Any consulting firm that you hire to help with your recovery plan should have the knowledge, training, and real-world experience to thoroughly analyze your business and develop a plan. Ask about prior engagements, obtain references, and look for industry certifications like Certified Business Continuity Professional (CBCP). Above all, ask for sample plans that demonstrate the scope of work and detail you can expect as the final deliverable. You may even be able to get "sanitized" plans: actual disaster recovery plans that have the names of organizations, people, and places removed for anonymity.

You can also purchase software to assist with the creation and future maintenance of a disaster recovery plan. Many organizations choose to supplement the do-it-yourself approach or the consultant approach with this software.
