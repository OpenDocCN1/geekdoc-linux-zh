## Chapter 12

- 1. To schedule a one-time reboot for tonight at 9:00 p.m., you would use the at command as follows:

```
# at 9:00 pm
> reboot
```

- 2. The Berkeley r-commands communicate in clear text and their rhost-based access mechanism. Clear-text traffic makes the r-commands vulnerable to network traffic sniffing, which can reveal passwords and other sensitive information. IP spoofing allows an unauthorized client to impersonate another client whose accounts may be authorized to log into your systems without a password.

- 3. rsync copies from a file the chunks of data that have changed. This makes rsync useful for periodically transferring large files that undergo small changes. Other transfer mechanisms like ftp and sc copy the entire contents of a file.

- 4. An account's authorized keys file contain the public keys of users who are allowed to log into that account using public key authentication.

- 5. The messages file was held open by the syslog process while you moved it to messages.1. The file was renamed, but it was never closed.

## Chapter 13

- 1. Authentication provides proof of your identity. Authorization grants or denies you access to resources based on your identity.

- 2. The concept of least privilege dictates that you should grant users access to only those resources that they absolutely must access in order to do their work.

- 3. A buffer overflow is a condition in which data is written beyond the end of a fixed amount of memory, onto the stack. Malicious users can use programs with buffer overflows to run code that they have inserted in the program.

- 4. The advantages of public key encryption are secure key distribution and the ability to digitally sign documents to prove their authenticity. The disadvantages are confusing key management mechanisms and the slow speed of using public key encryption compared to conventional shared key encryption.

- 5. Insert the following line in /etc/hosts.den:

```
fingerd: 10.1.2.3
```

## Chapter 14

- 1. A physical network diagram illustrates physical connections between devices on your network. A logical diagram illustrates the layout of devices relative to their respective networks, regardless of physical connections.

- 2. This procedure does not specify the name of the Jumpstart boot server or the location of the Jumpstart configuration.

- 3. To edit the file foobar, which is checked into RCS, you would check out the file from RCS, edit it using an editor like vi, then check the file back into RCS as follows:

```
-l foobar
vi foobar
ci -u foobar
```

- 4. You should notify the owner of an RCS lock before breaking the lock. This notification enables the lock owner to close the file or otherwise prepare for your actions, in order to prevent any potential loss of data that might occur if you make changes to the current version of a file.

- 5. The people who administer or actually use services on a system should be notified of future changes. This allows everyone affected by an upcoming system change to voice their concerns or suggestions before the change takes place, potentially disrupting their work.

## Chapter 15

- 1. One of the goals of hackers is for their actions to go unnoticed. A quiet user could potentially be a hacker who is working his or her way into your system.

- 2. A sudden increase in disk usage might indicate the presence of malware, or pirated-ware.

- 3. You should use the wall command to announce a system shutdown that is taking place in 5 minutes. wall broadcasts a message to the terminals of users logged into a system, forcing users to read your announcement immediately. Sending email would not be effective in this case because most people would not get a chance to read it before the system is shut down.

- 4. Use the whos command to locate the owner of 172.16.0.1, as follows:

```
whois -h arin.net 172.16.0.1
```

- 5. IDENT is an unreliable form of identification because it trusts the information reported by the remote server, which could be inaccurate or tainted by a hacker.

## Chapter 16

- 1. Listing specific examples of the most common violations indicates potential violators of your policy that you are aware of those actions and will be looking for them. Also, examples can help the user's understanding of prohibited types of usage, to eliminate any question regarding whether a particular activity violates the policy.

- 2. The list of prohibited activities should be open-ended because you do not want to limit your policy to specific activities. Users may come up with unacceptable uses for your system that you haven't even considered.

- 3. A banner displayed before the login process takes place ensures that a user will see the implicit agreement before he or she actually gains access to the system.

- 4. The primary protection afforded to users by User Rights & Responsibilities is the privacy of their data.

- 5. An administrator should have the technical knowledge to understand the systems and services addressed by his or her, and sufficient privilege on those systems to monitor and enforce the policy.
