# Types of Rights

You may be wondering what kind of rights a user can have while using your services. Some of the more common types of rights listed in these policies are listed here:

+   - Privacy of data
- Customer support
- Adequate notice before scheduled outages

Of all of the rights listed, privacy is the most important right you can give to your users. A statement of privacy rights assures that their email, files, and network communications will not be viewed or modified in any way by staff in a position of authority; such as a system administrator. In addition, privacy rights imply that system administrators will do their best to prevent other users and third parties from breaching the security of their systems and possibly compromising user data.

Not all organizations provide the same rights to their users—organizations provide user rights based on the nature of the organization and its security needs. For example, although a small ISP may protect against internal staff reading others' email, a government agency may require that all incoming and outgoing email be screened for inappropriate or suspicious activities.

# Balancing Rights and Responsibilities

it might be difficult to enforce user responsibilities if you are unable to properly monitor your users due to the rights you provided them. For example, preventing employees from sending personal email is quite difficult if your rights specify that IT staff won't read those messages without consent. Verify that user rights don't prevent you from fulfilling your responsibilities as an administrator.

# Types of Responsibilities

The user rights in this chapter do not come freely; users earn rights by fulfilling certain responsibilities. System administrators determine responsibilities by setting guidelines for appropriate user behavior. User responsibilities differ from site to site, but users should be expected to observe the following globally applicable guidelines:

+   - Respect the privacy of other users.
- Respect the intended use of system resources.
- Respect the integrity of the system and network (that is, do not attempt to hack into a system).
- Respect the right of other users to use shared resources.
- Protect passwords, smartcards, and other authenticators that could be used to impersonate you.
