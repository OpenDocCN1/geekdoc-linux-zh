# CHAPTER 9

# # PREPARING FOR DISASTER RECOVERY

You will learn about the following in this chapter:
- The major types of disasters that threaten your data center
- How to minimize the impact of power outages
- Fires, floods, and other disasters that destroy or prevent access to your data center
- Security breaches, viruses, error, and other disasters that result in data loss
- The importance of analyzing the business impact of disasters
- The different types of secondary sites and how they are used
- How to create, test, and maintain a disaster recovery plan
- Conducting disaster drills

In previous chapters of this book, you've learned about some of the issues surrounding service of outages, from slow email retrieval to complete network failure. These types of outages are all serious problems, but they pale in comparison to a full-fledged disaster. A disaster that damages or destroys your data center, its equipment, or your office can bring your business to a standstill. Depending on the nature of the disaster and your organization's ability to respond to it, a disaster can interrupt operations for days, weeks—even months. You cannot anticipate or avoid every disaster, but as a system administrator, you should be prepared to deal with disasters that directly affect the technology side of your business. A good disaster recovery plan along with thorough testing can be the difference between a disaster causing a temporary "hiccup" in your operations or a full-fledged catastrophe. This chapter helps you prepare for the responsibility of creating and managing such a plan. This chapter examines several types of disasters and how they affect information technology; you learn about secondary sites, disaster recovery planning, recovery procedures, and recovery partnerships.
