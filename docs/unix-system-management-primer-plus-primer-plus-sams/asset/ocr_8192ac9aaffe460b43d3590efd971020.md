# Calculate Total Heat Generation

Don't forget to include the BTU ratings of all of your hardware when calculating total heat generated by your equipment. Tape drives, disk arrays, routers, switches—even the fans used to cool racks—generate heat. Your HVAC contractor needs this information to calculate the necessary capacity of your heating and cooling system.

# Controlling Humidity

The HVAC system also controls the humidity of your data center. Controlling the humidity of the air surrounding your computer equipment is nearly as important as controlling the temperature. Static electricity can seriously damage—even destroy—sensitive electronic equipment, and in dry air, you can easily generate static electricity simply by walking across the floor. Excessive humidity, on the other hand, can result in condensation forming on cooler parts of computer hardware, especially when the ambient temperatures change slightly. Condensation can be as dangerous to you as your hardware as static electricity.

An overly humid environment also makes it more difficult to cool the air in your data center. The air conditioning system has to work much harder to cool humid air, and that can stress the system beyond its limits. In this case, purchasing an additional or a larger air conditioning unit is warranted.

The optimum relative humidity for a data center hovers around 50%, protecting against static electricity without risking condensation or HVAC stress. Air conditioning removes moisture from the air, so to ensure a 50% relative humidity level, your HVAC system should be equipped with a humidifier set to that level.

# Humidity in Office Buildings

Some office buildings are not equipped with humidifiers in their HVAC systems. This is one reason to install a completely separate HVAC system if you build a data center in your office.
