# Chapter 16

+   1. Listing specific examples of the most common violations indicates to potential violators of your acceptable policy that you are aware of those actions and will be looking for them. Also, examples can help expand the user's understanding of prohibited types of usage, to help eliminate any question regarding whether a particular activity violates the policy.

+   2. The list of prohibited activities should be left open-ended because you do not want to limit your policy to specific activities. Users may come up with unacceptable uses for your system that you haven't even considered. You don't want rigid, finite policies to limit your ability to regulate your system's usage.

+   3. A banner displayed before the login process takes place ensures that a user will see the implicit agreement in the banner before he or she actually gains access to the system.

+   4. The primary protection afforded to users by a User Rights & Responsibilities agreement is the privacy of their data.

+   5. A site security administrator should have the technical knowledge to understand the systems and services addressed by his or her security policy, and sufficient privilege on those systems to monitor compliance with and enforce the policy.
