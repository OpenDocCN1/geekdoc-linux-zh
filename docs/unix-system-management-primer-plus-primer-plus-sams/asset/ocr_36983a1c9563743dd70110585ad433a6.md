When deciding whether a vendor is right for you, you should be able to answer "yes" to most of the following questions:

+   - Is the vendor responsive to email and phone calls?
- Are the vendor's prices and discounts competitive with other vendors?
- If the vendor is a reseller, is it an authorized reseller of the products it sells? If doubt, you can contact individual manufacturers to verify this.

- Does the vendor employ industry-certified engineers or work directly with manufacturers to support your purchases?
- Does the vendor do business in all of your organization's locations?

# Soliciting Quotes and Completing Purchase Orders

A quote simply a list of prices at which a vendor is willing to sell you goods, whatever those goods are hardware, software, or consulting services. Quotes are exact; whatever price is in the quote is what the vendor will charge—no more and no less. When asking for vendors, make sure you've nailed down every detail of what you need from them—down to the last cable—to make sure your quote includes all the costs. When possible, supply your specifications to write in, so there's no mistake about what you're asking for. When you receive a quote, check it against your specs to make sure the vendor or reseller left out anything.

In most cases, you'll craft quotes into purchase orders, which are your company's own forms for making purchases. Chances are that you, as the purchaser, will be filling out the purchase orders, so sure they match the quotes letter for letter! The finished purchase order is usually approved by management, then sent off to the vendor.

# Copying and Pasting Purchase Orders

Receiving electronic quotes and filling out purchase orders electronically can save you time and avoid errors. If vendors email quotes to you, you can just copy and paste the information into your electronic copy of the purchase order.

# Receiving Shipments

Receiving your purchased goods can be as easy as signing for a small box at the door or as difficult as transporting fifty 85-pound 36x48-inch boxes upstairs in your undersized freight elevator, then arranging for the secure storage of that million dollars' worth of equipment.
