# UNIX SYSTEM MANAGEMENT PRIMER PLUS

The pinnacle of hardware support is represented by a staff of full-time onsite support engineers armed with a staff of spare parts. This arrangement would be a dream come true for any administrator, it's also the most expensive option, and it's impractical in many environments because it requires maintaining staff and parts you may never use.

# Colocation Facility On-Site Support

Some colocation facilities or disaster recovery sites maintain their own onsite support staff and replacement parts. SunGuard Recovery Services, the largest disaster recovery facility in the country, has a large support staff specializing in Sun, EMC, and Cisco technologies. SunGuard directly supports its own massive infrastructure and it sells the same support services to its colocation customers when necessary.

Most companies opt for a 24/7 full support arrangement, in which support is available 24 hours a day, a week to help you diagnose problems and to deliver any parts you may need. Support contracts for these services include service-level agreements for parts delivery that detail the delivery time you should expect for replacement parts (next-day, two-day, and even four-hour delivery by local couriers are common). Choosing a service agreement, decide what is practical for your company. You can hold out for a day if a critical piece of hardware fails, or do you need things fixed right?

# Hardware Support Needs Vary

Some hardware requires higher levels of support than others do. For example, you may want 4-hour onsite support for your production database server, but you may be able to get away with a 24-hour response time for a development web server.

In addition to parts delivery, many vendors offer onsite support engineers. After diagnosing your problem online or over the phone, the vendor dispatches an engineer to your site who will confirm the problem and replace the failing hardware. The upside is that you don't have to worry about fiddling around inside your expensive equipment. The downside is that you have to wait for the engineer to show up, regardless of whether you have the parts in hand; most agreements dont guarantee an engineer's arrival time. If you are comfortable working with the hardware you may not need support engineer to come out and help you replace the part; this saves a lot of time and reduces the downtime for your systems while you're waiting for the engineer to arrive.

# Real-World Example: Waiting for Parts

However, 4 hours later, the part had not arrived.
