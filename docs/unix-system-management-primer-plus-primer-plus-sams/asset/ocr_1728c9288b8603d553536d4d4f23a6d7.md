# Four-Post Racks

Four-post racks are one step up in stability and convenience from two-post racks. These racks support your equipment with four steel posts—two in front and two in back—usually 30 or 36 inches apart. Each post is either prepunched with screw holes or with square holes meant for cage nuts (see the "Mounting Hardware" section later in this chapter for more information on cage nuts). You can mount any piece of computer hardware to a four-post rack, and these racks simplify both hardware maintenance and space management.

Four-post racks are appropriate for mounting heavy hardware such disk arrays and larger (over 50 lbs.) servers that need support on all four corners to stay securely mounted. These racks can house the same hardware as a two-post rack supports, as long as the hardware does not require center-mounting. The advantage of using a four-post rack in this case is that you do not have to use shelves to support the heavy rack-mountable hardware, reducing cost, complexity, and logistical problems during maintenance.

Most server hardware comes with rack-mounting kits designed for a four-post rack, which adds to the appeal of these types of racks. These usually consist of adjustable rails and bolts. Some come with sliding rails that allow you to slide a machine almost completely out of the rack for maintenance rather having to remove the equipment completely from the rack to another work space. Sliding rails are especially useful for working with heavier machines, as you can work on them without removing them from the rack; you can read more about sliding rails in Chapter 3.

Four-post racks create fewer space-management problems because their outer dimensions are fixed—unlike the open two-post system. No hardware (unless very oddly shaped) can protrude beyond the bounds of the posts, so you do not have to worry about machines from one rack encroaching on the space of another nearby rack.

# Rack Mounting Kits

You will find that there are as many ways to mount hardware as there are types of hardware. Vendors usually provide rack mounting kits with enough rails, brackets, bolts, nuts, and complicated instructions to make your head spin, and none of them seem to work the same way. Take your time, follow directions, and recruit others to help you—every little piece of the kit helps the hardware in place and should be installed properly. Skipping a step or forcing something into place may compromise the stability of the rack and cause hardware to fail.

# Cabinets

Cabinets are enclosures that hold your hardware in a sealed and secure compartment. The most basic cabinets are less than enclosed four-post racks. Other cabinets have six posts (three pairs in parallel) so you can mount hardware of different sizes in the same cabinet; the posts in the center are usually adjustable.
