As you can see, anyone can be asked to wear hat at any time in smaller, less-structured organizations. Having a flexible technical support structure staffed by individuals who are prepared to assist in a variety of roles can be cost-effective and the best service model for your customers. Managing this kind of technical support structure can be difficult, though, especially during large-scale outlets. If each person is qualified to find and repair the problem, you may have so many people trying to "help" solve the same problem that you run into the law of diminishing returns—too many people working on one task can hinder your progress. It appears that multiple administrators are just getting in the way of each other, send some people home or assign them to other tasks.

# # Developing On-Call Procedures

Like a doctor, a system administrator working in a 24/7 operation needs to be available for emergencies at all times—even during non-business hours (off hours). And, like a doctor, providing this availability is known as being "on call." As system administrator, you are responsible for the on-call procedures for your technical support organization. Organization vary widely among companies, but all must address three major concerns: delegation, regulation, and communication.

# # Delegation

Delegation—designating others to share on-call responsibilities—is essential for any system administrator. To understand how important delegation can be, imagine you work for a large ISP with a site hosting 100 servers, 500,000 customers, and a help desk tending an average of 100 calls per day. Now that you are the only person who is on call for that—that you have to deal with every single problem that the help desk cant handle on its own, every hour of day of every week of every month. That's not a life anyone would want to live. Even if only 5% of all support calls make the help desk, you would be dealing with five problems every day, in addition to your usual daily workload. Responsibilities that can lead to job dissatisfaction, inefficiency, poor work performance, and burnout.

The obvious answer to this problem is to delegate some of the responsibility for responding to help desks. In your support organization, arranging for your coworkers to share in the call duties makes life easier for everyone in the long run. System administrators in most companies participate in an on-call rotation, where, for example, each person is assigned on-call responsibilities for one week. During that week, individual is responsible for all off-hour production duties. No one else has to worry about off-hour calls except the individual designated for that's on-call day. The next week, the on-call responsibilities move to another individual, and so on, until the duties rotate back to the first individual, and the rotation begins again.

Rotation tend to work well; they help avoid employee burnout and give people flexibility in arranging their schedules; individuals can swap on-call times with other coworkers when necessary.
