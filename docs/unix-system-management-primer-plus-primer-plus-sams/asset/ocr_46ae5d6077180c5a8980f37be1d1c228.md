# UNIX SYSTEM MANAGEMENT PRIMER PLUS

+   - **Use Partially Loaded Hardware:** Your application may require two CPUs today, perhaps next year it will require four CPUs. One way to prepare for this without exceeding your budget is to purchase a server that is capable of using four CPUs, but only chase two of those. You now have a fully functional dual-CPU server, and all you have to do in the coming year is increase processing capacity is to build and install two more CPUs. Using partially loaded hardware is cost effective because it spreads the total cost of the server over a longer period of time.

# Don't Wait Long for Your Planned Hardware

If you wait too long to expand your partially loaded servers, the hardware you need no longer be available. In addition, more powerful hardware will likely be available at that time that can replace your current servers at a lower price. If you do not plan to expand for more than two years, it may be worth the extra cost to purchase larger, half-loaded hardware.

# Real-World Example: Overloaded Application Servers

One company's application servers were running on a pair of single-processor 400MHz Sun Netra 105 servers. During peak usage the CPU utilization on each server skyrocketed to 100% for several hours, slowing down the entire site. Initial plans proposed replacing the Netras with fully loaded dual-CPU Enterprise 220 servers with 2GB of memory. However, not knowing the proceeding needs would be a year from now, the system administrator instead purchasing half-loaded Enterprise 420 servers, which would allow expansion to four CPUs and 4GB of memory if needed. With this purchase, the site is performing well, and nobody needs to worry about future scalability as more processors and can easily be added to each server.

+   - **Design Horizontally Scalable Services:** A horizontally scalable service is one in which the capacity and performance can be increased by simply increasing the number of nodes running. This should be a familiar concept; the load-balancing techniques discussed in Chapter 10, "Providing High Availability in Your Unix System," are all applicable to horizontally scalable services.

+   - **Useively Scalable Hardware:** With massively scalable hardware, you can scale resources tens, or even thousands of times to meet the needs of applications. The Sun Enterprise 10000 (E1K) is such a server, supporting a total of 64 UltraSPARC II processors, 16 system boards, 64GB of memory, and disk arrays to support hungry storage. The E1K can allocate individual components, including processors, to domains that each can run their own isolated instance of Solaris. These domains can be dynamically reconfigured at will—if you need more processors, you can add them via software, assuming they are available. All of this scalability comes at a price, of course; the list price for a base configuration Enterprise 10000 is almost $1 million.
