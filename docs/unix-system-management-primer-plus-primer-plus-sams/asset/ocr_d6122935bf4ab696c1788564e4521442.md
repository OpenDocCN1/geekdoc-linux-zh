Some general questions to ask when researching application and database software include the following:

+   - What operating systems does it support? Is it supported on our current operating system platform?
- What are the disk, memory, and CPU requirements?
- How does its performance compare to similar products from other vendors? Vendors will skew performance numbers in their favor. It's important to get third-party test results from companies with no relationship to the vendor or your own performance tests.
- Is the new software compatible with other software in production?
- How does the software scale when the business grows?
- Does the vendor offer technical support? Does that include onsite support or simply phone support? (You learn more about evaluating support contracts in "Evaluating Vendor Support Contracts," later in this chapter.)
- Are patches made readily available to customers?

# Checking Real-World Requirements

You might benefit from asking application users about the product requirements rather than the vendors, since many application requirements reflect an optimum configuration. People using the Real-World have practical knowledge of what they require to run efficiently.

Your goal is to gather enough information to be able to spec out a reasonable hardware platform for one, that you can easily support and that will scale with your business.

# Choosing Operating System Software

Unix is unique in that it supports many different operating systems. While Microsoft Windows users are stuck with, well, Windows, Unix users can choose from a variety of popular Unix operating systems in use today, including:

+   - Solaris
- Linux (which has its own distributions)
- HP-UX
- AIX
