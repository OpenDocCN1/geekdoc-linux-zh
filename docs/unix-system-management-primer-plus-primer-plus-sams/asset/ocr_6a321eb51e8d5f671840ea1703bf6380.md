The site security administrator must have the knowledge to understand and implement all parts of the security policy, as well as sufficient privileges to monitor and enforce the policy. In many organizations, a system administrator fits nicely into this role; in larger organizations, the site security administrator can be a full-time position.

# Defining Your Security Goals

The majority of a security policy consists of your organization's security goals. One example of a security goal is to encrypt all company communications over public networks. Another might be to require access cards on every entrance and exit to your company's offices and data centers. Security goals generally fall into the following categories:

+   * Access to resources
* Electronic data security
* Service restrictions
* Physical security

In addition to setting forth security goals, security policies also can specify the means by which your organization implements security. For example, if your policy requires that all company traffic that passes through a public network is encrypted, it can also suggest the use of SSH for interactive login sessions and a VPN for other types of traffic.

# Use Generic References to Hardware and Software

Although security policies can include information specifying the means for implementing system security, it's best to minimize references to specific hardware and software in your policy. For example, you do not want to change text in your security policy just because you switched to a different SSH client.

# Limiting Access to Resources

Every security policy should define the conditions under which people can gain access to electronic resources. The concept of least privilege, which was explained in Chapter 13, dictates that people should be given only those privileges necessary to do their jobs. Your policy should define different roles in your organization and the privileges assigned to each role. Some examples are shown in Table 16.1.

**Table 16.1** One Company's Roles and Their Associated Privileges

| Role | Type of Access |
|---|---|
| System Administrator | Full access to all electronic resources, offices, and data centers |
| Chief Executive Officer | Full access (on request) to all resources, offices, and data centers |
| Sales & Marketing | Sales file shares, customer records |
