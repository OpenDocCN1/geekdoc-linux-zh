# Prioritizing MX Records

MX records with a lower number in the priority field are preferred over MX records with a higher number. This may be the opposite of what you would expect.

If MX records have the same priority, RFC 1123 requires one example to be chosen at random by a mail transfer agent because sendmail. This can be more efficient than using multiple A records for a DNS root record when one server in a pool of mail goes down, only the MX record for server is skipped, not the single MX record representing the entire pool of mail hubs, which are still available. Listing 10.3 shows an example of an MX record with a backup MX record of lower priority; server1 and server2 have equal priority and will be chosen at random by mail transfer agents. server3 has a higher number in the priority field and will be used as the last-resort mail exchange.

# Summary

Businesses today rely heavily on the Internet, whether for complex e-commerce Web sites or email for three or four people. For the Internet to remain a viable medium for business, it is important for system administrators to employ high-availability technologies and techniques to keep sites up and running. While a wide range of hardware and software can help you meet this goal, this chapter discussed some of the more practical and effective of these technologies. You learned how to use load-balancing techniques to spread your services across multiple redundant mechanisms. You also learned how different RAID levels function and the benefits and risks associated with each of them.

Multipathing and redundancy can provide highly available network links, and individual network services like DNS and SMTP have their own built-in high-availability mechanisms. Redundancy is the most important concept to take away from this chapter though; it is the basis for almost every high-availability mechanism. Knowing how to apply the high-availability mechanisms from this chapter to your systems will help you make the right choices when you decide that your site needs to be available all day, every day.
