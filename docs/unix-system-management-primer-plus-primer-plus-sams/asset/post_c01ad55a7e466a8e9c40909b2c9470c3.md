# Chapter 4

- 1. A quality assurance team is a group of people dedicated to testing the functionality and usability of your systems. They move the significant task of testing out of your hands, allowing you to focus on system administration tasks such as monitoring services and maintaining hardware.

- 2. A unit test checks individual functional components of a system to verify that each system component works according to its original specifications. In many cases, you won't need to perform unit tests on every functional unit of a system (OS and software vendors, for example, typically test products before they ship them); however, unit tests should cover all components and functions that affect the intended use of the system. You didn't need unit tests or functions that will not be used in your system (and, in fact, should consider eliminating or disabling such elements to avoid potential problems from nonsensical components).

- 3. The two major types of load testing for a system involve the following:
    - Testing the number of operations a system can perform in a fixed amount of time. Variable-load tests introduce as much load as possible in a fixed amount of time and measure performance based on the average number of requests filled in that time frame. This measurement is useful for determining a system's breaking point, or the level of stress that causes the system to stop functioning within specifications.
    - Testing the amount of time a system requires to perform a fixed number of operations. Constant-load tests introduce constant levels of stress (usually in the form of service requests) into a system and report performance based on the average time the system takes to complete each request. This data is useful for predicting a system's performance under known conditions.

- 4. Members of a linear progression differ by a fixed amount, while members of a geometric progression differ by a fixed ratio. Ascertaining the type of progression that performance measurements take as you increase the stress on a system is critical to accurate capacity planning. A system whose performance has a geometric relationship to its inputs will degrade in performance more quickly than a product with one with a linear relationship.

- 5. An alpha test allows potential users to test a product that is not functionally stable or complete. Users can provide valuable input on the functionality and what they'd like to see in the finished product. A beta test allows users to test a functionally complete (or mostly complete) product. Beta tests are good for finding bugs and other annoyances before a product is released to the general public.

# Chapter 5

- 1. A system administrator should set aside free time each day to accommodate emergency situations that might occur, interfering with his or her other daily activities. If everything is quiet, time can be used to catch up on other work, reading mailing lists and checking on the latest operating system patches.

- 2. You can reduce desk staff turnover by providing adequate training and tools to help help desk staff perform their jobs effectively. Meeting with HD staff periodically to assess current support problems and gather input on various issues is another key to staff retention. You also encourage staff to remain with the company by providing help desk a clear and obtainable career path that might lead to becoming a system administrator; former help desk staff usually make very astute system administrators because they have a real understanding and knowledge of customer needs.

- 3. The law of diminishing returns affects a system administrator when too many people are working to fix a single problem, getting in each other's way and potentially exacerbating the problem, while other issues and needs go unattended.

- 4. Any set of on-call guidelines should, at minimum, define these three:
    a. They should delegate responsibility for responding to each reported problem.
    b. They should stipulate conditions that regulate locations, activities, and response times of on-call personnel during their hours of service.
    c. They should clearly assign and describe means of communication among system administration staff, management, and help desk staff.

- 5. An escalation procedure defines how and at what point a problem is handed off to different levels of support until it reaches a group that can address the problem. All support processes need escalation procedures to specify how a central point of contact—such as a help desk—eventually routes problems to the groups that can fix them in a timely manner.

- 6. Predefined forms force the user to enter information necessary to help identify, define, and resolve a particular problem. By requiring users to enter the answers to questions help desk staff must ask in order to begin the resolution process, forms help reduce delays in problem resolution. A free-form email leaves it to end-users to determine what information the help-desk might need to begin resolving the problem.
