# What Is an IT Disaster?

People define disasters in many different ways, but in the information technology world, the disaster is any event or condition that disrupts operations for an extended period of time. The definition of "extended period of time" really depends on your business. An outage of a bank's master account database might be considered a disaster if it lasts for several hours. A small business might be able to operate for up to a week without an email server before the situation reaches its disaster stage.

Every area and every business is subject to its own unique natural and man-made disasters. Several types of disasters, however, are common to a wide number of businesses. Include the following:

+   * Power outages
* Fires
* Floods and other natural disasters
* Data loss

The following sections examine these types of disasters—how and why they occur, what can happen as a result, and what you can do as a Unix system administrator to minimize your data center's losses in their wake.

# Power Outages

Most people have experienced power outages, both at home and at work. At home, power outages can cause real inconveniences, loss of property, and—where the home's electrical supply powers critical medical equipment—even threaten the health and well-being of residents. Typically, however, homeowners prepare for and minimize the damage of power outages by following some simple procedures.

+   * Have candles, flashlights, and batteries on hand.
* When an outage occurs, notify the electrical company of the problem.
* Keep the refrigerator and freezer doors closed to keep food and other perishables as possible.
* Use kerosene heaters or fireplaces as auxiliary heating supplies.
* If essential services depend upon electricity, maintain gas generators to supply power until the electrical service is back up and running.
