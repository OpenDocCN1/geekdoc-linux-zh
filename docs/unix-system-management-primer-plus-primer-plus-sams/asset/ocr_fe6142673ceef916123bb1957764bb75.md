# Real-World Example: RCS Recovers Critical Files

An administrator was making changes to his DNS configuration when he accidentally deleted the master copies of all the production zones files. After a few deplets, he remembered that the files were all checked into RCS. He listed all the .v files in the RCS directory and checked them out (without the -l flag) one by one until all of the files were back where they belonged. RCS is not a backup tool, but it served as one in this case!

# System Activity Schedules

As a Unix system administrator, you are responsible for scheduling all system activities. You are also responsible for ensuring that all users affected by the activity are notified in advance of when the activity will occur. Determining the right time for issuing the notification and the best method for circulating it takes careful thought and planning.

Suppose you need to reboot your company's corporate Web servers to apply patches. You schedule the reboot a week from today to give everyone adequate time to respond and notify clients of the potential outage. A week is more than adequate lead time for a change, but few people will remember this announcement a week later when the outage occurs.
