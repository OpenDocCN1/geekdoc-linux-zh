# Don't Spam Your Owners

Spam refers to any unsolicited email, usually of a commercial nature, that is of extremely low value to the recipient. Most users do not appreciate being sent spam that fills their mailboxes, may just delete messages from people they don't know. When sending bulk email to users, state the purpose clearly in the subject. Drawing from the example used in this chapter, heading such as "Test the next generation of Nearest Integer Bank Online" would be appropriate. Also careful not to send too many messages from you on a daily basis, they are more likely to ignore your messages as time goes on.

You must also provide some sort of incentive to entice your users to participate in your testing. Sometimes the pressure of testing is enough. Wording your announcement to stress this importance, as in, "You can participate in the development of tomorrow's online banking technology," gives users a sense of worthiness. Sometimes incentives need to be more tangible. Companies pay users to help test, while others give discounts on future services or provide the product free of charge to testers after it is released to the general public.

# Pay Attention to User Demographics

Choosing the right users for testing is just as important as the testing itself. You may find that most users willing to participate in an alpha or beta test are technically savvy, because everybody users are generally satisfied with the status quo. Try to recruit a representative sample of your users so you don't ignore any potential use of your system. You can do this by choosing a large number of users at random and offering them the opportunity to test a new service in exchange for demographic information, including their experience with computers and how often they use your services. You can use this information to determine whether any particular demographic group is underrepresented, at which time you can ramp up the recruitment process.

# Alpha Testing

Alpha tests enable customers and users to test new systems before they are functionally complete. Users can comment on bugs, features that they would like to see added, and the value of the system in general. The advantage of alpha testing is that you can get input from end users in the early stages of the development process.
