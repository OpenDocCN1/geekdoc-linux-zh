System administrators maintain two basic types of network diagrams: physical and logical. A physical diagram shows the actual physical connections between each device on your network. Physical diagrams also provide information about other physical aspects of each device, including network interfaces, switch ports, cable types, cable identifiers (if you label your cables), and the make and model of each device.

Physical network diagrams are useful for the following purposes:

+   - Identifying to which port a device is connected
- Surmising the physical location of a device
- Identifying the make and model of a device
- Planning a build-out of a new or existing network

A logical network diagram shows the layout of devices relative to its network, and includes few, if any, physical details about a device.

In practice, logical diagrams tend to be more useful than physical diagrams, because after you have installed a device on your system, all you care about is its IP address, the network on it resides, and its access to other networks. You can manage all of those issues through software, without physical intervention.

Logical diagrams are useful for the following purposes:

+   - Identifying on which network(s) a device resides
- Identifying all devices on a particular network
