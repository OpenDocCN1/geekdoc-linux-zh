# Change Management

As the system administrator of a Unix network system, you are responsible for deciding what changes to make to that system to keep it up to date and functioning at peak efficiency. You are also responsible for scheduling system changes and for communicating information about upcoming changes to the system's users. All of these responsibilities fall under the general category of change management. Change encompasses the approval, documentation, and auditing of all your systems. Even the slightest change to a running system can have a huge impact on a production service. Although you, as the system administrator, may think a change you are making is urgently necessary, others using the system might not think so. Change management ensures that ample communication passes between the people making and those affected by the changes. This communication can take the form of a simple announcement from IT staff or a complex approval chain with management sign-off that could take days.

# Change Approval

A-approval process prevents any change from occurring before it is thoroughly analyzed by peers, clients, and management, and given the necessary approval. System administrators at smaller organizations may be able to get away with a casual email asking for any objections to a change. Here's an example:

> From: Mike Admin
To: IT Department
Subject: new web server

Hey guys,

I'll be putting new web server into production tomorrow at 5 AM. I don't expect
to have any downtime whatsoever. If anyone has any concerns, please let me know by end of business day.

However, larger organizations, especially those with auditing requirements, need more rigid change controls. In those situations, change requests go through a strict approval process in which every department might be affected by the change is required to sign off on the proposal. This type of approval process can be long and arduous, especially if conducted through phone calls and email.

Change approval tools help manage the process. Remedy, described in Chapter 5 as a trouble ticket management system, provides change management functionality using an interface similar to the one it uses for trouble ticket management. For change management, instead of escalating trouble tickets to support staff, Remedy escalates change tickets through the approval chain until the status is marked as approved. At that point, the person who issued the change proposal is notified that he or she can proceed with the change.
