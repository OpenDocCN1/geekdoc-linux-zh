SQL is the easiest way to learn programming.




SQL which stands for Structured Query Language is a highly used programming language for working with databases. It is the standard language used to manage and operate databases. A database is a systematic collection of data. It is a data structure that stores organized data usually in a form of tables. You can think of a database as an electronic filing cabinet. A cabinet is a physical structure for storing documents and a database is an electronic structure for storing documents. A cabinet usually stores information in folders and a database usually stores information in the form of tables.

A company database may include tables for products price, employee names, and financial records. Databases can store very large data (AKA Big Data) and make it is easy to search for data, add new data, edit or delete old data. SQL statements are used to perform tasks such as insert data on a database, retrieve data from a database, database creation, deleting data, fetching rows from tables, modifying rows and columns, etc. You write a SQL statement on a Database Management System (DBMS) the same way you write a sentence on a word document. The DBMS will read the SQL statement and send the message to the database to carry out your command. DBMS is software that allows users to communicate with the database using the SQL language. It is the layer between the data and the end-users.
Database modeling
Database modeling is a process of producing a diagram showing the logical structure of a database which shows the relationships and constraints that determine how data can be stored and accessed in the database. It shows how data is connected and how it can be processed and stored inside the database. It is similar to putting labels and names on a filing cabinet. This helps us to think systematically about the key data points to be stored and retrieved. The four types of data models are the Hierarchical model, Network model, Entity-relationship model and Relational model.

SQL is very easy to grasp because of its English-based syntax. It is simpler than most programming languages. The grammar is smaller and the amount of different concepts is smaller which is why it is very popular and widely used. You can learn SQL on your own by practicing it without knowing any programming languages. You can download and install free SQL software and start practicing by running simple queries using your data. Once you've mastered the fundamentals of SQL and know how to run queries then you can get certified. Google “SQL statements” and click on images to see what SQL statements look like. YouTube SQL for a visual presentation of SQL.

Data management
Data management is the practice of storing and maintaining data as a valuable resource for organizations to unlock their potential. Managing data requires a robust data strategy and plans of how to access, integrate, cleanse, govern, store and prepare data for analytics. Data Architects have to design how IT infrastructure can be used effectively and efficiently in order to support and maximize the use of data by your organisation.
Data architecture
Data architecture is an outline or a plan of how IT infrastructure supports your data strategy by showing how data is acquired, transported, stored, queried, and secured. It is a set of rules, policies, standards, diagrams and models that govern and explains how data is used, stored, managed and integrated within an organization to ensure efficient use of data. The purpose of data architecture is to show the relationship between the data, business objectives and operations strategy.

Data Analytics (Charts & Dashboards)
Data produced by the data management team helps the analytics teams to create dashboards which helps drive the business and ensures that business intelligence is one of the key components of competitive advantage for the organisation. The analytics teams will receive the dashboards requirement from the business units and request the data from the data management team. After a dashboard is created, the analytics team will maintain dashboards and the data management team will maintain the data feeding the dashboards. The data requirements for dashboard creation can be either from internal analytics teams or business units across the organisation. The key goal of the data management team is to support the analytics team to produce as many dashboards possible depending on resources available (money, material, machine, and manpower).
Automation
Data processes for new dashboards will be optimized and automated after the project ends, provided that the output adds value to the organisation.
Reliability and Tracking
The number of outputs produced should be benchmarked across all teams and business units to ensure that the team is sustainable, consistent, and reliable.
Agile  (Project Management Methodology)
The data management team will receive 90% of the projects from the analytics team. After requirements are received, the team will use the Agile method to complete the task as soon as possible. This entails braking down the tasks into smaller milestones then completing the milestones in 2-weeks sprints. Both failed and successful projects will be counted as part of the 90%.
Quality Assurance & Control
The data management team will be responsible of quality monitoring during the project implementation phase and after automation. Data quality compliance will be completed by the data management team.

Listening


Communication, Listening, Information, Growth, Success and Wealth...
Do not interrupt.



 

Listening is the ability to accurately receive and interpret the message being communicated to you. Listening is key to all effective and efficient communication. Without the ability to listen effectively, messages are easily misunderstood and information is lost. Without information, success is not easy to achieve. Listening is one communication skill you should master if you want to be successful. Effective listening helps to resolve conflicts, build trust, inspire people, increases productivity, and increases growth. Spend most of your conversations listening and you'll absorb information, become a good communicator and make good decisions.
How to listen
Pay attention to the speaker's verbal and non-verbal language to fully understand what they're expressing to you. Paying attention means listening and observing the speaker without interrupting. Do not interrupt the speaker for any reason. If you want to say something, wait until the speaker is done talking then you can talk. Listening effectively means listening to key points of the speaker's message and taking notes mentally or otherwise.
Listening and problem solving
If someone is asking you a serious question or they want your advice, listen attentively, do not rush to respond, try not to respond immediately and think for a day or two about what the speaker was saying then respond. Thinking takes time, energy, and effort. Don't be quick to respond. Just tell the speaker that you want time to process what they said and you will get back to them. This strategy works in every situation even if you don't need to think just take time to think. You will make wise decisions if you take your time to think.

Active listening
Active listening is a technique that requires the listener to fully concentrate, understand, and remember what is being said. You have to empathize, reflect and take key points of what is being communicated to you. Active listening is used in counseling, training, and solving disputes or conflicts. You can solve many problems by just listening attentively without even responding. This is called the rubber duck effect.
Barriers to Listening
Listening is a very challenging skill because the are always distractions that interfere with the message being communicated. Distractions to a speaker's message can be caused by a listener's body movements, internal thoughts, lack of focus, sounds in the surrounding environment, or biased listening. Biased listening or selective listening is one of the biggest problems in communication. Biased listening is when someone is just listening for information that they want to hear. This is dangerous because what if the information that you are not listening to is the information that leads to success.
Key listening techniques
• Pay Attention.
• Paraphrase and summarize.
• Delay feedback.
• Do not judge or jump to conclusions.
• Show that you're listening by nodding.
• Do not interrupt the speaker no matter what.
• Face the speaker and make eye contact.
• Do not think about responding.
• Ask questions.
• Look at the body language.
• Focus.

Avoid escalating the conflict
When two people are in conflict with each other, one way to de-escalate the problem is if one member can avoid engaging with the issue at hand. This can be achieved by focusing on other issues that are not problematic and writing down issues that are faced with difficulties. Sometimes conflict is caused by lack of information and avoiding the issue until you have all the information can work or even better, delegate the issue to someone who has the relevant information, this is where counseling comes in.
Collaboration
My personal favourite of all the conflict resolution strategies is the collaboration strategy. Everyone around you has an opinion and in their perspective, their opinion is one hundred percent correct. The trick is for you to view things from their perspective then you will be able to see value in their opinion and work together with them to harness the value of their input. The is no knowledge that is not power.
Accommodating a different perspective
Conflict is usually caused by two people having two different opinions and they believe in their opinions so much that they believe that someone else’s opinion is wrong. This is very dangerous. Both parties believe that they are right, and the other person is wrong. The key here is to accommodate or consider a different opinion even if you don’t believe in it. Just acknowledging a different perspective from another person’s opinion can help resolve unnecessary conflict about who is right and who is wrong. You don’t have to believe in other people’s opinions however you acknowledge that everyone around you has a right to have an opinion and accommodate that opinion.

Compromising
Comprising can help you reach your goals. Some conflicts are not constructive, they do not add value to you or everyone around you, and they do not help anyone reach their goal. Such conflicts have no value and should be avoided at all costs. If you cannot avoid them, you can compromise your perspective to let the other person have their perspective for the sake of the bigger picture.
Competing
It is a bit challenging to compete without engaging in conflict. The easiest way to do it is to compete based on fact, not opinion or emotions. Use data to prove your point and make informed decisions. Big data, statistics, mathematics and analytics play a huge role in helping human beings make their decisions.

Credit is the ability to borrow money or access goods and services with the agreement that you'll pay later provided that you have a good credit score. A credit score is a representation of your creditworthiness. It is a view of your credit history and payment behaviour presented numerically to show how you manage your debt. It is based on credit report information from credit bureaus. A good credit score means that lenders are more likely to lend you money. When you use credit, you create debt. Debt is the result of borrowing money and the more responsible you are at handling your debt, the more access you may have to credit in the future. Credit approval is determined by a borrower's credit rating, income, collateral, assets, and pre-existing debt. Use credit to buy assets only. That is called good credit. Assets are things that bring money into your pocket e.g. a rental property. If you use credit to buy liabilities, then chances are you will be poor. Liabilities are things that take money out of your pocket e.g. luxury cars.

It is the risk that a lender may not get back the money they lent to the borrower, which results in an interruption of cash flows and increased costs for collection for the lender.

Price
The price the customer pays for the product should be linked to the product's real and perceived value while taking into consideration supply costs, seasonal discounts, and competitors' prices. You may raise the price to give the product the appearance of being a luxury or you may lower the price so more consumers can try the product but low prices can also give the impression that the product is less exclusive.
Place
When deciding on a place to sell your product, you should get your products in front of consumers that are most likely to buy them and plan for how to deliver the product to the market.
Promotion
Create a promotional strategy for your product. The purpose of promoting a product is to reveal to consumers why they need it and what they will benefit from it. With the rise of the internet, it is vital to promote your product both online and offline.
Acceptability
A product or service must meet or exceed the needs and expectations of customers. Acceptability is divided into two perspectives: functional and psychological acceptability. Functional acceptability refers to the ability of the product or service to deliver on what customers expect. The product should be reliable. Psychological acceptability is usually associated with the status of the brand or product. People prefer to buy a product they can trust.
Affordability
The target market should be economically able and psychologically willing to pay a product’s price. Affordability also has two perspectives: economic and psychological affordability. Economic affordability is focused on whether or not the target market can afford to pay the product’s price. Psychological affordability refers to a customer’s willingness to pay for a given price, the product’s value should be relative to the cost of the product or service.
Accessibility
Accessibility describes whether customers have access to the product and if they can use the product or service. There are two dimensions of Accessibility: availability and convenience. Availability measures if the is enough stock to match customer demand. Convenience is concerned with how easy it is for customers to get the product or service.
Awareness
Customers must be informed about a product and how it will benefit them. This will persuade customers to give the product a try and remind existing users to keep buying the product. The two dimensions of Awareness are product knowledge and brand awareness. Customers should believe in the brand and relevant information about the product or service should be provided.

Kanban is a framework used to optimize production processes which requires work items to be represented visually on a Kanban board, allowing team members to see the state of every piece of work at any time and allows for real-time communication and full transparency of workflow. Kanban is aimed at optimizing the production flow by regulating the supply of raw material and delivering what the process needs exactly when it needs it. It controls inventory by pulling supplies to the production floor based on customer demand and limits the number of work items that can exist in the production process at any given step at any given time. It is usually used in just-in-time (JIT) manufacturing processes. One of the first principles of Kanban is to make small incremental changes to existing processes instead of drastic changes that might lead to a high risk of failure.

Kanban is designed to reduce the idle time in a production process by visualizing the production process and the actual work passing through that process to identify potential bottlenecks in the process and fix them so work can flow through it cost-effectively at an optimal speed or throughput.

Gross domestic product
Gross domestic product (GDP) is the market value of all the finished goods and services produced within a country's borders in a specific period used to measure a country's economic health.
Production Theory
The theory of production depicts the connection between production outputs and the inputs which help to determine the profit-maximizing output by managing the marginal and average costs of production to minimize the cost of production.
Opportunity Cost
Opportunity cost is the profit lost when one alternative is selected over another. This concept helps with prioritization by reminding one to examine all reasonable alternatives before making a decision.
Price Theory
The price theory is a principle that uses the concept of supply and demand to determine the appropriate price point for goods or services which helps with price adjustments as market conditions change. Organizations use the price theory to set the right price for their goods and services.
Supply and Demand
Supply and demand is the relationship between the price and quantity of goods and services that producers wish to sell and consumers wish to buy. When the price is low, the demand for goods and services is high and the opposite is true.
Elasticity
Elasticity measures the change in the total quantity demanded of goods or services regarding the price change of those goods or services. The price of a product is considered to be elastic if the quantity of demand of the product changes more than proportionally when its price changes. The means that the price has a huge impact on its demand.
Consumer Price Index
The consumer price index (CPI) is a measure of weighted average prices of a basket of consumer goods and services, such as transportation and medical care calculated by taking price changes for each item in a basket of goods and averaging them.
Product Price Index
The producer price index (PPI) is a measure of the average fluctuation in selling prices received by domestic producers over time. The CPI includes only components of personal consumption that are directly paid for by the consumer, whereas the PPI includes components that are not paid for by the consumer.
Trade Balance
The trade balance, also known as the balance of trade (BOT) is the difference between the value of a country's exports and the value of a country's imports for a given period.
Recession
A recession is a period of temporary economic decline when the economy slows down for at least six months which is identified by a fall in GDP in two successive quarters, fewer jobs, low salaries, less spending, and businesses stop growing. A recession can be caused by a range of financial, psychological, and economic factors
Inflation
Inflation is the rising prices of goods and services within a particular economy leading to a decrease in the purchasing power of consumers.
Market Structure
Market structure refers to how firms are differentiated and categorized based on the nature of the services and goods they sell and the degree of competition in the market.

Corporate governance is the system of rules, practices, and processes by which a business is managed and controlled. It is how a company is directed and identifies who has power and accountability, and who makes decisions. The key issues associated with corporate governance are diversity, remuneration, stakeholder accountability, conflicts of interest, and transparency.  

Corporate law and governance shape what businesses do and how they do it. Corporate law prescribes the formation and the activities of corporations, while corporate governance regulates stakeholder's interests. Boards of directors are responsible for the governance of their companies. The shareholders' role in governance is to appoint the board of directors and the auditors and to make sure that an appropriate governance structure is in place.

Ethics, risk management, compliance, and administration are all elements of governance. Corruption, deceit and unfair policy are good examples of bad governance. A conflict of interest arises when a person chooses personal gain over company duties in which they exploit their position for personal gain in one way or the other. A company that applies the core principles of good corporate will be able to attract investors, whose support can help to finance further growth. Good Governance is measured by factors such as Participation, Rule of Law, Transparency, Responsiveness, Inclusiveness, Effectiveness, Efficiency, and Accountability.

Depression is a mood disorder that causes a persistent feeling of sadness and loss of interest which affects how you think and behave and can lead to a variety of emotional and physical problems. It is a serious mental illness that can interfere with a person's life and cause long-lasting and severe feelings of sadness, hopelessness, loss of interest in activities you once enjoyed, changes in appetite, weight changes unrelated to diet, and can lead to lack of sleep or sleeping too much. It can also cause physical symptoms of pain and brain damage which can lead to difficulties remembering and concentrating once the disease is over. Depression is linked to heart diseases, it makes heart diseases worse, makes it more difficult to recover from heart diseases, and increases your risk of having a heart attack.
Causes Of Depression
There are many possible causes of depression such as faulty mood regulation by the brain, genetic vulnerability, medications, medical problems, stressful life events such as family death, divorce, illness, redundancy, and job or money worries. Depression causes the amygdala in your brain to enlarge. This is a part of the brain associated with emotional responses. When it becomes larger and more active, it causes sleep disturbances, changes in activity levels, and changes in other hormones.

Job Interviews

For every job you find, you will have to ace the interview.



An interview is nothing else but a conversation between you and the interviewers so that they can get to know your personality. In this case, your personality means your skills, qualifications and experience. Therefore, it is very important to write down a summary of skills, qualification and experience before you go to the interview. This will help you organize your thoughts to clearly understand yourself and how your fit the position that you are applying for. Also, this will help you be clear and have confidence in your answers.
Never doubt your skills, qualification and experience, the fact that you have been called for an interview means that the company has seen your CV and they like it just the way it is. Even if it means that you don’t have half the skills they require, never doubt that you will get the job. The interviewers have read your CV and they know your skills, qualification and experience already, so they are calling you for an interview for two reasons:
  1. They want to confirm their thoughts about you being the best candidate.
Remember the company has a gap in their team that they are trying to fill, and they already have a mental image of the kind of person they are looking for, the good news is, if they called you for an interview then that means that they think you have the personality they are looking for so they would like to confirm what they think by asking you a series of questions. These questions are what make up an interview and may either be behavioral or competency type of questions depending on what the company thinks is more important for that particular “gap”. So, it is very important to know what kind of candidate they are looking for and present yourself accordingly so that they see that you are the kind of fellow that they are looking for. To do this, study the company’s requirements for that position and match them with your skills, qualification and experience e.g. if the company needs some experience in SQL then check if your CV has SQL. This will help you understand what kind of fellow they are looking for and how close are you to be that fellow. If you feel like you don’t have most of the requirements that they are looking for just remember one thing; they already know that you don’t have the qualities that they are looking for and they still think that you are the best person for the job so just prepare thoroughly for the interview and you won’t go wrong.

  1. They want you to support and give evidence of what you were telling them in your CV
This is the most important reason for an interview. All your interview answers should be about evidence (success stories). Remember, a CV is a very summary of your life, so they want you to elaborate on that. For example, If they ask you why should we hire you, they already saw from your CV that you have experience in that particular field and they know that is why they need to hire you but they just want you to support that experience with success stories (evidence) and if possible to give hard evidence. Don’t worry if you don’t have written proof to support some of the skills and/or experience on your CV, the most common way to support your claims is to tell success stories, which is all the evidence they need. You will see in the questions below how easy it is to support your answers with success stories but first, let us look at the structure of interviews.

Preparing for an Interview
Let everything revolve around your skills, qualifications and experience


Researching the company
When you research the company, don’t just browse through their website, go deeper than that. This will help you answer your questions appropriately and will help you show your interest in the company which will make you stand out from other candidates who didn’t invest time in their research. Researching the company will also help you prepare your questions for the interviewer.
Matching skills, qualifications and experience to the job post
This is a very important part of preparing for an interview as questions will revolve around the job requirements and duties. Interviewers will ask questions to see if you have the required characteristics and to see if you will be able to carry out the duties. Matching your skills and qualification to the job’s requirements and duties will give you a good sense of what characteristics the recruiter is looking for and what duties will you be required to do. This will help you know which of the required characteristics do you have, and which ones don’t you have so that you can start doing your research and studying those characteristics that you don’t have. The trick here is, never lie to the interviewer about characteristics you don’t have; be honest and tell them that you will do everything in your power to learn.
There are countless books written about these skills, make sure that you read the books on a daily basis for self-improvement and development. If you haven’t had time to read the books the read at least one article about each of these skills before an interview. Remember these are just common skills, there are more specific skills that a company will require e.g. advanced excel.

Research the job post
Make sure you know everything about the job duties written in the post. The questions will be centered on the duties and sometimes recruiters can ask very specific and challenging questions, e.g. say one of the requirements is knowledge of AWS, the recruiter might ask, what is the output voltage of the AWS motherboard. Now that’s deep. Not to worry though, recruiters turn to ask more specific questions for senior positions and sometimes recruiters just want to see your thought process. Basically, the job requirements are like a scope of an exam, it tells you what they will test you on and you must go and hit the books hard. If one of the requirements was to have experience in operating the hplc machine and you have zero experience with that machine, then you need to make sure that when you go to an interview you have read about the machine and you have some serious knowledge about that machine. When asked about the machine, never lie and say you have experience on the machine, NO! Tell them straight that you have no experience with that machine, but you have a good understanding of how the machine works then continue showing the knowledge that you have gained from reading about the machine. Now you know why it’s very important to invest a whole lot of time preparing.
Prepare success stories and know them by heart
When answering interview questions use the SARS method of answering. SARS stands for Situation, Action, Results and Summary which means try to highlight the Situation you were facing, the action you took, the results and then summarize your story to show how it is relevant to the question and maybe how the lesson learned can be used in the job that you are applying for.
Stay on topic, rather not talk at all than to be all over the place. Don’t just talk about your skills; show them how your skills can benefit the company, for example, don’t just say I have a BSc degree in meteorology, rather say, I have a BSc degree in meteorology that can benefit the company in so many ways. Look at what the company wants (post duties) and tell them how you can do it for them. Match your features to their need. What value are you adding (what other values do you have that benefit the company).

Job Interview Tips

Don’t panic  
Always remember an interview is just a conversation between you and your interviewer to discuss your skills, qualifications, and experience, everything else is a professional waste of time. Just stick to talking about your skills, qualifications, and experience and you will not go wrong.
Have a copy of your resume
Have your CV in a nice folder dedicated strictly for interviews. Statistically, it takes about 16 interviews to get one job. So, keep that folder safe. You might need it a couple more times.
Be positive about everything
Never say a negative comment about your previous job. When they show you something, give a positive comment. Never say anything negative about your day or anything else for that matter.
Don’t ramble  
Keep your answers short and concise. Elaborate as much as you need to get your point across, however, do not ramble. Do not talk for more than two minutes. I know 2 minutes sounds like nothing but write your story down and record yourself, you will be surprised how much you can sap in two minutes. Using the SARS technique will help make your stories short. Stay on topic; rather not talk at all than to be all over the place.
Be prepared  
Go through all the six steps explained in this book to help you prepare thoroughly. If you are underprepared do not panic otherwise you will not fail the interview because you are underprepared but because you are panicking. Read the guidelines above about how not to panic.
Success stories  
It’s all about evidence; you must back up anything you say with examples or physical evidence. The more evidence you can provide the better, don’t generalize, explain in detail.
Checklist  
Make a checklist of everything that you need to do when preparing for an interview or use a checklist provided in this book.

Questions… Questions… Questions…  
Make sure you practice at least 40 popular interview questions. Make sure you know everything about the job requirements and duties as the questions will be centered on this.
Grow  
Personal growth and development are some of the most important skills that a human being can learn. List all the mistakes you have done in the past interviews you attended and how you can fix them. If you cannot find a way to fix a mistake just keep on reading interview books, then you will eventually hit an answer or use the internet or ask someone. With all this flood of social networks, you might as well use them for a good benefit.

How to boost your confidence
Find out which skills does your company appreciate the most for that specific post that you are applying for. There should not be a lot e.g. for most finance posts, the skills required the most are MS Excel and data analytics.  

Always remember to remain professional at all times even if the interviewers are playful or even a bit unprofessional.  Know what makes up the company’s perfect candidate and present yourself as that person. Check the skills, qualifications and experience required. Job titles do not matter.

Remember to control your breathing; breath slowly to boost your confidence. Also, walking around before the interview or drinking cold water during the interview can help boost your confidence.  It is okay to be nervous, this is no surprise to the interviewer. Do your best to relax and don’t stress too much over little mistakes.  

Remember to breathe and think about your answers. Usually, people start to panic when they get asked questions that they cannot answer. Remember, interviews have only a few numbers of questions and the interviewers are not expecting you to answer all of them, therefore, if you read this book, you should be able to get most of the questions right. Do not worry about the other ones that you got wrong and do not hesitate to say if you do not know the answer.  

If you don’t know the answer admit it immediately, think about it out loud and let them know that you are thinking, and admit that you do not know it. It shows that you are engaged and that you want to answer well.  

Never try to be creative in an interview, answer exactly as prepared. Even when they ask you a question that is creative just stick to the basics. Creativity will be seen in your basic answer, you do not try to make it.

What not to do

• Don’t be late.
• Don’t wear too much cologne.  
• Don’t be afraid to repeat what is already in the CV.   
• Don’t give opinions when answering. Give evidence, success stories.  


Remember, these people want to hire you already, they believe in you, they already saw what kind of a person you are they just want to confirm it. So, do not try to be exceptional, follow the basics. Just shut up, answer what they are asking and go out. These people will never interview a person who they do not want to hire. Remember, they are for you not against you.
