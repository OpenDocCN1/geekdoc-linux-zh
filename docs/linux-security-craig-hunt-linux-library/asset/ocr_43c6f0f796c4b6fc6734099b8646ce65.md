# # Electronic Mail

Parts 1 and 2 of this book explore the security challenges associated with the systems and network layers of the Linux server. The next step in this progression is to examine the security issues inherent to the application layer. This chapter kicks off Part 3, “Application Security,” by taking a close look at a number of electronic mail server applications, their installation, setup, and their impact on the overall security of your server.

While the World Wide Web has seen its traffic (HTTP) grow faster than any other protocol in recent Internet history, electronic mail (SMTP) packets still outnumber any other protocol in usage today. With recent legislative decisions in favor of the validity of electronic signatures, you’re likely to see the amount of electronic mail use grow at an even faster rate as companies start using the Internet for the distribution and signature of legally binding documents and their supporting materials.

However, electronic mail is one of the most challenging applications to secure due to the public and widespread nature of the service. Mail servers often must handle messages of very disparate sizes, origin, and composition. Electronic mail is easily the part of your server to which you should devote the most attention on a day-to-day basis, since it’s the application that will experience the most amount of traffic.
