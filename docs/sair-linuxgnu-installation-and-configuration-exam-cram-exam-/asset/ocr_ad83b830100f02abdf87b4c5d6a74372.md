# Monitor Graphic Modes

You can change the graphic modes on most monitors. In other words, you can change the number of pixels shown on your screen and the number of colors used for each pixel. Even if you thought you configured this setting with your video card, you have to do it again with your monitor. After all, they are separate components with separate drivers.

Older monitors support only one or two graphic modes. The multisync monitors of today support several modes. To make sure that you keep your monitor working, you need to balance its capabilities against the settings you create for your video card.

The graphical capability of a monitor depends on its screen size and dot pitch. Larger screens and smaller dot pitch sizes allow you to set up more detailed graphic modes on your monitor.

If you set your graphic mode to the maximum resolution of your monitor, you may be disappointed. Monitors let you set graphic modes with more pixels than are physically available on the monitor. For example, one 14-inch monitor with a .25mm dot pitch has a maximum screen size of 240mmx180mm. In other words, it has 960 horizontal dots in a row and 720 vertical dots in a column. Yet this monitor allows you to set a graphic mode of 1024x768 pixels. When your monitor tries to fit 1024x768 pixels on a screen that has only 960x720 dots, some pixels don't quite make it. The result is a blurry monitor.
