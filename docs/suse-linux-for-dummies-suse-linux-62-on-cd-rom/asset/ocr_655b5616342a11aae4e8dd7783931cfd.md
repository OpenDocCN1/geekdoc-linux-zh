# Appendix A

# Hardware Compatibility

The version of Linux on the SuSE Linux For Dummies CD-ROM supports a wide range of hardware but, like other distributions of Linux, it doesn't support all types of hardware.

We made every attempt to present correct material in this appendix. Some equipment listed, however, might not work with SuSE Linux. In addition, hardware is introduced all the time, and some of it might work with Linux but not be here.

The information in this appendix was taken from the Linux Hardware Compatibility HOWTO by Patrick Reijnen and the PCMCIA HOWTO by David Winds, as well as the XFree86 organization.

After you have your system running, review some of the hardware HOWTOs to see whether any other hardware is listed that you might want to use in your Linux system.

# Hardware Architectures

Linux runs on a variety of hardware architectures. These architectures are usually defined by the model name of the main CPU's manufacturer, such as the Intel architecture, the Alpha (Compaq) architecture, and the SPARC (Sun) architecture. Linux has been ported to run on handheld devices and mainframes. Just recently, there was an article about an Italian company that has created a telephone that runs Linux! This book and its CD-ROM cover only the Intel architecture, which includes AMD and Cyrix CPUs from the x386 configurations all the way to the class of CPUs for the Pentium ils.

In addition to the hardware architecture of the CPU, you have to consider the bus structure. A bus is made up of electrical paths, wires, and sockets that transmit electrical impulses to and from the CPU.

ISA, EISA, VLB, and PCI buses have been around a long time and are all supported in Linux. Recently, the Microchannel bus has received support in the Linux kernel, but it's early code, so it may not work on your system. Most modern systems are PCI-based, with one or two ISA slots for back-compatibility.
