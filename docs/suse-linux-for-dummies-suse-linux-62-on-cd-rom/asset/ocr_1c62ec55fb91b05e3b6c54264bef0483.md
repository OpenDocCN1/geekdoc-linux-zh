Because you will probably be attached to the Internet for relatively short periods of time, your Internet service provider will supply you with machines which send and deliver your e-mail when you are not attached to the Internet. Then when you do attach to the Internet, your computer tells the server to deliver the e-mail or send your messages. You will be given a login name for the mail server (usually the same as your incoming login name), and you will be told the name is for both your incoming and outgoing mail server. These two names are usually the same, but if you are using a special mail service called POP (Post Office Protocol), which allows you to download e-mail to your system from the server, your ISP will give you a name for the POP server. IMAP4 is an even newer protocol for doing procedures similar to POP, and if your ISP has an IMAP4 server, they will tell you that also. In this screen, you can configure your outgoing mail server and your outgoing mail server username. To configure your incoming mail server, read Step 6 that follows.
