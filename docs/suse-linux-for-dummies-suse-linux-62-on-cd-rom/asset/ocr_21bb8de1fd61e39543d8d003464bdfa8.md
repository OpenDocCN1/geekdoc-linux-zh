# Chapter 11: Windows on the World

Another way to resize a window using KDE is to simply grab a window border or a side with your mouse cursor and drag. To grab the window border, move your mouse cursor slowly across either corner of the window on one of the sides near the center of the side. As you move slowly across the window border, the cursor changes. At that point, click but not release the mouse button, and drag the window border to where you want it. Then release the mouse button.

# Making a molehill out of a mountain

If you want to make the window fade into the distance for a while without stopping its program, we have just the thing for you:

+   1. Click the hyphen symbol.
2. Click the Iconify option.
   Oh, where did your program go? Aha. Check the status bar at the top of the screen and you'll find it.
3. To bring that window back, click its icon in the status bar.
   You've not made the window bigger, you've also given it focus.

Now that we had you through the menu, clicking away, do you want to know an easier way to minimize a window? Look at Figure 11-7. See those squares in the title bar? Click the one that is the third from the right (it looks like a dot) and your program is minimized. That dot is called the Minimize button.

# Making a mountain out of a molehill

To minimize your window, click the button that is the second from the right (it looks like a square). The window takes over the entire screen — some-times with breathtaking results, depending on the subject matter. To bring the window back to its original size, you need to use the resize option that you just found out if you read the section titled “Resizing to your heart’s content.” You did not already forget how, did you?
