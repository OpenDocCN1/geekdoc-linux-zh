# Chapter 17: Sources of Help

# Linux HOWTOs

Don't forget about the Linux HOWTOs, which are on the SuSE Linux For Dummies CD-ROM. These excellent guides are covered under the Linux Documentation Project copyleft, which means you can print them. You can also read them with more(1) or less(1). After you have installed SuSE Linux, the HOWTOs can be found in the directory /usr/doc/howto/en/

# School Days

Another source of information on UNIX and Linux is through formal courses, perhaps at a local community college. A lot of colleges have courses on UNIX, and many have started using Linux to teach the UNIX courses. With Linux, you can do a lot of your homework on your system or on your notebook anywhere. (I typed for the first edition of this book in a hotel in Auckland, New Zealand, and updated text for the second edition in the United Airlines lounge in Chicago.) What I would have given in college for the capability to do my computer projects sitting in the comfort of my own pub... dorm room. Instead, I had to sit in a room with a lot of punch-card machines... well, never mind. I would have been a lot more comfortable and productive with a Linux system.

# In the News

You can obtain additional information on the Linux operating system from the Internet. A facility called netnews has tens of thousands of newsgroups, and each newsgroup is about a specific topic. Note more than 30 newsgroups devoted to Linux topics.

Access to reading netnews is usually a service provided by an ISP (Internet service provider). You can use a news reader (such as trn, tin, or pine) or one of several Web browsers (such as the Netscape browser included on your SuSE Linux For Dummies CD-ROM) to read netnews.
