# Chapter 5: Installation: Down to the Final Stretch

+   - Scenario 3. You have one hard drive on your computer with another operating system on the first partition and you want to install Linux on the rest of the hard drive. The “rest of the hard drive” refers to space that is currently unpartitioned. In this case, YaST will recognize that your hard drive contains free unpartition space and asks whether you want to use this space for installing Linux. We recommend that you select Yes in the dialog box and press Enter.

In each of the three cases, YaST partitions the appropriate hard drive (or the appropriate unpartitioned space on the hard drive). YaST also creates a file system on the partitions. The next step in the installation process is to select the software packages to be installed, but you may not be ready to do yet. If YaST successfully completed your drive, you can skip the next section and move on to “Selecting and Installing the Packages.” If your drive is not partitioned successfully, use the next section to find your way through this detailed process.

# Partitioning Your Hard Drive Manually

This section is for those readers who must partition their hard drive(s) manually, or those who are just too curious and want to learn how to do it. We discuss the concepts of partitioning and the procedure of partitioning using YaST. If you have followed our recommendation and have chosen to have YaST partition your hard drive, you can skip over this section.

# Partitioning — the concepts

Partitioning a hard drive is actually quite simple. As most things in life, if you have no idea what you are doing, the process may appear confusing. In this section, we explain the basics of partitioning and the tools that are available to perform basic partitioning tasks. In the next section, we walk you through an example of the steps necessary to partition your hard drive with YaST.

Different people have different partitioning philosophies, and different computers have partitioning needs. Thus, no “correct” way to partition your system exists. You have a range of ways that you may choose to partition a drive. You could install Linux and your personal files all in one large partition, which is what YaST does if you tell it to partition your drive for you. The disadvantage of this option is that when you need to reinstall Linux (such as you get a new version), you have to back up and restore all your data files, because no extra partition is available to store files.
