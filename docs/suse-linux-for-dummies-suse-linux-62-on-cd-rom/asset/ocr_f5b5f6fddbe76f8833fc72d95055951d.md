When people ask me why I think Linux and the Open Source Code development projects are so important, I answer that I am not egotical as to think all good programs come from the United States. I tell them that the next Einstein of computer science might come from Korea, or China, Venezuela, or even Helsinki, Finland, and the world cannot afford to miss the opportunity of finding him or her. I repeated while standing with the president of the University of São Paulo, Brazil, in front of his school’s computer built by Beowulf technology running Linux. He answered my observation with the words, “We, too, considered that.”

I would also like to point out that this is the first operating system in major use today that started outside the United States of America. All other operating systems, although they received support and construction from non-U.S. participants, were conceived and maintained in the United States. Even then, it was an amazing operating system, running with less than 2MB of RAM on Intel systems at amazing speeds. This free system included features found in operating systems costing hundreds of dollars per user:

+   - Multitasking (running more than one job simultaneously)

+   - Multiple simultaneous users

+   - Demanded-paging virtual memory system (using the hard drive for additional temporary storage) to run programs more than the amount of RAM

An estimated 125,000 people were active users in 1994. (Because no licenses are issued for individual users, a more precise number can’t be determined.) In 1997, just three years later, the estimated number of Linux users was 3 million and climbing rapidly. By the fall of 1998, the estimates were more than 7 million.
