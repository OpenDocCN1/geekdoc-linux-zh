In the literature that came with your disk, you should be able to find times for the following:

+   - Moving the head from cylinder to cylinder
- A full seek over the entire surface of the cylinder-to-cylinder seek
- An average seek (average being between the cylinder-to-cylinder seek and moving the disk head completely across the disk)
- One rotation; half this rotation is called the average rotational delay

Normally, in finding out disk capacity (or the average potential through your disk), you add one rotational delay, plus the average seek time, plus the rotational delay for the data to be read under the disk head. Depending on how much data you are reading, this can be up to one full rotational delay. If you need to read more data than that, you have to move the disk head again and reinstate the read, which may mean waiting another average rotational delay before the disk lines up with the data again.

On average, take one-half the rotational delay and one average seek for each read. This is your average access time. Suppose this is 100 milliseconds, or one-tenth of a second. Maybe the system can do 10 reads or writes to the disk, on average (or 600 reads or writes to the disk every minute). If you look at information from successive procinfo commands, you can get a feeling for how many disk I/Os your system is doing as opposed to how many it could be doing.

Remember the numbers reported by procinfo are averages taken over time. If the numbers are close to the maximum, you may have periods in which the disk is trying to answer every request but is overwhelmed by the number of I/Os. The bottom line is that you have to wait for your disks.

Trying to reduce the number of I/Os to any one disk by spreading the load over several disks is important and can sometimes help a slow system — without the need for a CPU upgrade.

# Multiple swap partitions

If your system is paging and swapping heavily you want multiple swap partitions, rather than having just one, and distributing them across your disk drives. This will help alleviate disk contention because your programs will be swapped and paged multi-disk instead of just one. On Intel systems, Linux is limited to 128MB per swap partition and up to 16 swap partitions per system.
