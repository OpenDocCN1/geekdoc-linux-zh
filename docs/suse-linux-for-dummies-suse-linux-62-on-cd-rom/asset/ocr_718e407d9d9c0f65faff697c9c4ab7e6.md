# Becoming a geek

Susan Pink, one of my editors (a darn fine one), hates it when I say things like "fsck your file system." She wants me to say "run the fsck program against the file system." I have gone along with her for the most part, but here is why I tell you that UNIX (and Linux) geeks the following:

Geeks talk about "disks" when Susan would make me say "drive drives," and geeks change the words X, X11, and X Window System, while Susan personally interjects (and every time), "Did you mean to say 'X Window System?'"

I also want to point out that geeks are not nerds. Geeks are people who love technical topics, but also have a wide range of other interests. Geeks are often outgoing, and many (but not all) enjoy being with people.

+   - Eff Check the file system instead of "run ES ES K on the file system

+   - VEYE (rhymes with "EYE") instead of "VEE EYE" when talking about the editor "vi"

I point out that fsck is a noun, verb, and an adjective:

+   - I fsck.

+   - I fscked the file system.

+   - I put the files into the fscked file system.
but this concept makes her shudder.

One of my favorite T-shirts (I have more than 3,000 XL T-shirts) reads:

It hard being a Geek, but it is getting easier.

So, if you really start to know the Linux operating system and want to learn more, you may well be a closet geek. Welcome to the club.

Perhaps you looked at other books before you bought this one and were intimidated by their use of technical terms. For what you wanted to do, and you wanted something task-oriented. You may want to look over them again because your knowledge level will be higher after completing this book. TCP/IP networking, compiler design, operating system theory, formal language theory, computer graphics, and systems administration training are all topics you can study in depth.

Lots of books specific to the UNIX operating system are either partially or completely applicable to Linux, such as books on Perl, a comprehensive interpreter. By getting one (or more) books on Perl and sitting down with your Linux system, you can have both a new tool for doing your work and a new appreciation for a complete programming language. If you want to see how Perl is written, the source code is for you to view.
