# ### 3.8 Changing Your Password

A good password is one that is easy for you to remember but difficult for someone else to figure out. Often the initial passwords assigned by system administrators fail these criteria. It is a good idea to change your password frequently to prevent unauthorized use of your account. If you have not already done so, you should take a minute to think about a password.

+   1.  **Enter the `passwd` command at the shell prompt.** Note that this is `passwd`, not `password`:
+   
+       $ `passwd` (RETURN)

The system will prompt for your old password:

Old password:

+   2.  **Enter your old password, then press (RETURN).** As usual, your password will not appear on the screen:
+   
+       Old password: `████████`

The computer will then ask you to enter your new password:

New password:

+   3.  **Enter your new password, then press (RETURN).** As usual, your password will not appear on the screen:
+   
+       New password: `████████`

To ensure that you have made no mistakes, the computer will ask you to repeat your new password:

Retype new password:

+   4.  **Repeat your new password.** If this is not done exactly as before, the system will not accept the new password, and you will have to start over:
+   
+       Retype new password: `████████`

You will know that the new password has been accepted when the shell prompt appears:

```
$
```
