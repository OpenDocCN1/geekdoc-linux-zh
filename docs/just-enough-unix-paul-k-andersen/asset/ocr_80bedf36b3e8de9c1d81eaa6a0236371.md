# Teletypes and Terminals

When Thompson and Ritchie rewrote UNIX to run on the PDP-11 for the Bell Patent Department, the primary input/output devices were Teletype terminals. These were slow, noisy electromechanical devices that produced their output on rolls of paper.

The Teletype keyboard had the usual typewriter keys for upper- and lowercase letters, numbers, and punctuation. It also had a special \"Control\" key that was used in combination with other keys to perform special functions. (For example, holding down the Control key and typing the letter B caused a bell to ring.)

Although Teletypes are now obsolete, they are not entirely forgotten. The keyboards of newer I/O devices still have a Control key that is used in combination with other keys to perform special functions. In the vocabulary of UNIX, the abbreviation tty (short for Teletype) is still used to mean \"terminal.\" And \"print\" is still used to mean \"display output on a terminal,\" even though most terminals today print to a video screen rather than paper.
