| PROGRAM | USABILITY | INTERFACE | COMMENTS |
|---|---|---|---|
| GIMP | Great | Great | It's the GIMP. It's the best and only advanced graphics program for Linux. Detractors of the program seem to always taint their complaints with regret that the GIMP is not the Photoshop or Illustrator that they are used to. I went through the same thing. When you get past the adjustment and learn a few of the quirks, the GIMP is your friend. This is a great program for screen graphics and Web graphics, but the GIMP is not up to speed for print work yet. |
| KPaint | Good | Good | This is just a simple program to doodle, play, or quickly do something basic with an image. For most basic image functions, the GIMP is still better, despite a complexity of options that might scare away some users. KPaint works well to draw in or give the kids a doodling space that you don't have to clean up later. |
| Digikam | Good | Great | This program is great for managing photo albums on your computer. The developers have done a good job of anticipating the most needed features and using terminology that the average user can understand. Some parts of the program seem new and underdeveloped. As this project matures, the oddities will get worked out. It has a lot of potential. |
| gThumb | Great | Good | Like lots of GNOME programs, gThumb isn't the flashiest or the prettiest, but it is the reliable workhorse program of its class. The program shares many features in common with Digikam, but gThumb feels more solid when you use it. The labels and terms in the program, such as catalogs rather than albums, could be adjusted to be more intuitive. |
