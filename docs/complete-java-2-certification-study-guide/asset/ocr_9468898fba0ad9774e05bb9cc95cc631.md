In our example, the name of the Map (idToPrescriptions) explains both the keys and the values of the Map. It's a good idea to use names that are descriptive as possible. Partially descriptive names, as idMap or prescriptionsMap, are especially irritating to people who have to figure out what code they wrote. The Program Exam doesn't test your skill at choosing names, but it's an essential skill in the professional world, and you can lose points on the Developer Exam for poor naming.

Java's two most important Map classes are java.util.HashMap and java.util.TreeMap. In our discussion of Sets, you will find that "Hash" mean "unpredictable order" and "Tree" mean "natural order." This applies to Maps as well: specifically, to Map keys. A HashMap's keys (delivered by the getKey method) are iterated in unpredictable order. A TreeMap's keys are iterated in natural order.
