# ABOUT THE AUTHORS

Matt Born likes quick, efficient things. Automation? Great. Elegant automation with PowerShell? Even better, he says. Matt began his automation career with Perl and the like in the late 1990s. After many languages across several years, he came to enjoy PowerShell with v1.0 and the VMware VI Toolkit. A couple of years later, in 2009, Matt and his friend AC started the http://vnuggets.blog, which is now a hearty sort of mainly virtualization-focused PowerShell nuggets, where the posts also focus on keeping things as fast as can be. After a few more years, Matt earned the VMware vExpert designation thanks to these blogging efforts and to the VMware Technology Network (VMTN) forum participating. He has vExpert status for four years so far, each year from 2012 to present (2015). Matt continues to find joy in making things faster, stronger, and better. Follow Matt on Twitter (@mtbornen).

Luc Dekens spends many moons in the mainframe world as a system programmer. While the companies he worked for Unix and Windows boxes on, it was a natural evolution for him to expand into those areas. A couple of years ago, Luc was impressed by a new scripting tool, Monad, that Microsoft was bringing to market. When the organization Luc works for was expanding their virtualization platform, he stumbled upon a product called VI Toolkit. It was ideal for automating many administrative tasks. Luc was admitted to the early beta program and started contributing to the ever-growing PowerShell community. After attending VMworld 2009 in San Francisco, he had a session with Hal Rotten and started a blog (http://lucd.info).

Brian Graf has in many different roles in IT for more than 10 years. He has always had a passion for technology and learning. For the past four years, Brian has focused mainly on virtualization and automation. Brian is a multi-vExpert and is currently VCAP5-DCA and DCD certified. Brian graduated with a Masters of Information Systems from the University of Utah. He enjoys taking trips and spending time with his wife and kids. You can follow Brian on Twitter at https://twitter.com/vBrianGraf or on his blog at http://www.vtagion.com.
