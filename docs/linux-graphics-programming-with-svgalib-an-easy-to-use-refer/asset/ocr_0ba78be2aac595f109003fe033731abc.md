# ## What Is A Fractal?

A fractal often appears to be more than the sum of its parts. The components themselves are nothing much—just points on a plane. When viewed on the screen, however, those points seem to have a life of their own.

As you may know, a fractal is created when you plot the points returned by specific equations. Keep in mind the word *specific*—not just any equation can produce a fractal. Whereas the plots of some equations form bell curves and other everyday shapes, a fractal's equation is quite different.

The one thing that all fractals have in common is *self-similarity*. If you were to magnify a fractal, you'd find that any section you view resembles the whole. No matter what level of detail you're able to produce, the same structures will be observed. See Figure 8.2 for a better explanation of this concept.

See how the pattern reproduces itself? This self-similarity, or *recursion* (or even *scale invariance*), is what makes fractals unique.
