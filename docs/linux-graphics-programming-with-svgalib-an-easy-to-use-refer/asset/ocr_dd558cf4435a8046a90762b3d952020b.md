Of course, the simplest polygon is the triangle. Is it any wonder, then, that the threeDKit uses several triangle routines? Keep this in mind; it’ll be important later.

Any good graphics book will tell you that there are three types of polygons. Not wanting to be left out, I’ve included them here for you as well. The three types are convex, nonconvex, and complex. See Figure 11.2 for examples.

Obviously, the purpose of a polygon routine is to create the outline of a specific polygon and then, generally, to fill in it. Some planning may be required; you’ll need to decide whether your polygon can overlap other polygons and, perhaps, whether it can be overlapped itself. This is an important consideration because diagonal lines aren’t clean (that is, smooth) like their horizontal and vertical counterparts; therefore, two or more polygons may vie for the same pixels. But, let’s not worry about that just yet.
