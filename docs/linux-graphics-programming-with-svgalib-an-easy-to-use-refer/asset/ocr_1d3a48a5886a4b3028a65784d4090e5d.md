# # VGA (And SVGA) Cards

Let's start with the basis of it all—the venerable VGA card.

As you probably know, the VGA card is an expansion board that plugs into any standard x86 motherboard. Its purpose is to convert the digital impulses of the computer into analog signals, which the monitor then uses to create images. You might be wondering why the monitor can't simply display the original digital signal—and indeed, a new generation of digital displays is being developed as we speak. I think the conventional VGA setup is a holdover from the early days of personal computing, when second-hand televisions were used in lieu of monitors, and digital-to-analog conversion was necessary to save money.

It's interesting to note that the older OGA and EGA cards did not perform the digital-to- analog conversion. Rather, the monitor had to do this.

As shown in Figure 2.1, VGA cards consist of a DAC (digital-to-analog converter) chip, memory, and often a graphics accelerator. The memory can store at least one screen's worth of information, known as a framebuffer. As memory increases, a greater number of colors becomes possible as well. Finally, the accelerator works to take some of the processing burden off of the computer's CPU. Although this was not a feature on the original IBM cards, just about every modern VGA adapter has one.

# ## VGA

Video Graphics Array. Found in nearly every PC today, the VGA adapter was first released in 1987 for IBM's PS/2 (which, interestingly enough, was also the first Intel-based system to use a 3.5 inch floppy drive). VGA offers numerous advantages over the earlier CGA and EGA chipsets, including higher speed and sharper resolution. The VGA standard has since been upgraded to SVGA, XGA, and UXGA, and cards are now produced by a large number of manufacturers. Despite these enhancements, the VGA family of adapters is still commonly known as VGA.

# ## CGA

Color Graphics Adapter. This was the first graphics standard for the PC. It provided low-resolution text and graphics, and was superseded by EGA.

# ## EGA

Enhanced Graphics Adapter. A medium-resolution adapter, and the precursor to VGA. The first sixteen colors in the VGA palette are still known as the EGA colors.
