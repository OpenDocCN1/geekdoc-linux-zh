This is it! We've finally made it to where the action is. No more boring forays into the wilderness of theory, definitions, and subjective academia. The good stuff is just around the corner.
I remember taking classes that were essentially a waste of time for me because there was an acute lack of practical examples. I find that I learn best when I can apply the lesson immediately to my own life. I also find some sort of laboratory activity helpful. Experts call this constructivism. At the very least, my interest must be maintained. However, if the lesson is heavy in theory and lacking in empirical, practical examples, I soon find myself gazing out the window and drifting into the semiconscious realm of daydreams. Perhaps this is my mind's defense against dullards. Perhaps I lack discipline. Whatever the case may be, I'm a hands-on kind of guy.
Not to be a hypocrite, this chapter cuts right to the chase. We're going to create a variety of simple and forthright SVG/Alib programs to get things rolling. These programs, when combined, will help give you a feel for coding graphics under Linux. You'll find that you can create an almost infinite number of variations on these simple themes by merely swishing colors, swapping functions, and choosing different pixel placements. Although these programs won't make you famous, they will pave the way for the advanced applications that dwell ahead.
