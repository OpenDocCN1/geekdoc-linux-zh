
# Introduction

> The Web is a medium for people who wouldn’t otherwise use computers.

Have you been looking for a simple graphics package for your Linux system? If so, look no further. SVGAlib provides an easy way to create graphical applications and eliminates the complexities and rigmarole of the X Windowing System. Even if you have no more than the most rudimentary grasp of programming in C, you can use SVGAlib.

## What Is SVGAlib?

SVGAlib is a low-level graphics library for Linux. It augments the C programming language, which (in its purest form) doesn’t provide support for graphics. SVGAlib bridges the gap between hardware and software, and allows you to spend your time doing the things that you want: namely, coding programs, and not fretting over complex issues more appropriately suited for video engineers.

In short, SVGAlib is a wonderful tool that allows you to draw all sorts of fantastic images on the screen. You can draw lines, squares, and circles, as well as more complex images of your own design. You’ll have a full range of colors to choose from (as many as your graphics card will allow), and a variety of screen resolutions are available, too. If you can dream it, you can draw it.

Fortunately, SVGAlib is provided with most Linux distributions, so you’ll be able to publish your programs, and circulate them among your friends.

SVGAlib offers numerous advantages to Linux developers, including the following:

- Full-screen graphics
- High-speed rendering (drawing)
- Quick startup/gentle learning curve
- Years of proven experience

## Intended Audience

The beauty of SVGAlib lies in its functionality and ease of use. Users span the gamut from raw beginners to graphics professionals, and this book is designed accordingly. If you lack experience with graphics, or even with programming as a whole, you shouldn’t feel afraid to follow along and learn as you go. You will definitely find the first three chapters to be of special importance.

Conversely, experienced users won’t be ignored either, as later chapters deal with applied knowledge and more advanced issues, such as 3D graphics, interfaces, fonts, and pointing devices like the mouse and joystick. Even if you think you know everything there is to know about graphics, you’ll find this book to be a handy reference.

In summary, if you can use Linux, you can use SVGAlib.

## Will I Need To Know Assembly Language?

Fortunately, no! SVGAlib provides you with a wide selection of powerful and efficient drawing primitives that interact with your hardware entirely behind the scenes. This arrangement eliminates the need for assembly language, which allows you to work solely with easy-to-use, high-level C/C++ functions. In turn, you’ll find the coding process to be much easier, as changes can be implemented in a matter of seconds, and your end result becomes infinitely more portable, too.

Thus, even an inexperienced programmer can quickly learn to doodle on the screen, and seasoned designers can rapidly create superb graphical masterpieces.

It’s recognized that many people will eventually want to learn what makes SVGAlib work. Accordingly, the complete source code is always available.

## Code Examples

Each chapter is filled with numerous code examples designed to help you with your programming endeavors. The majority of the programs aren’t finished products like you’d find in a store, but rather simplistic code, or even snippets, which highlight a particular function or procedure. In this way, you can learn the many aspects of SVGAlib without having to wade through pages and pages of extraneous material.

After reading each chapter, you should be able to join the many disparate elements together to form a complete application of your own design.

The code for every example is provided on the accompanying CD-ROM, and you’re welcome to copy, embellish, improve, and redistribute the material as you please.

## Chapter Summary

Chapters are designed to progress in an order of increasing skill level, and the majority rely on the knowledge presented in previous examples. However, you're welcome to skip forward if you're interested in a particular subject, and if you think you can handle it out of sequence. That said, here's a brief rundown of what you can expect to find in the pages ahead:

Chapter 1, *Getting Started*, introduces SVGAlib and covers the process of compilation and installation. You'll also learn what makes SVGAlib necessary, and how it connects with your programs.

Chapter 2, *Graphics Basics*, teaches you the general graphics theory that you'll need to know for the upcoming chapters. Topics discussed include screen modes and modern video hardware.

Chapter 3, *First Programs*, is designed to help you write and compile your first programs using SVGAlib. Common program layout, essential functions, basic drawing primitives, and compilation are all covered extensively.

Chapter 4, *Complex Shapes, Formulas, And Functions*, demonstrates how to develop and implement shape drawing functions that SVGAlib currently lacks. Circles are covered in detail, as are other useful geometric designs.

Chapter 5, *Color*, explains the theory behind color usage in the VGA card. Of course, numerous code examples are presented to help you learn about palettes and RGB color.

Chapter 6, *Shades, Fills, And Patterns*, covers special techniques such as screen fades, antialiasing, and providing graphics at boot time.

Chapter 7, *Fonts*, introduces you to the world of graphical text. Here you'll find information on creating your own fonts, as well as utilizing existing ones.

Chapter 8, *Fractals*, gives you the rundown on this esoteric art. You'll find plenty of theory, followed by actual examples.

Chapter 9, *Graphic Files*, explains how to easily read and display the images encoded in files such as GIFs and JPGs. You'll also learn how to make screen shots, and how to save the resultant data into a new file.

Chapter 10, *Basic Animation*, gets you started with moving shapes and images.

Chapter 11, *Polygons*, discusses that vital element of 3D images, the polygon. Triangles, as well as SVGAlib's threeDKit, are covered in detail.

Chapter 12, *3D Images*, shows how to render an image with depth and shadow.

Chapter 13, *Raytracing And Photorealism*, explains how the experts apply a photographic touch to make images appear lifelike.

Chapter 14, *Game Basics*, returns to the world of fractals to produce stunning renditions of natural terrain, clouds, and even planets, that can all be used as backdrops in programs of your own design.

Chapter 15, *Using The Mouse And Joystick*, tells you everything you need to know about including pointing devices in your own code. It’s much simpler than you might expect!

Chapter 16, *Landscapes*, finally jumps in and combines the elements of previous chapters to get you started with game programming. This is one of the major applications of graphics, and is something you won’t want to miss.

Chapter 17, *A Look At Existing SVGAlib Applications*, examines what others have done to bring you entertainment by way of SVGAlib. Numerous games are presented, as well as information on porting new games to Linux/SVGAlib.

Chapter 18, *Simple Paint Program*, combines the knowledge gained thus far to create a general purpose painting/drawing/image manipulation program with a professional look and feel.

Chapter 19, *Thoughts On A GUI*, gives examples of how a graphical user interface, today’s trendy topic, might be constructed with SVGAlib.

Chapter 20, *Video Card Drivers*, finally gives some insight on how driver code is written for new video cards.

Appendix A, *GNU General Public License*, gives you a reference as it applies to the software this book was written about. However, the GPL does not apply to the text of this book.

Appendix B, *libvga Functions*, gives a complete list of every function contained in libvga.so.1.4.0.

Appendix C, *libvga Functions*, complements Appendix B by providing a complete list of the functions present in libvga.so.1.4.0.

Appendix D, *ASCII Character Codes*, gives you a handy reference table for all 256 ASCII characters.

Appendix E, *Chipsets Supported By SVGAlib*, give a complete list of the video chipsets presently supported by SVGAlib, as well as information on the generic VESA driver.

Appendix F, *A Brief History Of SVGAlib*, shows the evolution as SVGAlib, and the people responsible for making it what it is today.

Appendix G, *FAQs And Troubleshooting*, covers common problems experienced by beginners, and provides the appropriate resolution.
