+   ◆ Price—Like Linux, it’s free. You can download it, use it, and redistribute it under the terms of the GNU Public License.
+   ◆ Functionality—It is truly platform-independent. A desktop running on a Linux machine may be displayed on a PC, a Solaris machine, or any number of other architectures.

Finally, users on MS Windows machines will be able to play Sasteroids! Visit the VNC Web site at www.uk.research.att.com/vnc/ for complete details.

# GGI

Finally, I suppose a word about GGI is in order. GGI stands for General Graphics Interface, and is billed as being a "reliable, stable and fast graphics system that works everywhere." Sounds great, right? Unfortunately, it’s nowhere near completion, and it still requires libraries such as SVGAIB to work.

One of GGI’s goals is to integrate graphics with the Linux kernel, and they did get their fbeon functions added recently. However, GGI isn’t all grins and giggles—Michael Weller (former maintainer of SVGAIB) explains below.

> #### Michael Weller On On GGI
> I liked this idea so much [kernel graphics], I even started coding a frame buffer device. After a short time, other people came out with the GGI idea. Right from their beginning, they claimed to be the only source of wisdom. I tried to join our efforts, but failed. In general, we have the same goals (read the GGI project pages for that).
> Anyhow, at that time a flame war started. I don’t really know why, I don’t see I did anything else than offering my opinions, work and experience. But that should be judged by others.
> Well, after some time I stopped bothering them. I was satisfied to learn later though that they actually came up with some conclusions I proposed first, but weeks or months later. But let us leave the past alone.

In my opinion, the problem with the X and GGI fanatics is their arrogance. Sure, SVGAIB has its drawbacks (along with everything else), but it also has many advantages—advantages that you can’t get with the other systems. Furthermore, Linux is supposed to be about freedom and choices, right? Many of us began using Linux because we didn’t like MS Windows, so what’s the sense in re-creating that environment here?

# Uninstalling

Finally, should you ever want or need to, you can remove SVGAIB from your system by typing make uninstall. You’ll have to remove the man pages and the /etc/vga files by hand, though.
