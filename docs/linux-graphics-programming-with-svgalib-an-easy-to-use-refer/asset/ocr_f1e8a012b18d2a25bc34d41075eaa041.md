# Refresh Rate

The number of times per second your monitor is reenergized. Your screen is constantly losing its charge and must therefore be refreshed. Measured in hertz, display systems typically range from 56Hz to well over 100Hz. A minimum of 70Hz is recommended to help prevent eye strain.

# Compilation

Now you're actually ready to build SVGAlib. Type **make install**, and the binaries will compile and copy to the appropriate directories, as defined in **Makefile.cfg**.

Assuming all went well, you can complete your installation with **make demoprogs**. This will compile several demonstration programs, including **vgtest**, which will be placed in the **/demos** and **/threeDKit** subdirectories.

> **Note**
> 
> You *must* compile and install as root. If you don't, nothing bad will happen during compilation, but certain tools will fail to become **setuid** root and won't work properly as a result.

# Printing The Man Pages

If you'd like to have a clean version of the *man* pages to print, simply run **make ascii** in the **/doc** subdirectory. This can be done at any time, because it isn't directly related to your compilation and installation process. The resultant three files will be named *svgalib.ascii*, *vgagl.ascii*, and *3d.ascii*.

# Setting Up libvga.config

The final step in configuring SVGAlib for your system is to edit **/etc/vga/libvga.config**. It may look intimidating, but all you need to do for now is define your monitor's settings, your chipset (that is, VGA card), and what kind of mouse you have (if any). If you don't know your monitor's refresh rate, you can usually get this setting from the manufacturer's Web site. Failing that, you can try some of the sample listings.

# Horizontal Sync

The number of lines illuminated on a video screen in one second. For example, a resolution of 400 lines refreshed 60 times per second requires a scan rate of 24kHz plus overhead (time to bring the beam back to the beginning of the next line). This is the same as the horizontal sync frequency in your TV.
