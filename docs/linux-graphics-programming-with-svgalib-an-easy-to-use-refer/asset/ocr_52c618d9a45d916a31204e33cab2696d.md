# Chapter 4
Complex Shapes, Formulas, And Functions

+   * *Key Topics:*
+   	* Using math to create shapes
+   	* Developing efficient shape functions
+   	* Augmenting vgaI

In Chapter 3, I demonstrated the most commonly employed “out-of-the-box” SVGAIIb functions for generating shapes. Although convenient, these functions have a tendency to be rather limited in their scope. This is readily apparent if you want anything drawn other than lines, boxes, and circles. In fact, these basic functions could be more accurately described as being “in the box.” Generic and simple, they’re the graphics equivalent of fast food. They’re fine for messing around with, but you wouldn’t want to take them home to meet Mom and Dad.

To augment the basic flavors of SVGAIIb, I suggested “kit-bashing” to create some of your own functions. (*Kit-bashing* is a hobbyist’s term that refers to the practice of taking pieces from various model kits to create something new.) This term could also be applied to *book_background*(), the simplistic “function of functions” I created as an example to color the background. Functions such as these work, but they’re slow and a bit clunky. Furthermore, they can’t do anything that a bunch of *vga_drawline*() or *gl_fillbox*() statements couldn’t accomplish on their own. What would you do, for example, if you wanted to draw a partial circle (i.e., an arc)?

Some people have created their own libraries to supplement SVGAIIb, and we’ll look into those later on. For now, though, I want you to be able to create your *own* functions. Learning to do so will be a great exercise, and even better, it’s not that hard.
