You might be thinking that I've made a mistake here; everyone knows that capital A is ASCII character 65, not 66. But, think carefully. ASCII codes range from 0 to 255, not 1 to 256. Therefore, capital A is, in fact, the 66th character, not the 65th.

Of course, you don't need to make your characters match up with the keyboard. You could have a drawing of a $b$ show up every time someone presses the A key. You can also create *wingsdings* (characters that are actually graphical drawings). You can even make a font of space invaders, as shown in Figure 7.7. The possibilities are virtually limitless.

But how does one know which font strips to use? Surely not by trial and error. Fortunately, I've listed them all for you. Figures 7.8 through 7.263 show all 256 possibilities. The strips themselves may seem to be arranged randomly, but they're actually ordered in a binary sequence. Binary numbers go in an ordered fashion: 0, 1, 10, 11, 100, 101, 110, 111, and so on, and the font strips work in exactly the same manner. Take a look and you'll see what I mean.
