# Chapter 12
3D Images

+   *Key Topics:*
+   * *Theory*
+   * *Creating 3D objects*
+   * *Texture mapping*

To be taken seriously in today's video-centric world, the visual programmer needs to be proficient in 3D graphics. Simply put, there's no other way to go. One could be well versed—yes, even commercially successful—in several other visual disciplines, but this is the one field that separates the men from the boys (figuratively speaking, of course).

New media companies such as Pixar get all the press, and that's not likely to change anytime soon. Although it's true that anything involving Steve Jobs (a founder of Pixar) is based on a certain amount of hype, there's an element of prophecy in what's being done. 2D is out of fashion, and 3D is in. This development is a bit unfair to those who are skilled in "traditional" computer graphics—a field that still has plenty of valid applications—but it also stands as an opportunity to excel in a genre that hasn't yet seen all its players.

Hollywood has long since used computer-generated graphic effects to wow audiences. Tron was an early example, and some productions—the movie Toy Story comes to mind—are now being made entirely with 3D animation. This futuristic trend isn't restricted to the cinema, either—just turn on the tube for a demonstration. Modern television's repertoire is chock-full of dazzling 3D displays. The news, weather, and even sports have all sorts of electronic wizardry designed to jazz up the dull spots. Flashy visuals have progressed to the point where the same old stuff shown night after night can be made to appear fresh and exciting. Even a genre as trivial as commercials now relies heavily on 3D techniques.
