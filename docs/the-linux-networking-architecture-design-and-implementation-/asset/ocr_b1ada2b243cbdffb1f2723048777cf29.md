This book deals mainly with the protocols of the Internet world. One and assumes that the Internet reference model would best describe the structure of the Linux-work architecture. Interestingly, the classification of the ISO/OSI reference model matches the structure of communication systems in local area networks much better. When taking a closer look at the IEEE 802 standards for local area networks, which are actually used in the Internet, and their implementation in the Linux kernel, we can clearly recognize the structuring of the ISO/OSI model.

# 6.1.1 IEEE Standard for Local Area Networks (LANs)

With its 802.x standards, the IEEE (Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers) found a veritable proliferation for local area networks (LANs). The best known LAN technologies are 802.3 (CSMA/CD), 802.5 (Token Ring), and 802.11 (wireless LANs). Figure 6–1 gives a rough overview of the 802.x standards and classifications within them the ISO/OSI layer model. As mentioned above, the data-link layer is divided into a logical-link control (LLC) and a media-access control (MAC) layer for networks with jointly used media. The LLC layer handles all media-specific differences and should provide a uniform interface for protocols to the higher layers; the MAC layer reflects the differences between different transmission technologies.
