+   * **Nonstandard serial ports** Several products are available that provide several serial ports on a single card and interrupt. Linux supports these products through options available when you enable the Nonstandard serial port support feature in the Character devices kernel configuration menu. Note that you must select the specific product you’re using in order to get support for that product.

As a general rule, a nonstandard serial port is a better choice than an extended dumb serial port when you need more than three or four serial ports. These nonstandard serial port cards were designed with an eye toward supporting multiple devices, whereas the extended dumb cards are extensions to standards that weren’t designed with large numbers of ports in mind.

# ## ISA and PCI Cards

As with many other expansion cards, when you add a serial or parallel port card, you must choose between the earlier Industry Standard Architecture (ISA) bus and the later Peripheral Component Interconnect (PCI) bus. None of the serial or parallel busses discussed in this chapter strain the limits of the ISA bus, so you’ll see no performance gain in your bus accesses if you choose the PCI bus.
