# Save Your Selections

When you make a complicated selection, chances that you will want to use it again. You can save any selection you create to an alpha channel which this saves the selection's shape and transparency. You can then use the selection again at any time by activating the channel. For details on activating a channel and using a saved selection, turn to page 199 in Chapter 13, *Channels and Layer Masks*

![](img/5164bc49fb24f10213e18cc994b7b40c_101_0.png)

**Figure 70.** You can use channels to save any selection you make. In this figure the middle and right shells are selected.

# To save a selection to a channel:

1. Create the selection you want to save using the selection tool of your choice (**Figure 70**).
2. Right-click on the image and choose Select→Save To Channel (**Figure 71**). The selection will be saved to a channel. It will automatically be named “Selection Mask copy”.

**Tip**

To see the selection you have saved to a channel, right-click on the image, choose Layers→Layers & Channels (**Figure 72**) to open the Layers & Channels dialog box and then click the Channels tab to display that tab. The saved selection will be at the bottom of the channel list on the Channels tab (**Figure 73**).

# Making Border Selections

With GIMP, it's easy to create a border selection. This can be very handy when creating a vignette from a photograph, for instance, or adding an outline.

![](img/5164bc49fb24f10213e18cc994b7b40c_102_0.png)

To make a border selection:

1. Create the selection that will become the inside boundary of the border (Figure 74).
2. Right-click on the image and choose Select→ from the pop-up menu (Figure 75). The Border Selection dialog box will appear (Figure 76).
3. Set the width of the border selection by entering the number of pixels in the Border selection by text box.
4. Click OK. The selection box will close and the outside boundary will appear the original (Figure 77).

Figure 75. Choose Select→ from the pop-up menu.

# Deleting an Area

When you delete a selection, the portion within the selection is removed. If the image has only one layer, the background becomes transparent (Figure 78). If the image contains more than one layer, the background remains.

To delete selected area:

Right-click on the image and choose Clear from the pop-up menu (Figure 79) or press Ctrl+K on the keyboard.

**Tips**

- If you start a new selection, the current selection will automatically deactivate.
- For information about alpha channels, turn to page 94.
- To learn what they are used for, turn to page 91.

# Deactivating and Restoring a Selection

It's easy to deactivate and restore selections with a few clicks of the mouse or key presses.

# To deactivate a selection:

Right-click on the image and choose Select→None from the pop-up menu (Figure 82) or press Shift+A on the keyboard.

or Select the Rectangular, Elliptical, or Free-hand Selection Tools from the GIMP Toolbox and click on the image.

# Removing a Selection Before You Finish It

If you are making a selection and realize you didn't want to create it, there's a way out. Before releasing the left mouse button (which is pressed when dragging a selection boundary), press and hold down the right mouse button. Release, the left mouse button and then the right mouse button. The selection you were will disappear.
