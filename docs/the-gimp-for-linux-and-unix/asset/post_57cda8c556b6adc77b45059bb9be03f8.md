## Changing the Stacking Order

The layers shown on the Layers tab page in the Layers & Channels dialog box are positioned one on top of the other in a stacking order. The uppermost layer in the layer window is the layer on top in the image window (Figure 27). The lowermost layer (usually the background layer) is on the bottom. You can change how the layers are stacked, moving them up or down the pile. (The background layer always remains on the bottom.)

![](img/5164bc49fb24f10213e18cc994b7b40c_117_0.png)

### To move a layer in the stacking order:

- 1. Open the Layers & Channels dialog box by right clicking on the image and choosing Layers→Channels from the pop-up menu (Figure 3). The layer will appear in the image window stacked from top to bottom. Notice how this stacking corresponds to the overlapping layers in the image window (Figure 27).
- 2. Highlight the layer you want to move up in the stacking order (Figure 27).
- 3. Click the Raise Layer button at the bottom left of the dialog box.

Right-click on the layer's name and choose Raise Layer from the pop-up menu (Figure 28).

Press Ctrl+F on the keyboard.
The layer will move up the list and move forward in the image window (Figure 29).

## Merging and Flattening Layers

Merging and flattening layers perform essentially the same thing, but with one very important difference. When layers are merged, only the ones that are visible (with the eye icon showing in the Layers & Channels dialog box) are combined into one layer. When layers are flattened, all visible and invisible are combined into one layer.

To merge all visible layers:

- Right-click on the image and choose Layers→Merge Visible Layers from the pop-up menu (Figure 32).

or

- In the Layers & Channels dialog box, right-click in the layer window and choose Merge Visible Layers from the pop-up menu (Figure 33).

or

- Press Ctrl+M on the keyboard.

The Layer Merge Options dialog box will appear (Figure 34).

## The Pencil and Paintbrush Tools

The Pencil and Paintbrush Tools work much like a pencil on paper or on canvas. Just press the left mouse button and drag to draw a line. Unlike an ordinary pencil or brush, though they have a few features: you can easily draw straight lines, and change the properties of the line that you will draw—shape, thickness, spacing, and opacity (Figure 2).

Figure 2. Various strokes with the Pencil and Paintbrush Tools: ① Circle fuzzy (19) brush, 100% opacity; ② Circle (15) brush, 35% opacity; ③ Circle (11) brush, 50% opacity, spacing set to 130; ④ Circle fuzzy (19) brush, 100% opacity; ⑤ Circle fuzzy (17) brush, 100% opacity, spacing set to 50; ⑥ Circle fuzzy (17) brush, out set to 70. Notice that strokes ⑦ and ⑧ are made with the same brush but different tools. No matter what you select, a Pencil Tool stroke will always have a sharp edge.

## To draw a line with the Pencil or Paintbrush Tool:

- 1. Use the Layers & Channels dialog box to select the layer on which you would like to draw. (For directions on how to do this, turn to page 92).
- 2. Choose a foreground color. (For details on how to do this, turn to pages 42–45.)
- 3. Select the Pencil Tool, click the Pencil Tool in the Toolbox or press Shift+P on the keyboard. The mouse pointer will change to a tiny pencil.

![](img/5164bc49fb24f10213e18cc994b7b40c_123_0.png)

or Select the Paintbrush Tool, click the Paintbrush button in the Toolbox or press P on the keyboard. The mouse pointer will change to a tiny pencil.

- 4. In the image window, position the mouse pointer where you would like to start the line.
- 5. Press the left mouse button and drag. A line will appear as you drag (Figure 3).
- 6. When you finished drawing, release the left mouse button (Figure 4).
