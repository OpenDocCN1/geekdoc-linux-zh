# ## Vector- and Pixel-Based Imaging Programs

Computer imaging programs are either vector based or bitmap based.

Figure 1. This vector image looks the same whether viewed close or far away.

In vector-based programs, such as CoreDraw and Adobe Illustrator, objects are created using drawing tools that make shapes defined by mathematical formulas. A vector object appears on the screen with smooth lines and continuous colors, whether you are looking at it from far away or close up (**Figure 1**). Vector drawings are *resolution-independent*, meaning that the printed quality depends only on the resolution of the printer.

In bitmap-based programs, such as the GIMP, Corel PhotoPaint, and Adobe Photoshop, images are made of tiny dots called *pixels* that are arranged and colored to form a pattern. The shape and color of a pixel image appear smooth from a distance, but if you were to view a pixel image close up, you would see tiny individual squares (**Figure 2**). Pixel images depend on the resolution at which they are saved for printquality.
