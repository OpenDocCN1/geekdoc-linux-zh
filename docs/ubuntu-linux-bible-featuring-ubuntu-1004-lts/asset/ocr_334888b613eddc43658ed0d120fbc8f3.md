# AisleRiot Solitaire

Backbone Solitaire since I was a kid and somehow thought that there was only one Solitaire game—that being, of the course, that I'd learned to play, which turned out to be Klondike. I became more enlightened when my wife asked me what other versions of Solitaire were available. A quick Internet search revealed that there were, indeed, a few zillion Solitaire games, so I bought her an analog storage device that came preloaded with information about Solitaire—that is, a book on the subject.

I was similarly enlightened when I suggested she play AisleRiot Solitaire, which sports a game of Klondike and is installed on her Ubuntu-based laptop. Unlike me, she read the online help, which immediately told her AisleRiot Solitaire actually provides 81 different Solitaire games. (Ironically, this was a higher number than the ones discussed in the book I bought.)

If you're a aforementioned who knows more than one game, you'll be happy to know that AisleRiot Solitaire provides the following games: Agnes, Athena, Auld Lang Syne, Anthony, Backbone, Bakerszen, Bakers Game, Belegased Castle, Block Ten, Bristol, Camel, Camelfield, Carpet, Chessboard, Clock, Cover, Cruel, Diamond Mine, Doublets, Eagle Wing, Eashaven, Eight Off, Escalator, First Law, Fortress, Fortunes, Fourteen, Free, Gords, Glenwood, Gypsy, Helsinki, Hotscotch, Iapsoch, Jamestown, Jumbo, Kansas, King Albert, King's Audience, Klondike, Ruby Lady, Jane, Monte Carlo, Neighbor, Odessa, Osmosis, Peek, Pike, Plat, Poker, Quartz, Royal, Saratoga, Scorpio, Scuffle, Seahaven, Sir Tommy, Spider, One Suiter, Two Suiter, Spiderette, Straight Up, Streets and Alley, Ten Cross, Thieves, Thumb and Touch, Triple Peaks, Union Square, Valentine, Westhaven, Whitehead, Will o' Wisp, Yield, Yukon, and Zebra.

If you're like me and just love Klondike, it's in there, and there's a great implementation of the classic game.

# Tali

Tali is an online version of a deck game that emulates a card game (pocket). If you're familiar with a popular analog game called Yaht from Hasbro, Inc., Tali may not be a shock to you or may instead find it familiar. By default, Tali is configured to enable one player (you) to take on four computer-generated opponents, but you can easily configure your preferences to support multiple players and reduce or eliminate the computer-generated opponents. I especially like the default prompt at the bottom of the screen when it's my turn: "Human!—You're up." Yes, Mr. Borg, sir!

# Board Games

Board games have always been quite popular and don't have the slight connotation that some people attach to card games. Although some of the games in this section are Linux implementations of existing computer games, I define a board game as any game that uses a grid or could conceivably be played on a board.
