The top field of this dialog is a dropdown that enables you to identify which of your Empathy accounts is associated with the new contact. The instant messaging service (and associated protocol) must be the same as used by the person that you want to contact. The identifier field is the name of your instant messaging account, and the Alias field enables you to specify a more memorable name for your contact. Any alias that you set will be the name that is shown for that contact in the “Contact List” dialog. For example, if you have problems remembering that the contact name for a college friend is robotlander66, you can enter your friend's real name as an alias, and you'll have an easier time distinguishing them from all your other contacts who have instant messaging accounts with the string robotoverland. If you don't specify an alias, Empathy will set the Alias to be the same as the Identifier for the account.

Once you enter the name of your new contact, the dialog shown in Figure 10-11 expands to look like the dialog shown in Figure 10-12.

This expanded dialog lets you specify which (if any) of the groups in your Contact List that the new contact will be added to. Groups are essentially section headings in the contact window that can be expanded or collapsed, making it easy for you to organize your contacts. You can also define a new group by entering its name in the text above the group list and clicking “Add Group.” Being able to define your groups makes it easy for you, like “Work Friends,” “Poker Night,” and “Family,” or. to organize your contacts into logical sets. On the other hand, you may not want to add someone whom you rarely chat to a specific group, which will mean that their Identifier or Alias will not be displayed in the “Contact List” dialog. As described earlier, you can still initiate a conversation with unlisted contacts by using the “Contact List” dialog’s Chat New Conversation menu command and manually entering the name of the contact that you want to converse with.
