Once you've added a number of slides to your presentation, you should save it using the File Save command. If this is a new presentation, you will be prompted to supply a name for the presentation.

After saving your presentation, you will probably want to see what it looks like so far. To preview your actual presentation, select View -> Slide Show or press the F5 key on your keyboard. Your presentation displays on your screen, using any transition effects and timing that you specified when you created the presentation.

# Tip

When you view your presentation, the preview starts at the first slide selected in the Preview pane. To see your entire presentation, select the first slide in the presentation before requesting a preview. ■

# Overview of Digital Graphics Terminology

If you're not a graphics artist (and I certainly am not one), everyone with a web site or a digital camera has some familiarity with digital graphics. Terminology-wise graphics is basically nothing more than storing images in files on your computer, regardless of whether these files contain pictures from your summer, fancy banners or other images that you are using on your website, artwork and drawings that you've created using graphics software, or that you've used to capture, images directly from your computer screen, or just about anything else that you can store in a file and display in a graphics application.

The following provides a quick glossary of graphics terms that you may encounter as you start to work with digital graphics:

- Anti-aliasing: A technique used to reduce the jagged appearance of the edges of objects and text in graphical images. Anti-aliasing softens these edges by blending them with the colors or areas that appear beside them.
- Bitmap: A graphical image that is composed of multiple picture elements known as pixels, each representing a bit of that image. Each image has its own value, generally expressed as a combination of red, green, and blue (RGB) color values. Bitmaps are one of the two main formats for digital images—the other being vector graphics. They are also referred to as raster images.
- BMP: A graphics format originally developed by Microsoft that supports a variety of resolutions and depths. A BMP format contains a single bitmap.
- CMYK: A color definition model that identifies the colors of Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, and Black in a given color. CMYK color designations are commonly used in printing because these are the four traditional ink colors used in commercial printing. Cyan, magenta, and yellow are referred to as subtractive primary colors because they are based on reflected light and thus begin with white light and subtract wavelengths to display specific colors. In the commercial (paper) print process, a separate printing plate is made for each of the CMYK colors.
