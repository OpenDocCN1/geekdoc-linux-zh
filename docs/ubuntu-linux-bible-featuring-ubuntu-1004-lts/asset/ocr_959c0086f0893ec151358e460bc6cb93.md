# Chapter 18: Consumer Electronics and Ubuntu

You can't walk the street without seeing people who are listening to music using a digital audio player. Zillions of companies make them, the best known of which is Apple Computer—which could conceivably be just a footnote today it was not for Mac OS X and the runaway success of its iPod digital audio player and attendant iTunes and music store. The iPod is a great example of what happens when people who understand industrial design meet software and usability. In many households, the iPod has replaced the automobile as the device most likely to be buffed, polished, and accessorized.

If you use a digital audio player other than an iPod, free applications that are available on Ubuntu systems provide complete support for downloading and managing the music on your device. However, if you're an iPod user (as I am), there is one big caveat. The iTunes application, which is the default from Apple Computer that is used to manage music on an iPod, is not available for Linux. The applications discussed in this section are an excellent replacement for iTunes as managing music on an iPod—but they don't enable you to buy music through iTunes. You can download MP3 from online sources that aren't tailored to a specific device, or purchase CDs and rip them. Once you have MP3 files, you can play them on your desktop or put them on your digital audio player (including an iPod) using the applications discussed in the next sections.

# ID3 Tag Editing on Ubuntu

As discussed in Chapter 16, most of the popular audio file formats like MP3 and OGG support ID3 tags to provide information about file contents such as the artist, album title, title date recorded, and on. All digital audio players can extract and display this information from supported audio file formats, and enable it to sort, search, and organize the files on a digital audio player. Before transferring audio files to your audio player, you should verify that they have valid ID3 tags, or it may be hard to figure out exactly who performs Track03.mp3. You can easily verify the ID3 information by simply playing your audio files in a graphical application such as Rhythmbox or XMMS, because these applications automatically display ID3 information as each audio player does.

Most CD-ripping applications will automatically fill out ID3 information during the ripping process, but you may occasionally encounter a CD that is not in the Internet CD databases, and for which you will therefore have to manually supply ID3 information. The Ubuntu repositories provide several applications for filling out or updating ID3 and ID3v2 tags. I have used id3, id3v2, and id3tool, all of which are command-line utilities, and EasyTAG, a great, graphical ID3 tag editor.
