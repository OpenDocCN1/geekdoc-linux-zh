You can rename a playlist at any time by clicking its name in the left pane and entering a new name, or you can delete it and start over by selecting Edit ⤳⤳ Selected playlist or right-clicking on the playlist and selecting "Delete but keep tracks" from the context-sensitive menu that displays. To remove a track without removing the associated audio file from your iPod, select the "Delete From Playlist" command. To remove the selected track from both the playlist and the iPod, select the "Delete From iPod" command.

You can manipulate existing playlists in several ways. For example, you can re-sort the playlist in different ways by clicking on any of the headings in the track-listing pane at the bottom of the gtkpod application. You can add tracks to an existing playlist by navigating to them and dragging them from the track-listing pane to the name of the playlist in the left pane. The gtkpod application provides a tremendous amount of control over playlists, making it easy for you to create playlists that enable you to hear what you want, when you want, in the order that you want.

# Writing Changes to your iPod

Once you've finished adding music to your iPod, creating and editing playlists, and so on, you will certainly want to save all of those changes to your iPod. Although files and directories of audio files, new playlists, and new items are added to your iPod at the time you create them, the database that your iPod uses to track these new and modified items is not updated until you explicitly synchronize gtkpod with your iPod. You should always synchronize gtkpod with your iPod after making any changes to ensure that those changes are not lost or saved in incomplete, intermediate state. After all, updating playlists or adding new music to your iPod doesn't do much if you can't access the new information!

To synchronize this information, click the "Save Changes" in the gtkpod toolbar. A dialog displays the information is synchronized. This dialog closes, it is safe to disconnect your iPod from your Ubuntu system, as described in the next section.

# Safely Disconnecting your iPod

Simply writing your changes to your iPod and exiting from gtkpod doesn't disconnect your iPod from your Ubuntu Linux system. Like any storage device on a Linux system, an iPod is mounted as part of your filesystem as part of the standard directory hierarchy. By default, iPods are mounted on the directory /media/ipod when you attach them to your system. If it is mounted as part of your filesystem, an iPod's screen will display an international No symbol and a "Do not disconnect" message. To safely disconnect an iPod from the filesystem after using gtkpod, you must first disconnect it to guarantee that all disk updates have been saved and that the iPod's filesystem is marked up to date and clean.
