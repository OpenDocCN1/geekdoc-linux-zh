# Chapter 10: Sending and Receiving Instant Messages

channel IRC is a client/server system, in which users connect to an IRC server in order to join an IRC channel that is supported by that server. While IRC enables user-to-user communications, it is primarily a user-to-channel message system reminiscent of chat rooms on bulletin-board systems. IRC was born on UNIX systems (big surprise), but clients quickly appeared for the Macintosh, Microsoft Windows, and DOS environments.

Regardless of how well it fits the IM model, IRC is still popular, quite fine, and occasionally useful. If you're using the standard GNOME-based Ubuntu Desktop, you can communicate via IRC using Empathy or Pidgin. What most people think about instant messaging first appeared in 1996, when Mirabilis introduced its IC (“I See You”) instant messaging software, which is still popular today (although now owned by Time Warner/AOL). IRC is a client/server instant messaging system, where desktop users use IRC client software to exchange messages with other users through centralized IRC servers. This is the same model used by most instant messaging software.

ICQ was amazing when it first appeared and is still quite cool, although certain aspects of it show its age. ICQ users are primarily identified by the Identification Number (UIN), which is a bit klunky nowadays. When was the last time you thought to say, “Wow, I got to tell 48121458 about this?” Although newer versions of ICQ allow identification by your e-mail address, most modern instant messaging systems let you choose your own username, which must be unique but can at be reminiscent of your name or how you want yourself to be perceived online. My usernames are usually something reminiscent of my name, but identities such as topdog and longstackoverflow are more attractive to others. Like any good technology, it's all up to you.

Most instant messaging applications provide core features such as the following:

+   - **Instant messages:** Exchange text messages with a friend who is online via a computer or cell phone.
- **Chat:** Have an electronic conversation with multiple people at the same time.
- **Exchange files:** Send audio, video, and other files to one or more contacts.

Of the most modern clients like Empathy also provide support for Voice over IP (VoIP) and video chat.

Instant messaging is a lot of fun, and can even be useful in business communications. Many companies rely on instant messaging for situations where instant contact is important or simply to save you from having to walk over someone else's desk and physically interrupting them (which can be tricky if you're in different parts of the country or the world).
