# Part II: Ubuntu for Desktop Users

The next two sections explain how to work with different types of digital audio players on your Ubuntu systems. The first section discusses the standard audio playback and management application used on Ubuntu (Rhythmbox) with iPods and other types of digital audio players. The following sections gtkpod, the GTK application for working with iPods I've used for years, and highlights some specialized applications that are tailored for other types of digital audio players and that are available in the Ubuntu repositories.

# Digital Audio Players and Rhythmbox on Ubuntu

Attaching an iPod or other digital audio player to your Ubuntu system with USB or FireWire cable displays a dialog like the one shown in Figure 18-1 near the beginning of this chapter. The "Open Rhythmbox Music Player" option is selected by default. Click OK to proceed.

When the Rhythmbox Music Player opens (you may have to click its icon in the Notification Area in the top GNOME panel to actually display it on screen), its left pane will contain a highlighted icon for your digital audio player in the Devices section, and the right pane will contain a list of all the audio tracks that it contains. You can click on the arrow to the right of the menu item in the left pane to show all of the playlists that are currently present on that device, as shown in Figure 18-13, which lists the playlists on an iPod, but also shows a Devices entry for a generic 4.1-GB audio player.
