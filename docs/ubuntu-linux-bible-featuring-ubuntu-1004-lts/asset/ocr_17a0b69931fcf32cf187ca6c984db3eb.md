# Configuring Screen Resolution

The Ubuntu installer does a good job of guessing the resolutions that you can use with your current monitor and selecting an initial value that is somewhere in the middle. Unfortunately, display resolution is a matter of personal taste —it really makes sense only when you can see what different resolutions actually look like. Consequently, if you are using a 30-inch monitor and the sexiest video card in the universe, you may find that your Ubuntu Linux system initially displays everything at a resolution of 1024x768, where each of the icons on your screen is approximately the size of a grapefruit. This is probably not what you had in mind, and is certainly not the best use of your hardware or screen estate. This section explains how to correct that problem by selecting a higher resolution screen. Internally, this means that your screen displays the computer industry, but the visual effect is that things on the screen are both smaller and sharper. In fact this is known as "the right thing"
