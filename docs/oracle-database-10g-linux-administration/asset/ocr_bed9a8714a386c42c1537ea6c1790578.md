The word “metacognition” refers to “cognition about cognition” or “thinking about thinking.” In the context of memory, metacognition is our knowledge of how well we are able to encode, store, and retrieve information. Metacognition is also our ability to control our own thinking and memory, including the use of mnemonics or memory aids, monitoring the performance of our long-term memory, and the ability to recognize when our memory fails. Metacognition is our awareness of what we know, what we don’t know, and how good we are at recalling and retrieving information from memory.

Memory researchers have found that metacognition plays an important role in our ability to monitor and control memory. For example, metacognitive strategies, such as self-quizzing and practice testing, have been shown to enhance memory. Self-quizzing and practice testing are effective because they encourage us to actively engage with the material, which promotes deeper processing and retrieval of the information.
