# Point & Click Linux!

If you have your Internet connection figured out now and you want to read about the development and capabilities of the KDE printing system, click on the underlined words “The KDE printing team” and the http://printing.kde.org Web site will open. But this is not really necessary. What we really need to do now is click “Next.”

![](bbox=[0.43, 0.1, 0.92, 0.44])

For this basic tutorial, we’re assuming you have a printer directly connected to your computer through parallel, serial or USB port, so we’ll select “Local printer,” then click “Next.”

(For more information on how to use a printer connected to another computer on your network, click the “Help” button to bring up a copy of *The KDE Print Handbook* or go to the printing.kde.org Web site, which may have a newer version.)
