# Foreword

It's nearly ten years since this guide has been officially updated, and over fifteen years since its initial public release. In that time, we've seen significant changes to the forensic industry, a massive growth in the development of software and techniques to uncover evidence from an ever expanding universe of devices. The purpose of this document, remains unchanged. I am looking to provide an easy to follow and accessible guide for forensic examiners; law enforcement officers, incident responders, and all computer specialists responsible for the investigation. This guide continues to provide an introductory overview of the GNU/Linux (Linux) operating system as a forensic platform.

Above all, this remains a beginner's guide. An introduction it is meant to be conducting forensic examinations. This document is about the tools and the concepts used to employ them, providing simple guidance and some ideas on how they can be integrated into an investigative process.

The content is meant to be “beginner” but as the forensic community evolves and the subject matter becomes more mainstream, the definition of “beginner” level material starts to blur. This guide makes an effort to keep the material as possible without omitting those subjects which are fundamental to the proper understanding of Linux. If you've been forensic examinations for five years or never, but delved into Linux, then this is for you. If you're a student at University and are interested in how forensic tools are employed, but cannot afford thousands of dollars in licenses—then this is for you.

GNU/Linux is a constantly evolving operating system. Distributions come and go, and there are now a few “stand out” Linux flavors that are commonly used. In addition to balancing the beginner nature of the content of this guide with the advancing standards in forensic education, I also find myself trying to balance the level of detail required to actually teach useful tasks with the specific nature of many of the commands and configurations used.
