There's something pleasingly old-school about the visual appearance of Synology's latest router. It's a large piece of kit, and draws attention to itself with four very prominent aerials – suffice to say it's not aiming to be as pretty and living room-friendly as the average ISP-issued wireless router. Synology has clearly focused on delivering an outstanding piece of networking kit, and in that respect it has delivered. Everything from initial set-up to ongoing management is handled through Synology Router Management, a web-based GUI that mimics the look and feel of a desktop operating system. With

a minimum of fuss, we had the device working in harmony with a cable modem and connected a full household of devices in less than half an hour. The GUI provides instant familiarity, and helps you sort through the settings available – of which there are an enormous number. As an example of the level of detail allowed in customisation, parental controls include device-specific time limits and website blocking, while the LEDs on the front of the router can be configured to avoid distracting blinking on a day-by-day, hour-by-hour basis.
