As a general rule, the best interface type is SCSI, because it's fastest and supports the larg-
est number of devices per interface (7 or 15, depending upon interface type, compared
to4 for IDE/ATAPI). IDE/ATAPI drives are acceptable for workstations or if you're work-
ing a tight budget. In the future, USB drives might be acceptable if you need portability, but in early 2001, USB tape drives and Linux are an unproven combination. Floppy-
and-interface drives are old news. You might use one to back up a low-capacity
system if you happen to have the drive alike, but for most serious applications, some-
thing is more appropriate.

# CD-R and CD-RW

A CD-ROM is a Once/Read Many (WORM) storage medium in which micro-scop pics are inscribed on the aluminum layer of a plastic disc to represent data. One
inscribed, the tracks formed by the pits are read optically by a noncontact head, which
cans in a radial pattern as the disc spins just above it. The head moves at a con-stant linear velocity, assuring a constant data rate. When the speed is conse-stant, the disc must rotate at a decreasing rate as the data track is scanned from its
beginning the center of the disc to its near the outer edge of the disc. (Many
modern CD-ROM drives don't use a constant linear velocity, though, and so vari-able data transfer rates from different parts of the disc)

A CD writer (CD-R or CD Recordable) is simply a CD writer with a reader strong
enough to burn optical equivalents of CD-ROM pits into a special CD-R disc that's
coated with an optical dye. Some of these discs are intended to be multisession, meaning
ath you can write some portion of the disc and then filling the multisession at a later
t, while others are written to and cannot be supplemented later. On a CD-RW (CD-Rewritable), the laser can only write data, it can erase the disc, leaving a clean surface
as if the CD-RW hadn't been used. In any of the forms, these discs are quite vul-nerable to scratching and heat damage.

Writable CD-ROMs are becoming quite popular for backups as well. CD-RW discs only
work in players equipped to recognize them. There is an increase in cost for the CD-RW
dis over the CD-R discs. For the price of 50-pack of CD-R discs with jewel cases starts
at around $19. The price for 50 CD-RW discs with jewel cases around $28 and
go up as the discs are certified at higher speeds. CD-ROMs hold approximately
650MB of data, so you'll probably need to do a full backup.

CD-R and CD-RW are relatively low-cost options for backups. Each disc will only
hold up to 650MB of data, and since hard-disk capacities are always going to exceed that,
you are stuck changing discs when one gets full or buying a CD changer in addition to
the writer and the media. This eliminates any real cost savings over tape. We prefer to use
tape or some other high-capacity option.
