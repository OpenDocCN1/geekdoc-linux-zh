# Detecting Intrusion

Intruders usually, not always, leave signs of intrusion. For some, being noticed is the game. Those who don't want to be noticed must stay on the system just long enough to adequately mask the intrusion but still get off the system before someone notices they are on. Crackers can alter the log files in such a way as to make it almost impossible for someone to notice they have been on the system, but this takes a great deal of time. Many crackers simply delete the log files that would identify or cut out the portions that detail their activities. This leaves time gaps in the logs, leaving a footprint. Still, you will usually only know that someone has been there and not know who.

Sometimes intrusion is easy to detect. Although crackers tend to disguise their intrusion, there are always tell-tale signs. On a Red Hat Linux, the /var/log/secure file logs all attempts to access services on the system along information about whether the attempt was successful or unsuccessful. Looking this log, you can determine that someone is trying to break in, because there are several failed attempts to access the system by a single IP or hostname. Failed login attempts by Telnet, FTP, or some other service, are easy to spot in the logs:

```
Jun 20 11:33:01 mydomain in.telnetd[1822]: refused connect
    from 121.137.226.29
Jun 23 01:23:22 mydomain in.ftpd[19650]: refused connect from
    209.151.118.23
```

You can then use nslookup on the IP to gain any information about where the attempted logins are being instantiated. Add this to your /etc/hosts.deny file to make things a little rougher. Most often, however, the IP is no longer valid, because the cracker was attempting to access your system through a dial-up account. If the IP does resolve, do not immediately assume that the-intruder actually has an account within that system; many times, crackers will break into a system and use that system to access another system. Sometimes the chain is quite long. In this case, the IP would belong to another of the cracker's victims and not the cracker. Sometimes the cracker will delete the portions of the /var/log/secure and /var/log/messages files that show the attempted breach, or the relevant logs may be deleted
