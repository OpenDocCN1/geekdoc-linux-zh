# ## 4.5.6 Sendmail

Sendmail is the standard mail-transport system and the default mail-transport on RedHat5.2 and RedHat6.0 Linux. As a user, you don't use sendmail directly--sendmail is the underlying engine that manages your mail in the background. To read/send mail in the text mode, you would probably want to use pine or elm (which stay with-it can be inconvenient to manage separate boxes). In KDE, you may choose to use the "mail" available from the K-menu. To communicate with the outside world through your p dial-out, you would probably like to use the Mozilla mail that is built into your Netscape and communicates directly with your remote Internet-Service-Provider-based mailbox.

Sendmail is very flexible and robust, but also difficult to manage if you needed to customize it to your specific needs. Luckily, RedHat (5.2 or 6.0) comes with the sendmail that runs out-of-box (with some limitations though)

On my home system (Red Hat setup), I can send mail to another user on the same machine with no problem (except using pine). I can send mail to anywhere in the world from after I am connected to my Internet Service Provider through the method . If I am not connected, the mail sits in the queues and waits for it (a minor annoyance). It gets sent once connected (It happens because sendmail is trying to do a DNS lookup that is not available on my system--RedHat default is cache-only). If you really want to avoid this on RH6.0, you may use netconf (as root) and specify that sendmail is not to use DNS at all (Linuxoconf with RH5.2 does not give you the choice). I do use DNS.

Under Linux 6.0, you may also want to configure other sendmail options (as root) netconf.conf. I entered the name of my Internet Service Provider and the names of other machines on my home network under "mail delivery system--relay to hosts". I also added the names of my home network machines under "relay for by".

You should also be aware of the limitation of your setup if you send e-mail from your home network (for example using pine), to the world beyond your network. If you invented the IP address and your domain name is unregistered, there is no chance you will receive a reply.
