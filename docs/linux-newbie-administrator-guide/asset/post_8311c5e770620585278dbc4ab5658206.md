# 7.1 Word Perfect 8 for Linux

This is a very good and powerful word processor. A free version is available for personal use. Try:
http://linux.core.com/download.htm. The free edition lacks the editor, built-in graphics editor, chart editor and the "art text", but it is otherwise a fully functional version of the best word processor in the world. The full version costs $50.

File-level compatibility with MS Word is very good in encountered cases that suggest WP8 may actually be more compatible with the different "sub-versions" of MS Word format than MS Word itself.

On the down side, Word Perfect for Linux often feels slow, particularly when scrolling larger documents.

WP8 is normally launched through an icon or the K-menu but you have to add this yourself after the installation. The main executable is /usr/local/wp8wpbin/xwp, with the location may vary depending where you installed it. Use the command

```
locate xwp
```

or

```
find / -name "xwp"
```

if you cannot find it.

To "manually" launch WP8, try in X-terminal:

```
/usr/local/wp8wpbin/xwp
```

Corel plans to launch its Linux distribution in late 1999/2000 (based on Debian), which apparently center around their powerful WordPerfect suite (Word wordprocessor, Quattro Pro spreadsheet, etc) from their current offering on Linux in that it will be based on the GPL "Wine" (=MS Windows-Application Programming Interface-Emulation) library.

# 7.2 Star Office Suite

Star Office is a complete office suite: word processor, spreadsheet and presentation. Full version is free for personal use, try
http://www.stardivision.com/free/ (large, 60-80 MB).

Star Office looks and acts very much like MS Office (this includes that it is big and slow) and may not be practical without at least 32 MB of memory. It also has good file-level compatibility with MS Office (read and write MS Word, Excel and PowerPoint file formats). Older versions of Star Office required registration after you obtain a "registration key". The registration key is based on your e-mail you gave to Star Office Corp., modem.

# 7.3 Applicware

Applicware is another complete office suite. It contains a word processor, spreadsheet, graphics, presentation, mail, html authoring, and a few more applications. Many so make sure you remember which e-mail you gave them and enter the name to the program when it pops the registration box, with the key. If you are really desperate for Applicware, you can download the demo at http://www.download.com do you really want it? I don't know because no free edition is available. [Sorry, I have to say this: why copyright lawyers keeping perfectly good software?]

# 7.4 AbiWord

AbiWord (http://www.abisource.com/) is a good light-weight wordprocessor. Worth trying for simple word processing needs. Although still incomplete, it is quite useful to me e.g. it supports spelling-as-you-type without the overhead of WordPerfect.

# 7.5 Spreadsheet

Besides the spreadsheet applications in the above (StarOffice and Applicware), you may want to have a look at "xess": http://www.ais.com/linux_corner.html. They also have a slightly scaled down "xessile" which can be downloaded from their site. We love the general design of xess (simple and almost self-explanatory). It seems have really good power for engineering applications and in our opinion it may be the best spreadsheet currently available on Linux.

You may want to keep your eye at "Gnumeric" (included with your RH6.0 distribution, part of the Gnome project). It is still relatively incomplete but it is already definitely usable. It is under heavy development and definitely has the potential to become really great in the near future--it already has a lot of built-in functions, but its printing is unreliable.
