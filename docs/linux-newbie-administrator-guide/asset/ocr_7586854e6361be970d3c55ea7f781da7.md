# 7.9 Kly and Latex

Kly is a "K-desktop" variant of Lyx. Both Kly and Lyx are front ends (WYSIWYG, running under X-Windows). Latex has been for years the most popular document preparation and typesetting program, particularly in academia (with equations, etc.).

The good news is that even if you do not know Latex, you may still be able to use Kly. Think of Kly as a word processor, although its philosophy is different from that of other word processors these do, and therefore it may require an adjustment of your mindset. Latex (and Kly) philosophy is to type in, define the "styles" and leave the formatting to the typesetting program. This means you adjust the spacing (between words, sentences, paragraphs, chapter, etc.) manually. When you do with your document, you "compile" to create a device independent file (\*.dvi\). The dvi file can be viewed by a dvi viewer and printed. The quality of the output is usually outstanding, but the process is typically much frustrating than using a regular word processor.

The strength of Latex is the quality of the prints, its capability to cope with long, complex documents (technical books, etc.), availability of all foreign characters, and its portability across many different platforms.

Try it out, Kly is free. As almost any piece of Linux software, you can download it from Linux:

http://idirect.linux.berg/kdehtml/off_word.html

or any other linux software depository on the Net.

If instead easier Kly, you want to try straight, hard-core Latex, there is some info here to get you started:
