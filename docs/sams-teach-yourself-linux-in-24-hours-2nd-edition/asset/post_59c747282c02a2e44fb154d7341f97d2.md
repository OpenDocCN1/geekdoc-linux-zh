### What is Linux?

Linux (pronounced lih-nuks) is a UNIX-like operating system that runs on many different computers. Although many people might refer to Linux as the operating system and included software, strictly speaking, Linux is the operating system kernel, which comes with a distribution of software. The Linux distribution included with this book is Caldera Systems, Inc. OpenLinux and features special software not included with other distributions (see the section “What’s Included on the CD-ROM?” later in the “Introduction”).

Linux was first released in 1991 by its author, Linus Torvalds, at the University of Helsinki. Since then, it has grown tremendously in popularity as programmers around the world embraced his project of building a free operating system, adding features, and fixing problems.

Linux is popular with today’s generation of computer users for the same reasons early versions of the UNIX operating system enticed fans more than 20 years ago.
