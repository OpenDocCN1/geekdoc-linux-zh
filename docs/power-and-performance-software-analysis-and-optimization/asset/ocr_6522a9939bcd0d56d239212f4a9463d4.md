While author personally believes that command-line interfaces are more flexible and efficient than graphical interfaces, he is in the minority. The majority of users depend on responsive user interfaces for interacting with their systems. As result of this direct interaction between the user and graphical interface, performance problems within graphical stacks tend to be painfully noticeable, even to the most casual of users. In, a significant portion of users evaluate the performance of devices based almost solely on the responsiveness of their interfaces. For example, if a user notices the graphical interface struggling to render fluidity, he is likely to judge the entire device to be of low quality.

Consider an analogy of a car's performance. The only contact that a car makes with the road is through tires. Regardless of how much horsepower a car's engine is capable of producing, the car's actual performance will be evaluated based on the amount of horsepower that it makes to the wheels, and therefore to the road. In other words, a highly optimized algorithm naught if the user interface is sluggish and unresponsive.

The first section of this chapter explores the traditional Linux graphics stack. Architecting a graphics stack is incredibly challenging. This is because computer graphics is an area where technology, and therefore user demands, are constantly evolving. This challenge is further compounded because any discrepancies between the hardware and software architectures lead to inefficiencies.
