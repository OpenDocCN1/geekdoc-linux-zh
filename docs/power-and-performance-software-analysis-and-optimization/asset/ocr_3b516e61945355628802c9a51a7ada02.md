The third principle was the hierarchical organization, designed to support complex and simple data flows.
To illustrate these principles, consider that with the 8086, there were two Intel processors available, the 8088 and 8089. The 8088 was an identical processor to the 8086, except that the external data bus was 8 bits, instead of 16 bits. The 8088 was chosen by IBM for the heart of the “IBM compatible PC.” The 8089 was a separate coprocessor, with’s own instruction set and assembler, designed for offloading data transfers, with Direct Memory Access (DMA) requests. Interestingly, the 8089 was not chosen by IBM for the “IBM compatible PC,” and was never widely adopted.
One or more of these chips could be mixed and matched, with a selection of peripherals, to provide systems highly tailored for software requirements.
