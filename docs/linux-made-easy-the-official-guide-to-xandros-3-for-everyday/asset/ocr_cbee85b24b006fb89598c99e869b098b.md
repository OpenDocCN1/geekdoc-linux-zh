Many people shy away from Linux because they envision it as a system for compu-geeks—an environment in which you do everything the hard way by using a command line. In this era of graphical interfaces, the idea of typing in commands to get things done seems a dreadful throwback to the days of DOS, and that puts many people off—especially those who remember what it was like in those “old days.” This reaction is fair enough, but it is not really an accurate reflection of the reality of the Linux world. After all, most Linux users today utilize some sort of graphical interface. They can, and often do, achieve all that they hope to achieve through drop-down menus and mouse clicks alone. In fact, with Xandros there is actually no need for the everyday user to resort to typing commands at all.
