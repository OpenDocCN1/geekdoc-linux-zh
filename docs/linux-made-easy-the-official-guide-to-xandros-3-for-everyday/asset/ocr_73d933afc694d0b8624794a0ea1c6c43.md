In that screen, you can use your mouse to select the game you want, and then you will be presented with a choice of skill levels. Once you select one of those, the game begins.

The game is all about typing. See a fish with a $w$ on it? Press **w** on your keyboard to guide Tux over so he can gulp it up. When you successfully complete a level, Tux will congratulate you (as in Figure 19-11), and when you don't, you'll be told so as well. That's just about all there is to it.

# ## MathWar

Now that we've covered quite a number of graphical and lexical applications, let's move over to the mathematical side of things by introducing *MathWar* (Figure 19-12).

MathWar is a simple mathematics tutor that pops out simple math problems to which you, or preferably your child, input the answers. The time in which you have to answer is set at 10 seconds (though you can adjust this in either direction), and questions are divided among three operators: addition, subtraction, and multiplication (no division). Although you can adjust the percentage of questions for each operator so that, for example, 20 percent of the questions would involve multiplication, 30 percent division, and 50 percent subtraction, you cannot configure the level of difficulty.
