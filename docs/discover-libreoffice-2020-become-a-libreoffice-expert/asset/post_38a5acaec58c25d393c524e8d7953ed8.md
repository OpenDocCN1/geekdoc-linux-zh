# The community behind LibreOffice

# Global Power

![](img/e39350c3a7c0d527d71b0e4097a0ba66_87_0.png)

Who makes LibreOffice? How does everyone work together? And what are they working on? Here’s an update on what’s happening in the global LibreOffice community. *By Mike Saunders*

LibreOffice is a worldwide project. Many of the software’s features are implemented by certified developers [1] who are working in the LibreOffice ecosystem – such as Collabora, CIB, and Red Hat. These companies use and make products based on LibreOffice and offer long-term support versions of the suite. But when they make improvements to LibreOffice, they also contribute their changes back to the community, thanks to the open source license.

While much of the “grunt work” programming is done by these developers, there are also hundreds of people in the wider community who work on other aspects: localization (translating the user interface), design, marketing, documentation, handling bug reports, and much more. Everyone is welcome to get involved [2] with the community and help to improve LibreOffice – meeting new people and building skills along the way.

So, what has the community been doing in the last couple of years to keep LibreOffice going from strength to strength? Read on to learn all about their work, and see how you can take part as well.

## ALBANIA – LOCALIZATION SPRINT

Tirana, Albania’s capital city, was the host of the LibreOffice Conference 2018. Since then, the local community has been active in the design, marketing and localization projects within LibreOffice. In November 2018, the community had a localization sprint, and one of the organizers, Sidorela Uku, described it as follows:

> “This was the first event I organized for LibreOffice, in collaboration with a friend of mine, Marcela Korreshi (our new contributor). The presentation included an introduction to LibreOffice and how people can contribute to the project in various ways, while the second part was focused on translations. (*In addition, I talked about whatcanidoforlibreoffice.org.*) We had 14 people participating at the event, and as far as I can see from the translations, there are around 460 strings translated to review – and around 200 strings translated and reviewed.”

## GERMANY – EVENTS AND MEETUPS

Germany and LibreOffice have a strong connection. StarOffice, the precursor to OpenOffice.org (which in turn was the precursor to LibreOffice), was first developed in Hamburg in the 1980s. Over the years, many other developers joined the team, in and around Hamburg and other parts of Germany. Even today, many years later, the German community is active, attending events and holding regular community meetups.

In August 2019, Community members attended FrOSCon in Sankt Augustin, a town just outside of Bonn. FrOSCon is one of the largest free and open source software (FOSS) conferences in the country, with around 2,000 attendees. Most of the visitors know about FOSS already, but some had only learned about it recently and were eager to discover more. Gerrit Großkopf, Uwe Altmann, Stefan Unverricht, and I (Mike Saunders) had a booth with flyers, stickers, and a computer demonstrating LibreOffice 6.3 and LibreOffice Online, the web-based version of the suite [3]. Many visitors to the booth had no idea that LibreOffice Online existed and were eager to try it out on their own infrastructure.

## JAPAN - LIBREOFFICE ASIA CONFERENCE 2019

The *LibreOffice Asia Conference 2019* was held on May 25 and 26 in at Cybozu's Tokyo office. Attendees from several Asian countries were present – including Japan, Taiwan, and Indonesia – while some LibreOffice supporters made the trip over from Europe.

On the first day, Mark Hung from Taiwan gave the opening keynote, talking about bugs, fixes, and success stories with LibreOffice in Asian languages. This was followed by talks in three tracks (one in Japanese and two in English). Talks included: educational practices in China, organizing translation sprints for local languages, running LibreOffice in a factory, Collabora Online, and the new ODF toolkit from TDF.

On the second day, participants took part in a hackfest, business workshop, and certification reviews. Eric Sun, a TDF member and a certification interview candidate at this conference, won unanimous approvals from the committee and became a certified migration professional and professional trainer.

## NEPAL - LOCALIZATION EVENT

On September 21, 2019, Software Freedom Day, the Nepalese LibreOffice community organized a localization sprint at Kathmandu University. Sanjog Sigdel and Saroj Dhakal organized the event and reported back: "14 new volunteers signed up. We presented the guidelines for doing the translations, and together we translated around 376 text strings into the Nepali language in an hour."
