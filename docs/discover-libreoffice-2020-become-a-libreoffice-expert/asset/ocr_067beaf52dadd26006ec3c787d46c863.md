# THANKS TO EVERYONE

It’s thanks to this worldwide community that LibreOffice keeps getting better and better with every release. Without them, the software wouldn’t be available in so many languages, making its way into schools, governments, businesses, and homes around the globe. Everyone is welcome to join the community – see the next page for more details about this!

# EVENTS IN 2020

You’ve probably noticed that the community events we’ve covered so far took place in 2019. Due to COVID-19, very few events have taken place in-person. This is quite frustrating for many people in the community, as they

![](bbox=[0.07, 0.67, 0.63, 0.94])

really enjoy meeting face-to-face and chatting over food and drink. Indeed, the LibreOffice Conference, which was scheduled to take place in Nuremberg, Germany, has moved online.

Still, it’s not the end of the world. Online events have their advantages: Anyone can easily take part, without having to travel long distances, for instance. Daniel A. Rodriguez, a LibreOffice contributor based in Argentina and TDF Board member, talks about an online meetup that took place during the early stages of the pandemic:

> “In May, the Hispanic LibreOffice community held an online event via TDF’s Jitsi instance, and streamed on YouTube. The event started 17:00 UTC and last almost 4 hours. More that 35 people took part, which benefited from the presence of several members who are well-recognised for their participation and collaboration in the project. Celia Palacios, from Mexico, told us about the work of the documentation team, while Mauricio Baeza, also from the Aztec land, talked about the potential of macros using Python. Xisco Fausil, from Spain, explained how quality control is applied to software in the development stages, and Ísmael Fario, another Spaniard, presented pivot tables. Finally, Gustavo Pacheco, from Brazil, talked about becoming a member of The Document Foundation and invited everyone to apply.”
