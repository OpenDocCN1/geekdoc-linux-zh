Presentation styles affect three elements of a slide master: background, background objects (such as icons, decorative lines, and text frames), and the text on the slide. Text styles are further divided into *Notes*, *Outline 1* through *Outline 9*, *Subtitle*, and *Title*. The outline styles are used for different levels of the outline. For example, *Outline 2* is used for the sub-points of *Outline 1*, and *Outline 3* is used for the sub-points of *Outline 2*, and so on. Image styles are not restricted and can affect many of a slide’s elements. Note that text styles exist in both the presentation and image style selections.
Let’s try it out! Start a new presentation, using the Alizarrin template, and add some text to the intro and first slides. Then, go to *View | Master Slide*, and you should see something that looks like Figure 7.
Now try changing the text formatting for the header and other parts of the slide (i.e., change to a different font). If you go back to *View | Normal*, you can see that the formatting changes have been applied to the slide(s). So you can quickly and easily make changes across many (or all) slides at once, which is a real timesaver if you have a bunch of existing presentations for your company, but need to update them for new branding guidelines.
To create a new master slide, right-click in a blank area of the Slides panel on the left, and choose *New Master*. Modify this master to suit your requirements. Note that it’s also recommended that you rename this new master. Right-click on the slide in the Slides pane and select *Rename Master* from the context menu. When you’re finished with creating the master slide, click *Close Master View* on the top toolbar and return to normal slide editing mode.
Now you can create a new slide and use the sidebar to apply a master slide design to it (see Figure 8). Just click the *Master Slides* icon in the right-hand panel, and then choose the design from the top.

The view you see on your computer display includes the current slide, the upcoming slide, any slide notes, and the presentation timer. Note that the Presenter Console works only on operating systems that support multiple displays, and only when two displays are connected (one may be the lap-top built-in display, for instance).
To enable the Presenter Console, go to *Tools | Options* (Windows and Linux) or *LibreOffice | Preferences* (Mac OS), and then select *LibreOffice Impress* on the left followed by *General*. Check the *Enable Presenter Console* button and click OK. Now, when you connect an external display and start a presentation, you should see the main presentation view on one screen and the Presenter Console in another (see Figure 9). (If it doesn’t work, and the second screen is simply mirroring the first one, you’ll need to change the display settings in your operating system.)
