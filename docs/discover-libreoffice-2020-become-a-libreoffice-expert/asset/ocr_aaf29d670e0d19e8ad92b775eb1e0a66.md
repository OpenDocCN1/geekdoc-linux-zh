# CREATING A PHOTO ALBUM

You can use Impress to create a photo album from a set of images, usually with one photo per slide. This photo album can use multimedia sources with graphics and images or even collect your latest holiday pictures into a single presentation file. To do this, first open an existing or blank presentation, and then choose *Insert | Media | Photo Album*. In the Create Photo Album dialog box that appears, click *Add* to select your images and browse your file system. Exactly how you select multiple images depends on your operating system, but usually you can do it by holding the Shift or Ctrl key while clicking.

After you've chosen your images, Impress offers some extra options (see Figure 2). One of the most important is *Link Images*, which means that the image data won't be copied directly into the presentation, but rather the images will be loaded from the file system on demand when you watch the presentation. This saves disk space, as you don't duplicate so much data. However, beware: If you remove the image file from your hard drive, the presentation won't have it either! Click *Insert Slides* when you're done, and you can continue to edit and show the presentation as usual.

To change the background of a shape or text box, right-click on it and choose *Area*, and you'll see the dialog shown in Figure 4. You also can use this dialog to add shadows and set the transparency of objects.

Want some more shapes and clip art for your presentation? Then check out the Gallery (Figure 5), which is located in the sidebar, the second icon from the bottom in the right-hand panel. Click this to see a range of things you can add – just click and drag them over. As with all other shapes, if you click the shape to highlight it, you'll see green handles that you can click and drag to resize it.

So, you have your slides, layouts, text boxes, text formatting, shapes, and graphics – all the essential tools to create presentations. But there's one more thing to consider: transition effects (i.e., the animations used to move from one slide to another). Choose a slide from the left-hand panel, and then choose the *Slide Transition* deck from the sidebar on the right (it's the third icon from the top).

You'll see a bunch of transitions, along with small images depicting them, in the main part of the sidebar. Feel free to explore – just click a transition to see how it works. Many transitions have extra options underneath, including the duration (in seconds) and additional sound effects. You can also use this part of the sidebar to make the presentation move on automatically, after waiting for a certain amount of seconds – see the *Advance Slide* options towards the bottom.

With the basics under your belt, I'll move on to a few advanced features: workspace views, master slides, and the Presenter Console.
