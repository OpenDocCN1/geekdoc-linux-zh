
## GETTING STARTED

### LibreOffice Impress

## CREATING A PHOTO ALBUM

You can use Impress to create a photo album from a set of images, usually with one photo per slide. This photo album can use multimedia sources with graphics and images or even collect your latest holiday pictures into a single presentation file. To do this, first open an existing or blank presentation, and then choose *Insert | Media | Photo Album*. In the Create Photo Album dialog box that appears, click *Add* to select your images and browse your file system. Exactly how you select multiple images depends on your operating system, but usually you can do it by holding the Shift or Ctrl key while clicking.

After you've chosen your images, Impress offers some extra options (see Figure 2). One of the most important is *Link Images*, which means that the image data won't be copied directly into the presentation, but rather the images will be loaded from the file system on demand when you watch the presentation. This saves disk space, as you don't duplicate so much data. However, beware: If you remove the image file from your hard drive, the presentation won't have it either! Click *Insert Slides* when you're done, and you can continue to edit and show the presentation as usual.

To change the background of a shape or text box, right-click on it and choose *Area*, and you'll see the dialog shown in Figure 4. You also can use this dialog to add shadows and set the transparency of objects.

Want some more shapes and clip art for your presentation? Then check out the Gallery (Figure 5), which is located in the sidebar, the second icon from the bottom in the right-hand panel. Click this to see a range of things you can add – just click and drag them over. As with all other shapes, if you click the shape to highlight it, you'll see green handles that you can click and drag to resize it.

So, you have your slides, layouts, text boxes, text formatting, shapes, and graphics – all the essential tools to create presentations. But there's one more thing to consider: transition effects (i.e., the animations used to move from one slide to another). Choose a slide from the left-hand panel, and then choose the *Slide Transition* deck from the sidebar on the right (it's the third icon from the top).

You'll see a bunch of transitions, along with small images depicting them, in the main part of the sidebar. Feel free to explore – just click a transition to see how it works. Many transitions have extra options underneath, including the duration (in seconds) and additional sound effects. You can also use this part of the sidebar to make the presentation move on automatically, after waiting for a certain amount of seconds – see the *Advance Slide* options towards the bottom.

With the basics under your belt, I'll move on to a few advanced features: workspace views, master slides, and the Presenter Console.

## WORKSPACE VIEWS

Under the **View** menu, you'll find various workspace options – each of these workspace views is designed to ease the completion of certain tasks. For instance, **Normal** is the main view for working with individual slides. Use this view to format and design slides and to add text, graphics, and animation effects.

Then there's the **Outline** view, which contains all of the presentation slides numbered sequentially (Figure 6). **Outline** view shows topic titles, bulleted lists, and numbered lists for each slide in the outline format. Only the text contained in the default text boxes in each slide is shown, so if your slide includes other text boxes or drawing objects, the text in these objects will not be displayed.

The **Notes** view lets you add notes to a slide. These notes are for your information only and are not seen by the audience when the presentation is shown. Meanwhile, the **Master Handout** view is for setting up the layout of your slides for printing. Click the **Properties** tab in the sidebar, and you can choose to print one, two, three, four, six, or nine slides per page in the **Layouts** section. In addition, this lets you add custom fields into the header and footer sections, such as the current date – click in one of those boxes, and then go to **Insert | Field** to see what's available.

## MASTER SLIDES

First, a bit of background: A master slide is one that's used as the starting point for other slides. It's similar to a page style in Writer, and it controls the basic formatting of all slides based on it. A slide show can have more than one master slide. Also, master slides have a defined set of characteristics, including background colors, graphics, and gradients, as well as other objects (such as logos, decorative lines, and so on), headers and footers, placement and size of text frames, text format, and so forth.

Styles control all of a master slide's characteristics. New slides created using a master slide will have styles that are inherited from whichever master slide was used. Changing a style in a master slide results in changes to all slides based on it. However, you can modify individual slides without affecting the master slide itself.

Master slides have two types of styles associated with them: presentation styles and image styles. The prepackaged presentation styles can be modified, but new presentation styles cannot be created. With image styles, you can modify the prepackaged styles and create new image styles as well.

![](img/e39350c3a7c0d527d71b0e4097a0ba66_30_2.png)

**Figure 6: Use the Outline view to focus on the text content and structure of your presentation, rather than the graphical aspects.**

Presentation styles affect three elements of a slide master: background, background objects (such as icons, decorative lines, and text frames), and the text on the slide. Text styles are further divided into *Notes*, *Outline 1* through *Outline 9*, *Subtitle*, and *Title*. The outline styles are used for different levels of the outline. For example, *Outline 2* is used for the sub-points of *Outline 1*, and *Outline 3* is used for the sub-points of *Outline 2*, and so on. Image styles are not restricted and can affect many of a slide’s elements. Note that text styles exist in both the presentation and image style selections.

Let’s try it out! Start a new presentation, using the Alizarrin template, and add some text to the intro and first slides. Then, go to *View | Master Slide*, and you should see something that looks like Figure 7.

![](img/e39350c3a7c0d527d71b0e4097a0ba66_31_0.png)

**Figure 7: Impress’ Master Slide mode: You can make changes that affect all slides based on it.**

Now try changing the text formatting for the header and other parts of the slide (i.e., change to a different font). If you go back to *View | Normal*, you can see that the formatting changes have been applied to the slide(s). So you can quickly and easily make changes across many (or all) slides at once, which is a real timesaver if you have a bunch of existing presentations for your company, but need to update them for new branding guidelines.

To create a new master slide, right-click in a blank area of the Slides panel on the left, and choose *New Master*. Modify this master to suit your requirements. Note that it’s also recommended that you rename this new master. Right-click on the slide in the Slides pane and select *Rename Master* from the context menu. When you’re finished with creating the master slide, click *Close Master View* on the top toolbar and return to normal slide editing mode.

Now you can create a new slide and use the sidebar to apply a master slide design to it (see Figure 8). Just click the *Master Slides* icon in the right-hand panel, and then choose the design from the top.

## PRESENTER CONSOLE

Impress includes a Presenter Console feature that can be used when an extra presentation display has been connected to your computer. This Presenter Console provides extra control over slide shows by using different views on your computer display and on the presentation display that the audience sees. The view you see on your computer display includes the current slide, the upcoming slide, any slide notes, and the presentation timer. Note that the Presenter Console works only on operating systems that support multiple displays, and only when two displays are connected (one may be the lap-top built-in display, for instance).

To enable the Presenter Console, go to *Tools | Options* (Windows and Linux) or *LibreOffice | Preferences* (Mac OS), and then select *LibreOffice Impress* on the left followed by *General*. Check the *Enable Presenter Console* button and click OK. Now, when you connect an external display and start a presentation, you should see the main presentation view on one screen and the Presenter Console in another (see Figure 9). (If it doesn’t work, and the second screen is simply mirroring the first one, you’ll need to change the display settings in your operating system.)
