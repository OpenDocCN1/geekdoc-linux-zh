# USING CELL STYLES

Calc is unique among spreadsheets in its use of styles – specifically, for cells and pages. As in Writer, Calc styles can be based on existing styles to save time and provide a unified look. Calc styles can be applied to single or multiple cells, an entire column or row, or, by clicking the empty space above row 1 and to the left of column A, to the entire worksheet. Press F11 to display and edit styles in the Styles & Formatting dialog. If you have used manual (direct) for-matting already, you should select Format | Clear Direct Formatting to avoid difficulties as you apply styles.

Calc installs with a number of de-fault cell styles. These come with a number of predefined settings, mostly for different purposes, such as head-ings. Other cell styles, listed under Stat-us, are intended to mark evaluations of the state of your work and can be changed as improvements are added. You may also want to create cell styles to mark subtotals or totals of the cells that contain particular functions. By contrast, Calc comes with only two page styles, although more can be added as needed.

Cell styles support Fonts and Font Ef-fect settings, and both Cell Style and Page Style dialogs have tabs for Borders and Background. Each of these tabs is almost identical to those found in other LibreOffice modules, and a variety of fonts, borders, and backgrounds is a way to make data arrays distinct from one another (Figure 11).

However, Calc styles do have some tabs that are unavailable elsewhere in LibreOffice. In Cell Style, one of the most important is the Numbers tab. Al-though Calc assumes by default that all characters are text to be displayed

starting at the left margin and all numerals are numbers to be displayed starting at the right mar-gin, you can spec-ify the kind of information a cell con-tains from the Numbers tab. From the same tab, you can select formats for information such as the date. The lan-guage and locale are also on the Num-bers tab, because they determine the default formats for different types of numbers, including the separator used to make large numbers easier to read (Figure 12).

A second unique tab for cells is Alignment. From the Alignment tab, you can position cell entries on an angle, which can save space in head-ings. Even more importantly, you can set text to wrap automatically and to hyphenate, two settings that make a worksheet more readable. You might also shrink content to fit cell size, al-though that can be a user-unfriendly choice (Figure 13).

A third unique tab for cells is Cell Pro-tection. There, you can set whether an entire worksheet or just the formulas are protected, or whether to hide a cell when printing. These settings are not intended for security so much as protec-tion against accidental damage and to reduce clutter.

Page styles only come into play when a spreadsheet is printed. For conven-ience, you can add headers and footers to each page, using tabs that resemble those in Writer Paragraph styles.
