The people are tired of this mock battle with criminal capital. They do not want to hurt business, but they do to get something done about the trust question that amounts to something. What good does do any man to read in his morning paper that the courts have "dissolved" the Oil Trust, and then read in his evening paper that he must pay a higher price for his oil than ever before? What good does it do the laborer who smokes his pipe to tell that the courts have "dissolved" the Tobacco Trust and find that he may not pay the same or a higher price for the short-weight package of tobacco? Yet all this is the practical result of the suits against these two trusts in the world.

Such business chaos and legal paradoxes as American business suffers from be found nowhere else in the world. Rival nations do not fasten legal ball and chain upon their business—no, they put wings on its flying feet. Rival nations do not tell their business men if they go forward with legitimate enterprise the penitentiary be their goal. No! Rival nations tell their business men that so long as they do honest business governments will not hinder but will help them.

But these rival nations do tell their business men that if they do evil that our business men do, prison bars await them. These rival nations do tell their business men that if they issue watered stock or cheat the people in any way, prison cells will be their homes.

Just is this all honest American business wants; just is this what dishonest American business does not want; just is this what the American people propose to have; just this national Republican platform of 1908 pledged the people we would give them; and just this important pledge the administration, elected on that platform, repudiated as it repudi the more immediate tariff pledge.

Both these reforms, so vital to honest American business, the Progressive party will accomplish. Neither evil interests nor reckless demagues can swerve us from our purpose; for we are free from both and fear neither.
