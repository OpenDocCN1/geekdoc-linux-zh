Haste thee, Nymph, and bring with thee Jest, and youthful Jollity, Quips
and Cranks and Wanton Wiles, Nods and Becks, and wreathed Smiles, Such
as hang on Hebe's cheek, And love to live in dimple sleek,-- Sport that
wrinkled Care deriders, and Laughter holding both his sides.

Come, and trip it as ye go On the light fantastic toe; And in thy right hand
lead with thee The mountain nymph, sweet Liberty: And, if I give thee
hon due, Mirth, admit me of thy Crew, To live with her, and live with
thee, In unpreèd pleasures free;

To hear the lark begin its flight, And singing, startle the dull Night From
his watch--tower in the skies, Till the dappled Dawn doth rise; Then to
come in spite of sorrow, And at my window good--morrow Through the
sweetbier, or the vine. Or the twisted eglantine; The cock with wisely
d Scatters the rear of darkness thin, And to the stack, or the barn-door,
Stoutly struts his dames before;

Oft listening how the hounds and horn Cheerly rouse the slumbering Morn,
From the side of some hour hill, Through the high echoing shrill;
Sometimes walking, not unseen, By hedge-row elms, on hillsocks green,
Right against the eastern gate, Where the great Sun begins his state, Robed
in flames and amber light. The clouds in thousand liveries doth, While the
plowman near at hand Whistles oer the fured land, And the milkmaid
singing blethe, And the mower wheets his scythe. And every shepherd tells
tis tale, Under the hawthorn in the dale.

THE SEA The sea, the sea, the open sea, The blue, the fresh, the fever free;
Without a mark, without a bound. It runeth the earth's wide regions round;
It plays with the clouds, it mocks the skies, Or like craled creature likes.
'm on the sea, I'm on the sea, I am where I would ever be, With the blue
and the blue below, And silence whoesever I go. If a storm
come and awake the deep, What matter? I shall ride and sleep.

I love, oh! how I love to ride On the fierce, foaming, bursting tide, Where
every mad wave drowns the moon, And whistles aloft its tempest tune, And
