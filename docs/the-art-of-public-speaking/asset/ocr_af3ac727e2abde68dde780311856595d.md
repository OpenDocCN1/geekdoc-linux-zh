# CHAPTER XXVIII

# 300

Now you have come to a dead wall after saying: "Joan of Arc fought for liberty." By this method you might get something like this:

"Liberty is a sacred privilege for mankind always had had to fight. These struggles [Platitude—but push on] fill the pages of history. History records the gradual triumph of the serf over the lord

Thus far you have linked one idea with another rather obviously, but you are getting your second wind now and may venture to relax your grip on the too–evident chain; and so may you say:

"With gunpowder the humblest serf in all land could put an end to the life of the tyrannical baron behind the castle walls. The struggle for liberty, with gunpowder as its aid, wrecked empires, and built up a new era for all mankind."

In a moment more you have gotten back to your outline and the day is saved.

Practising exercises like the above will not only fortify you against the death of your speech when your memory misses fire, but it will also provide an excellent training for fluency in speaking. _Stock up with ideas.
