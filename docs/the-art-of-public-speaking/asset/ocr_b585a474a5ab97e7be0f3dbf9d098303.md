"This young man" referred by Mr. Holbrook was Tennyson.

Henry Ward Beecher said: "I do not believe I have ever met a man on the street that I did not get from him some element for a sermon. I never see anything in nature which does not work towards that for which I give the strength of my life. The material for my sermons is all the time following me and swarming around me."

Instead of saying only one man in a million can, it would strike nearer the truth that none of us sees with perfect understanding more than a fraction of what passes before our eyes, yet this faculty of acute and accurate observation is so important that no man ambitious to lead can neglect it. The next time you are in a car, look at those who opposite you and see what you can discover of their habits, occupations, ideals, nationalities, environments, education, and so on. You may not see a great deal first time, but practise will reveal astonishing results. Transmute every incident of your day into a speech or an illustration. Translate all that you have seen into terms of speech. When you can describe all that you have seen in definite words, you are seeing clearly. You are becoming the million man.

De Mauassant's description of an author would also fit the public-speaker: "His eye is like a suction pump, absorbing everything; like a pickpocket's hand, always at work. Nothing escapes him. He is constantly collecting material, gathering—up glances, gestures, intentions, everything that goes on in his presence—the slightest look, the least act, the merest trifle." De Mauassant was himself a million man, Master.

Ruskin took a common rock-crystal and saw hidden within his stupid heart lessons which have not yet ceased to move men's lives. Beecher stood for hours before the window of a jewelry store thinking out analogies between jewels and the souls of men. Gough saw in a single drop of water enough truth therewith to quench the thirst of five thousand souls. Thoreau sat still in the shadowy woods that birds and insects came and opened up their secret lives to his eye. Emerson observed the soul of a man so long that length he could say, 'I cannot hear what you say, for seeing what you are.'
