But the women are on the march. They are walking upward to the sunlit plains where the thinking people rule. China has ceased binding their feet. In the shadow of the Harem Turkey has opened a school for girls. America has given the women equal educational advantages, and America, we believe, will enfranchise them.

We can do little to help and not much to hinder this great movement. The thinking people put their O.K. upon it. It is moving forwards to its goal just as surely as this old earth is swinging from the grip of winter toward the spring's blossoms and the summer's harvest.[1]

THE IRREPRESSIBLE CONFLICT ... At last, the Republican party has appeared. It avows, as the Republican party of 1800 did, in one word, its faith and its works,"Equal and exact justice to all men." Even when it first entered the field, only half organized, it struck a blow which just failed to secure complete and triumphant victory. In this, its second campaign, it has already won advantages which render triumph both easy and certain. The secret of its assured success lies in that very characteristic which, in the mouth of scoffers, constitutes its great and lasting disbelief and reproach. It lies in the fact that it is a party of one idea; but that a noble one—an idea that fills and expands all generous souls; the idea of equality of all men before human humans and human laws, as they are all equal before the Divine tribunal and Divine laws.

I know, and you know, that a revolution has begun. I know, and all the world knows, that revolutions never go backward. Twenty senators and a hundred representatives proclaim boldly in Congress–to–day sentiments and opinions of freedom which hardly so many, even in this free State, dared to utter in their own homes twenty years ago.
