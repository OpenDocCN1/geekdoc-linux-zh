
## CHAPTER XXXI

Dynamite the "I" out of conversation. Not one in nine hundred can talk about himself without being a bore. The man who can perform that feat achieves marvels without talking about himself; the "I" is not even permissible in talk. If you habitually build conversation around your own interests, it may prove very tiresome to the listener. He may be thinking of birds or dry fish while you are discussing the fourth dimension, or the merits of a cucumber. The conversationalist is prepared to talk in terms of his listener's interest. Washburn says he knows a man of mediocre ability who can charm much more than himself when he discusses electric lighting. Avoid platitudes and hackney phrases. If you meet a friend on State Street, it is not necessary to observe: "How small this world after all!" This observation was doubtless made prior to the formation of Pike. "This old world is getting better every day." "It is not so much the cost of living as the cost of high living" that flashed in bold headlines or daily columns after week.
