... I WOULD CALL HIM NAPOLEON_, but Napoleon made his way to empire_over broken oaths and through a sea of blood._ This man never broke his word. "No Retaliation" was his great motto and the rule of his life;_AND THE LAST WORDS UTTERED TO HIS SON IN FRANCE WERE THESE: "My boy, you will one day go back to San Domingo; forget that Cromwell murdered your father." I WOULD CALL HIM CROMWELL_, but Cromwell_was only a soldier, and the state founded down into his grave. I WOULD CALL HIM WASHINGTON_, but the great Virginian_held SLAVES. THIS MAN RISKED HIS EMPIRE rather than permit the slave-trade in the humblest village of his dominions._

_YOU THINK ME A FANATIC TO-NIGHT_, for you read history,_not with your eyes, BUT WITH YOUR PREJUDICES._ But fifty years later, when Truth gets a hearing, the Muse of History will put _PHOCION for the Greek,_ and_andBRUTUS for the Roman, HAMPDEN for England, LAFAYETTE for France,_ choose_WASHINGTON as the bright, consummate flower of our EARLIER civilization, AND JOHN BROWN the ripe fruit of our NOONDAY_, then, dipping his pen in the sunlight, will write in the clear blue, above them all, the name of _SOLDIER, THE STATESMAN, THE MARTYR, TOUSSAINT L'OUVERTURE_._

--Wendell Phillips, Toussaint l'Overture.
