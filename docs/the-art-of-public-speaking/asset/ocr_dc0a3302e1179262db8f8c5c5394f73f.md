# CHAPTER XXV

_3 Ple._ There's not a nobler man in Rome than Antony.

_4 Ple._ Now mark him, he begins again to speak.

_Ant._ But yesterday, the word of Cesar might have stood against the world: now he lies there, and none poor to him do reverence. Oh, masters! if I were dispos'd to stir Your hearts and minds to mutiny and rage, I should do Brutus wrong, and Cassius wrong. Who, you all know, are honorable men. I will not do them; I rather choose To wrong the dead, to wrong myself, and then, I will wrong such honorable men. But here's a parchment, with the seal of Caesar; I found it in his closet; 'tis his will: Let but the commons hear this testament— Which, pardon me, I do not mean to read— And they would go and kill Caesar's wounds, And dip their napkins in his sacred blood; beg a hair of him for memory, And, dying, mention it within their wills, Bequeathing it as a rich legacy unto their.

_4 Ple._ We'll hear the will: Read it, Mark Antony.

_All._ The will! the will! we will hear Caesar's will.

_Ant._ Have patience, gentle friends: I must not read it; It is not meet you know how Caesar lov'd you. You are not wood, you are not stones, but men; And, being men, hearing the will of Caesar, It will inflame you, it will make you mad: 'Tis good you know not that you are his heirs; For if you should, oh, what would come of it!

_4 Ple._ Read the will; we'll hear it, Antony! You shall read us the will! Caesar's will!

_Ant._ Will you be patient? Will you stay awhile? I have o'ersham myself, to tell you of it. I fear I wrong the honorable men Whose daggers have stab'd Caesar; I do fear it.

_4 Ple._ They were traitors: Honorable men!
