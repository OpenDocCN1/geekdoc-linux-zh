Habitual thinking is just that—a habit. Habit comes from doing a thing
repeatedly. The lower habits are acquired easily, the higher ones require
deeper groves if they are to persist. We find that the thought—habit
comes only with resolute practise; yet no effort will yield richer dividends.
Persist in practise, and whereas you have been able to think only

Thinking is the most fascinating and exhilarating of all mental exercises.
Once realize that your opinion on a subject does not represent the choice
you have made between what Dr. Cerebrum has written and Professor
Cerebellum has said, but is the result of your own earnestly–applied
brain–energy, and you will gain a confidence in your ability to speak on
that that nothing will be able to shake. Your thought will have given
you both power and reserve power.

Someone condensed the relation of thought to knowledge in these
pung, homely lines:

> "Don't give me the man who thinks he thinks, Don't give me the man who
thinks he knows, But give me the man who knows he thinks, And I have
the man who knows he knows!"
