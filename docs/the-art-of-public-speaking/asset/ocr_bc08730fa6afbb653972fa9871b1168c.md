_ANTON’S ORATION OVER CÆSAR’S BODY—Friends, Romans, countrymen! Lend me ears to me; I come to bury Caesar, not to praise him. The evil that men do after them; the good is oft interred with their bones: So let it be with Caesar! The Noble Brutus hath told you Caesar was ambitious. If it were so, it was a grievous fault, And grievously hath Cæsar answered it. Here, under leave of Brutus, and the rest—For Brutus is an honorable man, So are they all, all honorable men—Come I to speak in Cæsar’s funeral. He was my friend, faithful and just to me: But Brutus says he was ambitious; And Brutus is an honorable man. He hath brought many captives to Rome, Whose ransoms the general officers fills: Did this in Cæsar seem ambitious? When the poor have cried, Cæsar hath wept; Ambition should be made of sterner stuff: Yet Brutus says, he was ambitious; And you saw, I thrice presented him a kingly crown, Which he thrice refuse. Was this ambition? Yet Brutus says he was ambitious; And sure, he is an honorable man. I speak not to disprove Brutus spoke, But here I am to speak what I know. You all did him once, not without cause; What cause withholds you to mourn for him? Oh, judgment, thou art fled to brutish beasts, And men have lost their reason!—Bear with me; My heart is in the coffin with Cæsar, And I must pause till it come back to me.
[_Weeps._]

_1 Plebeian._ Methinks there is much reason in his sayings.

_2 Ple.— If thou consider rightly of the matter, Cæsar has had great wrong.

_3 Ple.— Has he, masters? I fear there will be a worse come in his place.

_4 Ple.— Mark’s ye his words? He would not take the crown; Therefore, 'tis certain, he was not ambitious.
