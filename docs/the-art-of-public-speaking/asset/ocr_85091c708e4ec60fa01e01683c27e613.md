"Plenty of them, plenty of them; all you have to do is just go and find them, then you have them." Al Hafed said, "I will go." So he sold his farm, collected his money at interest, left his family in charge of a neighbor, and went away in search of diamonds. He began properly, to my mind, at the Mountains of the Moon. Afterwards he went around Palestine, wandered into Europe, and at last when his money was all spent, and he was in rags, wretchedness and poverty, he stood on the shore of that bay Barcelona, Spain, when a tidal wave came rolling through the Pillars of Hercules and the poor afflicted, suffering man could not resist the awful temptation to cast himself into that incoming tide, and he sank beneath its foaming crest, never to rise in this life again.

When that old guide told me that very sad story, the camel I was riding and went back to the baggage on one of the camels, and I remember thinking to myself, "Why did he reserve that for his _particular friends?" There seemed to be no beginning, middle or end—nothing to it. That was the first story I ever heard or read in which the hero was killed in the first chapter. I had but one chapter of that story and the hero was dead. When the guide came back and took the halter of my camel again, he went right on with the same story. He said that Al Hafed's successor led his camel into the garden and as that camel put its nose down into the clear water of the garden brook Al Hafed's successor noticed a curious flash of light from the sands of the shallow stream, and reaching in he pulled out a black stone having an eye of light that reflected all the colors of the rainbow, and he took that curious pebble into the house and left it on the mantel, then went on way and forgot all about it. A few days after that, this same old priest told Al Hafed how diamonds were made, came to visit his successor, when he saw that flash of light from the mantel. He rushed up and said, "Here is a diamond—here is a diamond! Has Al Hafed returned?" No, no; Al Hafed has not returned and that is not a diamond; that is nothing but a stone; we found it right here in our garden." "But I know a diamond when I see it," said he; "that is a diamond"
