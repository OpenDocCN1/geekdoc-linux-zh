# Part III, Annual Report of the Secretary of Internal

Only those who believe attempt the seemingly impossible, and, by attempting, prove that one, with God, can chase a thousand and that two can put thousand to flight. I can imagine that the early Christians were carried into the Coliseum to make a spectacle for more savage than the beasts, were treated by their doubting companions not to endanger their lives. But, kneeling in the center of the arena, they prayed and sang until they were devoured. How helpless seemed seemed, measured by every human rule, hopeless hopeless their cause! And yet within a few decades the power which they invoked proved mightier than the legions of the emperor and the faith in which they died were triumphant o'er all the land. It is said that those who went to mock their sufferings returned themselves, "What is it that can enter into the heart of man and make him die as these die?" They were greater conquerors in their death than they could have had they perished life by a surrender of their faith.

What would have been the fate of the church if the early Christians had as little faith as many of our Christians of to-day? And if the Christians of to-day had the faith of the martyrs, how long would it be before the fulfillment of the prophecy that "every knee shall bow and every tongue confess?"

I am glad that He, who is called the Prince of Peace—who can bring peace to every troubled heart and whose teachings, exemplified in life, will bring peace between man and man, between community and community, between State and State, between nation and nation throughout the world—I am glad that He brings courage as well as peace so that those who follow Him may take up and each day bravely do the duties that to that day fall.
