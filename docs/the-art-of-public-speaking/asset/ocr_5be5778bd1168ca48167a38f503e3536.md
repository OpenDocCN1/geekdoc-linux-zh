In fancy he saw the canal already built and the argosies of the world passing through it in the immense evolution of American trade the fulfilment of all his dreams, the reward of all his labors. He, I need not say, an ardent protectionist, never more sincere and devoted during those last days of his life. He regarded reciprocity as the bulkhead of protection—not a breach, but a fulfilment of the law. The treaties which for four years had been preparing under his personal supervision which he regarded as ancillary to the general scheme. He was opposed to any revolutionary plan of change in the existing legislation; he was careful to point out that everything he had done in faithful compliance with the law itself.

In mood of high hope, of generous expectation, he went to Buffalo, and there, on the threshold of eternity, he delivered that memorable speech, worthy for his loftness of tone, his blameless morality, his breadth of view, to be regarded as his testament to the nation. Through all his pride of country and his joy of its success runs the note of solemn warning, as in Kipling's noble hymn, " Lest We Forget."

The next day spent the bolt of doom, and for a week after—in an agony of dread, broken by illusive glimpses of hope that our prayers might be answered—the nation waited for the end. Nothing in the glorious life we saw gradually waning was more admirable and exemplary than its close. The gentle humanity of his words when he saw assailant in danger of summary vengeance, "Do not let them hurt him; chivalrous care that the news should be broken gently to his wife; the fine courtesy with which he apologized for the damage which his death would bring to the great Exhibition; and the heroic resignation of his final words, "It is God's way; His will, not ours, done," were all the instinctive expressions of a nature so lofty and pure that in nobility once softened and enhanced the nation's sense of loss. The Republic grieved over such a son—but is proud of producing him. After all, in spite of his tragic ending, his life was extraordinarily happy.
