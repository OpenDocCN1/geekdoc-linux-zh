The vowel sounds are the most vexing source of errors, especially where diphthongs are found. Who has heard such errors as are hit off in this inimitable verse by Oliver Wendell Holmes:

Learning beyond the reach of hope The careless lips that speak of her erotic exiles from her fair abode the clownish voice that utters less to him who calls his believing in a bath. She pardoned one, our classic city's boast. So at Cambridge, instead of her angry foot

The foregoing examples are all monosyllables, but articulation is frequently the joining of syllables that do not belong to. For example, no one finds it difficult to say beauty, but many persist in pronouncing duty or duty. It is not only from untaught speakers that we hear such slovenly articulations as column, and for pretty, but even great orators occasionally offend quite as unblingly as less noted mortals.

Nearly all such are errors of carelessness, not of pure ignorance—of carelessness because the ear never tries to hear what the lips articulate. It must be exasperating to a foreigner to find that the elemental sound gives him no hint for the pronunciation of bough, cough, thorough, and, we can well forgive even a man of culture who occasionally loses his way amidst the intricacies of English articulation, but there can be no excuse for the slovenly utterance of the simple vowel sounds which form at once the life and the beauty of our language. He who is too lazy to speak distinctly hold his tongue.

The consonant sounds occasion serious trouble only for those who do not look with care at the spelling of words about to be pronounced. Nothing carelessness can account for saying Jacop, Baptist, seven, alwas, or sadisf.
