But Columbus, as he sat upon that and looked out upon the ocean, noticed that the ships, as they sailed away, deeper into the sea the farther they went. And since that time some other "Spanish ships" have sunk into the sea. But Columbus noticed that the tops of the masts dropped out of sight, and he said: "That is the way it is with this hoe handle; if you go around this hoe, to the East Indies." the farther you go the farther you go. I can sail around to the simple—majestic like the simplicity of a mountain in its greatness. Who are the great inventors? They are ever the simple, plain, everyday people who see the need and set about supply it.

I was once lecturing in North Carolina, and the cashier of the bank sat directly behind a lady who wore a very large hat. I said to that audience, "Your wealth is too near to you; you are looking right over it." He whispered to his friend, "Well, then, my wealth is in that hat." A later, as he wrote me, I said, "Wherever is a human need there is a greater fortune than a mine can furnish." He caught my thought, and drew up his plan for a better pin than was in the hat before him, and the pin is now being manufactured. He was offered-five thousand dollars for his patent. That man made his fortune before he got out of that hall. This is the whole question: Do you see a need?

I remember a man up in my native hills, a poor man, for twenty years was helped by the town in his poverty, who owned a wide—spreading maple tree that covered the poor man's cottage like a benediction from high. I remember that tree, for the spring—there were some roguish boys around that neighborhood when I was young—in the spring of the year the man would put a bucket and stretch to catch the maple sap, and I remember that bucket was; and when I was the boys were, or so mean, that they went to that tree before man had gotten out of bed in the morning, and after he had gone to bed at night, and drank up that sweet sap. I could swear they did it. He didn't make a great deal of maple sugar from that tree. But one day he made the sugar so white and crystalline that the visitor did not believe it was maple sugar; thought maple sugar must be red or black.
