Suddenly his eyes fell upon me, and then the good old man came forward and invited me to come up the stand with the town officers. Invited me up the stand! No town officer ever took notice of me before I went to war. Now, I should not say that. One town officer was there who advised the teacher to "whale" me, but I mean no "honorable mention." So I was invited upon the stand with the town officers. I took my seat and let my sword fall the floor, folded my arms across my breast and waited to be received. Napoleon the Fifth! Pride goeth before destruction and a fall. When I had gotten my seat and all became silent through the hall, the chairman of the Selectmost and came forward with great dignity to the table, and we all supposed he would introduce the Congregational minister, who was the only orator in the town, and would give the oration to the returning soldiers. But, friends, you should have seen the surprise that ran over audience when they discovered that this old farmer was going to deliver that oration himself. He had never made a speech in his life before, but he fell into the same error that others had fallen into, he seemed to think that the office would make him an orator. So he had written a speech and walked down the pasture until he had learned it and frightened the cattle, and brought that manuscript with him, and taking it from his pocket, he spread it carefully upon the table. Then he adjusted his spectacles to be sure that he might see it, and walked back on on the platform and then stepped forward like this. He must have studied the subject much, for he assumed an elocutionary attitude; he rested heavily upon his left heel, slightly advanced the right foot, threw back his shoulders, opened the organs of speech, and advanced his right hand at an angle of forty-five. As he stood in that elocutionary attitude this is just the way that speech went, this is it precisely. Some of my friends have asked me if I do not exaggerate it, but I could not exaggerate it. Impossible! This is the way it went; although I am not here for the story but the lesson that is back of it:
