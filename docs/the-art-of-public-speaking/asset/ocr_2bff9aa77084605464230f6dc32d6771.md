Why do we teach history in that way? We ought to teach that however humble the station man may occupy, if he does his full duty in his place, he is just as much entitled to the American people's honor as is a king upon a throne. We do it as a mother did her little boy in New York when he said, "Mamma, what great building is that?" "That is General Grant's tomb." "Who was General Grant?" "He was the man who put down the rebellion." Is that to the way we teach history?

Do you think we would have gained a victory if it had depended on General Grant alone? Oh, no. Why is there a tomb on the Hudson at all? Why, not simply because General Grant was personally a great man himself, but that tomb stands because he was a representative man and represented two hundred thousand who went to death for their nation and many of them as General Grant. That is why beautiful tomb stands on the heights over the Hudson.

I remember an incident that will illustrate this, the only one that I can give to—night. I am ashamed of it, but I don't dare leave it out. I close my eyes now; I look back through the years to 1863; I can see my native town in the Berkshire Hills, I can see cattle—show filled with people; I see the church and the town hall crowded, and bands playing, and flags flying and handkerchiefs streaming—well do I recall at this moment that day. The people had turned out to receive a company of soldiers, and that company marching on the Common. They had served one term in the Civil War and had reenlisted, and they were being received by their native townsmen. I was a boy, but I was captain of that company, puffed out with pride on that day—why, a cambric needle would have burst me into pieces. As I marched on the Common at the head of my company, there was not a man more proud than I. We marched into the town hall and then they seated soldiers down in the center of the house and I took my place on the front seat, and then the town officers filed through the great throng of people, who stood close and packed in that little hall. They came up on the platform, formed a half circle around it, and the mayor of the town, the "chairman of the Selectmen" in New England, took his seat in the middle of half circle.
