Not a person did he know; Had the money—hunger bad— Mad for money, piggish mad; Didn't let a joy divert, Didn't let a sorrow hurt him, His friends and him desert, While he planned and plugged and hurried On his quest for gold and power. Every single wakeful Hour a money thought he'd lower; All as he grew older, And grew bolder, he grew colder. And he thought that day Would take the time to play; But, say—he was wrong. Life's a song; In the spring Youth can sing and can fling; But joys wing When we're older, Like birds when it's colder. The roses were red as he went rushing, And glorious tapestries hung in the sky, And the clover was waving "Neath honey-bees' slaving; A bird there Roundelivery a soft air; But the man couldn't spare Time for gathering flowers, Or resting in bowers, Or gazing at skies that glanced the eyes. So he kept on and swept Through mean, sordid ways. Now he's up to ears in choicent of stocks. He owns endless blocks of Houses and shops, And the stream never stops Pouring into his. I suppose that he hopes Pretty near the top. What I wouldn't stop His ambition title; And yet with my little I don't care to trade With the bargain he made. Just watch him to—day— See him trying to play. He's come back for blue skies. But they're in a new guise— Winter's here, all is gray, The birds are away, The meadows are brown, The leaves lie around, And the gray bout wound With a swirling and whirling Of Waters, is furling Its bosom in ice. And he hasn't the price, With all of his gold, To buy what he sold. He knows now the cost of the spring—time he lost, Of the flowers tossed From his way, And, say, He'd pay Any price if the Could be made so gray.—He can't play.
