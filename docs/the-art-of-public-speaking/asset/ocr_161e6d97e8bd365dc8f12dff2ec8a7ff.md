There are mountains stored with exhaustless treasures; forests—vast and primeval; and rivers that, tumbling and loitering, run wanton to the sea. Of the three essential items of all industries—cotton, iron and wood—that region has easy control. In cotton, a fixed monopoly—in iron, proven supremacy—in timber, reserve supply of the Republic. From this assured and permanent advantage, against which artificial conditions cannot much longer prevail, has grown an amazing system of industries. Not maintained by him contrivance of tariff or capital, afar off the fullest and cheapest source of supply, but resting in divine assurance, within touch of field and mine and forest—not set costly farms from which competition has driven the farmer in despair, but amid cheap sunny lands, rich with agriculture, to which neither season nor soil has a limit—this system of industries is mounting to a splendor that shall dazzle and illuminate the world. That, sir, is the picture and promise of my home—a land better and fairer than I have told you, and but fitting in its material excellence for the loyal and gentle quality of its citizenship. Against that, we have New England, recruiting the Republic from its sturdy towns, shaking from its overcrowded hews swarms of workers, and touching this land all over with its energy and its courage. And yet—in the Eldorado of which I have told you fifteen cent of its lands are cultivated, its mines scarcely touched, and its population scant that, were it equidistant, the sound of the human voice could not be heard from Virginia to Texas—while on the threshold of nearly every house in New England stands a sock, seeing with troubled eyes, some new land which to carry its modest patrimony, the strange fact remains that in 1880 the South had fewer–born citizens than she had in 1870—fewer in '70 than in '60. Why is this? Why is it, sir, though the section line is now but a mist that the breath may dispel, fewer men of the North have crossed it over to the South, then when it was crimson with the best blood of the Republic, or even when the slaveholder stood guard every inch of its way?
