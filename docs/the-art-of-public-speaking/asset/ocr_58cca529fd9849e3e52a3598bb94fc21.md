What we secretly cherish in our hearts is a symbol of what we shall receive. Our trains of thoughts are hurrying us on to destiny. When you see the flag fluttering to the South, you know the wind is coming from the North. When you see the straws and papers being carried to the Northward you realize the wind is blowing out of the South. It is just as easy to ascertain a man's thoughts by observing the tendency of his character.

Let not be suspected for one moment that all this is merely a preachment on the question of morals. It is that, but much more, for it touches the whole man—his imaginative nature, his ability to control his feelings, the mastery of his thinking faculties, and—perhaps most largely—his power to will and to carry his violations into effective action.

Right thinking constantly assumes that the will sits enthroned to execute the dictates of mind, conscience and heart. _Never tolerate for an instant suggestion that your will is not absolutely efficient._ The way to will is will—and the very first time you are tempted to break a worthy resolution—and you will be, may you be certain of—make your fight then and there. You cannot afford to lose that fight. You must win it—don't swerve for an instant, but keep that if life depended on the victory; and indeed your personality may actually lie in the balances!

Your success or failure as a speaker will be determined largely by your thoughts and your mental attitude. The present writer had a student of limited education one of his classes in public speaking. He proved to be a very poor speaker; and the instructor could conscientiously do little but point out faults. However, the young man was warned not to be discouraged. With sorrow in his voice and the essence of earnestness beaming from his eyes, he replied: "I will not be discouraged! I want so badly to know how to speak!" was warm, and from heart. And he did keep on trying—and developed into a creditable speaker.
