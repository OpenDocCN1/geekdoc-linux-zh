An observant auditor will be interested in noticing the various devices a monologist will use the first round of laughter and applause. He works so hard because he knows an audience of units is an audience of indifferent critics, but once they get to laughing together and each single laugh sweeps a number of others with him, while the theatre is aroar and the entertainer has scored. These are meretricious schemes, to be sure, and do not savor in the least of inspiration, but crowds have not changed in their nature in a thousand years and the one law holds for the greatest preacher and the pettiest stump—speaker—you must fuse your audience or will the devices of the great orator may not be so obvious as those of the vaudeville monologist, but the principle is the same: he tries to strike some universal note that will have all his hearers feeling alike at the same time.

The evangelist knows this when he has the soloist sing some touching just before the address. Or he will have the entire congregation sing, and that is the psychology of "Now_every_body sing!" for who know will not join in the song are yet outside the crowd. Many a time has the popular evangelist stopped in the middle of his talk, (and a sensitive speaker can feel that condition most depressingly) and suddenly demanded that everyone arise and sing, or repeat a familiar passage, or read in unison; or perhaps he has subtly left the thread of his discourse to tell a story that, from long experience, he knew would not fail to bring his hearers to a common feeling.

These things are important resources for the speaker, and happy is he who uses them worthy and not a despicable charlatan. The difference between a demagogue and a leader is not so much a matter of method as of principle. Even the most dignified speaker must recognize the eternal laws of human nature. You are no means to become a trickster on the platform—far from it!—but don't kill your speech with dignity. It is icily cool as silly as to rant. Do neither, but appeal to those world-old elements in your audience that have been recognized by all great speakers
