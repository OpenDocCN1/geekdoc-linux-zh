But when Jacob Astor saw a lady pass, with her shoulders back and her head up, as if she did not care if the whole world looked on her, she studied her bonnet; and before that bonnet was out of sight he knew the shape of the frame and the color of the trimmings, the curl of the—something on a bonnet. Sometimes I try to describe a woman's bonnet, but it is out of style. So John Jacob Astor went to the store and said: "Now, put in the show window just such a bonnet as I describe to you because," said, "I have just seen a lady who likes just such a bonnet. Do not make up any more till I come back." And he went out again and sat on that in the bench, and another lady of a different form and complexion faced with a bonnet of different shape and color, of course. "Now," said he, "put such a bonnet as that in the show window." He didn't fill his show window with hats and bonnets which drive people away and then sit in the back of the store and bowl because the people go somewhere else to trade. He didn't put a hat or bonnet in that show window the like of which he had not seen before it was made up.

In our city especially are great opportunities for manufacturing, and the time has come when the line is drawn very sharply between the stockholders of the factory and their employees. Now, friends, there has also come a discouraging gloom upon this country and the labor men are beginning to feel that they are being held down by a crust over their heads through which they find it impossible to break, and aristocratic money—owner himself is so far away that he will never descend to their assistance. That is the thought that is in the minds of our people. But, friends, never in the history of our country was there an opportunity so great for the poor to get rich as there is now in the city of Philadelphia. The very fact that they get discouraged is what prevents them from getting rich. That is all there is to. The road is open, and let us keep it open between the poor and the rich. I know that labor unions have two problems to contend with, and there is only one way to solve them. The labor unions are doing as to prevent its solving as are the capitalists to—day, and there are positively two sides to it.
