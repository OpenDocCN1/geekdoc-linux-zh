# ABOUT THIS CHAPTER

Now that you have learned about assessment techniques, how do you put them together? How do you make a test? What should you tell students about your test so they will prepare and do their best? Should you teach students how to take tests? What do you do with students who experience test anxiety? Are there ways to use the responses students make to your items to improve the items and your teaching? This chapter discusses these issues.

In the first section of the chapter you will learn about preparing students so they can do their best on your assessments. To perform well, students need more than subject-matter knowledge. They need to know how to take

tests and how to handle anxieties that may arise as a result of the evaluation process. You shouldn’t assume that all students automatically know how to do their best

The second section of the chapter discusses assembling and preparing for administration of your assessment. We discuss practical matters that will improve the validity of the assessment results.

In the final section, you will learn how to use information from students’ responses to your items to judge how successful the items were. These responses can help you to improve your items, fix what was flawed, and increase the validity of the results. They can help improve your teaching also.
