The first 14 items are pictures, which the examinee names.
The next 32 items are words, which the examinee defines. |

Examinee identifies what is funny or foolish in the pictures. |
| 14. | **Verbal Relations (18 items)**
Examinee is presented with four concept names and asked to describe how the first three are alike but different from the fourth. |

Lower level items require putting shapes into holes; higher level items require modeling patterns using cubes. |

At lower levels, examinee copies pattern built by examiner. At higher levels examinee copies geometric patterns using pencil and paper. |

Examinee is given a matrix and selects the object, letter, or pattern that completes the relationship shown in the matrix. |

| 3. | **Quantitative (40 items)**
Examinee answers arithmetic questions. |
| 12. | **Number Series (29 items)**
Examinee is shown a series of numbers and must induce the pattern and state what two numbers come next. |

Examinee is given a set of numbers, one or more arithmetic operators, and an equal sign. Examinee must use these to make a true equation. |

# INDIVIDUALLY ADMINISTERED TESTS OF GENERAL SCHOLASTIC APTITUDES

# Stanford-Binet Intelligence Scale

History The Stanford-Binet Intelligence Scale is a widely used, individually administered test of general scholastic aptitude. First prepared in 1916 by Lewis M. Terman as a translation

and revision of the Binet-Simon Scale, the test was revised in 1937 (with Maud A. Merrill), revised again in 1960, renamed in 1972, and revised and renamed in 1986.
