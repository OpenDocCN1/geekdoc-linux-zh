# Figure 10.5 Example of a simple rating scale to use as a scoring rubric for assessing the quality of a student's oral or written presentation of an argument.

# 1. Did the student clearly state the thesis or main point?

0 Did not state or imply the main point or thesis
1 Implied the main point or thesis but did not state it clearly
2 Stated the main idea or thesis clearly but only matter-of-factly
3 Stated the main idea or thesis clearly, enthusiastically, and interestingly for the audience

# 2. Did the student define the key terms when necessary to do so?

0 No attempt to define key terms, even when it was necessary to do so
1 Attempts to define the key terms, but was not effective in doing so
2 Clearly and effectively defines the necessary key terms
NA The presentation was such that defining key terms was unnecessary

# 3. Did the student use sound reasoning to support the main point or thesis?

0 Offered no supporting reasons for the thesis or position taken
1 Supporting reasons given but they are off-target or they do not lend direct support for the thesis
2 Gives relevant supporting reasons, but could have given better or more diverse reasons
3 Gives excellent supporting reasons, good diversity, directly applicable

# 4. Did the student use relevant facts in appropriate ways to support the thesis?

0 Gave no facts, used completely irrelevant facts, or cited facts from noncredible sources
1 Gave facts to support the thesis but the generalizations from them were weak, somewhat inappropriate, or incomplete; facts are cited from credible sources
2 Gives several appropriate facts that support the thesis, generalizations from facts are appropriate, sources for facts are credible
3 Gives highly appropriate facts, excellent generalizations from facts that support the thesis, sources of facts are credible, facts used well in making the argument

# 5. Did the student portray and evaluate alternative positions fairly?

0 Alternative positions not mentioned and not evaluated
1 Alternative positions are mentioned but they are either not portrayed fairly, not evaluated properly, or not relevant to the thesis
2 Some of the relevant alternative positions are mentioned, they are portrayed properly, and evaluated properly; other important alternative positions are omitted
3 All relevant and important alternative positions are mentioned, presented fairly, and evaluated properly

# 6. Did the student rebut the alternative positions well?

0 No attempt was made at rebuttal
1 Attempts at rebuttal were made but they are ineffective or incomplete
2 Rebutted adequately, but could have been more effective in explaining the shortcomings of the alternatives
3 Rebutted well, was effective, clear about the inadequacies of the alternatives, convincingly presented

# 7. Did the student present a well-organized argument?

0 Organization was disconnected, lacked direction, confused the thesis or main point
1 Organization was clear, but not effective, connections to thesis or main point were not sharp; details were often out of place
2 Organization was good and contributed to the effectiveness of the argument, but a few details were out of place; sometimes the connections to the main point were weak or out of place
3 Organization was very clear and enhanced the argument; the presentation kept the audience interest focused on the main issues
