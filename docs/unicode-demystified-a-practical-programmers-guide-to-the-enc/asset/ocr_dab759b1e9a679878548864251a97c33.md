# Titlebar

A titlebar (Figures 6-6 and 8-4) appears at the top of most windows and in many controls the window it is attached to. By changing the window decoration (page 325), you can change the appearance and function of a titlebar, but you will usually have at least the functionality of the buttons shown in Figure 6-6. Titlebars under the default Red Hat Blueberry theme look the same under KDE and GNOME (Figure 6-6). Figure 8-4 shows a titlebar with KDE2 decoration (K menu: Control Center->Look & Feel->Window Decoration->General->Window Decoration).

# Push Pin

The Push Pin (Figure 8-4) “sticks” the window you are working with to the glass of your monitor: The window appears in the same position, no matter what workspace you view. Use the Push Pin to place a clock on every window area you work with. The Push Pin is also a convenient way to move a window from one workspace to another: Click the Push Pin to stick the window and change workspaces; click the Push Pin to unstick the window in the new workspace.

# Scrollbar

When are not using Bluecurve window decoration (page 325), the scrollbar (Figure 8-5) has one more button than most. At the bottom—or if it is horizontal—of the scrollbar is the typical down/right button that scrolls the display forward or left: moves the up/left on the screen. Just above this button is an extra up/left button. This button performs the same function as the up/left button at the top/left of the scrollbar—scrolls the backward/right—but keeps you from having to move the mouse pointer the height/width of the image to move between the up and down buttons. The slider is standard, with its height proportional to the amount of text or other material in the buffer.
