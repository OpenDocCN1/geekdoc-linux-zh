Unless you specify otherwise, a sort field extends to the end of the line. from the beginning of the line (and skip zero fields), use a +0 sort field speculator, as shown in the next example, which also shows one problem to avoid when you are using sort. In this example the objective is to sort by manufacturer and then by price within manuf. The command line instructs sort to sort on the entire second pass, sorting on the fifth field all lines first-pass sort fields were the same (+4):

```
$ sort +0 +4 cars
chevy impala 65 85 1550
chevy nova 79 60 3000
chevy nova 80 50 3500
fiat 600 115 450
ford bronco 83 25 9500
ford ltd 83 15 10500
ford mustang 65 10000
ford thundbd 84 10 17000
honda accord 81 73 6000
plym fury 77 73 2500
toyota tercel 82 180 750
volvo gl1 78 102 9850
```

Because no two lines are the same, sort makes only one pass, sorting on each line. (If two lines differed only in the fifth field, they would be sorted properly on the first pass anyway, so the second pass would be unnecessary.) Look at the lines with the 1td and mustang. They are sorted by the second field rather than the fifth, demonstrating that sort never made a second pass and never sorted by the fifth field.
The next example forces the first-pass sort to stop just before the second field by defining the end of the first sort field (-1). Now the 1td and mustang are properly sorted by price. But look at the bronco. It is less expensive than the other Fords, but has that most. The sort utility put the list in ASCII collating sequence order, not in numeric order: 9500 comes after 10000 and 9 comes after:

```
$ sort +0 -1 +4 cars
chevy impala 65 85 1550
chevy nova 79 60 3000
chevy nova 80 50 3500
fiat 600 65 450
ford mustang 65 45 10000
ford ltd 83 15 10500
ford thundbd 84 10 17000
ford bronco 83 25 9500
honda accord 81 30 6000
plym fury 77 73 2500
toyota tercel 82 180 750
volvo gl1 78 102 9850
```
