# Scope of Coverage

A Practical Guide to Red Hat® Linux® covers a wide range of topics, showing you how to use Red Hat Linux from your screen and keyboard.

+   - Part II, Chapters 6–17, covers intermediate and advanced aspects of Red Hat Linux: GUI interfaces, networking, the web and emacs editors, the Bourne Again, TC, and Z shells and shell scripts, programming tools, and system administration.

+   - Part III offers a comprehensive, detailed reference to more than 85 GNU/Linux utilities, with numerous examples. If you are already familiar with UNIX/Linux, this part of the book will be a valuable, easy-to-use reference. If you are not an experienced user, you will find Part III a useful supplement while you are mastering the subjects and tutorials in Parts I and II.

+   - Part IV includes appendices on regular expressions, where to find help on the Web, system security, POSIX, and the definition of free software. This part also includes a glossary and the index.

# Experienced Users May Want to Skim Part I

If you have used a UNIX/Linux system before, you may want to skim over or skip some or all of the chapters in Part I. All readers should take a look at “Conventions” (page 24), which explains the typographic and layout conventions that this book uses, and “Documentation” (page 42) which points you toward local and remote sources of GNU/Linux and Red Hat documentation.

The advanced material in each chapter is presented in sections marked “Optional,” which you are encouraged to return after mastering the basic material presented in the chapter. Review exercises are included at the end of each chapter for readers who want to hone their skills. Some of the exercises test your understanding of material covered in the chapter, whereas others challenge you to go beyond the material to develop a more thorough understanding. Answers to some exercises are at sobell.com.
