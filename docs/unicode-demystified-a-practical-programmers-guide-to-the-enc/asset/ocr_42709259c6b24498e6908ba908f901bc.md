# Read the Instructions

Find, print out you like, read, and follow the installation instructions no matter if they are printed in manuals, in PostScript or plain text files, on a CD-ROM, or on the Internet. This section is simply an overview of some parts of the installation process and does not replace the instructions that are part of the Red Hat distribution you are installing.

# Downloading, Burning, and Installing a CD-ROM Set

You can download and burn your Red Hat CD-ROMs. Although you will not get hard-copy manuals or free, limited customer support, you will not pay anything. One of the beauties of free software (Appendix E) is that it is always easy for obtain and use its GNU/Linux code by providing ISO images (refer to “ISO9660” on page 1475) of its CD-ROMs online. These files are large, about 650 megabytes, they take days to download unless you have something faster than a 56k modem, in which case they can take hours.
Red Hat's FTP site and mirror sites maintain the ISO images you need (see note 6 on page 905). The pathnames in Table 17-1 outline the usual structure of the hierarchy that holds the Red Hat 8.0 release, named Pyche. When download a different release of Red Hat, you need to make appropriate changes to these pathnames, all of which are relative to ftp.redhat.com or the redhat directory of the mirror site.
