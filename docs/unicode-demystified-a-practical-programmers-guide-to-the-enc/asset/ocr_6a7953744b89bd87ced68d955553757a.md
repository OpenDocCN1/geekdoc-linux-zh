Brace expansion is useful when there is a long preamble or postamble. The following example copies the four files main.c, f1.c, f2.c, and tmp.c, located in the /usr/local/src/C/ to the working directory:

```
$ cp /usr/local/src/C/{main,f1,f2,tmp}.c
```

Brace expansion can also create directories with related names. Because the directories do not already exist, pathname expansion does not work in this case:

```
$ ls -1
total 3
-rw-rw-r-- 1 alex group 14 Jan 22 08:54 file1
-rw-rw-r-- 1 alex group 14 Jan 22 08:54 file2
-rw-rw-r-- 1 alex group 14 Jan 22 08:55 file3
$ mkdir version{A,B,C,D,E}
total 8
-rw-rw-r-- 1 alex group 14 Jan 22 08:54 file1
-rw-rw-r-- 1 alex group 14 Jan 22 08:54 file2
-rw-rw-r-- 1 alex group 14 Jan 22 08:55 file3
drwxwxrxr-x 2 alex group 1024 Jan 25 13:27 versionA
drwxwxrxr-x 2 alex group 1024 Jan 25 13:27 versionB
drwxwxrxr-x 2 alex group 1024 Jan 25 13:27 versionC
drwxwxrxr-x 2 alex group 1024 Jan 25 13:27 versionD
drwxwxrxr-x 2 alex group 1024 Jan 25 13:27 versionE
```

If ambiguous reference notation had been used to specify the directories instead of the preceding notation, the result would be very different (and not what was desired):

```
$ ls -1
total 3
-rw-rw-r-- 1 alex group 14 Jan 22 08:54 file1
-rw-rw-r-- 1 alex group 14 Jan 22 08:54 file2
-rw-rw-r-- 1 alex group 14 Jan 22 08:55 file3
$ mkdir version[A-E]
ls -1
total 4
-rw-rw-r-- 1 alex group 14 Jan 22 08:54 file1
-rw-rw-r-- 1 alex group 14 Jan 22 08:54 file2
-rw-rw-r-- 1 alex group 14 Jan 22 08:55 file3
drwxwxrxr-x 2 alex group 1024 Jan 25 13:38 version[A-E]
```

Because it found no filenames matching version[A-E], the shell passed string to mkdir, which created a directory with that name.
