After starting info, press the SPACE a few times to scroll the display. The asterisk at the end end of the line means that this is the beginning of a menu item. Following the asterisk is by a colon, the name of the package (in parentheses) that the menu belongs to, 9 other information, and, on the right, a description of the item.

The name of the item is what you type in a menu command to view information, followed by a RETURN. When you type m (for name), the cursor moves to the bottom line of the window or screen and displays Menu item:. Typing sleep displays s1leep on that line, and pressing RETURN takes you to the menu item you have chosen.

Figure 2-8 shows the top node of information on sleep. A node is one group of information that you can scroll through with the SPACE bar. To get to the next node, press n. Press p to get to the previous node. You can always press d to get to the initial menu, shown in Figure 2-6.

As you read and learn about new utilities, you can use man or info to find out about the utilities. Although you may have access to a hardcopy (GNU/Linux manual, the electronic copies are generally more up-to-date. If you can print PostScript documents, you can print a manual page with the utility using the -t option (for example, man -echo lpr). Better yet, use your browser to look at the documentation on the Red Hat Documentation CD-ROM (page 48), www.redhat.com and www.tldp.org, and print the information from your browser.
