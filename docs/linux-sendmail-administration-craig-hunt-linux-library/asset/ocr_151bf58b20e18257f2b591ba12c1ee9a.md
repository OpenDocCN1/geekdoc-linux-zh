Rulesets can be thought of as subroutines, or functions, designed to process e-mail addresses. They are called mail definitions, from individual rulesets, from header definitions, or directly by the Sendmail process. Six rulesets are called directly by Sendmail for normal address processing:

+   - Ruleset canary, also known as ruleset 3, is called first to prepare all addresses by the other rulesets.

+   - Ruleset parse, also known as ruleset 0, is applied to the mail delivery address to convert it to the mail (host, user) triple, which contains the name of the mail that will deliver the mail, the recipient hostname, and the recipient username. Ruleset parse contains the rewrite rules that select which mail will deliver the message.

+   - Ruleset sender, also known as ruleset 1, is applied to all sender addresses.

+   - Ruleset recipient, also known as ruleset 2, is applied to all recipient addresses.

+   - Ruleset final, also known as ruleset 4, is called last to convert all addresses internal formats into external formats.

+   - Ruleset localaddr, also known as ruleset 5, is applied to local addresses after alias processing is completed.

NOTE sendmail.cf files do not contain all of these rulesets.

There are three basic types of addresses: delivery addresses, sender addresses, and recipient addresses. A recipient address and a delivery address sound like the same thing, but there is a difference. Think of a mailing list. There can be many rulesets for a piece of mail, but mail is delivered to only one person at a time. The recipient of the one person to whom the current piece of mail is being delivered is the delivery address. Different rulesets are used to process the different types of addresses.
