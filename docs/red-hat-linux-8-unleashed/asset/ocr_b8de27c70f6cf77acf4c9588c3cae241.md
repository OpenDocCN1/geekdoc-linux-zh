# Red Hat Linux 8 Unleashed

The GPL is the brainchild of Richard M. Stallman, the founder of the Free Software Foundation. Stallman, the famous author of the emacs editing environment and gcc compiler system, crafted the GPL to ensure that covered software would always be free and available in source code. The GPL is the guiding document for Linux and its ownership, distribution, and copyright. Torvalds holds the rights to the Linux trademark, thanks to a combination of his generosity, the Internet, thousands of programmers around the world, GNU software, and the GNU GPL, Linux will remain free and unencumbered by licensing or royalty issues. See the later section “Licensing” to learn more about the GNU GPL and other software licenses.

Linux, pronounced “lich-nucks”, is free software. Combining the Linux kernel with GNU software tools—drivers, utilities, user interfaces, and other software such as The XFree86 Project’s XFree86 X Window System—creates a Linux distribution. There are many different Linux distributions from different vendors, many are derived from or closely mimic Red Hat Inc.’s distribution of Linux, Red Hat Linux.

# What Is Red Hat Linux?

Red Hat Linux is the product of Red Hat, Inc. in 1994, Marc Ewing and Bob Young combined forces to create Red Hat (named after a Cornell University lacrosse team hat) in order to develop, release, and market an easily installed, easily managed, and easy-to-use Linux distribution. Five years later, Durham, N.C.-based Red Hat, Inc., would have one of the most successful Initial Public Offerings (IPOs) on the stock market. The company, based in Raleigh, N.C., has grown from a handful of employees to more than 500 in 22 locations around the world.

Red Hat, Inc, was one of the first to adopt, promote, and use open source as a business model for supporting development, technical service, support, and sales of free software to the computer industry. Its business practices have spawned a shift in paradigm of proprietary attitudes prevalent in the monopolistic software industry, and the company is a role model and business leader in the open source movement. You learn more about Red Hat, Inc. and Red Hat Linux in Chapter 1, “Introducing Red Hat Linux.”
