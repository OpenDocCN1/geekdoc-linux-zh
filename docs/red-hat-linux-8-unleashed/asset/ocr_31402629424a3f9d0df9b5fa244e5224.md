Streaming audio is handled in Red Hat Linux in the .m3u format. The MPEG formats are also used for streaming audio. Red Hat doesn't provide any applications that use streaming audio, however. one is the Real Player, available from http://www.real.com/realone/. An RPM version compatible with Red Hat is available from http://ftp.ibibilio.org/pub/Linux/distributions/contrib/texstar/linux/distributions/mandrake/9.0/rpm/realplayer-8.0-2tex.i586.rpm.

An excellent resource for music and sound in Linux is http://linux-sound.org/. The Network Audio section of Web site contains an extensive list of streaming audio applications.

The Icecast application, not provided with Red Hat, is a popular streaming audio server. You can use Icecast to stream your MP3 music collection over your home LAN. You can learn more about Icecast at http://www.icecast.org/. A nice tutorial on Icecast is available at http://www.linuxnet.com/en/issue4/micecast1.html.

# Viewing TV and Video

You can use Red Hat Linux tools and applications to view movies and other video presentations on your PC. This section presents some TV and motion picture video software included with the Red Hat distribution you received with this book.

In addition to the information you read here, you should review Chapter 3, “Installing Red Hat Linux,” and Chapter 6, “The X Window System,” to learn more about installing and configuring your graphics card.

# TV and Video Hardware

To watch TV and video content on your PC, you must install a supported TV card. A complete list of TV and video cards supported by Red Hat Linux is at http://www.exploits.org/v41.

Linux support for TV display from video cards that have a TV-out jack is poor. That support must come from the X11 driver, not from a video device that Video4Linux supports a device driver. Some of the combo-tuner/video display cards have support, including the Matrox Marvel, the Matrox Rainbow Runner G-Series, and the RivaTV cards.
