Communication by electronic mail—commonly referred to as email—is easily the most popular activity on the Internet and intranets. It is fast, free (apparently), and easy to use. Hidden behind seeming ease, there is a complex infrastructure that can be accessed and controlled with the power of Red Hat Linux.

In this chapter, you learn how to configure Red Hat to act as an email server. You also take a look at the various email clients that Red Hat makes available for you, and you learn some techniques for working with them. Although you might not need to administer a mail server, understanding how they work can make you a better mail user.

# A Brief Introduction to Email Terms

The world is filled with acronyms and technical terms. The first of these covered here describes the components of the email system. Some applications combine features from these three groups.

The Mail Transfer Agent (MTA) works behind the scenes to send and receive email between computers. Examples would be Sendmail, Postfix, Exim, or Qmail.

The Mail User Agent (MUA), or mail client, allows the user to read and compose email. This is the application most users are familiar with; it presents a text or graphical interface to the user. Examples would be pine, Mozilla Mail, kmail, or Evolution.

The Mail Delivery Agent (MDA) is similar to an MTA, but does not handle deliveries between systems and does not provide an interface to the user as an MUA. Examples would be Procmail or SpamAssassin; both provide filtering services to the MTA.
