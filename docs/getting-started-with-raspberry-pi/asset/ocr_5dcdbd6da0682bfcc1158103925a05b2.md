# Device Example: Super I/O Chip

To get a first taste of Linux-Irda, let's get two laptops talking to each other over IR. Each laptop is IR-enabled via National Semiconductor's NSC PC87382 Super I/O chip.[²] UART1 in Figure 16.4 shows the connection scenario. The PC87382 chip can work in both SIR and FIR modes. We will look at each in turn.

> [2] Super I/O chipsets typically support several peripherals besides IrDA, such as serial ports, parallel ports, Musical Instrument Digital Interface (MIDI), and floppy controllers.
