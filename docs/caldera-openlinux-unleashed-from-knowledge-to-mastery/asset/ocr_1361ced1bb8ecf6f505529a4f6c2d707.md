The future is unknown and no one knows what might happen. Perhaps your system goes \ndown in the middle of making a backup, and you’ve been using only a single backup \nmedium, which might be destroyed as well. This would leave you with nothing of your \nhard work. Or perhaps while restoring something, you notice you forgot to back up \nsomething important, such as the payroll for your division. Maybe your backups all went \nﬁ ne, but your tape drive broke, and they no longer make tape drives to read that kind of \ntape. As you can see, a proper backup protocol is of crucial importance to the protection \nof your investment. In fact, when it comes to backups, paranoia can be said to be a con-\nservative disposition.

Because a good backup strategy involves both archives and backups, you should under-\nstand the difference between the two. Backups are ﬁle operations to save your data at \nregular intervals, either in whole or incrementally. Archives are ﬁle operations to save \nyour data for long periods of time.

This chapter covers the qualities of a good backup and the process of selecting a backup \nmedium. Some backup tools will be discussed, along with strategies for incremental and \nfull backups.

# # Qualities of a Good Backup

In the best of all possible worlds, backups would be perfectly reliable, always available, \neasy to use, and extremely fast. In the real world, however, trade-offs must be made. For \nexample, backups stored oﬀsite are good for disaster recovery but are not always avail-\nable. In order to choose the proper backup strategy, you must look at the following fac-\ntors:

+   - Cost
+   - Reliability
+   - Speed
+   - Availability
+   - Usability

Cost is important because an inexpensive medium will allow you to have adequate stor-\nage space for your data.
