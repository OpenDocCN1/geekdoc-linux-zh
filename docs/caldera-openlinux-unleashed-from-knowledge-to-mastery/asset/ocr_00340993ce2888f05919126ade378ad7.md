# Preventing Duplicate News Postings

When you connect to your ISP and request newsgroup updates, one of two methods is usually used to ensure that you don't get duplicate postings when you use your newsreader. The most common technique is called *have/sendme*, which informs the machine at the other end of your newssfeed (such as your ISP's server) which message IDs you already have and which ones are lacking. Then, only the missing postings are transferred to your Linux machine.

The *have/sendme* protocol is excellent for updating a few newsgroups, but starts to bog down dramatically when handling very large volumes of newsgroups. For this reason, a method called *batching* is used to transfer large newsgroup feeds. With batching, everything on one end of the newssfeed is transferred as a block. Your machine then sorts through the download, discarding any duplicates. Batching adds more overload to your local Linux machine than *have/sendme*, but it involves a lot less messaging between the two ends of the newssfeeds. As is often the case, the choice of update protocol involves choosing the most efficient use of your own resources (bandwidth vs. local processing power).

# Pushing and Pulling

Two other terms are used to describe the transfer of newsgroup postings from one machine to another: pushing and pulling. These terms apply especially to smaller systems that don't download the entire newssfeed every day. Your system can download articles from the newssfeed using the *have/sendme* protocol, a technique called *pushing*. Alternatively, your machine can request specific postings or entire newsgroups from the newssfeed based on the date of arrival of the posting, a technique called *pulling*.
