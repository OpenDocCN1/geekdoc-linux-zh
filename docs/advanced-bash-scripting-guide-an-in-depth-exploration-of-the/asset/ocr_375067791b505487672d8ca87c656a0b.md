# Advanced Bash-Scripting Guide

The "fog index" of a passage of text estimates its reading difficulty, as a number corresponding roughly to a school grade level. For example, a passage with a fog index of 12 should be comprehensible to anyone with 12 years of schooling.

The Gunning version of the fog index uses the following algorithm.

+   1. Choose a section of the text at least 100 words in length.
2. Count the number of sentences (a portion of sentence truncated by the boundary of the section counts as one).
3. Find the average number of words per sentence.

```
AVE_WDS_SEN = TOTAL_WORDS / SENTENCES
4. Count the number of "difficult" words in the segment – those containing at 3 syllables.
Divide this quantity by total words to get the proportion of words.
```

```
PRO_DIFF_WORDS = LONG_WORDS / TOTAL_WORDS
5. The Gunning fog index is the sum of the two quantities, multiplied by 0.4, then rounded to the nearest integer.
```

```
G_FOG_INDEX = int (0.4 * (AVE_WDS_SEN + PRO_DIFF_WORDS) )
```

Step 4 is by far the most difficult portion of the exercise. There exist various algorithms for estimating the syllable count of a word. A rule-of-thumb might consider the number of letters in a word and vowel-consonant mix.

A strict interpretation of the Gunning fog index does not count compound words and proper nouns as "difficult" words, but this would enumerate the script.

# Calculating PI using Buffon's Needle

The Eighteenth French mathematician Buffon came up with a new experiment. Repeated a needle of length l onto a wooden floor of long and parallel boards. The cracks separating the equal-width floorboards are a fixed distance d apart. Keep track of the total drops and the number of times the needle intersects a crack on the floor. The ratio of these two quantities turns out to a fractional multiple of PI.

In the spirit of Example 16-50, write a script that runs a Monte Carlo simulation of Buffon. To simplify, make the needle length equal to the distance between the cracks, n = d.

Hint: there are actually two variables: the distance from the center of the needle to the nearest crack, and the inclination angle of the needle to crack. You may use bc to handle the calculations.
