The techies among say the Internet is a worldwide network of networks. The term internet (without capitalization) is a short form of internetworking — The Internet Protocol (IP) was designed with connecting many separate networks.

In physical of connections, the Internet is similar to a network of highways and roads. This similarity is what prompted the popular press to dub the Internet "Information Superhighway." Just as the network of highways and roads includes some interstate highways, state roads, and many more residential streets, the Internet has very high-capacity networks (for example, a 10-Gbps backbone can handle 10 billion bits per second) and a large number of lower-capacity networks ranging from 56 Kbps dial-connections to 45 Mbps T3 links. The high-capacity network is the backbone of the Internet.

In terms of management, the Internet is not run by a single organization, nor is it managed by any central computer. You can view the Internet as a "network of networks" managed collectively by thousands of cooperating organizations. Yes, a collection of networks managed by thousands of organizations sounds amazing, but it works!
