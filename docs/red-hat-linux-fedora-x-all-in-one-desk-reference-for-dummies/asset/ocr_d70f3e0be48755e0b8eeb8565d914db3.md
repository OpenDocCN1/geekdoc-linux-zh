# Connecting with a Cable Modem 281

The cable modem places upstream data — data that's being sent from your PC to the Internet — in a different channel than the downstream data that's coming from the Internet to your PC. By design, the speed of downstream data transfers is much higher than that of upstream transfers. The assumption is that people download more stuff from the Internet than they upload (Probably true for most of us).

The coaxial cable that carries all hundreds of cable TV channels to your home is a very capable signal carrier. In particular, the coaxial cable can carry signals a huge range of frequencies — hundreds of megertz (MHz). Each TV channel requires 6 MHz — and the coaxial cable carries hundreds of such channels. The cable modem places the data in a small frequency band and expects to receive the data in a whole other frequency band.

At the end of your cable connection to the Internet is the Modem Termination System (CMTS) — also known as the head end — that your company installs at a local facility (refer Figure 1-4). The CMTS connects your TV network to the Internet. It extracts the upstream data sent by your modem (and those of your neighbors also), and sends all of it to the Internet. The CMTS also puts digital data into the upstream channels so your cable modem can extract data and provide it to your PC via the Ethernet card.

Cable modems can receive downstream data at the rate of about 30 Mbps and send data upstream at around 3 Mbps. However, all the cable modems in a neighborhood share the same downstream capacity. Each cable modem filters out — separates — the data it needs from the stream of data that the CMTS sends out. Cable modem follow a modern standard called DOCSIS, which stands for Data Over Cable Service Interface. You can buy any DOCSIS-compliant modem and use it with your cable service internet; all you need to do is call the company and give them the modem's identifying information so that the CMTS can recognize and initialize the modem.

In practice, with a cable modem you get downstream transfer rates of around 1.5 Mbps and upstream rates of 128 Kbps. These are maximum rates and your transfer rate is typically lower, depending on how many users in your neighborhood are using cable modems at the same time.

If you want to check your downstream transfer speed, go to bandwidth place.com/speedtest and click the link to start the test. For your cable modem connection (for example), the tests reported a downstream transfer rate of about 1.4 Mbps.
