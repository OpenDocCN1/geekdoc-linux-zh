# Connecting with DSL 277

Because of the need to set up your line at the central office, it takes some time after you place an order to get your line ready for DSL.

The first step for you is to check out DSL providers that provide service and see if you can actually get the service. Because DSL can work only over certain distances—typically less than 2.5 miles—between your location and the central office, you have to check if you are within that distance limit. Contact your phone company to verify. You may be able to check this availability on the Web. Try typing into Google (www.google.com) the words DSL, availability, and your local phone company's name. You probably get a DSL site where you can type in your phone number to find out DSL is available for your number.

If DSL is available, you can look for the types of service — ADSL DSL and the pricing. The price depends on the download and upload speeds you want. Sometimes phone companies offer a simple residential DSL (basically the Gite form of DSL) with a 1500/128 speed rating—meaning you can download up to 1,500 Kbps and upload at 128 Kbps. Of course, these are the maximums, and your mileage may vary.
