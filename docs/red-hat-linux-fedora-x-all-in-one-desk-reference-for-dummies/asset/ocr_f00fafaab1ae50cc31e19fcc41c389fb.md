DSL Modem Your PC

Your PC sends digital data over the Ethernet connection to the DSL modem. The DSL modem sends the digital data at different frequencies than those used by the analog voice. The voice signals occupy a small portion of all the frequencies the phone line can carry. DSL uses the higher frequencies to transfer digital data, both voice and data can travel on the same phone line.

![](bbox=[0.135, 0.605, 0.195, 0.695])

The distance between your home and the central office — the loop length — is a factor in DSL's performance. Unfortunately, the phone line can reliably carry the DSL signals over only a limited distance—three miles or less, which means you can get DSL service only if your home (or office) is located within three miles of your phone central office. Your phone company can tell you whether your location can be DSL or not. It has a Web site where you can type in your phone number and get a response about DSL availability. For example, www.dslavailability.com for U.S. locations.
