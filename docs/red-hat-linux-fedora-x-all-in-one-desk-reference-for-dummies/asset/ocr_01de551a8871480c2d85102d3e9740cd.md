# Setting Dial-Up Networking 287

Before you dial out an external modem, you have to make sure that the modem is properly connected to one of the serial or USB ports of your PC.

If you have an external modem, make sure that modem is properly connected to the power supply and that the modem is connected to the telephone line. Buy the right type of cable to connect the modem to the PC, You need a straight-through serial cable to connect the modem to the PC. The connectors at the ends of the cable depend on the type of serial connector on your PC. The modem end of the cable needs a 25-pin connector. The PC end of the cable is often a 9-pin connector. You can buy dummy modems at most computer stores. You often find 9-pin-female-to-25-pin-male cables under the label AT Modem Cable. Connect USB modems using a USB cable.

If your PC has an internal modem, all you have to connect the phone line to the phone jack at back of the internal-modem card. If it’s a winmodem, you still connect the phone line, but you also have to do a bit of research on the Internet and download a driver that makes the winmodem work in Fedora Core. After you install a working Linux driver for a winmodem, it works just like the older serial ports. See the sidebar “Winmodems: **We only do Windows**"

# Setting the serial-device names

The PC typically has two serial ports, COM1 and COM2 in MS-DOS parlance. Many PCs with Universal Serial Bus (USB) have only one serial port. The PC can also support two more serial ports: COM3 and COM4. Because of these port names, serial ports are often referred to COM ports.

Like other devices, the serial-port devices are represented by device files in the /dev directory. The serial ports are interrupt-request (IRQ) numbers and I/O port addresses. Two IRQs — 3 and 4 — are shared among the four COM ports. Table 1-2 lists the serial-device names corresponding to the COM ports, as well as the I/O port addresses assigned to the four serial ports.

| Table 1-2 Device Names for Serial Ports | |
|---|---|---||
| COM port | Device name | IRQ | I/O address |
| COM1 | /dev/ttyS0 | 4 | 0x38 |
| COM2 | /dev/ttyS1 | 3 | 0x28 |
| COM3 | /dev/ttyS2 | 4 | 0x38 |
| COM4 | /dev/ttyS3 | 3 | 0x28 |
