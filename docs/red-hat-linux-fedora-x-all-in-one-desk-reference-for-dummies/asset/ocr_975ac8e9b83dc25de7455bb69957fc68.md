The General Accountability Office (GAO) is the audit, accountability and investigative agency of Congress. It serves Congress in its oversight of constitutional functions and to help assess the performance and financial accountability of the federal government. The assistance provided by the GAO is used by Congress to make informed decisions about legislation, appropriations, and oversight. The agency also works to improve the efficiency, effectiveness, and economy of the federal government's financial programs, services, and operations. The GAO provides reports and testimony to Congress and its committees, as well as GAO reports, presentations, and other GAO-related materials prepared for Congress to improve transparency and accountability to the American people. The GAO promotes good governance through its independent research, analysis, and evaluations.
