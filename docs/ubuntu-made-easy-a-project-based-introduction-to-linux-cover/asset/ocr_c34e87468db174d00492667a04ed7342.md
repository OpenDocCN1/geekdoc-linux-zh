Switching between different applications is easy enough to do by clicking the icons for the application you want to switch to. You can also do this from your keyboard by repeatedly ALT-TAB until you get to the application you want to use.

Yet another way to switch applications (or start an application that isn't currently running) is to hold down the Windows key. After a second or so, the icons on the Launcher will become numbered, and you can then switch to the application by pressing the number on your keyboard that corresponds to it. If more than one window of application is open, the most recently used window will gain focus.

Even when you've gotten to the application you want, you might still not be in the right place—want to deal with that moment. For example, let's say you have six manager windows open and they're all piled on top of another. The one with focus is Documents, but you are looking for the one with your photos of trip to Cyprus last summer. You could wade around with your mouse, dragging windows out of the way until you find the one you're looking for.

A slightly easier way of going about this is to click any one of the windows to give it focus, and then repeatedly press ALT TAB (on most keyboards, this is the key with the accent grave and tilde symbols) until you reach the window you want. Alternatively, you can find the window in a more direct manner by clicking the Launcher icon for your application (in this case home button) once more, after all the open windows related to the application that Launcher icon will appear at once (Figure 3-19). You then just click the window you want to go to.

It is also possible to see all of the windows currently open, not just for a particular application, at the same time. To do this, press the Windows and W keys. If you want to hide all of the windows so you can see the desktop itself, press CTRL-ALT-D.
