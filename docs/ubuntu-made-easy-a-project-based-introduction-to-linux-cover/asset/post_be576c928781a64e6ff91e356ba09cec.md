## Save It for Later: Using History and Bookmarks

Chances, are, you have a few websites that you return to again and again, perhaps even checking some of them a few times a day. Typing the entire address every time you want to check Facebook can get tedious, and putting the same search terms into Google five times a day feels clumsy. Surely there's a better way? In fact, Firefox is crammed with better ways! Let's take a look at some of them.

## Bookmarks

First up is Firefox's Bookmarks feature. To save a web page for perusal of your convenience, all you need to do is click the empty star in the right-hand corner of the address bar. When you do that, the star will turn yellow, indicating that the page has been saved. Saved where, you were, ask? Go to the Bookmarks menu and look at the Recently Bookmarked menu item. A list will expand that contains your most recent bookmarks, including the one you just added. All it takes to revisit the page is to click the appropriate item in that menu.

There are other ways of getting at your bookmarks besides the Bookmarks menu, some of which you may find convenient. One is the Bookmarks sidebar. Click View -> Sidebar and select Bookmarks to bring it up (or press CTRL-B if you're keyboard shortcut inclined). A new pane will appear the left-hand side of the window with an expanded list of bookmarks, as well as a search box (shown in Figure 5-4). Click through the lists or start typing in the search box to find the web page you need. When you're finished, you can hide the sidebar by clicking the X in its top-right corner or by deelecting it in the View -> Sidebar menu.

Another way of accessing bookmarks is through the Bookmarks toolbar, which is hidden by default so you can't use it. Take the toolbar from its slumber, click View -> Toolbars and select Bookmarks Toolbar. A new strip will appear immediately after the address bar, containing only two links (Figure 5-5). One of these, Most Visited, contains a list of the websites you visit most frequently. Simply click Most Visited, and you'll be taken directly to one of your favorite sites. The second item is a link to some instructions on Getting Started, which you may wish to peruse. Now, let's concentrate on populating the Bookmarks toolbar with regular bookmarks.

Adding bookmarks to the toolbar is quite straightforward. When you first bookmark a page by clicking the star in the address bar, click the (now yellow) star again. Edit This Bookmark will appear, like the one in Figure 5-6, giving you a number of options. To save the page in the Bookmarks toolbar, simply change the Folder option appropriately and then click Done. Your bookmark will appear on the toolbar, which you can access with a single click.

If you're an intrepid web adventurer, you might soon find that you have an unwieldy number of bookmarks building up. Rather than curtailing your adventures, try a bit of spring-cleaning. Show All Bookmarks, and a Library window will open up. From this window, you can organize all of your bookmarks, renaming, deleting, and filing them as you see it. To see how this works, select Bookmarks Menu from the list on the left-hand side of the window and click Organize New Folder. A new folder will appear, which you can rename as you like. With that done, try dragging some of the bookmarks to the folder. Repeat this procedure a few times, and you'll soon have your bookmark neatly categorized.

## History

The problem with bookmarks is that you have to remember to bookmark a page in the first place. That's all well and good if a website strikes you as keeping for when you first visit it, but what if you found a few days ago that seem important only in retrospect? For such eventualities, Firefox keeps a detailed history of all the websites you've visited over the last few months, or even longer. To view your browsing history, click **View Sidebar** and select **History** or CTRL-H. This will bring up the **History sidebar**, which has a full list of websites you've visited, sorted by date. You can browse or search through them, just as you could with the Bookmarks toolbar. Clicking any of the pages in the list will bring it up in the main Firefox window.

To find a page, it's also possible to sort the list by something other than date. Click the **View** button immediately to the right of the search box in the History sidebar and select one of the options from the menu that pops up to re-sort the list. For example, to view your browsing history grouped by website, rather than by date, select **By Site**.

Of course, traw through your web history every time you want to find a previously viewed website isn't everyone's idea of fun. To end this, Firefox has what I think you'll agree is a awesome feature: the Awesome Bar. This is actually just another name for the address bar, which might leave you with the impression that there's perhaps more to the dull text box meets the eye. Indeed, there is: Open a new tab and start typing. As the letters appear in the address bar, a list will appear immediately below it, containing links to websites you've previously visited (shown in Figure 5-7). Firefox is directly searching web history as you type, finding anything you entered in the address bar. Refine the search by typing a few more letters or scroll down the list until you find the website you're looking for. With a single click, the page will load, negating any need to type its address out. Slick, use the arrow keys on the keyboard to select items from the list and use the ENTER key to visit the page that is currently highlighted—no clicking required.
