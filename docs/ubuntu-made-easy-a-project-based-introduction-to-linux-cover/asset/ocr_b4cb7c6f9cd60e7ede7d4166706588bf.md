+   13. It's best to select **Require my password to log in** from the options at the bottom of the screen. Although this will necessarily increase the time between switching on the computer and the Ubuntu desktop's appearance, putting a password entry box in the way makes it much more difficult for anyone to gain access to your computer and all of the precious (potentially confidential) files stored on it.
When you're done, click **Continue** to move on to the next step.

> The security-conscious you might consider considering the Encrypt my home folder option. This will scramble the contents of your Home folder (where all your documents are stored) so that no one can see any of the files without your password. It won't affect how you access your files, however, as they are descrambled when you log in. This is nice to have if you work with confidential information, since the data will be pretty even if your computer is stolen.

+   14. **Migrate documents and settings.** If your machine has another operating system on it and you are setting up a dual-boot system, the installer will scan your hard disk to find certain settings, folders, and documents that it can automatically transfer to your new Ubuntu environment. This step shouldn't really matter, because (hopefully!) you made backup copies of all your important documents and settings before you started the installation.
