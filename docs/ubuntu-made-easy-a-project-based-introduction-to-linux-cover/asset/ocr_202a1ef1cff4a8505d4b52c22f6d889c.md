Despite the wonderful, everything-goes nature of your computer in terms of DVD playback, a drive is a serious caveat to bear in mind. Depending on the manufacturer of your drive, you will be able to switch and back between DVDs of different regional encodings only four or five times. After that, the drive will be locked into the region encoding of the disc you are playing at time... forever. This is unrelated to your operating system—it is strictly a hardware matter. The only exception to this region-lock are DVDs labeled Region Free or ALL (sometimes inaccurately labeled Region 0, which can be played on any DVD player in any region and thus do not register as a regional encoding when you plop one of them in your computer's drive.

If your drive does eventually lock into one region for one which you have few DVDs, there is some good news. That comes in the form of Videolan's libvdread2, the library that is included in the libvdread4 package you installed in Project 16A. The libvdread2 allows you to play back DVDs in theory to play back DVDs from multiple regions even if your drive is already locked into one region. This can be by performing a cryptic attack (to use Videolan's for it) on your drive until you find the disc key for that drive. Of course, this process of cryptic bombardment can take several minutes, so it is not the optimal way of doing things. It's better than being stuck, though. Whether this process of bypassing regional encodings is legal remains a subject of debate, so if you are concerned about such things, you should do a little research.
