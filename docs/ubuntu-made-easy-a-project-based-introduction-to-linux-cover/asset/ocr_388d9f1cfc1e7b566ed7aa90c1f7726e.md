# Music

With iPads and other audio devices having ever higher capacities and online music stores gaining popularity, large digital music collections have become the norm. A quick peruse of my one collection reveals 3,206 songs by 202 artists. The is the equivalent of sometime nights of music! No matter how your audiophile compares to mine, you can admit that finding that one favorite song among the rest of it would be a daunting task without some trusty computerized assistance. You'll learn more about managing your music collection in Chapter 14, using a handy Dash Rhythmbox, but in the meantime, the Dash has plenty to offer

Click the musical symbol at the bottom of the Dash, and your music collection will be spread out before you, assuming you've transferred it to the computer (if not, see Chapter 14 to learn how to do this). In exactly the same manner as you work with other types of file, the Music search is capable of matching more than just a song's filename—it also looks as song title, artist name, and album title. What's more, the search results come back with album artwork displayed for each song—a nice touch that makes the experience feel a little bit closer to rooting through physical stacks of records.

When you search for something, the results will be displayed in three bands this time—Songs, Albums, and Available for purchase. The last one has to do with the music store that's built into Ubuntu—more than in Chapter 14. The first two show only those songs and albums that are saved on your computer. From here on, it's pretty similar to what you had with the Files & Filters lens—you can click Show results to see a full list of everything that matches your search terms, and you can click Filter results to narrow your search. The only difference is, that time, you can filter by Decade and Genre rather than by Size and File type. Neat, huh?
