# THE HACKER CULTURE—A BRIEF ARISE

If Linus hadn't shared his hobby project with the world, we wouldn't be talking about Ubuntu, or any form of Linux (Chapter 1 if you want a refresher on the history). The open source movement is founded on the hard work and ingenuity of people like Linux, who are affectionately known by the community as hackers. By this, I don't mean people who break into computers, write horrible viruses, or otherwise. Another meaning of the term hacker goes much further in computing history: Simply put, it means one who writes computer programs as passion. The guys who founded Microsoft and Apple were hackers—enthusiasts playing around with early desktop computers in their respective garagesnThe hacker culture extends beyond computers to all sorts of activities. It's about experimenting, coming up with cool ideas, and sharing them with similar-minded people, and it is this philosophy that has made the open source movement.

# The Ubuntu Forums

An online forum is a place where you can post messages about some topic that other users can read and respond to publicly. There are thousands of forums on the Web, bringing together people from all over the world to talk about sorts of stuff. The Ubuntu forums are a great place to ask questions, get help, and converse with other people using Ubuntu. There are over 50,000 active users, and more than a million posts have been made to date. If you have a question, there's a good chance that it's already been answered or that someone will be able to answer it for you.

Dust off your explorer's hat, and let's take a look around the forums. Head over to http://www.ubuntuforums.org/, and the first thing you'll notice is the many categories on the front page. Choosing the right category is pretty straightforward—people seeking help with computer problems will post their questions in one of the Main Support categories, for example. For now, let's check out the part of forum set for new Ubuntu users. Click the Absolute Beginner talk link and scroll down to the list that Sticky Threads (Figure 2-1).

A thread is a collection of messages about the same topic. You can post replies to it or start new threads. A sticky thread is one that the forum moderators (the head honchos who make everything runs smoothly) deem important enough that it should stick around, for everyone to read. Normal threads don't stick around and slowly make their way down the list as new threads are started and added to the top. To read a thread, just click its name.
