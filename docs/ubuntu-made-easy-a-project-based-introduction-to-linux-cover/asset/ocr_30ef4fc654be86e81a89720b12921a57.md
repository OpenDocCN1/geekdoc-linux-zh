If you install an update to find that your system won't start up again, don't worry. All is not lost. In fact, nothing is lost. ... most likely. Just start up your machine again. If you have a dual-system, you will arrive at the GRUB boot menu, just like always. If you're not a dual-booter, you'll have to get to this menu by holding down SHIFT as soon as the computer starts. Don't let go of the SHIFT key until the GRUB menu appears (or Ubuntu starts, in which again). In the GRUB menu, you will see that you missed your case, and you'll have to restart the computer and try have more than one Ubuntu system installed on your hard disk, which is true to extent. For a short-term fix, you can take advantage of this traveling back in time to the core system (the Linux kernel) that you had in place before your ill upgraded. Use your arrow keys to select a kernel that ends in a number- lower than the highest (3.2.0-22 after 3.2.0-24, example), press ENTER, and voilà! You are back to the system you have come to know and, you'll have to do this each time you start your machine, but at least will have the chance to back up your valuable files before doing research on and trying again later.

With this disaster and recovery talk, I hope I haven't scared away from the idea of updating your system. After all, changes are great nothing like this will ever happen to you. Still, it is good to know that if things go away, there is no need for panic. You've got backup.
