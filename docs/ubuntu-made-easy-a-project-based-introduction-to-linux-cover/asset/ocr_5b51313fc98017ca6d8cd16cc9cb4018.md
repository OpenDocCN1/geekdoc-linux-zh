# Installing MP3 Support for Audio Apps

All that talk about Ogg Vorbis and FLAC aside, many people still like or need to deal with MP3 files. You may, for example, already have numerous MP3 files ripped from your music collection, or you may enjoy listening to one of the many Internet radio stations that are broadcast in MP3 format. Unfortunately, MP3 playback and encoding support is not included in Ubuntu because of licensing concerns. If you are on planning to use the Ogg Vorbis format in your future ripping and encoding endeavors, MP3 support is a good idea so as to cover your audio bases. It's also a very easy process. If you opted to install third-party software when you originally installed Ubuntu, you most likely have all the support you need.

If you did not install third party software when you first installed Ubuntu, then the easiest way to install MP3 playback is to double-click an MP3 file that you have on hard disk. A window will appear, asking whether you want to search for the plug-ins to play the file. Click **Search**, after which another window will appear showing what you need to install (Figure 14-1). Check both boxes in that window, click the **Install** button, and in the confirmation window that appears, click **Confirm**. You will be asked for your password, so provide it and then wait until the process is complete. Once done, you will be able to play not only your MP3 audio files but also a variety of other audio and video formats.
