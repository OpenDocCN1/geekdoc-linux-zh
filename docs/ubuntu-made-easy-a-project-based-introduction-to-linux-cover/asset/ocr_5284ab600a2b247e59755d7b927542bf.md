Now that you know which disk icon is which, it's time to get it mounted! Just click the icon for the Windows partition in the Nautilus sidebar. Authenticate window may appear; if it does, type your Ubuntu password and click the **Authenticate** button. A new window showing the contents of your Windows partition will appear.

If you hibernated rather than quit Windows, you will most likely receive a "Cannot mount volume. You are not privileged to mount this volume." *Error message when trying to mount your Windows partition. Some hibernated Windows partitions cannot be safely mounted for reading and writing in Linux, adding new files while Windows is hibernated could seriously confuse it when it starts up again. To get full access to the drive, be sure to shut Windows rather than hibernating.

# 19A-2: Unmounting Your Windows Partition

You can unmount your partition immediately after finishing your work with it, or you can just wait until you down your system, whereupon it will automatically unmounted. Before you unmount it, you must make sure that none of your programs are accessing files or folders on the partition. Nautilus and the Terminal are the usual suspects, before unmounting, close any Nautilus or Terminal windows that are browsing that partition. Once you've done that, click the little eject icon next to the Windows partition in the sidebar of a Nautilus window (Figure 19-2) or right-click the eject icon and select **Unmount**.
