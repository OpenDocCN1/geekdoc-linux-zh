# Ubuntu Software Centre

For now, or on earth are you supposed to waged against the gigantic mound of software that’s just been dumped on your doorstep? The Software Centre has a few routes to open. On the left side of the window is a list of categories. If you click any of them, you will jump to a list of all the items available for category, possibly with more subcategories for you to explore, as in Figure 6-2. Applications that are already installed are marked with a green checkmark over their icons, those that are not installed just display the plain icon. Each application also has a rating, telling you how good other users think it is. In all, it looks and acts very much like an online store—think of it as an Amazon.com for Ubuntu software.

To see a list of all installed programs, click the Installed button on the toolbar at the top of the window. A list of categories will appear; click any of them to see a list of what’s already installed. You will have already seen many of the listed applications before, in the Dash or the Launcher. Now, take a look at the bottom of the list. You’ll find a small button if you’d like to show a large number of “technical items.” Click the button if you like; the screen will update, and then you will open up some of the categories, you’ll find more items in the list, of them unfamiliar. What you’re looking at are the hundreds of other applications and tools that are installed by default in Ubuntu, ones that you’ll never use yourself. You see, the Software Centre isn’t just a way of getting your hands on new, exciting apps. It also keeps track of important system software that is necessary for Ubuntu to work—the “technical items” that the button refers to.
