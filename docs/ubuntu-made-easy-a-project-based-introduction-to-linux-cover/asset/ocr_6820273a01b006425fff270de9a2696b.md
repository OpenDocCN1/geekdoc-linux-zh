What does a router include with the defense of your computer? Most routers include a firewall, which essentially functions to keep all of the bad stuff on the Web away from your computer, in the way that the firewall in your car keeps the heat, fumes and noise from your engine out of the passenger compartment. This built-in firewall is one reason that even people with one computer, who could easily connect to a cable or DSL modem, use a router. Of course, just how much security in your router depends on which filters you select in the firewall setup. For example, a very common and useful filter (particularly for those with a wireless network) limits Internet access to those computers specified on the firewall's access list. This prevents your next-door neighbors from hitching a wireless ride via your ISP connection. They aren't paying the bill, after.

The setup for a router is built into the router, so you don't have to worry about software installation and system compatibility. Using your router with Linux is not different from it using Windows. You can access the software and modify your settings via a simple web browser, as you can see in Figure 20-1. Just type the IP address of the router (usually provided in the owner's manual) in the browser's location bar, press ENTER, and you'll be ready to go.
