# Copying Songs from Your Audio Device to Your Hard Disk

One feature of most digital media players that aren't available in iTunes (at least without the installation of a special freeway plug-in) is the ability to copy songs from your iPhone/iPod to your hard disk (or to an external USB drive). Luckily, Rhythmbox allows you to do this easily.

To copy music from iPhone/iPod/iPod, Android-based phone, or other digital media device with Rhythmbox, select the tracks you want to export (hold down the CTRL key to make multiple selections or use the tabs to select artists or genres if you like) and drag the selected items to your desktop, music folder, or wherever you want. The only catch with this mostly simple approach is when copying files from your iPhone/iPod, you end up with files on your hard disk with funky filenames, such as ZPAD.m4a or ATDT.mp3, rather than the name that appeared on your iPhone/iPod within Rhythmbox. This means you'll have to rename the files once they have been transferred to your hard disk. It isn't too much of a chore unless you're dealing with lots and lots of files or if you've forgotten which file is which. In the latter case, right-click the file, click Properties, and then click the Audio tab, where you will find the original filename (Figure 15-6). Once you know the filename, click the Basic tab, type the correct name in the Name box, and click Close.
