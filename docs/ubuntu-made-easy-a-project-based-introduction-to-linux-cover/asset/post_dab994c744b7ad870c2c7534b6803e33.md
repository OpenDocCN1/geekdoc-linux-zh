
# Ripping Audio CDs with Rhythmbox

Like iTunes, Rhythmbox allows you to rip songs directly from a CD; convert them to Ogg Vorbis, MP3, or another audio format; and add the ripped tracks to its music library. The steps are as follows:

- 1. Open the Preferences window (Edit ▸ Preferences), click the Music tab, and selecting the encoding format you want to use (Ogg Vorbis is the default). Once you're done, click Close. Unless you change formats often, you won't have to do this more than once.
- 2. If you have selected an encoding format for which you have not yet installed support, such as MPEG Layer 3 Audio (MP3), an "Install additional software required to use this format" will appear underneath the preferred drop-down menu (Figure 14-3). Click that button and install everything suggested, even the unchecked items (to kill as many birds as with one stone, so to speak).

![](img/d35fa962e6f9664d66dc83916f426418_286_0.png)

Figure 14-3: Selecting encoding formats in Rhythmbox

- 3. Once the installation is complete, close the Preferences window and select your audio CD in the left pane of the main Rhythmbox window.
- 4. All of the tracks and album information will appear in the right pane of the window (Figure 14-4). Check information there, make any corrections you feel necessary, and then click the Extract button at the top left of the pane. Rhythmbox will begin ripping your CD, saving the files to your Music folder while indicating its progress in the lower-right corner of the window.

# Browsing the Rhythmbox Library

The Rhythmbox Library, which you can make visible by clicking Music in the left pane of the Rhythmbox window, is a collection of all the music you add to it. This can prove to be a bit unwieldy as your collection grows. Fortunately, Rhythmbox has a nicer browser function, like the one in iTunes, which can be toggled on and off by clicking the Browse button, at the top left of the main pane. This function allows you to see lists of artists and albums in two separate panes above the main Library list. If you click a specific artist in the Artist pane, a list of albums by that artist will appear in the right pane. You can double-click one of the albums in that right pane to play it. If you want to play all the albums you have by that artist, double-click the artist's name in the left pane. In all in, it's a very handy function.

If you want to add a layer of categorization to the browser, you can do so by adding a Genre pane. To do this, just go to the Edit menu, select Preferences, select the General tab of the window that appears, and select Genres, artists, and albums. After clicking the Close button in the Preferences window, you will have a three-pane browser in Rhythmbox (Figure 14-5).

# Creating Playlists in Rhythmbox

Of course, you can tailor things even further to match your situation and every mood. There are days, after all, when you're feeling a bit too ethereal (and thank goodness for that). To prepare for such moments, you can create playlists, which are lists of songs to be played in a predetermined order. Just think of each playlist as an all-request radio station... where all the requests are your own.

![](img/d35fa962e6f9664d66dc83916f426418_287_0.png)

![](img/d35fa962e6f9664d66dc83916f426418_288_0.png)

Figure 14-5: The Rhythmbox browser with expanded categories

To create a playlist in Rhythmbox, select **Music > Playlist > New Playlist**. When the Playlist icon appears in the left pane of the Rhythmbox window, type a name for the list and press ENTER. Click the Music icon in the left pane to show the contents of your music library. Then add the songs you want by dragging them from the Library to the Playlist icon. The songs themselves will remain in the Library, so you aren't moving anything—just creating aliases.

You can also create *automatic playlists*, which are lists that automatically scan the Library for songs that match your criteria. For example, might choose to create a list for all the songs in your Library by a particular artist or of a specific genre. To do this, select **Music > Playlist > New Automatic Playlist**. A window will appear in which you can specify what the list is to contain. You can even specify how many songs you want in the list.

Once you've created your lists, you can play one song by clicking it once and then clicking the **Play** button near the top of the window (or by double-clicking the list).

# Ubuntu One Music Store via Rhythmbox

Similar to the iTunes store, Rhythmbox provides you with access to the Ubuntu One Music Store, which provides music by major artists from the 7digital catalog. The songs sold are free from the digital rights management (DRM) limitations found in many songs purchased from other stores. The Ubuntu One Music Store is also cloud enabled, whatever you is delivered to your Ubuntu cloud, from where you can download your purchases to your other devices. (An Ubuntu account is required, but you can get one, with 5GB.)

# Other Features in Rhythmbox

Rhythmbox includes a couple more features worth noting. One of these is the ability to automatically display lyrics. To use this feature, you must first activate it by going to the Edit menu and selecting Plugins. In the window that appears (Figure 14-6), check the Song Lyrics box and click Close. After that, to see lyrics, go to the View menu and select Lyrics. Rhythmbox will search the Internet for the lyrics and display them (Figure 14-7).

# Project 14: Listening to Streaming Media with Rhythmbox

In addition to allowing you to play audio CDs or the music you have on your hard disk, Rhythmbox also allows you to play radio streams.

# 14-1: Adding Radio Streams to Rhythmbox

It is also possible to add streams to Rhythmbox. To get started, let's try adding FIP, a French music station specializing in eclectic. The process for adding FIP is essentially the same as adding any other stream. Here are the steps:

- Click **Radio** in the left pane of the Rhythmbox window.
- At the top of the main pane, click the **Add** button.
- In the window that appears, type `http://www.tv-radio.com/station/fip_mp3/fip_mp3-128k.mp3` and click **Add**.
- Double-click the new entry for your stream to play it.

# 14-2: Changing a Stream's Title and Information in Rhythmbox

The only problem with what you've just done is that the title of the stream appears in the Rhythmbox window is the URL, and the genre appears as *unknown*. Needless to say, this isn't all that handy a way to find things, especially once you have more than a couple of streams listed.

Remedying this state of affairs is a simple process. Just right-click the stream and select **Properties**. In the window that appears, change the content of the Title and Genre boxes so that they are more meaningful to you. As you can see in Figure 14-8, I used *Radio France - FIP* for the title and *Eclectic* for the genre.
