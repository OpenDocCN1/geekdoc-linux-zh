Assuming that you got GNOME PPP installed using a borrowed Internet connection, you can follow these steps to set up your modem:

+   1. Open the Dash, search for GNOME PPP, and click to open it.
2. Click Setup and make you're looking at the Modem tab.
3. Click the Detect button to identify your modem. If you get a message saying that no modem was found on your system, you're out of luck—the best you can do is ask for advice on the Ubuntu forums (https://www.ubuntuforums.org/) or try selecting items from the Device list by trial and error.
4. How you choose the rest of the settings depends on how your modem and ISP are set up, select the defaults for now. If they don't work, trial and error is a potential way forward again.
5. Click Close to get back to the GNOME PPP window. Type your username and password and enter the phone number provided by your ISP.
6. Click Connect. You should hear your begin begin dialing, spitting, and churning sequence as it makes the connection with your provider.
7. If you manage to get connected, you can start your browsing, or whatever else you do online. If you get an error message, there's little you can do but take a deep breath and click the Setup button to tweak your settings.

# What to Do If Your Modem Isn't Compatible

Though it may sound that way, what I've just described is pretty much a best-case scenario. What happens, however, if your modem and Ubuntu do not see eye to eye? Well, there are a few options. My, first radical suggestion is to dump your dial-up ISP and find a broadband provider that covers your local area. If that is not an option, then you have two ways to go. One is to try get around with your modem to see whether you get it to work. This is a slightly complicated process, but there are instructions at https://help.ubuntu.com/community/DialupModemHow/. If you are of heart or a novice user, you may find the process to entail a bit more than what you're willing to deal with.

The simpler, although costly, way to get your modem to work is to chase a hardware modem. Hardware modems are not software defined, so they work with any operating system. You can think of them as telephones without a headset.

Some might come in two forms: internal and external. For the internals, the USRobotics modems 56K V.92 Performance Pro Modem (internal slot) and 56 PC Card Modem (PC card slot, for laptops) are true hardware modems that are easily available and ready to work. You can check out the USRobotics site (http://www.usr.com/) for more information on these models, though checking the Ubuntu for suggestions is always a good idea. Perhaps the safest of all solutions is to buy an external dial-up modem.
