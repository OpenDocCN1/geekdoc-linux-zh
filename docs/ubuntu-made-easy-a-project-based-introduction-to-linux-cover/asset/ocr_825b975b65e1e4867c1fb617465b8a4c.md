Linux Speaks Your Language

![](bbox=[0.169, 0.477, 0.355, 0.626])

These days, almost all operating systems are multilingual, or at least capable of becoming so. This is true of Linux. Just open your web browser, and, without performing any special installations, you can read pages in any European language, including those with non-Roman alphabets such as Bulgarian, Greek, Russian, and Serbian. You can even view pages in Chinese, Japanese, Thai, Arabic, and Hebrew, to name but a few.

The multilingual capabilities of Linux are much greater than this, and the way that it handles multilingual matters makes it easy to take advantage of these capabilities. As you will soon see, you can even set up your system to give you a totally foreign-language environment, allowing you to function completely in the language of your choice. Add to this the ever-expanding number of free programs available for language study, and you have a truly meaningful language-learning tool.
