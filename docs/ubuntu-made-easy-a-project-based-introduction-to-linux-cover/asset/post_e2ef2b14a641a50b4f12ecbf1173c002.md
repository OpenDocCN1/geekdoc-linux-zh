## Changing Dash Properties

By clicking the dash tab, you can see the options available to you in MyUnity for customizing the Dash (Figure 9-12). These include being able to toggle on and off the displays of used apps and apps available for installation from the Ubuntu Software Center (they're both on by default). There is also a setting for Blur, which adjusts the background blur of the Dash to make it less crisp. There's a Dash size option, which lets you switch between Dash size, which covers only a portion of the screen, and Netbook size, a full-screen alternative.

## Changing Panel Transparency

From MyUnity's panel tab, the only thing you can do is adjust the transparency of the top panel (Figure 9-13), but this feature will work a lot. Of course, the desktop background you will have an effect on how a more transparent panel will look, so bear in mind as you experiment.

## Changing Desktop Features

From MyUnity's desktop tab (Figure 9-14), you decide what items you want to show up automatically on your desktop (nothing is the default, as you know). These can include your home folder, drive, network, trash, and more. You can also toggle on and off window animations, if you are trying to speed up an old machine or if you don't much care for such things, and you can adjust the number of virtual desktops you have, both horizontally (H Desktop) and vertically (V Desktop).

## Customizing Visual Effects with CompizConfig Settings Manager

Starting where Unity leaves off, CompizConfig Settings Manager, also known as CCSM (Figure 9-18), allows you to tweak more system elements and to configure them. Compiz is the composing engine at work in Ubuntu that provides it with its 3D effects, such as shadows and window animations. Its job is to bring together all the various elements at work in your system in conjunction with your 3D-compatible video card, making everything seem to be working as one graphical unit. You might say it is the eye-candy engine for your system, and as it is a busy of of software indeed! CCSM is the graphical interface by which you can control Compiz's features.

![](img/d35fa962e6f9664d66dc83916f426418_209_0.png)

> 9-18: CompizConfig Settings Manager

Compiz is automatically enabled at startup if you have a graphics card that supports its set of features. An easy way to tell whether Compiz has kicked in on your machine is to open any window (your Home folder, for example) and check whether that window has a drop shadow. If it does not, then Compiz is not at work, and this section will not be of much of use to you skip to the next section. If it does have a shadow, however, Compiz is at work, so keep on reading.

The graphical effects that are active by default in your system are really the tip of the iceberg. Depending on the capabilities of your graphics card, you will get the drop shadows and a few bells and whistles, such as cooler window-opening transitions, but that's about it—on the surface, and at any rate. If you like, you can get a feel for it right now by kicking the effects up a notch.

First run CCSM by going to the Dash, clicking the Applications lens, and then clicking the Customization filter button and looking for it in the results. Or if you prefer, just compiz in the Dash and press ENTER.

The very first thing you will see, along with the CCSM window, is another warning that CCSM is an advanced tool and that you should proceed with caution. The warning is right, so if you're not sure what something in CCSM is or does, perhaps you won't feel with it. You should be safe trying the things I mention here, however, starting out with wobbly Windows.

To give Windows a try (and thus a hand-on feel for CCSM), click the Effects tab in the CCSM window and find the Wobbly Windows plugin. Check the box to enable it. If you don't like it, uncheck the box. To adjust the effect, click the gear button in the CCSM window. In the window that appears, you can adjust the sliders to change how the windows wobble. You can also enable other effects, such as Blur, shadows, and animations.

When you're done with the settings, click the OK button to close the CCSM window.
