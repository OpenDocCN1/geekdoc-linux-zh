# FEATHERED FLIPPERS

Linux Gaming

![](bbox=[0.172, 0.483, 0.356, 0.623])

Many people hate to admit it, but games
are a big attraction for almost everyone
at the helm of a personal computer. Even
those who denying gamers often find them-
elves caught in the act of knocking off a quick round
solitaire between work projects. As a Linux user, you
will likely find that one of the most common users of other
operating systems will ask you about Linux is "What games does it have?"
Well, although there might not be as many games in the Linux world as
there are on Planet Windows, Linux still has plenty to choose from. In fact,
most distributions come bundled with a number of games.
Ubuntu, for example, comes with the games AiRiot Solitaire (a collection
of more than 80 solitaire card games, shown in Figure 17-1), FreeCell
Solitaire (a standalone version of one of the most popular solitaire games
around), Mahjongg (a tile-matching game similar to the Xmahjongg
introduced in Chapter 8), Mines (the Linux world's answer to Minesweeper),
and Sudoku (the Japanese number puzzle). Many free games are available-
via the Ubuntu Software Center (and other sources), some of which you
will learn about in this chapter.
