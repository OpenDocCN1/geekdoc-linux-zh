> Most access points employ an added level of security in the form of a Wired Equivalent Privacy (WEP) or a Wi-Fi Protected Access (WPA) key. If you were not successful in making your wireless connection (the system will tell you that's the case), you have a few possibilities beyond the annoying card scenario. First, double-check to make sure your card is physically turned on. I know that sounds dumb, but I have been guilty of oversight myself on numerous occasions. Many laptops have a key you can press to enable or disable the wireless card; on mine, you have press the function key (FN) and F2 at the same time (there's a little wireless antenna on the F2 key to denote this).

Another possibility is that you are not entering the correct WEP or WPA password. The first thing to check is case sensitivity. It sometimes matters the letters you type are upper- lowercase, make sure you type them as written.
