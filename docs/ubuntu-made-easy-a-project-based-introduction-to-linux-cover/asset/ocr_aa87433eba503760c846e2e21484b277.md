# ## Hardware

If you have led a wired existence or are just inexperienced in this area,
there are a few things worth knowing. To get started, you need to have the
right hardware. If you just want to go wireless, then all you need is a wireless
network card (sometimes almost referred to as an 802.11a/b/g/n or Wi-Fi card).
These are built into all modern laptops, for older models they are
usually add-ons in the form of cards that pop into the PCMCIA slot on
t side of your computer (as shown on the right of Figure 4-4).

Some wireless cards plug in to one of your machine's USB ports, or
the case of desktop models, of its internal PCI slots. Although support\for cards of this type has improved in the past couple of years, there are still
gaps, and some cards can be tricky to deal with. You will find tips on how
deal with awkward cards in Chapter 22, but if you're looking for a sure thing,
cards listed as having a Ralink 2500/RT2400, Realtek RTL8180, or Atheros
AR9170 "chipset" are definitely supported by Ubuntu.
