A channel is a place where you can go to talk to other people. There are lots of them from IRC, each one dedicated to a different topic. In XChat, the channels that you're connected to are listed in the pane on the left side of the window, you should be able to see that you're currently in #ubuntu, which is for general Ubuntu help and support. You could select Join a channel to connect to a different channel, but for there's no need since we're already in the right channel. In the right pane is a list of other users who are currently connected to the same channel; #ubuntu is very popular, so there are probably quite a few of yours out there.

When you enter a channel, a bunch of messages passed by the pane of the screen. The first few are automated messages that describe what the channel is for, set some ground rules, and so forth, but by now you should see messages from other people scrolling up the screen (Figure 2-11). The nickname of the person you are talking is to the left dividing line, and his message is on the right. Everyone in the channel can see the messages, and typically several conversations are going on at once. You also see messages in different colors when someone joins or leaves the channel. People are coming and going all of the time, so you can ignore these messages.
