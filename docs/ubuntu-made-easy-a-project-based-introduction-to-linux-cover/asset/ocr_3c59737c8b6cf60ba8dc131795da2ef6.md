# DVDs

Your system allows you to play DVDs; however, due to licensing concerns, playback is limited to unencrypted discs by default. Unfortunately, this rules out a vast majority of the DVD movies you buy or rent at your local video shop and leaves you with a limited choice of films on your computer. Given the limited offerings in the unencrypted DVD world, you will no doubt want to enable your system to play the encrypted variety.

# Project 16: Installing Support for Encrypted DVDs

I As already mentioned, Ubuntu does not allow you to play encrypted DVDs from the start, unless you opted to install third-party support when you installed Ubuntu. If you did not do that, then for you to watch such DVDs (and would be the vast majority of them), you need to install a whole bunch of stuff—most crucially the package libdvdread4. Fortunately, this has become easy to do when Ubuntu tells you what you need when you need it, just as it in the previous chapter.
The first is the easiest: Insert a DVD movie disc in your drive. In few moments, a window will appear telling you that you have inserted a DVD (duh!) and asking you what you want to do. In the drop-down menu, select Movie Player and click OK. A new window will appear as Ubuntu figures what you need to install before you start DVDs. Once it is done, accept the default packages suggested in the window by clicking OK and entering your password. Then sit back for a minute or less until the installation process is complete. Once it is done, you will have successfully installed libdvdread4 and a bunch of other necessary files. Now you're ready to play your DVD, right? Well, not quite.
With just any other application, you would be done at this point, but as with now, Movie Player will be open but your DVD will still not be playing. The reason is that libdvdread4 requires that you also run an installation script that comes with it in order to get the job done. To run the script, open the Terminal, type the following, and press ENTER:

```
sudo /usr/share/doc/libdvdread4/install-css.sh
```

You will be prompted for your password, so type it in and press ENTER. Once the Terminal returns you to the user prompt, the process will be done. Close Movie Player, reinsert the DVD movie disc in your drive, and once again select Movie Player in the window that pops up. Once you click OK, Movie Player should open and start playing the DVD. (You'll learn more about Movie Player sooner.)
