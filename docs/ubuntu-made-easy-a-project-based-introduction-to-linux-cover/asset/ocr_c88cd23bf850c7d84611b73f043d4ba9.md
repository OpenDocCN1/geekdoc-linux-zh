# Customizing Visual Effects with CompizConfig Settings Manager

Starting where Unity leaves off, CompizConfig Settings Manager, also known as CCSM (Figure 9-18), allows you to tweak more system elements and to configure them. Compiz is the composing engine at work in Ubuntu that provides it with its 3D effects, such as shadows and window animations. Its job is to bring together all the various elements at work in your system in conjunction with your 3D-compatible video card, making everything seem to be working as one graphical unit. You might say it is the eye-candy engine for your system, and as it is a busy of of software indeed! CCSM is the graphical interface by which you can control Compiz's features.

Compiz is automatically enabled at startup if you have a graphics card that supports its set of features. An easy way to tell whether Compiz has kicked in on your machine is to open any window (your Home folder, for example) and check whether that window has a drop shadow. If it does not, then Compiz is not at work, and this section will not be of much of use to youskip to the next section. If it does have a shadow, however, Compiz is at work, so keep on reading.

The graphical effects that are active by default in your system are really the tip of the iceberg. Depending on the capabilities of your graphics card, you will get the drop shadows and a few bells and whistles, such as cooler window-opening transitions, but that's about it—on the surface, and at any rate. If you like, you can get a feel for it right now by kicking the effects up a notch.
