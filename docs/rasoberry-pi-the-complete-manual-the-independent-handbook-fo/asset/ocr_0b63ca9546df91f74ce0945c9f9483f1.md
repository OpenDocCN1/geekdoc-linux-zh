# Add a battery pack to your Raspberry Pi

> "You can use your device for mobile projects as diverse as launching it into near-Earth orbit"

**Make your order**  **01**  *If you're buying your components online you should be able to get them all within five days. However, if you're ordering offline (specifically the UBeC), you should avoid traditional electronics stores and instead visit a model enthusiast store, as these circuits are regularly used in RC devices.*

**Check your UBeC**  **02**  *Two types of UBeC are available to choose from. If you used the store that we suggest in the resources box to the left, you'll receive one with a micro USB power connector for easy connection to your Raspberry Pi. However, if you bought one from eBay, then there is a strong chance that you will receive one with a 3-pin connector.*

![](bbox=[0.05, 0.64, 0.35, 0.8])

**Move connector pins**  **03**  *In order to use the UBeC with a 3-pin connector, you'll need to alter the position of the pins so that they occupy the two outer slots. Just use a small jeweller's screwdriver to lever up the small plastic pad and remove the red wire from the central slot, before sliding into the unoccupied outer slot.*

**Connect to battery box**  **04**  *With five batteries in the UBeC (battery box, connect it to the UBeC led-red, black to black) by twisting the wires, soldering or employing a 3 way terminal strip, cut down to two pins. It can be cut to size using a modelling knife.*

**Add a battery to boot**  **05**  *With your Pi ready to use and your WiFi dongle plugged in, connect the UBeC to the micro USB port and insert the sixth battery into the battery box. The Pi's power and status lights should indicate that the computer is booting up, which gives you a fully portable computer.*

**Connect the 3-pin UBeC**  **06**  *If you purchased the UBeC with the now-modified 3-pin connector, you need to connect this to the Raspberry Pi GPIO header. Connect the positive +5V (red) connector to Pin 2 and the negative 0V connector to Pin 6.*

**Measure uptime**  **07**  *You should have already set up the device for SSH use, so connect to the device via Putty after giving it time to boot fully (at least 60 seconds). In the terminal, enter:*

```
`sudo idb csi3m sf:/Users/rache.larrabi/Deskt op/ArcHLinux/ archnl-linux-HF-2013-02-11.img >dev/disk1`
```

*This command will display the system uptime and also keep the Wi-Fi connection active.*

**Judge your uptime results**  **08**  *Uptime results depend upon the type of battery you use and the Raspberry Pi model. Single-charge batteries will last a little bit longer, but this is a more expensive option. Meanwhile, newer models (with greater power requirements but more for less time. For more power, add a better battery)*

**Power extreme!**  **09**  *More batteries added in parallel should result in almost double the uptime (at least 16 hours on a 255Wm Raspberry Pi Model A), but instead of alkaline or rechargeable batteries, you might consider a modern lithium-based AA cell, which will last considerably longer than alkaline batteries.*
