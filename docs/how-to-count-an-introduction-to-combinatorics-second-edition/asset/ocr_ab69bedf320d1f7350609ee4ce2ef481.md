Each term in the series for $P(x)$ is positive. Hence $P(x)$ is larger than any single term in the series. That is, for each positive integer $n$,

$$p(n)x^n < P(x),$$

and hence

$$p(n)<\frac{1}{x^n}P(x) .$$

It follows that

$$\log (p(n))<n\log \left(\frac{1}{x}\right)+\log (P(x)) .$$

It is a standard inequality that, for $0<h$, $\log (1+h)<h$. Putting $h=(1 / x)-1$ in this inequality gives

$$\log \left(\frac{1}{x}\right)<\frac{1}{x}-1 .$$

By Equations 8.27, 8.29, and 8.30,

$$\log (p(n))<n\left(\frac{1}{x}-1\right)+\frac{\pi^2}{6}\left(\frac{x}{1-x}\right) .$$

We now apply calculus to find the minimum value of the right-hand side of inequality 8.31. We put

$$f(x)=n\left(\frac{1}{x}-1\right)+\frac{\pi^2}{6}\left(\frac{x}{1-x}\right) .$$

Then

$$f^{\prime}(x)=-\frac{n}{x^2}+\frac{\pi^2}{6(1-x)^2} \quad \text { and } \quad f^{\prime \prime}(x)=\frac{2 n}{x^3}+\frac{\pi^2}{3(1-x)^3} .$$

Thus

$$f^{\prime}(x)=0 \Leftrightarrow 6 n(1-x)^2=\pi^2 x^2 \Leftrightarrow \sqrt{6 n}(1-x)=\pm \pi x \Leftrightarrow x=\frac{\sqrt{6 n}}{\sqrt{6 n} \pm \pi} .$$

It follows that when $x=\sqrt{6 n} /(\sqrt{6 n}+\pi)$, we have $0<x<1, f^{\prime}(x)=0$, and $f^{\prime \prime}(x)>0$. Thus $x=\sqrt{6 n} /(\sqrt{6 n}+\pi)$ gives the minimum value of $f(x)$, for $0<x<1$, namely,

$$f\left(\frac{\sqrt{6 n}}{\sqrt{6 n}+\pi}\right)=\pi \sqrt{\frac{2 n}{3}} .$$
