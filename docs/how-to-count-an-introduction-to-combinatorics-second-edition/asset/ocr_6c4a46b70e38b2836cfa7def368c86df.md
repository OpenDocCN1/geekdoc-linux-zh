# **PROBLEM 8.2**

What is the coefficient of $x^{89}$ in the following product?

```markdown
$(1+x^3+x^6+x^9+....)(1+x^8+x^{16}+x^{24}+....)$    $(8.1)$

# **Solution**

It is fairly readily seen that when terms in the first parenthesis are multiplied by terms in the second bracket we obtain the term $x^{89}$ in just four ways:

```markdown
$x^9 \cdot x^{80}, \quad x^{33} \cdot x^{56}, \quad x^{57} \cdot x^{32}, 	ext{ and } x^{81} \cdot x^8,$

and hence the coefficient of $x^{89}$ in the product is 4.

We have seen a problem of this type before—Problem 7.1 in Chapter 7. Looking at the solution of Problem 7.1, we can see that the coefficient of $x^n$ in the product in expression 8.1 is the same as the number of ways of writing 89 in the form $3m_1+8m_2$, where $m_1$ and $m_2$ are nonnegative integers. In other words, the coefficient of $x^n$ in the product of expression 8.1 is the number of partitions of $n$ using only parts of sizes 3 and 8.

We can readily generalize this. Since a partition of the integer $n$ may use parts of any size ($\leq n$), to obtain a series whose coefficients count *all* the partitions of $n$, we will have to multiply together (infinitely many) infinite series corresponding to parts of size $1, 2, 3, \ldots$. Indeed, we have the following result, which gives us the generating function for the sequence ${p(n)}$ of partition numbers.

# **THEOREM 8.1**

```markdown
$(1+x+x^2+...)(1+x^2+x^4+...)(1+x^3+x^6+...)=1+P(x)+p(2)x^2+p(3)x^3+....$
```

In other words, if $P$ is the generating function for the sequence ${p(n)}$, then

```markdown
$P(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1+x^k+x^{2k}+x^{3k}+....)$    $(8.2)$
