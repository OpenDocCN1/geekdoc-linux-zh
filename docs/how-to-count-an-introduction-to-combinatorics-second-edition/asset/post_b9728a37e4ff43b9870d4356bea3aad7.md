**15.3.2A**

When an integer is divided by *n*, there are *n* possible values for the remainder, namely, 0, 1, . . . , *n* - 1. So, given a set of *n* + 1 distinct integers, by the pigeonhole principle there are at least two integers in the set, say *a* and *b*, that have the same remainder when they are divided by *n*. It follows that *a* - *b* is divisible by *n*.
