Hence it cannot include any of the vertices $2, 5, 8, \dots, 3n-1$, as the edge from $1$ to any of these vertices is red. The edges joining the vertices in each of the $n-1$ pairs, 3 and 4; 6 and 7; 9 and 10; ...; $3n-3$ and $3n-2$ are red. So $G$ can include at most one vertex from each of these pairs. Consequently, the subgraph $G$ has at most $n$ vertices. So there is no blue $\mathcal{K}_{n+1}$.

We have therefore seen that we can color the edges of $\mathcal{K}_{3n-1}$ red and blue so that there is no blue $\mathcal{K}_{n+1}$ and no red $\mathcal{K}_5$. Hence $R(n+1, 3) \geq 3n$.
