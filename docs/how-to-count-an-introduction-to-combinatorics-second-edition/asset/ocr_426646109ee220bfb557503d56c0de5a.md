so that $u_{3}(x)=x^{6}+x^{7}+2 x^{8}+3 x^{9}+4 x^{10}+\cdots$ .

Clearly, the coefficient of $x^{n}$ in $u_{3}(x)$ gives us the number of partitions of $n$ into three *distinct*, that is, *unequal*, parts. In particular, the number of partitions of 10 into three *distinct* parts is the coefficient, namely 4, of $x^{10}$ corresponding to the four partitions

$10 = 1 + 2 + 7 = 1 + 3 + 6 = 1 + 4 + 5 = 2 + 3 + 5$.

Even more generally, the number of partitions of $n$ into any number of distinct parts is the coefficient of $x^{n}$ in the infinite sum $u_{1}(x)+u_{2}(x)+u_{3}(x)+\cdots$, which we may write as $(u_{1}(x)+u_{3}(x)+u_{5}(x)+\cdots)+(u_{2}(x)+u_{4}(x)+u_{6}(x)+\cdots)$, where the coefficient of $x^{n}$ in the first parenthesis counts the number of partitions of $n$ into an odd number of distinct parts and that in the second bracket the number of partitions of $n$ into an even number of distinct parts.

If we now put $a = -1$ in $P_{a}(a, x)$, we obtain

```
```math
(1-x)(1-x^{2})(1-x^{3}) \cdots = 1 - u_{1}(x) + u_{2}(x) - u_{3}(x) + \cdots \\
+ (u_{2}(x) + u_{4}(x) + u_{6}(x) + \cdots) - (u_{1}(x) + u_{3}(x) + u_{5}(x) + \cdots).
```
```

This shows us that the coefficient of $x^{n}$ in Equation 8.6 is the coefficient of $x^{n}$ in $1 + (u_{2}(x) + u_{4}(x) + u_{6}(x) + \cdots)$ minus the coefficient of $x^{n}$ in $(u_{1}(x) + u_{3}(x) + u_{5}(x) + \cdots)$. So, if we let $u_{\text{e},d}(n)$ be the number of partitions of $n$ into an even number of distinct parts ("$e$" for "*unequal*"), and $u_{\text{o},d}(n)$ be the number of partitions of $n$ into odd number of distinct parts, we have just proved the following theorem.

# THEOREM 8.4

The coefficient of $x^{n}$ in the infinite product $(1-x)(1-x^{2})(1-x^{3}) \cdots$ is $u_{\text{o},d}(n) - u_{\text{e},d}(n)$.

Our next task is to try to get more to grips with this quantity $u_{\text{e},d}(n) - u_{\text{o},d}(n)$. This clearly requires that we look at decompositions of $n$ into distinct parts. We shall see that the above quantity is, quite often, zero!

# THEOREM 8.5

For all positive integers $n$, $u_{\text{e},d}(n) - u_{\text{o},d}(n) = 0$ except when $n$ is of the form $\frac{1}{2}(3k^{2} \pm k)$, in which case $u_{\text{e},d}(n) - u_{\text{o},d}(n) = (-1)^{k}$.
