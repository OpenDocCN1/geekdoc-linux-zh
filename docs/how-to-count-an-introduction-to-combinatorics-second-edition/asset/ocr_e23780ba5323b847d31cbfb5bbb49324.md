It took until 1977 before a proof of the four-color theorem was published by Kenneth Appel and Wolfgang Haken.† Their proof made a great deal of use of a computer program to generate and check a large number of cases. For the details see the first footnote in Section 9.8. A proof that could be completely checked by a single person is still awaited.

Graphs that are not planar need not be four-colorable. Indeed, the complete graph $K_n$ has the chromatic number $n$, so there are graphs with chromatic numbers as large as we like. We will not discuss colorings of nonplanar graphs in any detail. We just point out that they have a practical application in scheduling, as shown by the following problem.
