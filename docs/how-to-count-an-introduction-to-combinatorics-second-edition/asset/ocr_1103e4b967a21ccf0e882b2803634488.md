# Hall’s Transversal Theorem*

The finite sets $S_1,\dots,S_n$ have a transversal if and only if, for all positive integers $k\le n$, for each choice of $k$ integers, $n_1, n_2,\dots,n_k$ with $1\le n_1< n_2<\dots<n_k\le n$

$\#(S_{n_1} \cup ... \cup S_{n_k}) \ge k.$

We can now show that Problem 17B has a positive answer.

Let the 10 groups of four cards be $C_1, C_2,\dots,C_{10}$. For $1\le i< 10$ we let $S_i$ be the set of those distinct integers which appear on the cards in $C_i$. We now show that for $1\le k\le 10$ and each choice of $k$ distinct integers, $n_1,\dots,n_k$ chosen from the range from 1 to 10 we have that $\#(S_{n_1} \cup ... \cup S_{n_k}) \ge k.$

For suppose that for some choice of $k$ and $n_1,\dots,n_k$ we have $\#(S_{n_1} \cup ... \cup S_{n_k})< k$. This means that there are at most $k-1$ distinct integers represented among the 4k cards in the union $C_{n_1} \cup C_{n_2} \cup ... \cup C_{n_k}$. But this would imply, by the pigeon-hole principle, that at least one of these integers occurs on at least 5 of the cards, contrary to what we are given.

Hence, by Theorem 17.6, the sets $S_1,\dots,S_{10}$ have a transversal, that is, we can pick one card from each of the groups $C_1,\dots,C_{10}$ so that the ten cards that we pick carry the numbers 1,2,\dots,10.

There is a lot more that could be said about these topics. The theory of transversals has a wide range of applications. We have shown how to calculate rook polynomials. This enables us to work out how many ways we can place non-attacking rooks, or equivalently how many matchings or transversals there are in a given situation. We have also described Hall’s Marriage Theorem which gives a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a transversal or a matching or a placement of non-attacking rooks. We haven’t provided any efficient algorithms for finding transversals, nor have we considered how to find an optimal matching when, for example, there is a cost of assigning a particular job to a particular employee and we want to minimize the total cost. But all good things come to an end, and to avoid this book becoming too large, we end at this point.
