# DEFINITION 11.3 **Isomorphism of Groups**

Let $(G, \bullet)$ and $(H, *)$ be groups. We say that these groups are $\textit{isomorphic}$ if there is a bijection $\Theta : G \rightarrow H$ such that for all $\mathbf{g}_1, \mathbf{g}_2 \in G$, we have

```
$$\Theta(\mathbf{g}_1 \bullet \mathbf{g}_2) = \Theta(\mathbf{g}_1) * \Theta(\mathbf{g}_2). \qquad (11.1)$$
```

Such a mapping $\Theta$ is called an $\textit{isomorphism}$ between the two groups.

For example, the mapping $\Theta$ from the permutations corresponding to the rotations of a square and the numbers modulo 4 (given by iii and ii in Figure 11.4), namely,
