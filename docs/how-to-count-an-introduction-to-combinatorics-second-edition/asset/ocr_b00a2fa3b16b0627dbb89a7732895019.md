We see, for example, that the color assigned to a square, say in position \( \mathcal{X} \), after the rotation is the same as the color that \( f_c \) assigns to the square that the permutation that \( \pi_c \) moves into the position \( \mathcal{X} \), that is, the square that was previously in position \( \pi_c^{-1}(\mathcal{X}) \).  Thus the action of \( a \) on the coloring \( g_f_c \) is given by

\[ a \triangleright f_c = f_c \circ \pi_c^{-1}. \]

The analogous formula applies in the general case, which we now describe. Let \( D, C \) be two sets. We let \( X \) be the set of all mappings from \( D \) to \( C \). Here “D” stands for “domain” and “C” for “codomain,” but you can also think of \( C \) as being a set of colors. We call the mappings in \( X \) colorings of \( D \).

Now suppose that \( G \) is a group of permutations of \( D \). Thus \( G \) will be a subgroup of the group, \( S(D) \), of all permutations of \( D \). We define an action of \( G \) on the set of colorings \( X \) by

\[ \pi \in G, \text{ and } f \in X \, (\text{that is}, f: D \rightarrow C), \quad \pi \triangleright f = f \circ \pi \tag{14.1} \]

We need to check that this does define a group action. To do this, we first require a simple result about groups.

# LEMMA 14.1

Let \( G \) be a group. Then for all \( g_1, g_2 \in G \), \( (g_1g_2)^{-1} = g_2^{-1}g_1^{-1} \).

# Proof

We have that \( (g_1g_2)g_2^{-1}g_1^{-1} = g_1(g_2g_2^{-1})g_1^{-1} = g_1eg_1^{-1} = g_1g_1^{-1} = e \). Similarly, it can be checked that \( g_2^{-1}g_1^{-1}(g_1g_2) = e \), and it follows that \( g_2^{-1}g_1^{-1} \) is the inverse of \( g_1g_2 \).

# THEOREM 14.2

Equation 14.1 defines a group action of \( G \) on \( X \).

# Proof

The identity element of \( G \) is the identity map \( 1: D \rightarrow D \). So, for each \( f \in X \), we have that \( 1 \triangleright f = f \circ 1 = f \circ 1 = f \). It follows that condition GA1 is satisfied.

Now, suppose \( \pi_1, \pi_2 \in G \) and \( f \in X \), then

\[ \begin{align*} \pi_1 \triangleright (\pi_2 \triangleright f) \qquad \quad \qquad \qquad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad 
= \pi_1 \triangleright (f \circ \pi_2^{-1}) = (f \circ \pi_2^{-1}) \circ \pi_1^{-1} = f \circ (\pi_2^{-1} \circ \pi_1^{-1}) = f \circ ((\pi_1 \circ \pi_2)^{-1}) = (\pi_1 \circ \pi_2) \triangleright f. \end{align*} \tag{14.2} \]

This shows that GA2 also holds. (Note that in Equation 14.2 we have used the associativity property for groups, Lemma 14.1, and the group action properties, as well as the definition in Equation 14.1 of the group action. In Exercise 14.1.2A you are asked to work out exactly which of these is used at each step.)

This completes the proof.
