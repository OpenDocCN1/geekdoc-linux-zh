Suppose the result holds for all $n \leq k$, and that $a_1, \dots, a_l, a_{k+1}$ is a sequence of positive integers where $a_1 \leq \dots \leq a_k \leq a_{k+1}$ and $a_1 + \dots + a_k + a_{k+1} = 2k$. It follows that $a_1 = 1$, and for some $r$, with $2 \leq r \leq k + 1$, $a_r \geq 2$. Let $l$ be the least positive integer such that $a_l \geq 2$. Now consider the sequence of $k$ positive integers

$a_2, \dots, a_{l-1}, a_l-1, a_{l+1}, \dots, a_k, a_{k+1}$ (that is, $1, \dots, 1, a_l-1, a_{l+1}, \dots, a_k, a_{k+1}$).

Then $a_2 \leq \dots \leq a_{l-1} \leq a_l-1 \leq a_{l+1} \leq \dots \leq a_{k+1}$ and $a_2 + \dots + a_{l-1} + (a_l-1) + a_{l+1} + \dots + a_{k+1} = 2(k-1)$. Therefore, by the induction hypothesis, $a_2, \dots, a_{l-1}, a_l-1, a_{l+1}, \dots, a_{k+1}$ is a degree sequence of a tree. Thus there is a tree, say $T$, with $k$ vertices, $v_2, \dots, v_{l-1}, v_l, \dots, v_k + 1$ with degrees $a_2, \dots, a_{l-1}, a_l-1, a_{l+1}, \dots, a_{k+1}$, respectively. Let $T'$ be the tree obtained from $T$ by adding one vertex, $v_1$, which is joined by an edge just to the vertex $v_l$. Then $T'$ is a tree with $k+1$ vertices with degrees $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_{l-1}, a_l, a_{l+1}, \dots, a_{k+1}$. So this is the degree sequence of a tree. Hence the result is also true for $n=k+1$. This completes the proof by mathematical induction.
