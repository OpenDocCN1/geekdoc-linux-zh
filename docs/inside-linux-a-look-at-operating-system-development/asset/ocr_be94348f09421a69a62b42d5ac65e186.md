I'll refrain from commenting on the design of such applications 

beyond pointing out that with all GUI (graphical user interface) systems, 

the designer must be very careful to offer some mechanism to get at the 

uncommon features and options, as well as the common and popular 

parts $ ^{6} $.

X clients rely on an extensive library of structures and functions to 

manipulate the extensive details of the X protocol. Additional layers 

of libraries provide more isolation from tedious and bothersome details. 

They also provide a bridge for the *semantic gap* between what the user 

wants and what the system offers.
