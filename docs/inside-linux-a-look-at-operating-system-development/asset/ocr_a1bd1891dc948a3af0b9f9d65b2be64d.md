Since Unix was used to develop Unix (once the bootstrap phase was complete), the developers built tools that aided them in building a better operating system. These tools included editors, compilers, compiler development tools, macro processors, and command line interpreters.

Building such tools was interesting, but Unix had to pay its way. The first official use of Unix was to do text processing for the patent application office at Bell Labs. For this reason, text processing tools were developed early in Unix’s life. By this, I mean tools to fill text, justify lines, highlight and underline words, build indexes and tables of contents, etc. Additional tools could locate strings of text from files, sort files on a line by line basis, extract information for reports, etc.

# ## 2.2.1 Low Cost Hardware

In 1960, Digital Equipment Corporation (DEC) introduced the PDP-1. At a price of $120,000 (compared to millions for other systems), it was a bargain. DEC firmly established itself in the field with further low-cost systems.

Unix was first implemented on a cast-off PDP-7, but once the basics were demonstrated, official support (in the form of $10K) was found to purchase a PDP-11/20.

For years, Unix was associated with the PDP-11. Although there were other inexpensive mini-computers on the market, the main point is that Unix was not initially targeted for the mainframe computers of the day.
