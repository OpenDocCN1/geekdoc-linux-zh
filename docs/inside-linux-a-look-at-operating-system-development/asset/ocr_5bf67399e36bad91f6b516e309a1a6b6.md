# Chapter 4

# System Kernel Requirements

Chapter 1 listed a number of features possessed by Linux, Chapter 2 has noted some of its antecedents, and Chapter 3 looked at how a user interacts with Linux. In this chapter, we will develop a list of requirements for a system kernel. A checklist, if you will, of what we’ve come to expect of a system kernel. In broad terms, a system kernel must maximize processor availability, protect users from mishap and attack, and provide interactive response and background processing. We can refine some of these requirements by tracing the development of what we call “system kernels” and the rest by noting shortcomings of different systems.

It would be a gross understatement to say the earliest computers were unwel­coming in their operation. Setting aside issues of large physical size, small storage size, modest speed, and low reliability, they still had a large number of architectural shortcomings. The high cost of logic meant they lacked instructions to perform even such common operations as multiplication. Operators had to load individual words of memory via front-panel switches and read results as strings of bits or digits on a display.

Better input/output devices soon appeared, but more importantly, it didn’t take long for the designers to realize the computer itself could be used to make its operation more user-friendly. In 1947, Alan Turing wrote, “There need be no real danger of it ever becoming a drudge, for any processes that are quite mechanical may be turned over to the machine itself[22].”
