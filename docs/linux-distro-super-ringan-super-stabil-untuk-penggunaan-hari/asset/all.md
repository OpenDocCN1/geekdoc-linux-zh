The history of Linux distro development dates back to 1991 when Linus Torvalds, a Finnish computer science student, released the first version of the Linux kernel. This open-source operating system quickly gained popularity due to its flexibility and the collaborative efforts of developers worldwide.

In the early years, various Linux distributions emerged, including Slackware, Debian, and Red Hat. These distributions aimed to package the Linux kernel along with other software components to provide a complete operating system experience.

Over time, more distributions like Ubuntu, Fedora, and Arch Linux were created, each catering to different user needs. Today, Linux distros continue to evolve, offering a diverse range of features, stability, and customization options.

A stable Linux distribution is a reliable and consistent operating system that is suitable for both daily life and critical environments. It provides a solid foundation for various tasks without frequent crashes or disruptions.

For daily life, a stable Linux distro should offer a user-friendly interface, a wide range of software applications, and seamless compatibility with hardware devices. It should prioritize ease of use, regular updates, and a strong community support system.

In critical environments, such as servers or industrial systems, stability and security are paramount. Thus, a stable Linux distro should focus on long-term support, rigorous testing, and robust security measures. Distributions like CentOS, Debian, and Red Hat Enterprise Linux (RHEL) are favored choices in these scenarios, as they offer extended support and have undergone extensive testing and verification.

Ultimately, the choice of a stable Linux distro depends on specific requirements and preferences. It is important to consider factors such as reliability, security, community support, and ease of use when selecting the best distribution for daily life or critical environments.

Several factors contribute to making a Linux distro stable for daily use:

1\. Reliability and Performance: A stable distro should have a track record of reliability, with minimal crashes or system failures. It should also offer good performance, ensuring smooth and efficient operation.

2\. Long-Term Support (LTS): Long-term support ensures that the distro receives regular updates, bug fixes, and security patches for an extended period. This is crucial for maintaining stability and addressing any vulnerabilities that may arise.

3\. Community Support: A strong and active community can provide assistance, troubleshooting, and guidance when issues arise. A stable distro often benefits from a large and supportive user community.

4\. Software Availability: A wide range of software applications compatible with the distro is essential for daily use. A stable distro should have a robust software ecosystem, including popular applications and tools.

5\. Hardware Compatibility: A stable distro should work seamlessly with various hardware configurations, minimizing compatibility issues and ensuring smooth operation across different devices.

6\. User-Friendly Interface: A stable distro for daily use should have an intuitive and user-friendly interface, making it easy for both beginners and experienced users to navigate and perform tasks efficiently.

7\. Testing and Quality Assurance: Rigorous testing and quality assurance processes during development help identify and resolve issues before the distro is released. This ensures a stable and reliable user experience.

8\. Regular Updates: Regular updates are crucial for fixing bugs, addressing security vulnerabilities, and introducing new features. A stable distro should have a well-defined update cycle to ensure ongoing stability and security.

9\. Compatibility with Existing Systems: A stable distro should be compatible with existing systems and infrastructure, allowing for seamless integration and interoperability.

Remember, the choice of a stable Linux distro for daily use may vary depending on individual preferences, requirements, and specific use cases.

# 2) Debian 12 LXDE OS

![image](d2d_images/chapter_title_below.png)

![image](d2d_images/image003.jpg)

Debian is an open-source operating system that was first released in 1993\. It was created by Ian Murdock with the goal of developing a free and community-driven distribution of the GNU/Linux operating system. Debian is known for its stability, security, and adherence to the principles of free software.

The history of Debian is closely tied to the GNU project, which aimed to create a complete Unix-compatible operating system using only free software. Debian adopted the GNU project’s software and combined it with the Linux kernel, resulting in the Debian GNU/Linux distribution.

Debian has a strong focus on open collaboration and is maintained by a large community of volunteers from around the world. It follows a strict code of conduct and a well-defined set of policies to ensure the quality and integrity of the distribution.

Debian 12, codenamed “Bookworm,” is the latest stable release of the Debian operating system. It was officially released on August 14, 2021. Debian 12 brings several updates and improvements to various aspects of the distribution, including updated software packages, bug fixes, and security enhancements.

Debian 12 LXDE is a lightweight desktop environment option available in Debian 12\. LXDE stands for Lightweight X11 Desktop Environment and is designed to work well with low-end or resource-constrained machines, such as older computers or netbooks.

# 4) Linux Mint 21.2 Victoria MATE Edition OS

![image](d2d_images/chapter_title_below.png)

![image](d2d_images/image016.jpg)

Linux Mint is a popular community-driven Linux distribution that was first released in 2006\. It was created by Clement Lefebvre, a French developer who initially started writing reviews and tutorials for various Linux websites. Based on the feedback and preferences of the Linux community, Lefebvre released the first version of Linux Mint, called Linux Mint 1.0 Ada, in August 2006 .

Linux Mint initially used Kubuntu as its base, but with the release of Linux Mint 2.0 Barbara in November 2006, it switched to a GNOME desktop based on Ubuntu 6.10 . Over time, Linux Mint gained popularity and developed its own identity, logo, and ambition to rival Windows and macOS . It quickly rose to become one of the most popular desktop Linux distributions, alongside Ubuntu .

Linux Mint introduced several unique tools and features between versions 4.0 “Daryna” and 9.0 “Isadora,” such as mintupdate, mintinstall, mintdisk, mintmenu, mintdesktop, mintnanny, mintbackup, mintstick, mintwelcome, and mintupload . These tools contributed to making Linux Mint a full-featured, stable, and widely used operating system .

The release of Linux Mint 17, named Qiana, marked a significant change in the development strategy. From Qiana onwards, Linux Mint decided to base its new versions on the source code of the current long-term support (LTS) release of Ubuntu until Ubuntu releases a new LTS version. This allowed Linux Mint to benefit from the stability and reliability of Ubuntu’s LTS releases while adding its own unique features and improvements.

Linux Mint 21.2 Victoria was released in July 2023 . It introduced several new features and improvements. One notable change is the upgrade to the Linux kernel version 6.2, which provides better compatibility with newer hardware The release also focused on stability, security, and user experience enhancements. Additionally, Linux Mint 21.2 Victoria included various software updates and bug fixes to ensure a smooth and reliable operating system Overall, Linux Mint 21.2 Victoria aimed to provide users with an improved and enjoyable desktop Linux experience.

Linux Mint 21.2 Victoria Mate edition introduced several new features and improvements. Here are seven notable features:

MATE 1.26: Linux Mint 21.2 Victoria Mate edition featured MATE desktop environment version 1.26, providing users with an updated and enhanced desktop experience.

Linux Kernel 5.15: The release included an upgrade to Linux kernel version 5.15, offering improved hardware compatibility and performance .

Extended Security Updates: Linux Mint 21.2 Victoria Mate edition received security updates until 2027, ensuring a secure and protected operating system.

Long-Term Package Base: Future versions of Linux Mint until 2024 will use the same package base as Linux Mint 21.2, making it easy for users to upgrade and maintain compatibility .

New Backgrounds: Linux Mint 21.2 Victoria Mate edition featured a superb collection of backgrounds from various artists, adding visual appeal to the desktop environment.

Improved Software: The release included updated software packages, bringing refinements and enhancements to various applications and tools.

Stability and Performance: Linux Mint 21.2 Victoria Mate edition aimed to provide a stable and reliable operating system, ensuring a comfortable and smooth user experience.

These features, along with other refinements and improvements, made Linux Mint 21.2 Victoria Mate edition a compelling choice for users seeking a user-friendly and stable desktop Linux experience.

Linux Mint Debian Edition (LMDE) is a Debian-based edition of Linux Mint that was first released in 2010\. It was created as an alternative to the Ubuntu-based editions of Linux Mint, providing a rolling release model directly based on Debian Testing. The goal of LMDE was to offer a more conservative and stable operating system, focusing on stability and security.

Unlike the Ubuntu-based editions, LMDE aimed to provide the same functionality and user experience while using Debian as its base. LMDE received updates from the Debian repositories designed for Debian Testing or Debian Squeeze. However, after LMDE 2, the Linux Mint team announced that they would no longer support the original rolling release version of LMDE.

LMDE 6, codenamed “Faye,” is the latest release of Linux Mint Debian Edition. It is based on Debian Bookworm and was released on September 27, 2023\. LMDE 6 features the Cinnamon desktop environment and includes the Linux kernel version 6.1\. The release introduced various enhancements, including an improved login screen, enhanced security for Warpinator (a file-sharing tool), proper support for HEIF and AVIF image formats, and under-the-hood improvements aimed at enhancing performance and user experience.

The development of LMDE 6 was announced on the Linux Mint blog, with a beta release on September 13, 2023, followed by the official release on September 27, 2023\. LMDE 6 aims to provide users with a stable, Debian-based Linux Mint experience with updated software and improved features.

# 14) Ghost BSD OS

![image](d2d_images/chapter_title_below.png)

![image](d2d_images/image011.png)

GhostBSD is a desktop-oriented operating system based on FreeBSD, with a focus on simplicity and ease of use. It provides a selection of commonly used software preinstalled and aims to offer a comfortable experience on the modern BSD platform. The project’s goal is to combine security, privacy, stability, usability, openness, and freedom, and it is free of charge.

The project had its beginnings in the early part of 2009, founded by Eric Turgeon and Francois Toussaint. The original goal was to produce a secure, installable FreeBSD Gnome live CD system to help new users adapt to FreeBSD. The first beta version, 1.0 BETA, was released on November 10, 2009.

The project continued to evolve, with the first graphical installer introduced in the 2.5 release. GhostBSD was initially based on FreeBSD, then briefly switched to TrueOS, and later returned to being based on FreeBSD. The project has gone through various base changes, aiming to provide a stable update and release cycle. The name “GhostBSD” originally stood for “GNOME hosted on BSD,” as it began as an effort to provide a ready-to-run GNOME desktop on FreeBSD. However, the main desktop was later changed to the GNOME 2 fork MATE, and the project began offering alternative desktop environments like XFCE, all using the GTK toolkit.

Today, GhostBSD is a simple, elegant, and friendly BSD operating system for desktops and laptops based on FreeBSD, with the official desktop environment being MATE. The installer leverages OpenZFS, making it easy to install GhostBSD on ZFS with other operating systems on the same drive, and is suitable for newcomers to FreeBSD. With modest hardware requirements, GhostBSD is ideal for modern workstations and 64-bit single-board computer hardware. Overall, GhostBSD aims to provide a simple, lightweight desktop experience on a Unix-like operating system, with a focus on ease of use and stability.

Endless OS, launched in 2016 by Endless Computers, is a Linux distribution designed primarily for users in developing regions with limited internet access. Its roots trace back to the company’s mission to create an accessible computing experience, particularly for education and productivity.

Built on Debian, Endless OS employs a customized GNOME desktop environment. The system features a unique approach to software management, allowing users to access a wide range of pre-installed applications and educational content offline. This offline capability is crucial, as it helps bridge the digital divide for those who may not have consistent internet access.

The OS is optimized for performance on low-end hardware, making it an attractive choice for schools and communities with limited resources. It emphasizes simplicity and user-friendliness, catering to users with varying levels of tech experience.

Over the years, Endless OS has evolved with regular updates, enhancing its user interface and expanding its application ecosystem. Its development continues to focus on improving accessibility and empowering users worldwide, embodying the philosophy of providing valuable computing resources to underserved populations.
