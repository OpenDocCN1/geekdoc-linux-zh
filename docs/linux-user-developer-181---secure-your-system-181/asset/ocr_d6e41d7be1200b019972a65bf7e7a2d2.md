# ## BashStyle-NG 10.0

Customise command-line settings via a GUI

If you've ever come to customise your Bash prompt and, it being a while since the last time you've probably had to Google for the command, perhaps you've wished for an easy GUI way to do it? You'll have dismissed that fancy and contradictory notion - after all, a GUI for Bash? - but here we present a GUI config that handles not just Bash settings, but Readline, Vim, nano and Git. Installation went smoothly once we'd added the Python configobj package to our test machine (it's in the Debian/Ubuntu repositories, as well as via pip). For Bash, most of the settings are for prompts and colours. There's a selection of predefined prompts; custom colours; a GUI for customising the prompt; and colour highlighting in grep. Extras include a customised cd, which can create directories on the fly; a tab completion blacklist (for blocking out backup files); and history settings. The Git settings - name, email, default editor, output colours - will be handy for new and occasional users. Have a dig through the other features of the package, like octal and temperature conversions. This is a niche piece of software, but some users will find it indispensable.

Above  Click a colourful icon and the GUI turns a more old-school grey for changing app parameters

+   - **Pros**
+       Takes away the burden of remembering all of the config file, shell variables and command-line settings

+   - **Cons**
+       Even if you like GUIs, it may seem as much trouble to open and use as looking for the config file option

**Great for...**  Quick config changes without diving into the man pages

# ## Mutt 1.8.3

Fast, efficient management of emails is a few commands away

![](bbox=[0.05, 0.73, 0.1, 0.78])

Those of us ancient enough to remember when email wasn't a smartphone app, but rather the main use of the internet, grew up with command-line clients like Elm and its clones Pine and Mutt. The move away from command-line clients has led to no gain in productivity, and with Mutt seeing a steady growth in improvements in the last two years, now could be the time for a revisit (or a first discovery).
It's one of the most efficient ways of dealing with a heavy email load. We need much more than a half-page review to tell you about the power of Mutt, so open the Newbie guide on the Mutt wiki, which also links to excellent third-party tutorials, then the

full HTML manual. There is a list of shortcuts across the top line, which changes by context, as you'll see after you arrow down to one of your mails and open it by pressing Enter. Mutt's power comes not just with practice of the basic commands, but in chaining them together to speed up tedious filing options.
Remember, those minutes spent hunting through menus in the likes of Gmail or Evolution and clicking checkboxes to move, archive or delete emails - all add up. Spend the time instead learning a few commands, and you can whizz through tasks without your fingers leaving the keyboard. And when you've shelved into your server - or your Raspberry Pi - you'll be able to fire off an email, or work through the /var/mail/ files in an instant.

+   - **Pros**
+       Productive shortcuts, context-specific settings; keyboard macros automate complex tasks

+   - **Cons**
+       Plenty to learn, but the Mutt slogan is 'All mail clients suck. This one just sucks less!'

**Great for...**
Finally get on your e-mail mountain's under control
