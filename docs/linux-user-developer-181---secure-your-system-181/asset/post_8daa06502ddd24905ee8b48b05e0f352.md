## 5 Tips from the pros

01 **Optimise your website images**
When uploading your website to the internet, make sure all of your images are optimised for web use! Try using jpegmini.com software; or if using WordPress, install the EWWW Image Optimizer plugin.

02 **Host your website in the UK**
Make sure your website is hosted in the UK, not just for legal reasons! If your server is located overseas, you may be missing out on search engine rankings on google.co.uk – you can check where your site is based on www.check-host.net.

03 **Do you make regular backups?**
How would it affect your business if you lost your website today? It is essential to always make your own backups; even if your host offers you a backup solution, it's important to take responsibility for your own data and protect it.

04 **Trying to rank on Google?**
Google made some changes in 2015. If you're struggling to rank on Google, make sure that your website is mobile-responsive! Plus, Google now prefers secure (HTTPS) websites! Contact your host to set up and force HTTPS on your website.

05 **Avoid cheap hosting**
We're sure you've seen those TV adverts for domain and hosting for £1! Think about the logic... for £1, how many clients will be jam-packed onto that server? Surely they would use cheap £20 drives rather than £1k+ enterprise SSDs! Try to remember that you do get what you pay for!

Chris: Thanks, Dan. As I said to the good doctor Hedberg, we do cater for a wide range of readers and no level does tend to start at around the enthusiast level. Our last survey indicated that most of our readers have been using Linux and computers for a number of years (or decades in some cases). However, we’ve taken on board what you and others have said and are introducing a Linux Essentials series at the front of tutorials (see p36, if you haven’t spotted it already).

We’re kicking things off with an intro to Bash. (You may have heard it referred to as “the terminal”, which conjures up something dark and mysterious.) Bash is the default shell that ships with Linux and can be used to navigate your file system and discover incredible amounts of detailed information about your system, access powerful utilities and script lots of mundane tasks. It’s a powerful component of Linux that might seem a little daunting if you’re a newcomer, but after a while you’ll wonder how you ever lived without it.

As the series progresses, we’ll dip into AWK (a programming language, which is great for reporting and data extraction), grep (a handy plain-text search utility) and sed (a stream editor for dividing text into component parts and transforming it). We hope that those who regularly muck about with the terminal will pick up a few handy tips as well. Linux Essentials is written by the charming John Gowers, a lecturer in Programming and Computer Science at Bath University, so you’re in safe hands.
