I was interested to read Dr Nikoalias Hedberg's letter [L&D180] to, pH4) an only finding 10% of L&LD relevant to him. I am not quite a “10 by percent (’f’ I were I probably wouldn’t buy the magazine, but it's great he gets so much out of that small percentage), but I would like to see some more noob-friendly content in my monthly hit of Linux-verse stuff. Chris: Thanks, Dan. As I said to the good doctor Hedberg, we do cater for a wide range of readers and no level does tend to start at around the enthusiast level. Our last survey indicated that most of our readers have been using Linux and computers for a number of years (or decades in some cases). However, we’ve taken on board what you and

others have said and are introducing a Linux Essentials series at the front of tutorials (see p36, if you haven’t spotted it already).
We’re kicking things off with an intro to Bash. (You may have heard it referred to as “the terminal”, which conjures up something dark and mysterious.) Bash is the default shell that ships with Linux and can be used to navigate your file system and discover incredible amounts of detailed information about your system, access powerful utilities and script lots of mundane tasks. It’s a powerful component of Linux that might seem a little daunting if you’re a newcomer, but after a while you’ll wonder how you ever lived without it.
As the series progresses, we’ll dip into AWK (a programming language, which is great for reporting and data extraction), grep (a handy plain-text search utility) and sed (a stream editor for dividing text into component parts and transforming it). We

hope that those who regularly muck about with the terminal will pick up a few handy tips as well. Linux Essentials is written by the charming John Gowers, a lecturer in Programming and Computer Science at Bath University, so you’re in safe hands.
