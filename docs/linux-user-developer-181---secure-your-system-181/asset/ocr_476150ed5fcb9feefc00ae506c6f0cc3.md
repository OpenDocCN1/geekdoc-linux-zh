As one of the oldest surviving Linux distros, Debian is a testament to the most endearing quality of the Linux ecosystem – collaboration. The project works on three branches of development – Stable, Testing and Unstable – and packages move from one to the next, as they mature. The latest Stable release is the product of over two years of gruelling testing, and the end result is an incredibly stable and reliable distro.

Along from its slow-cooking release policy and vast software repositories, another feature that distinguishes Debian from its peers is its software

policy. The distro only features applications released under a free software licence. Proprietary codecs, drivers for hardware devices, such as printers and graphics cards, are relegated to the non-free software repository, which isn’t enabled by default.

It’s due to the strict technical requirements that must be met for inclusion of software in Debian that for over ten years it didn’t ship with two of the most popular open source applications: Mozilla Firefox browser and Thunderbird email client, replacing them with the rebranded Iceweasel and Icedove.
