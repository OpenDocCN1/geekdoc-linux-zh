> “ There's been a flurry of activity on the Linux Mint website (www.linuxmint.com) in recent days as a succession of blog posts herald the release of four spins of Linux Mint 18.2 Sonya (reviewed, p32), one of which uses its officially supported desktop environment, Cinnamon, while the others are MATE, KDE and Xfce. This release adds a host of features (See What's New in Linux Mint?, p28), such as better Bluetooth support and a new display manager, LightDM. During this hectic time, we spoke to Clément 'Clém' Lefebvre about the progress of the freshest distro around.

How did Linux Mint come to be and what is its aim?
I started in 2006. I was writing for http://linuxforums.org and I wanted to host some of my articles myself. I needed a domain name and I chose 'linuxmint.com'. It was short and it conveyed the idea of freshness and technology that just works. That's where the name came from: it was the domain name for a collection of articles, reviews and tutorials dedicated to Linux.

At the same time, I had been reviewing distributions for a while and tinkering with a lot of things, so I had good knowledge of how they worked and what I liked or didn't really like in each one of them. At some stage I got something out and I wrote a bit about it on linuxmint.com. I was surprised by the feedback I got and a few months later it was clear people were more interested in that little project than in my articles.

There was no plan to create a new distribution initially, and certainly not one that could rival the likes of Microsoft Windows, Apple macOS or even the established Linux distros. People showed interest in what I was doing and so I responded to that. By the time version 2.1 came out, the goal had changed and this was no longer an experiment.
