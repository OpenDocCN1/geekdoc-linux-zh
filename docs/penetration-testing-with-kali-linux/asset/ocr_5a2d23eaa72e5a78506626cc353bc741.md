# 3.5.2 Standards and Frameworks

PCI DSS. The Payment Card Data Security Standard (PCI DSS)⁹⁷ is an information security standard, first published in 2004, for organizations handling customer payment data for major credit cards. It is managed by the Payment Card Industry Standards Council. It's purpose is to ensure that payment data is properly secured in order to reduce the risk of credit fraud with other frameworks, PCI DSS consists of a number of requirements, compliance with which must be assessed annually. Most of these requirements resemble other best practices regulating network and security system, access control, vulnerability management, monitoring. For example, Requirement 2 prohibits the use of vendor-supplied defaults for system passwords and other security-related parameters. Other requirements are credit-card formulations of other familiar best practices. For example, Requirement 3 outlines types of credit card data that should be stored and must be protected.

CIS Top 18. The Center for Internet Security (CIS) Critical Security Controls, also known as CIS Controls⁹⁸ are a set of 18 (previously 20) recommended controls to increase an organization’s security posture. While not these as laws or regulations, these controls pertain to areas that regulations are concerned with, including data protection, access control management, continuous vulnerability management, malware detection, and more.

These controls are divided into a number of safeguards (previously known as sub-controls), which, in turn, are grouped into three implementation groups⁹⁹ intended to prioritize safeguard implementation. IG consists of controls that are considered the minimum standard for information security to protect against the most common attacks and should be implemented by every organization. They are practically implemented by businesses with limited IT expertise that manage data of low sensitivity. IG consists of additional safeguards that are meant to help more complex organizations, typically with multiple departments and staff dedicated to managing IT infrastructure with sensitive customer and proprietary data. IG, which consists of all safeguards, is typically implemented by dedicated cybersecurity experts managing sensitive data that may be subject to oversight.
