# 4.5.4 The SQ3R Method

The SQ3R method169 has students follow a pattern of study activities - survey, question, read, recite. We will detail the SQ3R method, but it is very similar to the PQ4R method,170 which is useful for reading comprehension. Students who find the following tactic useful may want to check out the PQ4R method as well.

A learner begins by surveying the topic, or reviewing a high level outline, scanning through the material that might be covered during the study session. In particular, it would be important to review any highlighted text, diagrams, and headings.

Let's give an example. In the case of our current Module, a student might encounter the various headings and subheadings: Learning Challenges to Learning Technical Skills, OffSec Training Methodology, and so on. They might then review the subheadings.

Next, we create, preferably in writing, a list of questions that they hope to have answered the material. This may or not reflect what the material will actually contain, but should be based largely on the survey. This is a very important step, as learners will return to the questions repeatedly.

Next, the student reads one section at a time. If there are videos or other activities for this section, they can also complete those.

Next, the learner returns to their list of questions for that smaller section. They should try and recite the questions back from memory and if they are now able to answer them.

Finally, in review, a student returns to all of the smaller sections from a larger topic or chapter to check whether or not the questions have been answered and they can recall the answers.

For learners who have been taught that note is simply "writing things down important", the SQ3R method represents an alternative that is much more effective.
