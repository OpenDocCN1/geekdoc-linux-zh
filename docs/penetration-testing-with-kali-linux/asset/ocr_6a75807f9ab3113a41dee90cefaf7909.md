#  3.5.1 Laws and Regulations

Much be written about cybersecurity laws and regulations, especially since countries and jurisdictions have all their own. Most of the items we'll discuss here are centered on the United States; however, some are applicable globally well. As a security professional, it is always important to understand exactly laws and regulations one may be subject to.

HIPAA The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996 (HIPAA)⁸ is a United States federal law regulating health care and the privacy of patient health information. Included in this law was a requirement to create a set of standards for protecting patient health information, known as Protected Health Information (PHI). The standards that regulate how PHI can be used and disclosed are established by the Privacy Rule⁸⁶ This rule sets limits on how information can be shared without a patient's consent and grants patients a number of records over their information, such as the right to obtain a copy of their health records.

Another rule known as the Security Rule⁸⁷ outlines how electronic PHI (e-PHI) must be protected. It describes three classes of safeguards that must be in place administrative (having a designated security officer, a security management process, periodic assessments, etc.), physical (facility access control, device security), and technical (access control, transmission security, audits, etc.). These rules also include provisions for enforcement and penalties for non-compliance. Importantly, HIPAA also requires that covered entities (healthcare providers, health plans, business associates, etc.) provide notification⁸⁸ that a PH breach occurs.

FERPA The Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act of 1974 (FERPA)⁸⁹ is a United States federal law regulating the privacy of education records. This law limits upon the disclosure and use of records without parents' learners' consent. Some instances where schools are permitted to disclose these records are school transfers, cases of health or safety emergency, and with a judicial order.

FERPA also grants parents and learners over the age of 18 a number of rights over their information. These rights include the right to inspect records, the right to request modification to inaccurate or misleading records, and more. Schools that fail to comply with these laws risk access to federal funding.
