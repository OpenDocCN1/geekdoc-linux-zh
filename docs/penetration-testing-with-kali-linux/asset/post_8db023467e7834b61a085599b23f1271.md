## Listing 380 - Display local groups in CLIENT220

The output displays the non-standard groups admin, BackupUsers, and helpdesk. The admin group may be of interest to us since it contains "admin" and has a custom description indicating the members are admitted to all workstations on the second floor. However, we don't currently have any further information regarding what departments are on.

Another interesting finding is the group BackupUsers as they have extensive permissions to perform their operations on the file system, making them potentially valuable targets.

From the non-standard, there are several built-in groups we should analyze, such as Administrators, Backup Operators, Remote Desktop Users, and Remote Management Users. Members of Backup Operators can capture and restore all files on a computer, even those they don't have permissions for. We must not confuse this with non-standard groups such as BackupUsers in our example. Members of Remote Desktop Users can access the system with RDP, while members of Remote Management Users can access with WinRM. For this example, let's review the members of admin and Administrators. We can do this with Get-LocalGroupMember, passing the group name as an argument.

## Listing 381 - Display members of the group admin

381 shows that only daveadmin is a member of admin. However, admin is not listed in the local Administrators group. We know that members of the group are administrators to workstations on the second floor. The information we have gathered far is not directly actionable, but it may become so we move deeper into the network and identify systems which remain on the second floor.

The output also shows that from the disabled local Administrator account, the users daveadmin and BackupAdmin are members of the local Administrators group. Therefore, we've identified two highly valuable targets.

While it is crucial to know which users are privileged, it is vital for us to understand which users can use RDP. Obtaining the credentials for one of these users may lead us to a GUI access, which often tremendously improves our means of interacting with the system.
