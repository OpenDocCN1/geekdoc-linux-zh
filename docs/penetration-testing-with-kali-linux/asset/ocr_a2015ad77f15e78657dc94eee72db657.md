The term cybersecurity came to mainstream from a military origin. For clarity, we'll use cybersecurity to describe the protection of access and information specifically on the Internet or other digital networks. While included within the broader context of cybersecurity, information also ensures the protection of physical-storing assets, such as physical servers or vaults.

As we explore various threats and threat actors throughout this Module, we'll mainly consider our online capabilities. Therefore, we'll generally use the term cybersecurity here, but won't be too concerned about using information security as a synonym.

# 3.2.1 The Evolution of Attack and Defense

Cybersecurity can be especially fascinating because it involves multiple agents trying to achieve mutually exclusive objectives. In the most basic example, a defender wants to control access to an asset they own, and an attacker wants gain control over the same asset. This is interesting because both role, defender and attacker, subsist on the continued persistence of the other. In particular, each will be more skilled and sophisticated because *of* the efforts (or imagined efforts) of their counterpart.

The attacker-defender relationship dynamic helps fundamentally explain why cybersecurity becomes exponentially complicated over time. To understand this dynamic better, let's introduce the fictional characters Alice and Bob. We'll make use of them throughout the OffSec Learning Library and the *cryptography* literature in contexts to demonstrate examples and thought experiments.

For this particular story, let's imagine that Bob has an asset that he wants to defend: a banana tree! Bob wants to make sure that only he can pick his bananas. Meanwhile, attacker Alice would love to nothing more than to steal Bob's bananas.

First, Bob doesn't pay any special attention to the security of his tree. It's relatively easy for Alice to walk up to it and steal a banana. As Alice gets better and better at stealing, however, Bob will also get better protecting his tree.
