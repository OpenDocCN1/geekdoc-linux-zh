# 4.7.6 Study Your Own Studies

Let's wrap up this Module by examining our responsibility not just for learning, but the assessment of strategy. Since many of the details of a "classroom" is constructed up to you (the student), you are also responsible for assessing and improving on strategy.

While this might sounds like a lot, let's review an easy and effective approach: at the end of a study session, take just 10 seconds to think about how well it went. It's a very small thing, but it can make a huge difference. To understand how, we'll look at the two most obvious and extreme outcomes of a study session.

If the study session was particularly difficult, much of self-reflection might lead you to think about some of the content made it difficult. Generally speaking, we want to ask why it was difficult. The easy answer here might be that "SQL Injection is just tough" but the difficulty of the material is at least out of our hands (though this might indicate a need to spend the next study session reviewing some foundational materials).

We're specifically interested in the things that, as learners, have some control over. Here is a list of potential questions to ask the study session:

+   1. What time did I start the study session?
2. How long was the study session?
3. Did I get interrupted (if, so, how did that happen)?
4. What did I do before I started studying?
5. What did I eat or drink before I studying?
6. What was my study location like? Was it quiet or busy?
7. What did I do during the study session specifically?

This is not a complete list of possible questions.

The answer to each of these things might lead us to locate a more specific point of frustration. For example, if we discover that a heavy meal immediately before a study led to us feeling unprepared and sluggish, then we can adjust when or how much we eat beforehand.
