Another way of thinking about this is that the OffSec training materials are organized around concepts, not commands.

Finally, teaching learners how to expect the unexpected is not easy to deliver. However, we often accomplish this by using an indirect route to our goal with the intention of realistically highlighting what you may experience in the field. Again, we hope to connect the logic behind our decisions of simply presenting commands and syntax.

In this example, we mentioned a potential pitfall with directory permissions (in a sidebar). We also know that /chmod + /usr/bin/chmod wouldn't work, but we included it and ran it. We'll walk through "unexpected" scenarios when we present new Modules and include unexpected outcomes in many of our challenges.

As students, it's imperative that we grow comfortable in situations where we don't fully understand and try things that might not work. The only way to be prepared for the "unexpected" is to comfortable in situations where we don't know how things will pan out.

Not only this, but we cannot afford situations where we feel stuck. In security, it's extremely rare that the first approach will work. In order to accurately represent this field, OffSec's approach is to teach the material in such a way that students can become more resilient and agile, working through a particular problem until we are "unstuck".
