# # 4.1.3 The Forgetting Curve and Cognitive Load

In a fictional tale by Jorge Borges, a character named Funes the Memorious¹135 could remember in vivid detail every thing he witnessed. Unfortunately, two of the most common problems we encounter when trying to learn something (or create a memory) are "too long ago" or "too much information at once".

Let's start by examining the process of forgetting. In 1885, scientist Hermann Ebbinghaus set to memorize a few documents, then tested himself repeatedly on what he remembered. He was only able to remember all of the details he tested himself immediately after memorizing. Ebbinghaus found he only remembered 100 percent of the information at the time of acquisition. After that, he started forgetting very quickly. When he waited 20 minutes, he could only remember 58%. A day later, he could only remember 23%. He called this The Forgetting Curve.¹136
