# Putting the general purpose in GPIO

GPIO pins are all general purpose because you can use them for anything you want the control of a program. They're input/output pins because the software can configure them to be either an input or an output. When a pin is an input, the program can read whether a high voltage or low voltage is being on the pin. When the pin is the output, the program can control whether a high voltage or low voltage appears on the pin. In addition, many pins have one or more superpowers—alternative functions as a secret identity, like so many book heroes. These powers are not shared by all pins, but are specialists able to do things without software intervention. That act as ways to tap directly, deep into the computer's inner workings. When you switch to these functions, they stop being general-purpose pins and do a specific job. For example, one pin can be used to output a continuous stream of high and low voltage levels in alternation, after they get going, continue without further intervention from the program. So, if you connect that pin to a speaker or an amplifier, you can generate a tone that keeps on sounding until you command it to stop.
