For its screen position, the alien needs to have a Y coordinate of 150, which is near the top of the screen. You don't want to use the full width of the Stage, because it looks odd when half the alien is off the edge of the Stage. From experimentation, we found that the ideal starting X position for our alien is between -180 and 180, but yours vary, depending on its size.

Drag in the Motion block you used previously to a particular X and Y position. If you click Operators at the top of the Blocks Palette, you can find a block to pick a random number from 1 to 10. Drag this block into the hole where you would normally type the X position, and then change the numbers in the random number to -180 and 180.

# # Detecting When a Sprite Hits Another Sprite

There's no point in throwing flaming fireballs at an alien if it won't even raise an eyebrow. To make this game fun, you need to make the alien react whenever it's hot. Most games involve sprites hitting other (bats and balls, targets and weapons, crashing and catching), so collision detection, as it is often called, a staple of game design.

You can detect whether the fireball is touching the alien sprite from the fireball, but it is the alien that must react, so that's where you need to put your script.
