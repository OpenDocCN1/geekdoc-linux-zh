The Pi is also being used to make weather stations, fitness gadgets, devices, audiobook players, electric skateboards, and much more, as we discover in Chapter 20.

Although those projects are grabbing headlines, another story is less visible but more important: the thousands of people of all ages who are taking their first steps in computer science, thanks to the Raspberry Pi.

Both of the authors of this book used computers in the 1980s, when the notion of a home computer became a reality. Back then, computers were less friendly than they are today. When you switched them on, you were faced with a flashing cursor and had to do something in to get it to do anything. As a result, though, a whole generation grew up knowing at least a bit about how to give the computer commands, and how to create programs for it. As computers started to use mice and windows, people didn't need those skills any more, and they lost touch with them.
