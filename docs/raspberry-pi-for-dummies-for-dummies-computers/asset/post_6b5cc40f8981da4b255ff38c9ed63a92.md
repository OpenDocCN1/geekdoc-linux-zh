- 3. Click the Flash! button on the right.

The image file is copied to your card and converted into all the files you'll need to run your OS.

## Inserting the SD Card

To start up, the Raspberry Pi requires an SD or microSD card with the operating system on it. If you do not have one, see Chapter 2 for advice on downloading the operating system and copying it to an SD or MicroSD card.

To insert a MicroSD card in a Model 2, 3, A+, or B+, turn your Raspberry Pi over, as shown in Figure 3-4, so that you're looking at its underside. The metal MicroSD card socket is labeled and is on the left edge of the board. Slide the MicroSD card into the slot face. On the Raspberry Pi 3 Model B, the card will slide into place. On the other models, it will click into place. The card will overhang the board slightly.

To insert an SD card in a Model A or Model B, flip your Raspberry Pi over. (See Figure 3-5.) On one of the short sides is a plastic fixture for your SD card. Slide the card with the left side facing, and gently press the card to make sure it's well connected. The fixture is not big enough to cover your SD card, so most of the card will stick out from the side of the board and will be visible when you turn your Pi back over again. You can remove the card by just pulling it out again.

## Connecting the Raspberry Pi Camera Module

There are lots of accessories and add-ons available for the Raspberry Pi, but the camera module has a ribbon cable that plugs into the Raspberry Pi. The camera module is a small circuit board, and you can see it in Figure 3-7. To open the camera connector on the Raspberry Pi, hold the ends between your finger and thumb and gently lift. The parts don't separate, but they move apart to make a gap. This is where you insert the camera's cable.

At the end of the camera's cable are silver connectors on one side. Hold the cable so that this side faces to the left, away from the side with the USB socket(s). Insert the cable into the connector on the board and press it gently home, and then press the socket back together.

## Connecting the camera on a Pi Zero

The Pi Zero W uses a different socket for the camera. To open the camera connector on the Raspberry Pi, hold the ends between your finger and thumb and gently pull. The parts don't separate, but they move apart to make a gap. This is where you insert the camera's cable.

On the Raspberry Pi, the camera connector is on the right. Insert the cable with the shiny contacts facing the front, and press the socket back again. On the Pi Zero, insert the cable with the shiny contacts facing the bottom of the board (flat side). When the cable is flat, the camera will be facing down, but you can bend the cable so that the camera sits on top of the board and faces up. One of the covers for the official Zero case has a hole for the camera.
