You can get NeoPixels in conventional LED packages – not the surface mount type, in other words as well as the DotStar's 5mm-square packages. Both are available as individual parts as well as long strips of LEDs. The major difference between the two lies in how they take their commands from the computer. The NeoPixel requires one signal wire, and the DotStar requires two. Given that fact, you might be forgiven for thinking that NeoPixels are easier to use. The problem is twofold:

+   This signal wire needs to be controlled to give a precisely timed pulse.

+   The Raspberry Pi, with its Linux operating system, is not good at precise timing.

There are ways around this with the help of various libraries, but around comes at a price. The library from Adafruit works with the original Model 1 Raspberry Pi as well as the Pi Zero, and the one from Pimoroni, working on all models of Pi, uses the hardware that normally generate the audio for the jack socket. (You can still get sound from the HDMI connector, but not from the audio jack because it can cause the LEDs to flash randomly.)
