We recommend using a good quality 60/40 tin/lead solder alloy, with a diameter of 0.7mm and a built-in rosin-based flux core. Anything else is making life difficult for yourself. We've found that solder with self-cleansing fluxes or non-fuming fluxes are harder to work with, as well being more expensive. The right kind of solder with a good soldering iron, preferably a temperature-controlled with one fine tip.

TIP

It is often said that you can use any old tool to learn on, and then get a good tool when you get at using it. This is rubbish. As a beginner, you are fighting how to do the job, so you don't want to fighting your tools as well. A good iron includes a stand and a sponge for,, removing flux that has accumulated during the soldering process. If it were not removed, it would form a glassy layer that would prevent the iron from making good thermal contact with the solder of joint. Use a proper soldering sponge—a natural one that won't melt on contact with the iron. Do not use a plastic foam sponge because your iron will go through it.

# Making a soldered joint

The first thing you should do when making a soldered joint is to make a mechanical joint. For example, if you're joining two wires, bend each end into a hook and squeeze together lightly with pliers.

Wipe the tip of the iron on a damp sponge, and melt just a spot of solder on the tip. This "wets" the tip and allows good thermal contact to take place between the tip and the work. Then apply the iron, solder, and wires all together. The secret is then to look at the joint and the solder closely, to see how it sits. Remove the solder, but keep the iron on the joint until you see the flow around the joint and see it into the cracks. Then is the joint hot enough for you to—draw your iron. It is a quick process and needs a bit of practice.

Many beginners make the mistake of putting too much solder on a joint. Try to use as little as possible. A joint is rarely because of too little solder, but it's often because of too much. When you are done, you see a small amount of black flux residue around the iron tip. Wipe that away on a damp sponge returning the iron to its stand. Do not move the joint as the solder sets.
