# FocusWriter

Whether you're writing the next blockbuster from your bedroom or you just need to get your work without distraction, FocusWriter might be the application for you. It's a word processor that is designed to be distraction-free. Most of the time when you're using it, the only thing onscreen is your writing.

When you move the mouse to the top of the screen, the menus for changing the settings and saving your files appear. To keep your motivation up, you can set a daily goal in the Preferences settings for time spent writing (better still) words written per day. When you move the mouse to the bottom of the screen, you can see the word count and your progress toward your daily goal.

To install FocusWriter, search for focuswriter on the Add/Remove Software menus or, in the shell, use

```
sudo apt-get install focuswriter
```

To start FocusWriter, go into your desktop environment and click the program's entry in the Office category of your Applications menu. You can find out more about the application at http://gotcode.org/focuswriter.

# Mathematica

Mathematica is what is known as a symbolic package, or a CAS (Computer Algebra System), and it's preinstalled in Raspbian. It is one of the best systems for exploring anything to do with numbers, from mathematics to complex multid graphics and music.

To get started, click the Applications menu, and click the Mathematica icon. When you see a splash screen, and then two windows open: a blank notebook and, in front of it, an invitation to visit three websites. Click the notebook to bring it to the front. Type 2AS and Press Return. This expression to the power of, but you don't see an answer. You've entered the expression into Mathematica, but in order to tell the program to evaluate it (and give you the answer), use Shift+Return.

Mathematica can expand equations for you:

```
Expand[(1+x)^6]
1 + 6x² + 20x³ + 15x⁴ + 6x⁵ + x⁶
```
