We don't have room to provide an in-depth guide to spreadsheets here, but we can show you how to work out a simple holiday budget.

A spreadsheet is basically a grid of information, but it's powerful because you can use it to perform calculations using that information. The boxes on the spreadsheet are called cells. To enter information into a cell, just click it and type what you want to enter. Alternatively, you can click a cell and type into (or edit the contents of) the Formula bar at the top of the screen. (Refer to Figure 6–3.)

Each cell has a grid reference, taken from the letter at top of columns and the number on the left of its row. The top-left cell is A1, and the next cell to the right is B1, and the one below it is B2. (Refer to Figure 6–3.)

To start with, enter a list of the different expenses you'll incur, your way down the screen in column A. Beside each item, in column B, enter how it costs. In column C, enter how many of item you will need. For example, row of our example shows the name of the hotel in column A, how much costs per night (in column B on the same row), and then a C for the number of nights Sean will stay there on that row. In Figure 6–3, we can see've also written titles in the cells at the top of the columns of data so that we can easily see what is in each column.

You can make a column wider so that you can easily enter the descriptions of your budget items. Click and drag the line between the letter at the top of the column and the letter at the top of the column to its right.

To show a currency sign in a cell, click the Format menu, choose Cells, and then change the category to Currency and the format to the layout and currency symbol you would like to use. You can select a group of cells and format them at the same time by clicking and dragging the cells before you go into Format menu.

You can enter formulae (or calculations) into cells, and the answers will appear in the spreadsheet. If you want to enter a formula into a cell, you type the equal sign (=), you use an asterisk (*) for multiplication and a slash (/) for division. For example, click any cell and enter

```
=7*5
```

The result (35) appears in the cell where you entered the formula. You can view or edit the formula itself by clicking the cell and then clicking the formula bar above the spreadsheet, or double-clicking the cell.

The magic happens when you start using the numbers in one cell to work out what should go in another one. You do that by using the grid reference of a cell in your formula. For the holiday budget, you multiply the cost of an item (such as a night in a hotel) by how many of them you need (six nights). The first of those values is stored in column B, and the second is beside it in column C, both on the same row. After the titles and spacing at the top of the spreadsheet, the first expense is on row 5. In column D5, you enter

```
=B5*C5
```

This multiplies the values in cell B5 (the price) and C5 (the quantity) and puts the result (the total amount spent on that particular item) into cell D5. You can click cell D5 and then copy its contents and paste them into the cells below. There are options for copying and pasting from the Edit menu, but LibreOffice also supports Windows shortcuts, including Ctrl-C to copy and Ctrl-V to paste.

You might think the same number goes into those cells, but it actually copies the formula and not the result, and updates it for the correct row number as it goes. If you copy the formula from cell D5 into cell D6 and then click D6 and look on the Formula bar, you'll see that says

```
=B6+C6
```

## Creating presentations in LibreOffice Impress

If you're looking to create a presentation, or if you want to deliver one, Impress is a powerful tool. If you're new to presentations, you might find it a bit daunting, but it's actually easy to learn. To create a presentation, follow these steps:

- 1. Start Impress and choose a new presentation from the File menu.
- 2. Click the Slide button on the right to add a new slide.
- 3. Choose a layout you would like to use from the options.
- 4. Type your title and other text into the text boxes.
- 5. To add more slides, click the New Slide button.
- 6. To add an image, use the Insert menu and choose Image.
- 7. To reorder slides, click and drag them in the Slides panel.
- 8. Repeat Steps 2 to 5 to fill in the slide.
- 9. To edit a previous slide, click it in the Slides panel.
- 10. To save your presentation, go to the File menu and choose Save.

When you're ready to show your presentation, click the Slide Show menu and choose From Beginning. You can navigate through the slides using the arrow keys or by clicking with the mouse. To exit the slide show, press the Escape key.

Impress lots of features to explore, including colorful templates (click the Master Pages button on the vertical menu on the right), transitions that animate the drawing of a slide (also found on this menu), and tools (similar to those in LibreOffice Draw) for making shapes.
