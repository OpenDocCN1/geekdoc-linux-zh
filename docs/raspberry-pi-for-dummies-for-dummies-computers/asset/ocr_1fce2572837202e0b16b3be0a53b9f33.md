We strongly recommend that you don't rely on physical layout diagrams, but instead learn the simple process of converting the schematic into a layout. There's no need to plan it all beforehand — just it one at a time and build it up. It's a skill that's easy to learn and will pay you back handsomely as you progress through your learning of this subject.

16-11 shows a photograph of the hardware for our project. We ended up using 3mm diameter LEDs for it. Note how it's even messier than the layout diagram. There are two causes for such messiness: First of all, you can't bend the wires out of the way so they don't obscure anything. Second, the whole point of using breadboard is so that you can reuse its components. This means you generally don't snip the component leads to be short and close to the board — you leave them sticking high above the board.
