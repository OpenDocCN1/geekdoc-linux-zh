# Determining What Else You Need

The creators of Raspberry Pi have stripped costs to the able to own a fully featured computer for less than $35, so you'll need to scavenge or buy a few bits and pieces in order to use your Pi. We say scavenge because the things you need are exactly the kind of things many people have lying around their house or garage or can easily pick up from friends or neighbors. In particular, if you're using a Raspberry Pi as your second computer, you probably have most of the peripherals you need. Said, you might find they're not fully compatible with the Raspberry Pi and you need to buy replacements to use with the Pi.

Here's a checklist of what you might need:

+   **Monitor:** The Raspberry Pi has a high-definition video feed and uses an HDMI (high-definition multimedia interface) connection for it. If your monitor has an HDMI socket, you can connect the Raspberry Pi directly to it. If your monitor does not support HDMI, it probably has a DVI socket, and you can use a simple and cheap converter that enables you to connect an HDMI cable to it. Older VGA (video graphics array) monitors require a device to convert the HDMI signal into a VGA. If you're thinking of buying a converter, check first whether it works with the Raspberry Pi. A lot of cheap cables,, what you need is a device that converts the signal from HDMI to VGA, not one that fits onto the screen or on Raspberry Pi. These converters can be expensive, so Gert van Loo has designed a device that uses the Raspberry Pi's GPIO pins to connect to a VGA monitor. He's published the design specs so anyone can build one, and sell it if he wants to. For more information, check www.raspberrypi.org/blog/berry-vga-adapter. If your monitor is connecting a blue plug and the connector has three rows of pins in it, it's probably a VGA monitor.

+   **TV:** You can connect your Raspberry Pi to a high-definition TV using the HDMI socket and should experience a crisp picture.
