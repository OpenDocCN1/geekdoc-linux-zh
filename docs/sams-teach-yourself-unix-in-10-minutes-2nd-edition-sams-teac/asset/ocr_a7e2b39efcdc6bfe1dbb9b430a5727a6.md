# Practicing with Unix: It Really Does Make You Perfect!

In this chapter we are going to learn to start Unix, but before we do, a quick word about practice. It can't be said enough: Practice makes perfect. As with anything foreign to you, as long as you can read this book, follow along, and walk through the steps outlined, you will eventually develop the speed you see from an experienced Unix operator and feel comfortable Unix.

When new learners see the speed with which experienced Unix users enter commands, they often panic and feel as if they will always be novices because they cannot recall commands as quickly as they would like. Do not think you aren't knowledgeable because you can't remembered commands quickly; as long as you know what you are doing, speed is not a factor. Start small and take baby steps, but practice to develop recall. To a new learner, Unix commands can look like a foreign language, and in a way, they are. Apply the same concept of how to learn a new language such as Spanish, English, or Chinese to learn Unix commands. Repetition builds up your recall. You will only be able to recall these commands and recall them quickly if you practice. When you get a copy of Unix, install it on your home PC and practice the commands; the commands will become familiar to you in no time. The more you practice the commands, the better you will remember them and the easier Unix will be. Of course, practice is not by any means mandatory for reading or completing this book. Getting a copy of Unix installed so you can practice and continue to expand your knowledge is completely up to you and for your own growth and benefit.
