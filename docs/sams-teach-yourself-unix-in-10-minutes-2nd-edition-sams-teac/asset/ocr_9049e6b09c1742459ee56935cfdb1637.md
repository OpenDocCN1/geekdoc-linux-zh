# KDE

In our final section of this lesson, we will cover KDE, which stands for the K Desktop Environment. There are many other environments that you can use, with one of the most popular being GNOME. Another environment is the Novel Linux Desktop (NLD), which is similar to GNOME and KDE. You can see this environment by going to Novel’s website at http://www.novell.com/products/desktop/.

Although many more environments are under development, and because there is a lot of documentation on it, it is easier to learn. (Remember, documentation and research are important to learning Unix.) The growth of use in KDE is similar to that with window managers; thus there are many environments, use the one that fits your needs. Remember, that KDE installs as the default shell in many popular Linux distributions, such as Novel’s SuSE Linux Professional.

KDE is an excellent window manager and once mastered, it will allow you to do most anything. For example, KDE will allow you to customize your environment in whatever way you want or need. If you want to change your shell or modify your environment further, you will learn more doing so in Lesson 16, “Modifying Your Environment.”

> Choose Wisely! Sitting on top of most copies of X days these days is the K Desktop Environment (KDE) or the GNU Network Object Model Environment (GNOME). Choose whichever one best fits your needs. The only way to understand which one is right for you is to first define your environment and research each desktop environment to see which one will serve you best. Install and utilize the environment that you want when you are ready.
