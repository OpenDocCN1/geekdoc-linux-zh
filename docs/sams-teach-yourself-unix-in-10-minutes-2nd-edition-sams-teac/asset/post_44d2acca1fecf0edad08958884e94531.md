The first lesson we will learn in this book is how to get started using Unix by connecting to logging in to your Unix system with a set of credentials. We will also cover the concepts behind text-based and graphical-based logins, as well as how to log out.

This was no overnight phenomenon, of course; Unix has expanded each year thanks to tons of ongoing open source developmental efforts, the continuing development of Linux, as well as the Unix base growing exponentially yearly. Not only is growing the presence of Unix, but also has its fan base, which includes users of Unix as well as Unix System Engineers. - - How to log in to Unix both graphically and textually
- - How to log out of Unix both graphically and textually

# Practice with Unix: It Really Does Make You Perfect

In this chapter we are going to learn to start Unix, but before we do, a quick word about practice. It can't be said enough: Practice makes perfect.

When new learners see the speed with which experienced Unix users enter commands, they often panic and feel as if they will always be novices because they cannot recall commands as quickly as they would. Do not think you aren't knowledgeable because you cannot commands quickly; as long as you know what you are doing, speed is not a factor. Start small and take baby steps, but practice to develop recall. To a new learner, Unix commands can look like a foreign language, and in a way, they are. Apply the same concept of how to learn a new language such as Spanish, English, or Chinese to learn Unix commands. Repetition builds up your recall. When you get a copy of Unix, install it on your home PC and practice the commands; the commands will become familiar to you in no time. The more you practice the commands, the better you will remember them and the easier Unix will be.
