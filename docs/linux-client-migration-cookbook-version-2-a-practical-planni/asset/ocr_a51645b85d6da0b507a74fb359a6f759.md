# The case for migration

This chapter presents a detailed discussion about when and why you might want to migrate to Linux on the desktop. A lot of discussion is triggered by understanding why Linux is different than Windows, and how those differences can justify a potentially costly migration process. So addition, we introduce the topic of differences between Linux and Windows. Those differences are discussed at a high level here, and in much more detail later in the book (see Chapter 5、“Linux architecture and technical differences” on page 109.

We do not go into detail total cost of ownership (TCO) or return on investment (ROI) models in this chapter. We also not try to “sell” a migration; although, this chapter presents arguments as to why you might want to migrate to desktop Linux.

Since this book describes a migration to Linux clients from Windows clients, a comparison with the Windows platform is unavoidable. However, we primarily introduce the reasons for migrating on their merits. A comparison with the Windows platform has been presented in various articles, papers, and reports many times, from various sources that are available on the Internet. And most of these sources tend to contradict each other anyway since they naturally reflect the bias of the authors writing.
