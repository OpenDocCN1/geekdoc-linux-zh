Intel supports advances in this area with LSB-compliant compilers and software development tools that contribute to stable, enterprise-caliber development.

LSB 3.0 and the most desktop-oriented LSB 3.1 set the stage for LSB 4.0, which addresses many of the prominent issues that have proven problematic for developers targeting this platform. As discussed in the section Milestones on the Road to Large-scale Adoption, the goal is to allow developers to write an LSB-4.0-compliant application that will be able to run across LSB-compliant distributions, integrating well with the two major desktop environments, GNOME* and KDE*

# Strengthening the Linux Operating System Ecosystem

From an Intel standpoint, a standards-based Linux OS running on an open computing platform contributes to the overall health of the Linux OS ecosystem. Within a healthy Linux OS ecosystem, innovation and collaboration come together to produce fresh business models and solutions stacks — helping to extend the desktop market into areas of the world where demand for cost-effective PCs has been rising. Having clearly standardized interfaces create more interest for ISVs to innovate and develop targeted solutions for the platform. In turn, this creates opportunities for hardware sales in areas of the market where inexpensive systems are required for large and small organizations.

Dirk Hohandel, director of Open Source Strategy in the Intel Software and Solutions Group, "Intel actively engages in Linux Standard Base efforts to help meet the requirements of our customers who are asking for Linux [OS] solutions. We are working to provide a healthy and stable set of solutions for customers in different parts of the world. Obviously, whatever stack our customers are choosing and for whatever reason, we want to make sure that Intel represents the choice for them. Our primary goals are to respond to customer demand and contribute to the progress in developing a strong, international ecosystem."
