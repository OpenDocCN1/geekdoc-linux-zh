In many ways, The GIMP is similar to Adobe Photoshop. Some people feel that GIMP's scripting features are comparable to, or even better than, Actions in Adobe Photoshop. One capability in which The GIMP has been behind Photoshop has been in the area of color management. With the latest features of The GIMP, however, you can calibrate screens and work with color profiles from your cameras and scanners (see http://docs.gimp.org/en/gimp-imaging-color-management.html for more information).

One of the easiest ways to become familiar with The GIMP is to crop, or trim, an image file already on your computer. To crop a file, follow these steps:

+   1. Start GIMP and open an image file.
+   
+   2. Right-click on the image. From the contextual menu that appears, select Tools > Transform Tools > Crop. The crop cursor appears (a cross and knife icon).
