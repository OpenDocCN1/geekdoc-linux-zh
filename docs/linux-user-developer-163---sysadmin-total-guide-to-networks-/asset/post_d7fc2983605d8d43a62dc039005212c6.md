## Canonical launches first 'converged' Ubuntu tablet

Partners with BQ for its first flagship mobile computing device

Canonical has announced the launch of its first 'converged' Ubuntu tablet device, in partnership with Spanish mobile maker BQ the Asahi M10 Ubuntu Edition. Canonical's vision of a future where the distinction between mobile and desktop computing becomes blurred, which, due to a failed attempt at crowd-driven development, never left the drawing board. The company's vision didn't die, however, the device which was nowhere to be seen in the first device to perform the role of both mobile and desktop computer was...

The Asahi M10 Ubuntu Edition, is the first of Canonical's convergence systems to offer off-the-shelf hardware. Used as a tablet, the M10 operates standalone or with a touch-centric interface; connected to an external display, keyboard, and mouse; the tablet seamlessly switches to a traditional desktop user interface. 'We're bringing everything you've come to expect from your Ubuntu PC,' claimed Canonical executive Mark Shuttle at the launch. 'This is the interface stretched to desktop size - it's the right user experience and interaction model for the given situation. Also, in terms of applications, we have no other OS can provide: a single, visual framework and set of tools for applications across all platforms.

Internally, the M10 is based on a MediaTek quad-core MT67363A processor running up to 1.5GHz with 2GB of internal storage, which is more than promised on the Ubuntu Edge - with micro-HDMI port provides connectivity to an external display, while the 7200mAh battery allows the device to be used on the go. The tablet's is a 10.1-inch full HD model with capacitive multi-touch, nested behind Asahi's Dragonfruit impact. Ubuntu Edition is our third mobile device to ship with Ubuntu,' BQ chief officer Rodriguez del Prado explained at the launch. Previous versions were BQ Earis Ubuntu Edition and BQ E5 HD Ubuntu Edition, and we were excited to be the first OEM to ship the converged Ubuntu experience. Its story of innovation that makes BQ and Ubuntu such great fit.
