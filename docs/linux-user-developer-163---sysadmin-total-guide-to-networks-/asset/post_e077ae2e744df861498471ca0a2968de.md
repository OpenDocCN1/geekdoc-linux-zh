# XBOX ZERO ARCADE

Let's make a self-contained machine out of old bits of kit, a spare Xbox pad and a Pi Zero!

The Raspberry Pi is tiny, ridiculously tiny. It's a wonderful, impressive piece of tech, but we can't because we're terribly serious adults, honestly where can we stuff the Pi for maximum fun? It's small enough to be hidden in a variety of household objects. Whatever you can find to fit in it, you can turn into some kind of smart machine. Wiring it to it in the hope of making an ersatz Roomba is probably a little tricky - but there are all sorts of electronics gadgets lying around your home which can be brought back to life with a little bit of effort.

Take old game controllers. If you're anything like us, you've probably got a couple of boxes full of old computer equipment you just can't bear to throw away - an Atari that hasn't been touched since the 90s, a Sega Dreamcast which you're sure you'll plug in again today, an old Xbox that lies languishing since you picked up something better and better, Nintendo just to keep them around - it's time to bring these old systems back to life!

We're going to show you how to gut an old console, replace its innards with a Raspberry Pi Zero, and then load it up with a treasure trove of retro games this project should take you under an hour to complete - then you'll be able to load the ROMs you legally own on your new console and enjoy them from the comfort of your sofa.

# 01 Gather your equipment

While the Zero doesn't take up much space, videogame controllers are often stuffed full of delicate electronics. The trick here is to find a games controller which has enough room for the Zero. We went with using the original Xbox controller, nicknamed the Duke; if you don't have one, they can be picked up for a couple of quid from second-hand electronics shops. Also, you can use newer USB game pads that are designed to look like controllers for classic systems like the SNES and Mega Drive. Make sure you choose a controller that has enough buttons for the games you want to play—fighting games, reality can't be played on a two-button NES controller.

# 02 Warning!

Working with electrical items and sharp objects can be dangerous. You risk damaging yourself or worse, breaking something; please ensure everything is unplugged before starting this project. With any electronics projects, you should take care to fully ground yourself before playing around sensitive components—the static electricity from your body can run them. Anti-static wrist straps or tape on laps can do the trick.
