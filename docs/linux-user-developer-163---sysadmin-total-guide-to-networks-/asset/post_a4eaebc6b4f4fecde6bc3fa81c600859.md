# Ease of setup

You will need a Google account to gain access to Hangouts. You should be able to talk to other contacts by name or email who also have a Google account. For browsers other than Chrome, the 'googletalk' deplugin is required, and you'll also have to go through the boring-routine of installing the plugin and granting access to your hardware.

Making a video call between two KDE desktops was painful and consumed a lot of time because of terrible install issues. Further, most users will see video call buttons greyed out, simply because either the telepathy-kde-call or the Framestep Gstreamer code, QT bindings were missing. Sadly, Telepathy video calls never work out of the box.

# Performance

Hangouts delivers excellent performance and provides plenty of video with a crisp sound. The quality depends on your network, and Hangouts adapts it accordingly. It has some preferences between internet gate, like better use of bandwidth and including video streams separately, audio and video devices by hand.

You can use your accounts to connect to yours, but this affects traffic. Traffic goes through the server and in case it was an issue. Because of this, it takes more time to establish a connection between two PCs in the local subnet than peer-to-peer networks. Nevertheless, video and audio quality was fine.

# Features

Hangouts is the only solution for Linux that supports video chat and enables conferencing up to ten people. Google supports Hangouts on well. Linux users can connect to any Hangouts users with their just friends.

The interface of established calls depends on the protocol used and the software of your recipient. In test case, the only way to get the call working was to install Framestep (a Google app), which enables the Fternet library and depends on Fternet.

# Limitations

If you don't use Chrome then you'll have to install the Google's 'googletalk' plugin, which exists only for Android and Debian plugins. Hangouts uses a backwards way instead of the XMPP protocol, which broke compatibility with third-party clients since time past. Plus, you cannot do anything without a Google account, which isn't possible.

While Telepathy's text works great, the Audio and Video Call bots are not good to use for many users, and it takes a lot of effort to find out the cause of an issue. Of course, there is no group video call — two-way calls are often slow due to the limited resources of the service provided.

# Overall

Hangouts is very advanced in terms of the technology underpinning it, but the tool is specifically tailored for Google Chrome (OS). Setting up out of Chrome is a hassle, and a Google account is required.

The Telepathy technologies are open — which is a key appeal for us — but require too much effort and power to set them correctly. We want to like it, but Telepathy is sadly unwincing in terms of VoIP calls.
