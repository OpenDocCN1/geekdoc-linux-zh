# LyX - LaTeX made easy

The intention of LyX is to make the use of LaTeX so easy and user-friendly that even newcomers can cope with it right from the beginning. For this purpose, LyX provides a WYSIWYG editor editor in which the LaTeX document can be entered. The text is displayed in nearly the same form as it will later appear in printing. (WYSIWYG stands for What you see is what you get. True WYSIWYG in combination with LaTeX is practically impossible during interactive elaboration of texts. However, LyX comes surprisingly close to this ideal concept.)

Maybe you are wondering why this chapter comes after the LaTeX chapter and not before. LyX is certainly much easier to handle than LaTeX, having some ideas of how LaTeX works extremely useful for understanding LyX. Therefore, please read at the introductory section of the LaTeX chapter before you begin working with LyX - it's well worth it!

Some words on the origin of LyX: both LyX (start 1995) and the KLy port (1997) were initiated by Matthias Etrich (who is also the initiator of KDE). A long list of co-developers can be displayed via the HELP menu. LyX and KLy are free software in the sense of the GPL.
