Now to the next four values (expressed in pixels) for the horizontal timing: a single green line with 640 visible pixels is actually composed of 800 virtual pixels. The first 640 pixels are actually displayed. During the remaining 160 pixels, the electron beam is moved back to the beginning of the next line by the HSync impulse. During this time, the electron beam has an intensity of 0.

+   640 display 640 screen pixels
664 blank 24 pixels
760 generate a 96-pixel-long HSync impulse
800 blank another 40 pixels, this amounts to a total of 800 virtual points

The indications for the vertical timing are interpreted in exactly the same way (expressed in screen):

+   480 display 480 lines
491 blank 11 lines
493 generate a 2-line-long VSync impulse
525 blank 32 lines, this amounts to a total of 525 virtual lines

The last values of the quadruples and the pixel frequency allow the horizontal line frequency and the vertical frame frequency to be determined: 25.175 MHz divided by 800 pixels per line results in a line frequency of 31.469 kHz. The line frequency divided by 525 lines per image results in a virtual frequency of 60 Hz. The Mode settings give no information about the number of colors available in this mode – from the monitor's point of view, this does not play a role. (Many graphics cards, can display high resolution modes with a smaller number of colors)

A palette of predefined ModeLine lines can be found in the configuration created by xf86config, and in the files Monitors and README.Config. Depending on the capabilities of your graphics card and your monitor, you can or adapt these values manually in order to obtain the best possible image.

If, for example, you want to shift the image to the right, you must increase the distance after the HSync impulse. (800) unchanged, you must either shorten the time before the HSync impulse or the length of the HSync impulse itself. You may, for example, want to try out 640 660 752 800.

If you want to widen or narrow the image, you change the proportion of the number of visible pixels to the virtual pixels. The following settings widen the image: 640 660 752 790. However, this leads to a small change in horizontal and vertical frequency and not all monitors can handle this.

In many graphics cards, you can even change the visible resolution. The following settings increase the horizontal resolution from 640 to 656 pixels: 656 672 800.

Similarly, you can also vary the settings for the vertical timing. Whether this leads to a stable image, however, strongly depends on your monitor and your graphics card technical data.
