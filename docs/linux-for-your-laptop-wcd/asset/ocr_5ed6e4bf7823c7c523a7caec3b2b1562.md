# Writing CD-ROMs

Until the advent of newer notebook models from Dell Computer, few (if any) laptops provided an internal CDRW drive. The most common way for mobile Linux users to copy, create, or collect audio tracks and data on CD-ROM is to use an external CDRW drive with a PCMCIA interface. Unfortunately, the current marketplace offers a wide and potentially confusing array of choices when it comes to picking an external CDR or CDRW drive. These external drives vary widely in quality, performance, price, and size. You’ll find slimmered-down, PCMCIA port-powered versions from well-known manufacturers (which might or might not work with Linux) to bulky, shoddily built, no-name pieces of junk that only serve to create write-once coasters.

Fortunately, you can check the CD-Writing HOWTO for an up-to-date list of drives that work with Linux. Browse to

```
http://www.gugug.de:8080/cgi-bin/yi-nn1/lsc-orig.pl
```

You can browse through a list of hundreds of drives from nearly 50 manufacturers, and read reports from users on how well a model performs.
