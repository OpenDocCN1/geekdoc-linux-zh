+   - IrDA port might or might not be compatible with Linux
+   - Thermal printers require more expensive or hard-to-find thermal paper in rolls or sheets
+   - Inability to handle as wide a range of paper weights, types, and sizes
+   - Relatively expensive ink cartridges with a limited number of pages per cartridge

The good news is that the positive points about this class of printer definitely outweigh the cons if you don't have a choice about printing on the road. You're sure to find a model that will fit your needs and price range. Nearly all are compatible with Linux, because most use Hewlett-Packard's Printer Control Language (HPCL). The bonus is that the Ghostscript printer drivers included with nearly every Linux distribution turn these diminutive output devices into tiny PostScript printers.
