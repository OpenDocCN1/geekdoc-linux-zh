**Modem and connector cable (RJ-11).** What? Your notebook doesn't come with an internal modem that works with Linux? You must have a notebook with one of those worthless software-driven modems that currently plague the notebook industry. The good news is that eventually the number of Linux notebook users will force the industry to reconsider the use of these evil little items, open up the specs, or revert to using non- CPU-burdening communication devices. In the meantime, choose a notebook that has a real modem. If your notebook has only one Type II slot, get a combination Ethernet/modem PC card. If your notebook has two slots, you should still get a combination Ethernet/modem card, but one that doesn't take up two slots (such as a Linksys EtherFast 10/100 + 56K V.90). If you need only a modem card, don't buy a winmodem PC Card (yes, these pieces of junk have infected PC Card modem lines, too). Buy a PC Card "real" modem, such as a Viking 56K V.90, or a modem with an XJACK connector. XJACK and similar connectors help you avoid proprietary dongles that can break, and you need only a spare phone cord.

**Notebook car power adapter (12VDC to 120VAC).** This is very handy if you're traveling a long distance by car or work out of your car. Many notebook manufacturers offer these adapters, which usually plug into a cigarette lighter or, if you drive an SUV, the "data power port" (which can probably still light cigarettes).

**Notebook computer.** Lighter, faster, more memory, more hard drive space, extended battery life, overnight warranty replacement, and more features using open- spec hardware—this is what mobile Linux users demand! But don't forget yours when you go on the road!

**Notebook printer cable.** As notebooks and printers get smaller and smaller, some things stay the same, such as thick DB-25 Centronics printer cables. However, if you're going on the road, you don't have to lug a 6-foot printer cable! You can buy shorter 24- to 30-inch printer cables at most computer stores. Some printers, such as the Canon BJC-50, include a proprietary lighter-weight cable (albeit 62 inches long). But if you have an infrared printer such as the BJC-50, the only reason to carry a printer cable is to hook up to someone else's printer.

**Pager.** Cheaper and lighter than most cell phones, pagers are a convenient way to be notified of phone calls, news, important events, sports scores, or stock reports. However, a pager can be a modern-day ball and chain, especially if you're shackled to one because of work. Many paging features are now being incorporated into new cell phone technology, so you might want to invest in a higher-end cell phone and ditch your pager.
