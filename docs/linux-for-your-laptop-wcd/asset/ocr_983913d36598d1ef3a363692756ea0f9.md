There might be a market for a book that details the exact instructions for bypassing the BIOS for a variety of notebooks, but you can bet many of the readers would be either independent repair technicians or disreputable characters. Nevertheless, you can probably find the information you need somewhere on the Internet. (Requests for this kind of information on Usenet in newsgroups such as comp.sys.laptops are generally met with much skepticism.)

In the case of the IBM 390 and 1700 series ThinkPads, there is no way to override the hard drive password or BIOS setup password—the hardware must be replaced.

# ## Stolen Notebooks

There is a lucrative market in stolen computer equipment. You can probably find many of these computers and notebooks for sale in your city. If you’re in the market for a used notebook, and you find a deal that seems too good to be true, consider this: How would you feel if your notebook were stolen? Don’t buy a “hot” notebook, and avoid potential trouble. At the least, you could lose your money, and at the worst, you could be arrested.

To avoid buying a stolen notebook, be sure to see the original receipt, and make sure the serial number matches the one on the receipt. You can also check various databases of stolen laptops. Browse to

http://home.snafu.de/wehe/stolen\_laptops.html

You’ll find links to a number of corporate and commercial databases. To protect your notebook before it is stolen, you can also register with a commercial tracking service. These services, which range in cost from a one-time fee to monthly or annual charges, generally work by providing a special tag and including your notebook’s information (such as make, model, and serial number) in a database. The unremovable tag will have an 800 number, along with a serial number. The service acts as an intermediary in getting your system back to you.

The bad news is that most stolen computer equipment is never recovered. But you can protect yourself. Until laptops come equipped with fingerprint recognition, you can take some basic steps:

+   - Never leave your laptop unattended.
+   - If you’re on the road, use the in-room or hotel safe.
+   - Never check your laptop through the airport baggage check.
+   - Some thefts occur at the airport’s x-ray machine. Someone blocks you after you put your notebook on the conveyor belt for scanning, and then someone else takes your notebook after it comes out the other end.
