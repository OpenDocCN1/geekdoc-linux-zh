Listening to music CDs while you work or travel can be a relaxing way to help pass the time. It can also provide extra stimulus to help you increase your productivity. Many different types of music and multimedia players are available for Linux. You'll soon develop your own set of favorite software to use during your Linux sessions.

If you don't use X11, you can still play music CDs. Some of the more popular console (text-based) audio CD player programs are cdp and tcd. The cdp command is also known as cdplay (the name of the symbolic link to the cdp command). This text-based player provides all the controls you need to play music CDs. If you insert an audio CD, type cdp and press Enter, the cdp program will start playing music starting at the CD's first track. When you use the cdp command, you'll be presented with an interactive menu that has controls to start, stop, pause, skip, and select tracks.

The tcd command is the text-based version of the GNOME gtcd audio CD player. Like cdp, this command may be used without X11. At the command line, type tcd and press Enter. You'll see the control dialog box shown in Figure 18.9.

The tcd player, unlike cdp, does not require you to use your keyboard's keypad keys. Instead, it relies on normal keys, such as P, U, E, S, M, G, and so on to control your CD. This makes tcd much easier to use on your notebook.

If you use X11, you'll find an overwhelming array of choices of graphical player software packages. Some of the most popular include kc cd, gtcd, and xplayed. The xplayed client is the oldest program and does not require either KDE or GNOME software libraries. However, gtcd and kscd have several interesting features, such as the ability (if you have an active Internet connection) to grab your CD's title, artist, and names of individual tracks. This works when you configure the player with the correct hostname of a remote music track database server. After a CD is inserted, the client uses the CD's serial number to search the remote database. When a match is found, the information about your CD is sent back and displayed by the program.
