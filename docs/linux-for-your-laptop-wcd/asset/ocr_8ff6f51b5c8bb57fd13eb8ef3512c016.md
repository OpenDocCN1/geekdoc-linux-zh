# Wireless Connections

Wireless networking, although available for several years now, has always been an expensive proposition and networking alternative—until now. In the last year, a number of inexpensive wireless networking cards with cost and capabilities close to typical 10Base-T performance have hit the market. These cards use 2.4GHz radio technology and offer 2MBps networking using IEEE 802.11 specifications known as frequency-hopping spread-spectrum networking. An installed and configured card appears as an Ethernet device (such as eth0) and provides a high-speed wireless link between a similar card installed in another PC or notebook. Range is typically 500 feet or less through walls and perhaps up to 1,000 feet with line-of-sight access.

The great news is that at least one or two models from different manufacturers work with Linux. Even better news is that one of the most inexpensive models, the Aviator 2.4, manufactured by Webgear, Inc., not only works well for nearly all Intel-based mobile Linux users, but also has drivers (developed by Corey Thomas) included with Card Services as a kernel module. Using these cards, you can quickly build a network of up to at least 61 different computers, or nodes.

Networking two laptops wirelessly is quite easy. Insert the wireless card, and then wait for Card Services to recognize, initialize, and configure the device. Use the dmesg command to look for output similar to this:

```
eth0: RayLink, irq 4, hw_addr 00:00:84:88:DB
ray_cs interrupt network "LINUX" started
```
