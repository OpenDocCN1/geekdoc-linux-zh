If you enjoy listening to audio CDs, you'll definitely want a notebook with either an internal or external CD-ROM. You should know, however, that only internal CD-ROMs are directly connected to notebook sound systems. If you use an external CD-ROM (connected with a PC card interface), you'll need a patch cable to patch the sound output on the external CD to your notebook's line-in jack.

Do you want to watch a Digital Versatile Disc or Digital Video Disc (DVD) while using Linux? You can't. At the time this chapter was written, the Motion Picture Industry Association of America saw fit to support the arrest of Jon Johansen, a 16-year-old Norwegian boy, for cracking DVD's encryption and distributing the software on the Internet. However, you might want to consider a notebook with a DVD drive. The drive will work as a regular CD-ROM drive, Linux can read the DVD drive format, and some multiple-CD Linux distributions, such as SuSE Inc.'s six-CD SuSE Linux, are distributed on a single DVD disk.

The good news is that you may soon be able to watch DVD movies while using Linux. At least one company, InterVideo Inc., has announced plans to release a low-cost software DVD player for Linux sometime in late 2000.

Other features, such as IrDA ports, an internal microphone, the number and type of PCMCIA slots, audio or keyboard controls, Advanced Power Management schemes, and spindle design, might or might not be important.

Support for USB was still under development at the time this chapter was written, although Linux supports some keyboards and mice in the 2.3.X series of development kernels. Also, IrDA support for printing is in flux and might not be finalized until the release of stable 2.4 Linux kernels.
