# ## Matching Your Needs

Before you choose a notebook for Linux, you should understand the general category and variety of size and weight, or *form factors*, for mobile computers available on the market. Computer manufacturers are currently in stiff competition as notebook computers gain more and more market share. This is evident in computer store showrooms, industry trade shows, and computer hardware catalogs. Notebooks are increasingly capturing market share as consumers demand more power in a portable platform. Some industry pundits believe that notebooks will eventually replace desktop computers, as the distinction and lines blur between model generations.

The range and power of computers changes every 18 months or so, usually as the result of new manufacturing techniques. These advances include faster central processing units (CPUs), higher-density random access memory (RAM), improved monitor and Liquid Crystal Display (LCD) technology, larger-capacity hard drives, and—most important for mobile Linux users—increased miniaturization.

For example, from 1995 to 2000, standard CPU speeds on typical Intel-based computers increased more than nine-fold, from 75MHz Pentiums to the newer 700MHz Pentium IIIs. Nominal RAM installation went from 16MB to 128MB, and hard drive storage jumped from 1GB to a more-or-less standard 12 to 16GB. At least one notebook model on the market today boasts an internal hard drive capacity of 75GB!
