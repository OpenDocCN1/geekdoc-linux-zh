This is an easy way to enable roaming access (within the limits of the wireless cards) while using a laptop. Other commercial cards might have additional distance capabilities, but by using this approach, you can build a small wireless network for less than $170 (U.S.).

# ## Wireless Modems

If you need to connect to your ISP while on the road, you can also try using Metricom’s Ricochet modem. Although it currently supports only wireless connection speeds of 28.8Kbps, the service is slated for an upgrade to 128Kbps in late 2000. The Ricochet modem is about the size of two decks of playing cards and attaches to your notebook’s serial port. The device weighs about 14 ounces and uses a NiMh rechargeable battery for about six hours of connect time.

Mobile Linux users report great success using the Ricochet device, but service areas are limited to several major U.S. cities, airports, colleges, and corporate campuses. You’ll also need to sign up for service with Metricom, because the Ricochet is designed to connect only with Metricom’s network and servers. The service provides dynamic IP addressing, e-mail, and news feeds. Dial-in access via land-line to Metricom’s servers is provided as an option.

# ## Wireless Cellular Connections

If you need wireless access to the Internet while on the road and you can’t find a phone jack, you can use one of several cellular-based systems and cell phones to dial your ISP. The type of technology, service, and cell phone you use might be dictated by your local phone service provider, service area (geographic) coverage, features, and price. Basically, four types of services are generally available to the casual mobile user, and each has advantages and disadvantages.

# ### CDMA

Code Division Multiple Access (CDMA) is another cellular technology that combines spread-spectrum frequency management with small cell radius to provide data transmission. Slower than TDMA or GSM (discussed in a moment), this digital service has an advantage in that you can use your phone as a modem and use a connector that plugs directly into your notebook’s serial port. Although there’s no actual modem inside the phone, dialing from your computer dials the phone hands-free, and a modem on the connecting network does the connection and transmission for you, usually at 19.2Kbps.
