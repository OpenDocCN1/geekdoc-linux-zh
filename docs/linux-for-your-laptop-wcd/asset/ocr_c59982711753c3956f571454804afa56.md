+   * **Video standby.** Although it's not part of APM, some notebooks offer a feature to turn off the LCD and backlight. Some of these features might be supported by the X Window System. This is a convenient method of saving some power (you'll save more with larger LCD displays).

Before trying to use APM features with Linux, make sure your notebook's BIOS supports APM. Check your laptop's manual, and, then, when booting, press the proper keys on the keyboard to access its BIOS. If APM is supported, you'll see menu items used to control power management through different schemes or toggling of features on or off. You must enable APM in your computer's BIOS in order to use APM with Linux!

# The Linux Kernel and APM

The Linux kernel supports a number of APM features, thanks to the efforts of Stephen Rothwell and support from the NEC Corporation. When you enable APM in the kernel, the Linux system time should be reset after the computer resumes. The /proc/apm file contains APM information such as battery status, time, and percentage of time remaining. And APM “event” notifications are transmitted and (hopefully) used by APM-aware programs.

# Linux and ACPI Support

Advanced Configuration and Power Interface (ACPI) is an open specification developed by some of the biggest players in the notebook market. Although not incorporated into many notebooks yet, patches for ACPI support are available for Linux, and ACPI support seems destined for the Linux 2.4-series kernels. Browse to Andy Henroid's ACPI page at http://www.geocities.com/SiliconValley/Hardware/3165/ for a patch to current 2.2-series kernels. You should also browse the ACPI4 home page at http://phobos.fs.tum.de/acpi for details, links, and the status of the port to Linux.

There are some limitations, however. APM does not control your computer’s hard drive, and it doesn’t turn off Display Power Management Signaling (DPMS)-aware computer monitors or Energy Star devices. Also, some notebooks might not have a compatible BIOS. You’ll find our rather quickly if your notebook doesn’t have a compatible BIOS, because the APM-enabled Linux kernel will crash immediately upon booting. Always keep a backup kernel and boot disk on hand!
