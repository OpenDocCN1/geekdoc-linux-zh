**Blank express delivery envelopes and labels**. Save time and effort by packing a few blank overnight envelopes before you go on your trip. Many airports, hotels, and businesses have overnight-service mailboxes you can use.

**Blank flopp y disks**. If your notebook has a flopp y port, take along a few blank flopp y disks. Even better, take along an emergency Linux boot disk. Use Red Hat's `mkbootdisk` command to create one before you go on the road.

**Blank Zip disks**. If your notebook has a Zip or LS-120 drive, take along a spare in case you want to share information.

**Business cards**. Always have some on hand. These aren't as trendy as beaming your Palm PDA business card to another Palm, but business cards, descended from Carte(s) de Visite (see http://www.storereviews.com/actors.html), serve quite well for introductions and contacts.

**Calling card**. If you have your calling card number memorized, that's great! If you don't have a calling card from your long-distance provider, research costs first. Some phone companies charge exorbitant fees for using a calling card. One alternative is to buy a specific-dollar-amount calling card and keep it on hand in case you need it. (Check for any expiration clause when you buy these cards.)

**Car confirmation number**. Don't forget your rental confirmation!

**Carrying case**. The essential road warrior's kit bag. Many mobile Linux users have more than one notebook case. Unless you have a Panasonic Toughbook (with a sealed LCD, magnesium case, sealed keyboard, internal dampeners, and dust-sealed hinges), you'll need a good notebook case. Cases come in many styles from many manufacturers and are made from a variety of materials, ranging from fine leather to nylon and metal alloys. But if you just need a slipcover case for your notebook with the slight protection afforded by neoprene, browse to http://www.thepouch.com to order a low-cost, custom-fit cover for your Linux laptop.

**Cash**. Until taxi drivers take plastic, you'll need some cash on the road. Although I've actually seen geeks at LinuxWorld pay for coffee with a credit card, cash still reigns in the fast-food line.

**Cell phone**. The smaller, the better. One-rate plans now provide coast-to-coast service. If you're going overseas, check on coverage and availability of service. Better yet, lease a satellite phone (see http://www.gmpcs-us.com/rental/). At the time this chapter was written, rates were only $450 per week for 64KBps at $13 per minute. It's too bad Iridium went down the tubes!
