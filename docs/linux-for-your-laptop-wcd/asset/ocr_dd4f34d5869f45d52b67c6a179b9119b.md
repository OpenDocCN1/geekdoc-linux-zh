# Booting from CD-ROM

T his chapter introduces you to the variety of ways you can install Linux on your
notebook. It’s important to know your options when you’re faced with the task
of installing Linux on a laptop, and practicing different installations can help
you gain experience and build confidence. Mobile Linux users are focused, cre-
atve, and able to adapt to a host of installation techniques, usually as a result of
hardware limitations, such as the lack of an internal CD-ROM or the inability to
boot directly to an installation.

You’ll learn about the myriad techniques you can employ to get Linux installed
on your laptop. Learning and practicing these techniques will enable you to
learn more about your notebook’s hardware and gain experience as a seasoned
Linux installer.

Fortunately, most modern laptops (aside from those in the ultralight category)
come with a bootable CD-ROM. Installing Linux is as easy as inserting the CD-
ROM, rebooting the notebook, and wiping the internal hard drive clean to install
Linux. If you use other operating systems, such as OpenBSD or FreeBSD, you can
opt to have two resident operating systems, creating a dual-boot system.

Booting to an installation from CD-ROM is without a doubt the easiest way to
install Linux. Nearly all Linux distributions (with the exception of certain smaller
collections) provide a main CD-ROM that is bootable. If you attempt to boot
directly to a Linux installation, but you find that your notebook insists on using
your floppy drive or laptop’s internal hard drive, check your notebook’s BIOS set-
tings. You’ll need to restart your notebook and then hold down the proper key or
keys to access the BIOS (check the manual provided by the manufacturer).
