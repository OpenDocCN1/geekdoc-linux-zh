# ## The Ideal Linux Laptop

What makes the ideal Linux mobile computer? My definition is "The notebook that is right for your needs, at the right price, with compatible graphics and sound subsystems, and with the right mix of features." Does this mean that the ideal Linux laptop features an internal CD-ROM? Certainly not! Not everyone needs to access CDs while on the road (especially if you have a large internal drive and play .mp3 audio files). Does your Linux laptop need a floppsy? Maybe, or maybe not. Floppy drives add weight, and if you need a backup medium, you might be able to use a flash memory card.

Certainly one constraint will be the keyboard. Ham-fisted keyboard pounders on the road for any length of time with a diminutive subnotebook will quickly lose their minds or resort to "hunt and peck" two-fingered typing. Another issue might be weight. Petite travelers, upon their first trip through the airport lugging a 10-lb. desktop replacement, will want to switch to a slimtop or ultralight (or build up those biceps).

Display quality and size can also affect the quality of life on the road. Dinky, poorly lit screens can destroy your eyesight, make the display unreadable in sunlight, or cramp your style during an X Window session. (Try running a terminal shell window using 5-point Agate type on a 640x480-pixel True Scan display to see what I mean.)

Some people like using an external mouse with a notebook. Some notebooks come with a touchpad, trackball, or eraser-type pointer (some come with a touchpad *and* eraser). Some of these pointers include one, two, or (even better) three buttons. If you have cold, clammy fingers or the body temperature of a reptile, a touchpad might not work for you. On the other hand, if you perspire a lot, you might want to think about a rugged notebook so that your palms' sweat glands won't short-circuit the keyboard.

Do you need to work away from an electrical outlet? Having a powerful laptop and working with a notebook sporting the power of Big Iron (mainframe computers) can be a wonderful thing. But if the battery drains in an hour and you're nowhere near an outlet (such as on a train), you're stuck with a useless paperweight. Battery time can be critical!

Choosing a notebook is about style as much as anything else. Selected manufacturers now market notebooks in different colors, a la Apple Computer's iBook (discussed in this book, and which runs Linux). Speaking of Apple, if you're a Macintosh fan, you can currently choose between an iBook or a G3 PowerBook—a much simpler choice than when you're faced with the mind-numbing array of Intel-based portables on the market.
