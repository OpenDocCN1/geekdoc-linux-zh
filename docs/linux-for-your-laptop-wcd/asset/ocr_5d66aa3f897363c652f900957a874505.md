**RJ-11 phone splitter.** This is very handy if you need to share a single phone line but want to have your phone and notebook plugged in at the same time. Be aware that some splitters, especially those that plug into the wall, are designed to provide two separate phone lines, not split a single line. (Most homes are wired for two lines by default. Only two wires of the four-wire RJ-11 are used for a single line.)

**Spare batteries for cell phone.** This is a good idea if you're going to be on the road for a while but don't want to take the recharging unit.

**Spare batteries for PDA.** Fortunately, Palm PDA users don't have to worry about replacing batteries very often, unless they go on month-long road trips. Users of WinCE (makes you "wince," doesn't it) had better stock up, because battery life on most supporting hardware platforms is measured in hours.

**Spare battery for pager.** Beep, buzz, or vibrate, pagers require batteries. Pagers with LCD screens, Web browsing, and news and stock quotes require even more juice.

**Spare dongles.** I'm not talking about the evil software-enabling dongle (you deserve what you get if you buy software that requires a hardware dongle), but a spare dongle for your modem or network card. See http://www.thesupplynet.com for replacement dongles for nearly any manufacturer's PC card.

**Spare notebook battery.** If your notebook doesn't support the use of two batteries at once, you can take along a spare. Many laptop batteries (especially NiMh) last only a year with daily use and recharging. LiI on batteries are generally rated for 400 discharge/charge cycles (for at least a 50 percent charge rate), so if you plan to use your notebook for a couple years, a spare battery is a good idea.

**Spare pointer eraser.** ThinkPad and some Toshiba and Hitachi users will definitely need to replace the eraser-type pointer caps at some point in time. Be forewarned that they are not cheap, and usually cost $3 to $6 each. Although these pointing devices work well, getting stuck out on the road with a broken pointer cap can be, well, somewhat embarrassing. ThinkPad TrackPoint tips are red (part #84G6537), although a blue variant is listed on IBM's ThinkPad accessories Web page (part #12J4552). You can order the red tips (four for about $22 with shipping) from http://www.compu-lock.com/default.html.

**Surge suppressor.** You'll find a number of smaller AC surge protectors available for mobile users with laptops. You should always use one to protect your computer equipment, especially while you're on the road.

**Three-pronged adapter.** Always carry a three-prong to two-prong AC adapter when traveling. Many old (two-prong) outlets are still in use, and many notebook AC adapters use three prongs.
