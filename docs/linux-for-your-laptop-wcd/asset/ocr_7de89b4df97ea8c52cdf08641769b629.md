Red Hat (and Mandrake) Linux users use the printtool client, shown in Figure 2.6. This tool contains specific settings for several hundred compatible printers and can configure local, remote, and networked printers. The printer definitions are entered into your system's printer capabilities database, /etc/printcap.

When you print with Linux, nearly all documents are first saved as PostScript. This PostScript stream is then "filtered" through a print filter named ps, which then converts the PostScript file to the proper printer control language. What does this mean? It means that when you use Linux, you can turn your cheap, bargain-bin $99 color inkjet printer into a level 2 PostScript printer! Raw PostScript files, graphics, and other documents are easily printed under Linux. Your distribution comes with literally hundreds of graphics utilities and conversion programs.
