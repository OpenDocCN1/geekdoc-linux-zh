
You can then look for the text by piping the output of the `strings` command through the `less` pager, like this:

```bash
# strings /tmp/hope | less
```

You’ll find other recovery techniques outlined in the *Linux Ext2fs Undeletion* mini-HOWTO (Red Hat and Mandrake users can look under the `/usr/doc/HOWTO/mini` directory for a copy). You’ll find techniques for protecting files from deletion, even by the root operator.

Finally, remember that the most dangerous Linux command might be the shortest, and points out the inherent danger of always running Linux as root. The following simple command:

```bash
# rm -fr / *
```

will wipe out not only your entire Linux filesystem, but also any other mounted filesystems, including Windows or remote NFS partitions.

This chapter presents some ideas, hardware, and software that may help mobile Linux users print documents while on the go. Although many of us relish today's lighter, more-powerful notebooks, unfortunately most printers still remain firmly in the camp of desktop users due to size, weight, and accessories. However, some newer models introduced in the last year or so are attractive in terms of weight and price, and they work well with Linux.

### Portable Printers

Portable printers are the obvious choice when you need to print while on the road. Unfortunately, these types of printers are made by only a small number of manufacturers, so your choices might be limited. Those on the market share many common characteristics. **Here are some of the good features:**

-   Lightweight—less than 2 lbs. for some models
-   Internal battery to provide printing without a wall outlet
-   Ability to run off DC power (a bonus for boat owners)
-   Smaller dimensions (than desktop printers)
-   Small components, such as ink cartridges
-   IrDA port for infrared printing with compatible notebooks and operating systems
-   Thermal print heads, which don't require replacement
-   Simplified operation
-   Somewhat less expensive than many desktop printers
-   Color and black-and-white capabilities
-   In some models, such as the small Canon printers, a replaceable print head that can turn the printer into a scanner (not supported under Linux)

Some of the less appealing features include the following:

-   Limited number of pages per battery charge
-   No sheet feeder, or the sheet feeder is an expensive option
-   Usually more expensive than printers with similar printing characteristics (such as speed, dots per inch printing resolution, and so on)
-   Inability (in most models) to handle envelopes or larger envelopes
-   IrDA port might or might not be compatible with Linux
-   Thermal printers require more expensive or hard-to-find thermal paper in rolls or sheets
-   Inability to handle as wide a range of paper weights, types, and sizes
-   Relatively expensive ink cartridges with a limited number of pages per cartridge

The good news is that the positive points about this class of printer definitely outweigh the cons if you don't have a choice about printing on the road. You're sure to find a model that will fit your needs and price range. Nearly all are compatible with Linux, because most use Hewlett-Packard's Printer Control Language (HPCL). The bonus is that the Ghostscript printer drivers included with nearly every Linux distribution turn these diminutive output devices into tiny PostScript printers.

### **HP 340cbi**

The HP340-series printer is a veteran portable printer that has been around for a while. Built with typical HP ruggedness, this printer is smaller than some of the smallest of the HP inkjet models, such as the HP400. This printer features the following:

-   Removable NiCad battery
-   IrDA port (an attachment to the Centronics port on the printer)
-   Standard parallel-printer cable
-   Dimensions: 12.2 inches × 2.6 inches × 5.8 inches
-   Weight: 4.3 to 5.4 lbs.
-   Speed: 2 pages per minute for quality black-and-white and 4 to 7 minutes per page for color
-   Dots per inch: 600×300 for black-and-white and 300×300 for color
-   Pages per charge: not available
-   Paper weight range: 16- to 24-lb. paper
-   Pages per cartridge: not available
-   Time to charge: not available
-   Lifespan of battery: not available
-   Sheet-feeder capacity: 30 sheets of 16-lb. paper

This printer, although labeled as a portable, would not be a welcome companion in your backpack or shopping bag. However, it is fairly rugged, and it’s more likely than most to survive shaking, banging, and vibration. Happily, it is quite compatible with Linux as a local or networked printer. Unhappily, its IrDA port does not work with the current Linux IrDA implementation or utilities. This is unfortunate, because this printer is a workhorse, includes a 30-page sheet feeder, and otherwise works well attached to a notebook or a LAN.
