+   Don't bang or drop your notebook's battery.
+   Don't expose your battery to heat by leaving your notebook and battery in the sun or inside a parked car during the summer.
+   Don't forget to condition your battery every 60 days by fully draining and recharging it.
+   Don't store your notebook with an inserted battery for long periods of time.
+   Do recharge your battery completely. (LiIon batteries are not as prone to “memory” problems as NiCads.)
+   Do recharge your battery if it has not been used for a long time.
+   Do replace your battery if it does not hold a charge.
+   Do understand that a battery will heat up if it is discharging or in use.
+   Do realize that battery life during usage depends on the number of peripherals in use (such as hard drive, audio, CD-ROM, modem, or networking port).
+   Do use Advanced Power Management when using Linux on your notebook.
+   Do use your battery until it is depleted, and then recharge it. To avoid corrupting your Linux filesystem when draining your battery, use a DOS boot disk, or boot a Linux distribution on a floppy. (Use a single-floppy Linux distribution, such as HAL9; see Appendix A, “Linux Laptop Resources,” for details.)

# # Getting Power Information

APM information is stored in the file `apm` under the `/proc` directory. This file is continually updated and can be used to provide a running commentary on the power state of your notebook. For example, you can get a quick snapshot by using the `cat` command:

```
$ cat /proc/apm
1.9 1.2 0x03 0x01 0x00 0x01 100% 200 min
```

Many different Linux commands and clients use this information to graphically display the state of your notebook's power or battery. See Chapter 7, “Advanced Power Management,” for more details about using APM and the structure of the data returned from the process directory.

The important task to keep in mind when configuring your notebook for the road is to enable APM for Linux by making sure the system starts the `apmd` daemon when you boot. You should then be familiar with the `apmsleep` command or other graphic APM utilities if you want to put your notebook into standby, sleep, or hibernation mode.
