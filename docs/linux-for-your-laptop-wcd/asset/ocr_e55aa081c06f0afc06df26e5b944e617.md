# Your Notebook's Battery

Without a doubt, mobile Linux users on the go are held hostage by one of the most critical notebook elements: the battery! Battery life is crucial to the success of a road warrior, and it determines the amount of productivity and time before a working piece of equipment turns into a paperweight.

Although there are many different types of batteries, such as alkaline, carbon zinc, silver oxide, zinc air, and manganese dioxide, only three types of batteries are commonly in use with portable computing equipment:

+   • NiCd (nickel cadmium). One of the heaviest of the rechargeable types, these batteries have fallen out of favor because of environmental concerns (cadmium) and the development of improved battery technology resistant to the "memory" effect of residual charging. This means that these batteries "remember" low-charge states. Although inexpensive, these batteries do not handle abuse, such as overcharging or undercharging.
+   • NiMH (nickel-metal-hydride). Used in many different notebooks, these batteries can have problems with temperature extremes and overcharging. They are more expensive than NiCd and might not last as long.
+   • LiIon (lithium-ion). These batteries are the latest darling in notebook technology. They are durable, hold a charge, provide higher power, and are not prone to the memory effect. Although more expensive, these batteries are somewhat more "environmentally correct," but like all batteries, they should be recycled.

Battery usage and capabilities vary between notebooks. Some models can use two batteries for extended life, and some manufacturers, such as Sony, offer quad-capacity LiIon batteries for 10 or more hours of full-tirt, open-sony mobile computing!
