This short chapter provides a list of “must-haves,” safety items, and other goodies for the mobile Linux user. Although you might think this list of notebook toys is overly simple, you’d be surprised at how many times people forget some of the basics when hitting the road.

I’m sure you’ll have your own list of favorite toys and essentials when you hit the road, such as a soldering iron, rosin, and heat sink if you’re a Linux hardware freak, or a six-pack of Jolt Cola and a case of caffeinated Penguin peppermints if you’re a Linux code geek.

# The List

**AC adapter and cables.** You’ll need at least one to keep your notebook’s battery charged in between day trips while on the road.

**AC extension cord.** You might or might not need one of these, depending on the length of your notebook’s AC adapter. Most cords for Sonys are 7.5 feet. Cords for IBM ThinkPads are 9.5 feet. Apple iBook cords are 10 feet, 2 inches.

**Acoustic coupler.** Unless you’re going to Timbuktu, Mali, you might want to forego an acoustic coupler—that is, until you find yourself at a pay phone without a phone jack when you need to check or send e-mail.

**Alarm system.** Unfortunately, there are many dirtbags who would love to snarf your brand-new $3,500 laptop. You might want to invest in a motion-sensitive alarm if you park your notebook on a desk or by your side at the airport. However, the best advice is to not leave your notebook unattended!
