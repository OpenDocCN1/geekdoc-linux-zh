# Your Notebook's Display

Although your notebook's CPU is an important internal component, your laptop's display can be a critical factor in your productivity. Early luggable computers used traditional *cathode ray tubes* (CRTs) to provide a display. However, the included wiring and glass added many pounds. Early notebooks, such as the Tandy Model 100, used a simple *Liquid Crystal Diode* (LCD) display to draw characters and graphics on the screen. Major advancements in display technology have been made since then, but like the CPU, the display is one of the most power-consuming components of a notebook.

The next generation of notebooks used LCD displays, with improvements slowly being made to improve density, clarity, and appearance. Because LCDs do not generate light, early improved notebooks used some form of backlighting to improve readability. Further improvements came along with Twisted Nematic or Super Twisted Nematic (SuperTwist) displays, albeit in monochrome. The next leap forward for the notebook industry was providing color LCD display. Early models were quite expensive, but as manufacturing techniques improved, along with increased volume sales and industry demand, in the last eight years, color has become a standard feature of any desirable notebook.
