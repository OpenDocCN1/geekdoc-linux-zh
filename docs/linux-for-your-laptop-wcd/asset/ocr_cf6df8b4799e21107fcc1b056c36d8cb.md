**Gel wrist pillow**. This is a great item to use, especially if you can find one that can double as a neck pillow for when you're commuting. Although many larger notebooks have taken ergonomic design lessons from Apple's PowerBook line, ultralight and mini-notebook users may avoid Carpal Tunnel Syndrome (see http://www.carpal-tunnel-syndrome.net/carpaltunnelsyndrome.htm).

**GPS**. Many different Global Positioning Satellite receivers are supported under Linux. In many cases, you can easily access raw satellite data just by hooking up the GPS to your notebook's serial port and then running xminicom with the correct speed and protocols. For example, the DeLorme Tripmate receiver works at 4800N81. After plugging in the unit, starting xminicom, and then typing `ASTRAL`, you'll receive standard National Marine Electronics Association (NMEA) data at your console. Most Linux software works by translating received strings of data and then properly displaying the data in a window (such as date, time, satellite number, altitude, speed, direction, and latitude and longitude).

**Hotel confirmation number**. Don't forget to write this down or put it in your PDA, especially if you're going to arrive late to LinuxWorld or Comdex!

**International phone and electrical adapters**. If you're traveling abroad, these adapters (usually sold in convenient pouches) can be a lifesaver. There's nothing like being able to overcome technical obstacles without having to search for an electronics store.

**Itinerary**. Itineraries help keep you organized, on track, and on time. Use your favorite Linux software or hardware PDA to input your schedule, and then print two copies. Leave one at home, and put the other in your briefcase.

**Linux installation CD**. Recovery floppy aside, this is the next best thing to having a backup. Of course, if you have a CD writer, you could try burning a bootable image of your Linux filesystem before you go on the road. See Vinfried Trumper's CD-Writing-HOWTO (found under the `/usr/doc/HOWTO` directory or at http://www.linuxdoc.org).

**Meeting schedule**. A possible subset of your itinerary, meeting schedules should also include names, phone numbers, and e-mail or Web information about the attendees. Keep a copy on your notebook, a hard copy back at the office, and an extra copy in your briefcase.

**Microdrive for Type II slot**. This is one of the newest and hottest accessories for the mobile Linux geek. These incredibly small (1-inch) hard drives developed by IBM are manufactured in the same form factor, and in many instances, they can replace Compact Flash cards in digital cameras.
