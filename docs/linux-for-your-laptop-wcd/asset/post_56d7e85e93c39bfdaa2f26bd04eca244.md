
**Tickets.** Plane, train, boat, or movie tickets—don’t forget them! Life is easier now that many ticketing functions are moving to the Internet. Airline E-tickets require only your driver’s license.

**Traveler’s checks.** These are safer than cash, and more convenient than checks, but bulkier than a credit card. It’s nice to keep a small stash in your briefcase when you’re traveling.

**Visas.** Don’t forget any required visas if you’re traveling to another country. Getting into other countries is usually not a problem, but you should be aware that some countries charge you $100 or more (in the form of an “airport” tax) before you can leave!

## Before You Leave

Before you take off, make a checklist of items you’ll need for your trip, and complete any necessary tasks to make life easier when you get back. This includes unsubscribing to mailing lists and perhaps creating a .vacation file or .forward file in your home directory to take care of incoming mail. For example, if you have a local ISP, you can temporarily transfer your mail to a new Web-based account, such as through yahoo.com.

Always keep a backup of any important configuration files, mail settings, IP addresses, or other user files you might need later. If you have a nationally based ISP, research any local access numbers before you go on the road, and possibly configure different accounts or settings, such as with the kppp client, so that you can easily connect while on the go.

Anticipate the worst. What would happen if your notebook disappeared? Do you have a backup? Can you get to that backup online, by telnet, or by ftp? What kind of plan have you made to get back up to speed as quickly as possible? If you’re prepared, losing your entire mobile environment doesn’t have to be a disaster.

### The Linux on Laptop Pages

The good news is that being a mobile Linux user, you can usually find the correct information, starting with a single URL:

- http://www.cs.utexas.edu/users/kharker/linux-laptop/

Kenneth E. Harker’s Linux Laptop pages contains a wealth of links to specific laptop models. It is the definitive Linux on a laptop Web site. Mobile Linux users enjoy a particular advantage when it comes to installation, configuration, and management of Linux on a notebook. You can generally find someone who has already gotten Linux to work well with a particular model. In many instances, that person has posted specific fixes and configuration files. All you need to do is find the Web page that matches your notebook.
