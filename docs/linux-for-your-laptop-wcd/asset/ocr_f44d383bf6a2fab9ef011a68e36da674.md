Passport. If you're traveling overseas, you'll need a passport. Don't let yours lapse or lose it! If you need a new passport or visa to another country, you can try one of the many new travel document services, such as http://www.traveldocs.com, to help expedite the paperwork. U.S. citizens can renew passports through any post office (ask for form DSP-82). You'll need two professional passport photos, your lapsed passport, and possibly other documents.

PDA. If you have one, don't forget it. If you're going to buy one, get a monochrome Palm PDA and ignore any Windows CE device (many manufacturers have dropped their CE device lines).

Phone-line tester. If you're worried about damaging your notebook's modem by inadvertently plugging it into a digital phone line, practice safe computing and get a cheap tester. These typically have an LED indicator to tell you if the line is safe.

Removable floppy drive. Many mini-notebooks do not come with an internal floppy drive. A good alternative might be an LS-120 drive, which can also be made into a bootable Linux 120MB filesystem. You'll probably need to treat the device as an IDE drive and change the boot order in your notebook's BIOS. These drives work with Linux, but the status in the USB versions is unknown at this time.

Rescue CD-ROM. One of the biggest hits at LinuxWorld in March of 1999 in San Jose, California was Linucare's distribution of emergency bootable Linux CD-ROMs in the shape of a business card. Stuff one of these babies in your notebook, reboot, and you'll boot to a 38MB Debian system loaded with software recovery tools (and a slide show of the developers). See http://www.linucare.com.

Ricochet modem. If you want to avoid juggling a modem, phone cord, or cell phone just to get your e-mail, take a look at the Ricochet modem at http:// www.metricom.com. These wireless modems, which use one of your notebook's serial ports and include a rechargeable three-hour battery, currently provide a 28.8Kbps connection with 128KBps in the works for mid-2000. More than 40 college campuses across the U.S. have full coverage. Several hundred cities and airports are also covered. Linux works well with the Ricochet network.

RJ-11 inline coupler. These handy items allow you to extend your reach from the wall's phone jack. Many PC card modems with proprietary dongles provide only 3 feet of length. Extending your reach might allow you to browse the Web or do e-mail while sitting on your hotel room's balcony.

RJ-11 phone cord. You can get these just about anywhere for a few bucks, but a much better product is a retractable phone cord that fits into its own credit-card-sized holder. Products from Targus and Fuji typically store 8 feet or more of phone line in an extremely compact package.
