Save the file and restart your computer. When you log in, the pppd server will assign the IP address 192.168.2.50 to your notebook (make sure your server has a different IP address!). You can then retrieve e-mail, use FTP, or, if enabled, telnet into the server to get to your home directory.

# Free Services

Another approach to staying connected while on the road is to sign up for a free Internet mail account using a Web browser, such as at http://www.yahoo.com. You can then use any open terminal, such as at a public library or cyber-cafe, to send and receive mail through your Web-based e-mail account.

If you have access to a phone and are traveling in or around most major cities in the U.S., you can sign up for free Linux-oriented dial-up PPP Internet service at http://www.teledyn.com/products/PreseMD/

You’ll need to register, but after that you’ll get a local access number. (If you need other numbers, write them down during registration.) This is a full, nationwide ISP service!
