The important thing to remember when choosing your Unix machine is that the more RAM you have, the better your system's performance will be. And since memory and hard disk prices have fallen so much recently, don't skimp. # THE INSTALLATION AT A GLANCE

That's it for the preliminaries. Here's an overview of the installation process you will follow to get up and running with Linux on your PC. It's a good idea to understand what's coming and to know about the possible gotchas before you start anything so drastic as installing a new operating system, so please read through to the end of this chapter before you begin the process. Trust me—you'll be glad you did!

Here are the steps in brief:

+   Step 1: Repartition your hard disk with FDISK or FIPS.
+   Step 2: Create a Linux boot floppy (optional).
+   Step 3: Start the installation program.
+   Step 4: Answer a few simple questions about your hardware.
+   Step 5: Create Linux disk partitions (optional).
+   Step 6: Configure the system clock.
+   Step 7: Choose a root password.
+   Step 8: Configure your video hardware for Linux.
+   Step 9: Copy the Linux files from the CD-ROM to the hard drive.
+   Step 10: Shut down and reboot.
