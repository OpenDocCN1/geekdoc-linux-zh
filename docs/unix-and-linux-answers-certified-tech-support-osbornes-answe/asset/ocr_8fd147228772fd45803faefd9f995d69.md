# Help! What can I do if I’m sure I have done something horrible to my file?

Use "quit-bang" to quit without saving. That’s :q! and it always gets you out.

# What’s the "smart" exit command?

Use :x if you want to save the current file if it has changed, or leave it unchanged if you haven’t modified it, and either way, exit back to your shell. This should probably be your "normal" exit command, unless you want the file’s date and time to be updated when you exit, even if you haven’t made any changes.

# How can I find out how many words or lines are in my file?

You can send your file through the UNIX wc word count utility to find out how many words or lines are in your file. It will even give you a total for all the files in the list you give it. To get a total word, line, and byte count for the seven dot-cat files in our /save directory, we get

```
 $ wc -lwc *.cat
      14      84    573 alfie.cat
     140     790   6346 guz.cat
     280    1580  12692 harold.cat
      56     311   2600 meep.cat
      70     395   3173 possm.cat
     266    1496  12119 pris.cat
     560    3160  25384 stanley.cat
    1386    7816  62887 total
```

Which shows us that we have a total of 1,386 lines, 7,816 words, and some 62,887 bytes of text in the seven files. And almost enough cats in our life.
