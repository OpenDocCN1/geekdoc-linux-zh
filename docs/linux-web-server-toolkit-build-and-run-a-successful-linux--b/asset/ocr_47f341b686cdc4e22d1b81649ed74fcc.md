# Chapter 1: Planning Your Web Site

The statistics are amazing, but not fun as the little things that have changed in the past four years. I was still explaining what a URL was 24 months ago. Now they're everywhere: on the side of every UPS delivery truck, on every movie poster, on every new business card, and on every annual report, no matter what the industry.

In fact, we've already moved beyond standard URL format that includes the protocol, server, and so on. Now you just see mci.com, and they expect you to know that it's for Web Access. And so on, you type it into your browser without the extra stuff (http://www/), and in most cases it works.

One thing that you should keep in mind is the Web is not really ubiquitous. Only about 20 million Americans have Web access, out of a nation of 250 million. But those 20 million represent the demographic everyone in business wants to reach. We've reached the point where you can assume that anyone in business, with a portion of the general public, knows exactly what you mean if you have your annual report on the corporate Web Site. With that infrastructure and understanding in place, the Web can really start to become useful for everyone.

# # Why You Need a Web Site

Apart from the fun of browsing museums and new movies, there are some solid business reasons for having a Web Site. To provide some facts in case your boss asks what you're doing with all your free time, here are a few of the serious reasons why you need to have a Web Site for your organization, it a business, nonprofit organization, user group, or about anything else:

+   - Your customers are asking you when you'll have a Web Site.
- All of your competitors have a Web Site.
- You can provide high-quality, cost-effective customer service.

# # Keeping Up with Customer Expectations
