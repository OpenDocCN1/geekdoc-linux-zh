# FRAME RELAY

Frame-relay technology allows you to connect into a shared digital “cloud,” to the multiplexed voice telephone conference shared earlier. Frame relay hardware consists of a single board in your Linux PC. A CSU/DSU (connection service unit/data service unit) is also required. It can be purchased separately or as part of the newer frame relay boards on the market.

You can generally choose a speed for your line by 56K and T1 speeds (see “T1 and T3” for T1), but because the connection is shared by many multiplexed users, you never have full bandwidth - in fact, you never really do. You can sign up for a certain burst rate and a guaranteed sustained rate. The telephone company assigns groups of people to different sets of shared lines to balance loads. But if someone in the group is making a huge download (which is called bursting), you can't burst at the same time because there's not much bandwidth left. The downside of what remains a very good system. Frame relay connections offering the benefits:

+   - Connections are highly reliable, much more so than modems because frame relay is digital and the connection is continually monitored by the telephone carrier.

+   - Relay permits higher speeds than most modems, and its packetoriented data stream (compared to character-oriented modems) makes for speed, more efficient handling by your web server.

+   - Frame relay costs much less than your telephone connection (less than $100 per month fixed cost for 56K I live).

+   - Connection equipment is inexpensive (way under $1000 for 56K speeds).

One drawback of frame relay – you just have to hope it's available where you live. Frame relay connections are gaining in popularity in some parts of the U.S. because they offer a digital shared connection at a very reasonable cost. Unfortunately, they are not available in all areas, and may go up in cost according to how hard telephone companies fight for increased rates to Internet traffic (as they see it; I suggest calling your congressional representative to fight rate increases for digital services).

Linux supports several frame relay cards from various vendors, as described in the configuration sections later in this chapter.
