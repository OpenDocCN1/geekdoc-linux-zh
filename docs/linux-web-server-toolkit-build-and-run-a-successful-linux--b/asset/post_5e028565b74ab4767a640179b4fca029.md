## WHEN IS YOUR SITE EXPERIENCING THE MOST HITS?

Knowing the heaviest traffic time for your site may be more important than knowing the slow time. It's the time you better be certain that your site is up and running at full speed. It's the time you want all the latest information in place, and it's the time when an Internet connection outage can least be tolerated.

## IS THE USE OF THE SITE GROWING OVER TIME?

By reviewing statistics over consecutive days, weeks, and months, you can easily track whether your site is becoming more popular. Save statistical information or graphs so you can compare different periods.

Remember that if you change your site substantially (such as redesigning it with a new look that uses many more or fewer graphics), the hits shown in your statistics are not as relevant across several time periods. However, you can still track trends.

If you are comparing daily totals, compare the same day of the week. Because traffic often varies during every week, just as business does from quarter to quarter, your daily comparisons should be, for example, on several consecutive Wednesdays. Comparing weekly totals may be an easier way for you to see growth trends.

## WHERE ARE MY VISITORS LOCATED?

Most statistics programs create a list of domains from which requests originate. These lists usually include all of the country domains organized by frequency. You might expect to see a list that includes the following domains (from the wwwstat program):

- .com
- .org
- .gov
- .edu
- .de
- .fr
- .it

One caveat to be aware of: Many international users have .com addresses. They connect through an Internet provider who has U.S. connections, to provide faster access or to avoid discouraging U.S. contacts who think they're too far away. You will miss some international contacts in this statistical analysis. You'll know them only when you get an e-mail in German with a .com return address.

## HOW MANY PEOPLE VISIT MY SITE EACH DAY?

This is a tricky question, but a very important one. With printed advertising, you can't expect a sale every time person sees your ad, but you want to know how many impressions you're making on a potential customer. All the rules don't hold true, but knowing how many users come to your site tells you a lot about how well you are publicizing it and how it is received and revisited.

The quick-and-dirty (and inaccurate) method of determining a user count is to simply use the number of hits. That is, "we have 5,400 hits, so we have 5,400 people visit our site." This method doesn't work, for all the reasons outlined earlier in this chapter.

A simple way to determine the number of unique users who visit your site is to run statistics for a single day and count the number of unique hostnames listed in that report. This doesn't account for people using the same hostname as a Web gateway, but it makes a fairly accurate guess.

If you generate daily statistics file, you can load it into a text editor, chop everything but the hostname list, then use the following command to see how many lines are in the remaining file (the first number that we reports is the number of lines).

```
$ wc hostname_list
452 1786 47.866
```

This method doesn't work well beyond a single day because you want to know if people are revisiting your site on multiple occasions. Counting hostnames within an entire month-long period doesn't account for that. (See the question below repeat visits.)

## WHAT TYPE OF BROWSER IS MOST USED BY VISITORS TO MY SITE?

Not all statistics programs report the percentage of browser use, and not all browsers report themselves either. If a statistic for browser type is available via your statistics package, it will give a percentage for each of the browsers reporting their names and versions, so you'll know how many people are using Netscape Navigator on your site.

In general, you'll want to know how many people are using Netscape Navigator, Internet Explorer, and other browsers. Knowing this helps you ensure your site is compatible with the most popular tools.
