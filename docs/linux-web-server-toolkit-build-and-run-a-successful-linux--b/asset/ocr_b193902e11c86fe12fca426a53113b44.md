# Chapter 1: Planning Your Web Site

# Providing Up-to-Date Information

Part of the appeal of variety on a Web site is tied to regularly displaying new material. Your goal when you create a Web site is usually to get users to return to your site. You can best accomplish this by having new information on it. Almost nothing will drive away visitors like seeing a nine-month-old “last updated” line at the bottom of your home page.

# Unique Information or Services

Unique can mean unique in the world or among all your competitors. That’s nice, but hard to accomplish. What you may be able to do is to offer some service or information that is not available from your organization in any other way. This highlights the value of your Web site and really encourages people to return often. The Web is now widespread enough that you usually don’t need to worry about providing certain types of information or service only on the Web. But don’t go overboard and provide product information on the Web only, while taking no phone calls, printing no brochures, attending no trade shows, and so on.

For example, you might have a locator service for your resellers or dealers, or a product review bulletin that shows the latest headlines about your products. If these things are only on your Web site, people will soon learn about them and return to check them off. The key to selecting a service to go onto your site alone is that the Web site needs to be the best place for it. Take the Federal Express example again; there was no other way to let its customers interact with the mainframe package-tracking system to see when something was delivered and signed for.

# Sensitivity to Bandwidth

It goes without saying that in order to access, view, and interact with your Web site, visitors must connect to the Internet. Your Web site visitors may have connections that are slower than those you’re reaping (assuming you’re reaping the benefits of a T1 line or better). If they have fast connections, they may have better things to do than sit and wait for the time it takes to download that full-screen picture of your’s CEO.

Good Web sites are sensitive to bandwidth issues. That means they don’t waste bandwidth on things that provide no value to the person viewing the page. These sites give a choice wherever possible and warn of large files that will require some time to download.

The Web protocol (HTTP) is not designed with large files in mind, and users don’t expect any single page to take more than 10 or 20 seconds to display on a fast connection. In addition, as the Internet becomes more crowded, we will need to pay attention to how we use available bandwidth. It can seem as though the amount of information we can exchange is unlimited if we’re not paying for it but, someone is, and things are getting crowded out.
