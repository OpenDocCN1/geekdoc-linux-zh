
# A Level Playing Field

The Internet can be infuriating if you take everything that can be found on it entirely. If you've ever browsed through newsgroup postings (like sci.history, for example), you know that everyone has an opinion. The challenge for anyone reading is: the comments of the most informed expert are indistinguishable from the comments of a 12-year-old with a large vocabulary. This leads to some angry discussions by a lot of uninformed people making their opinions public.

On the upside, the same thing applies to Web sites. I will restate what I said previously, this time in your favor: a Fortune 500 company's Web site can be as slick and well-organized as a Web site owned by a two-person start-up company that has little money and artistic skill. The value that large companies add to the Internet is the information they provide and the Web-based transactions they make possible. The Web makes these things possible for businesses of all sizes.

Name recognition counts for a lot, of course, but you can have a fabulous Web site with less expense than a fabulous office or wardrobe. If you want to compete with larger companies in your field, choose the Web as the place to fight the.

# Reaching Customers

After all that discussion, you still have the same need: to bring your existing customers to your site. You can bring people to your site using standard advertising methods or using the Internet.

# Advertising Your Site Offline

Standard advertising methods have become very acceptable for promoting a Web site. Almost everyone knows what you're talking about, even if they don't regularly access the Web. Common methods include printing or mentioning your URL in the following ways:

- On your business cards
- On your product brochures
- On your annual report
- On your letterhead
- In your radio advertising
- In television advertising
- On billboards
- On posters
- In magazine advertising

# Advertising Your Site on the Internet

Using the Internet to reach customers has both advantages and disadvantages over traditional advertising techniques. The Internet is much more focused, because it puts your URL in front of someone who already uses the Internet. It is also easier to use — an Internet advertisement is a link to your home page, and the reader is just a mouse-click away from your site.

One disadvantage of advertising your site only on the Internet is that it reaches only a small percentage of potential customers compared to providing your URL in traditional advertising if you feel encourage people to use the Web. The Internet also has a very immature advertising system. It's growing rapidly, but it presents frequent opportunities to advertise, less research on the customer base it reaches, and more uncertain costs. The most popular sites, such as Yahoo, ZNet, and Microsoft Network, are already expensive and have some data to show value to potential advertisers. But many others are still feeling out the market, learning how they can help advertisers and consumers, and deciding what to charge for their role.

Reaching a customer via the Internet can be accomplished using either the Web or e-mail. The Web presents more and more opportunities to advertise. Search engines and other very popular sites provide the most opportunity to advertise. Advertisement posts are often referred to as *banners*. Figure 8-1 shows an example of a banner advertisement.

Search sites that sell self advertisement space offer the option to display your banner when a user searches for specific keys that relate to your product or service.

Web sites typically charge for banner advertisements in one of two ways. Either for the number of *impressions*, which means the number of times your banner is displayed in users' browsers, or for *click-through*, which means the number of times people access your site by clicking on your banner advertisement — in this case, the user's browser sends to your site the address of the search engine as the *referrer* of the link.

You may find other methods of charging: Web advertising is still under development, even by publishing houses. At the time I wrote this, costs per impression were running less than traditional print advertising, mostly because there isn't yet a way to track precisely how effective Web advertising is (not that print advertising has a perfect record), and because the supply and demand have been wildly in flux as people rush to the Internet both to accept advertising and to advertise.

# Bringing People to Your Site

E-mail provides the most targeted method available to reach customers. It places a message in front of a targeted individual inviting them to visit your site. I can't say enough to be careful using e-mail to contact users and invite them to your Web site. If you contact only existing customers who know your business, you are using a push approach which it may be wanted—consider how you feel a telemarketing firm calls you you sit down for dinner. You may alienate or worse, even annoy customers. The best way to use e-mail is to send it only to people who have specifically asked to be part of a mailing list to be notified of new offers, products, and so forth.

The haphazard method of advertising your site via e-mail is to find anyone's email address you can, and e-mail them a special offer and tell them how your Web site is. This is called spam. Many people agree that this is about the most annoying aspect of the Internet. Flame experts without pause, pictures if you must—this is accepted Internet behavior—but by all means avoiding unsolicited e-mail. You will do more harm to your business and your reputation on the Internet than you will boost sales and Web visits.

Many organizations will tempt you with offers of e-mail lists and special methods of contacting preferred customers. Treat these like the chain letters of yesterday and simply delete them. Ethical and professional businesses on the Web do not spam but instead use the Internet effectively without destroying its usefulness by invading their private mailboxes. Trust the lead of these businesses; trust your judgment and don't spam.

# Using Newsgroups to Promote Your Site

One other method of using the Internet to promote your business or Web site is to use newsgroups. These categorized "newsrooms" with postings from around the world provide a place where you can announce a new Web site, new products, special offerings, and so forth.

Posting on the newsgroups can be intimidating (but you've already gotten a Web server up and running). It's easy to spam newsgroups in just the way people spam using e-mail. There's a danger of spam newsgroups without really meaning to.
