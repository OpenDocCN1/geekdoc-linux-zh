# Defining Your Presence on the Web

Before you get too far into the process of installing Web server software and connecting to the Internet, it's a good idea to think through the critical decisions that will make your Web site a successful one. There are many nontechnical issues that you will need to resolve before it makes sense to start creating pages for your site. The following sections highlight a few of the top priorities for your decision-makers.

# What Is the Purpose of This Web Site?

Like a corporate mission statement, defining the purpose of your Web site will direct and prioritize everything you subsequently do with the site. It will be a big help in making all the other decisions. Look to your underlying purpose and judge things based on the goals you have already defined.

For example, your primary purpose in having a Web site might be to increase sales of your products. In this case, you can think about the information you provide and the organization of that to suit that end. If your primary purpose is to inform people about your company and products, the information you provide and the way you present it would differ.

Others might have entertainment as their primary purpose, without trying to promote a company or sell products. Others might wish to share information about a favorite hobby or common interest - something like the Internet newsgroups.

# What Feel Should This Web Site Have?

To get consistent, strong design, even from a professional Web designer, you should provide direction for the "feel" of your Web site you intend to create. One of the greatest things about the Web for small businesses (and there are a lot more small businesses online than large businesses) is that everyone can look as big as they want on the Web. All you have to do is decide how to use a look that says what you want to say about your company.

For example, even a small company can decide to have a conservative, corporate feel on its Web site. To do this, might forego the fluorescent colors that so many employ, use more graphic elements and fonts, and write the HTML pages to reflect a conservative approach. Another organization might want to highlight what a fun group of people works, so your site could be very colorful, with graphics and copy that reflect a less stuffy approach. Both goals are perfectly valid. Each looking to achieve a different end.

It's helpful to have one or two words in mind (and passed on to designers) when you are working on your Web site design, graphics, and copy.
