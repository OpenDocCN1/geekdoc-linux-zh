# Chapter 8: Bringing People to Your Site

E-mail provides the most targeted method available to reach customers. It places a message in front of a targeted individual inviting them to visit your site. I can't say enough to be careful using e-mail to contact users and invite them to your Web site. If you contact only existing customers who know your business, you are using a push approach which it may be wanted—consider how you feel a telemarketing firm calls you you sit down for dinner. You may alienate or worse, even annoy customers. The best way to use e-mail is to send it only to people who have specifically asked to be part of a mailing list to be notified of new offers, products, and so forth.

The haphazard method of advertising your site via e-mail is to find anyone's email address you can, and e-mail them a special offer and tell them how your Web site is. This is called spam. Many people agree that this is about the most annoying aspect of the Internet. Flame experts without pause, pictures if you must—this is accepted Internet behavior—but by all means avoiding unsolicited e-mail. You will do more harm to your business and your reputation on the Internet than you will boost sales and Web visits.

Many organizations will tempt you with offers of e-mail lists and special methods of contacting preferred customers. Treat these like the chain letters of yesterday and simply delete them. Ethical and professional businesses on the Web do not spam but instead use the Internet effectively without destroying its usefulness by invading their private mailboxes. Trust the lead of these businesses; trust your judgment and don't spam.

# Using Newsgroups to Promote Your Site

One other method of using the Internet to promote your business or Web site is to use newsgroups. These categorized "newsrooms" with postings from around the world provide a place where you can announce a new Web site, new products, special offerings, and so forth.

Posting on the newsgroups can be intimidating (but you've already gotten a Web server up and running). It's easy to spam newsgroups in just the way people spam using e-mail. There's a danger of spam newsgroups without really meaning to.
