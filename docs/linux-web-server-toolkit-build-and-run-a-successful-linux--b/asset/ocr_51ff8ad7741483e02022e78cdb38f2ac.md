Initially, collocating may be more expensive because you pay for hardware, collocating fees, and an account to administer the server.
Over time, costs are likely to be less than you have a slow connection for administration.
However, collocating fees vary by ISP.

Start-up costs may be about the same; you save collocating fees, but pay for a higher-speed connection to your site.
Though locating at your office may seem to add cost because of this, connection fees are much more competitive at this time than collocating fees.

Having said all of these things about hosting and collocating, I will proceed as if I hadn't mentioned either and if you've decided to set up an onsite web server.

> Some of the Internet connection information in the sections to follow will not apply if you choose to colocate your server at your ISP.

# Connection Technologies

There are many technologies you can use to connect to your Internet Service Provider. Some of the technologies described in the sections that follow won't be available in your area; others will be recommended or discouraged by your ISP. If you've come to trust your ISP and feel your bandwidth and stability requirements are met, you may want to go with your ISP's recommendations. Following your ISP's recommendations will generally make it easy for the ISP's staff to support you, though they are not always experienced in the available options as they should be. The result can be added cost for you.

Becoming an Internet Service Provider is not very difficult. Providing top-quality, informed service is another matter. Research all you can about connection options and prices among ISPs in your area, so you'll be able to recognize the experience level at the ISP you choose. The more the staff know, the more money you'll save in the long run, and the more stable your connection will be.
