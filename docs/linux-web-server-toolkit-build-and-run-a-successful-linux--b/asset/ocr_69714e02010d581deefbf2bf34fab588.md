# Understanding Business on the Web

The Internet is changing the way people conduct business. I prefer not to think of it as the incredible revolution that the press declares it to be. Remember what I said in Chapter 1—less than ten percent of the American public are active users of the Internet. But regardless of that statistic, the Internet is affecting our country and the world. One of the best ways to judge trends is to see what the wealthy are doing, and like it or not, Web users outside business are generally in very high income and education brackets.

The changes ushered in by the Web are not shocking and sudden, as many investment bankers would have you believe when they discuss the latest Internet technology stocks. Television, failed by visionaries as a mighty change to our way of life, to develop networks, programming, signal distribution systems, and sales channels for receivers, advertising, and so forth before it was more than a novelty by many people, and before its effects approached the magnitude of what had been predicted. Furthermore, the effects of television were not exactly what futurists had foreseen. Their pictures of the future were either much more benign or more utopian (depending on whom you listened to) than reality turned out to be.
