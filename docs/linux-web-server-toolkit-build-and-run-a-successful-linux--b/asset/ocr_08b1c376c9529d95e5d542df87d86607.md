# Using Registration Forms

You can get a lot of good information from Web server logs but you can get a lot more by asking people to enter their names or other information when they your site. The two common ways of doing this are:

+   - Registration to receive notification or updates regarding your products or services: to do this, you would collect a name, perhaps a company and title, and some method of contacting the user who is registering, usually an e-mail address or phone number (will prefer e-mail).

+   - You can indicate on your registration form exactly what a user is registering for, and include guarantees that the e-mail names will not be sold or will be used except for the single stated purpose. Or, you can simply indicate something like "To be on your mailing list, fill this out." People who your organization will fill out, trust you to send them the occasional, brief e-mail about upcoming specials, new products or events, and so forth. Always state clearly how a user can be removed from your mailing list.

+   - A survey to collect facts or opinions about the visitor, the visitor's employer, preferences, buying habits, and so on: to do this, you could collect a name and contact information, that might also be optional, depending on your goals and the types of questions you are asking. Visitors to your site deserve to know a little about what you intend to do with the information. It's common to post the results of user surveys on your Web site after the survey is over, but you don't have to do this.
