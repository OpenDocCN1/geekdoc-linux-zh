# Advertising Your Site on the Internet

Using the Internet to reach customers has both advantages and disadvantages over traditional advertising techniques. The Internet is much more focused, because it puts your URL in front of someone who already uses the Internet. It is also easier to use — an Internet advertisement is a link to your home page, and the reader is just a mouse-click away from your site.

One disadvantage of advertising your site only on the Internet is that it reaches only a small percentage of potential customers compared to providing your URL in traditional advertising if you feel encourage people to use the Web. The Internet also has a very immature advertising system. It's growing rapidly, but it presents frequent opportunities to advertise, less research on the customer base it reaches, and more uncertain costs. The most popular sites, such as Yahoo, ZNet, and Microsoft Network, are already expensive and have some data to show value to potential advertisers. But many others are still feeling out the market, learning how they can help advertisers and consumers, and deciding what to charge for their role.

Reaching a customer via the Internet can be accomplished using either the Web or e-mail. The Web presents more and more opportunities to advertise. Search engines and other very popular sites provide the most opportunity to advertise. Advertisement posts are often referred to as *banners*. Figure 8-1 shows an example of a banner advertisement.

Search sites that sell self advertisement space offer the option to display your banner when a user searches for specific keys that relate to your product or service.

Web sites typically charge for banner advertisements in one of two ways. Either for the number of *impressions*, which means the number of times your banner is displayed in users' browsers, or for *click-through*, which means the number of times people access your site by clicking on your banner advertisement — in this case, the user's browser sends to your site the address of the search engine as the *referrer* of the link.

You may find other methods of charging: Web advertising is still under development, even by publishing houses. At the time I wrote this, costs per impression were running less than traditional print advertising, mostly because there isn't yet a way to track precisely how effective Web advertising is (not that print advertising has a perfect record), and because the supply and demand have been wildly in flux as people rush to the Internet both to accept advertising and to advertise.
