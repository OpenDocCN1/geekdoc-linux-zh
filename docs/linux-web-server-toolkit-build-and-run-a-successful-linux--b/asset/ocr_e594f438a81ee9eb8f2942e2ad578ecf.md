# Find a Friend

A kind of ISP that you won't find advertised may be your best choice of all. Many companies have Internet connections, some of fairly high speeds. Most don't use their entire bandwidth. Instead, they want a digital connection to have stability and full-time access.

If you have a friend in a high place at a company that has a connection, you can approach him or her about selling you part the company's bandwidth. You still make a connection to that company's site, with the associated telephone company charges and hardware. But the company is probably not trying to make a profit as an ISP, so your costs could be a lot less.

The cavesats are:

+   * If you use too much bandwidth, you won't be very long and you'll have to start looking for an ISP again (and).
* You can expect some assistance from the company in setting up your connection if you're paying for it, but don't plan on regular assistance. You should develop a professional relationship with the staff but have expertise in-house as hard.
* Don't try to get a free run unless it's your uncle's company. When others find out the company subsidizing your access, your friend could be on the street.

If you really want to save some money, think about collocating at the company's site, managing your Web server through the company's Internet connection. Just set up your server in a locked, well-ventilated closet. Your only charges are a fee to the company and a dial-in Internet account ($20 per month) so you can administer the server. You can even set up a modem pool on your Linux Web server so you directly to your Web server, and others in the company can reach the office and the Internet from home using an account on your Linux server. By providing this service to the company, maybe you can even reduce your fees to zero.
