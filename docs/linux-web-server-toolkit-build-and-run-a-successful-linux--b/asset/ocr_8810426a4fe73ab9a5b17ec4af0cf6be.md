# USING ISDN ON LINUX

ISDN connections use a special adapter card or, preferably, an external ISDN modem device that communicates over ISDN telephone lines. Because the ISDN standard has been active much longer in Europe, most of the available Linux support comes from Germany.

Several ISDN cards are fully supported in Linux, including the Teles cards and the Combine EverWISE 1000 series. Support for the Teles card is already standard in the Linux kernel, in fact.

Some special cards that rely on ISDN are also supported, as the ISTEC board for a PBX system. Several cards are also available that appear to the the Linux system as regular Ethernet cards or serial mediums.

If ISDN sounded like a good option as you reviewed the tables in this chapter, begin by contacting your chosen ISP and your telephone company to see if they offer ISDN service. Once you've determined that they do, a good place to start learning about your options for Linux hardware is by reading the information at http://www.ix.de/ix/linux/linux-isdn.html.

Unfortunately for many of us, the information referred to on that ISDN "home page" is written in German. If you verstehe nicht, like me, you can also contact one of the growing number of U.S. ISDN card makers and inquire about Linux support. Search for ISDN at http://www.yahoo.com/

# FRAME RELAY AND LEASED LINES

As ISDN connections, the offerings of your ISP and telephone company will determine what options are available to you for very high-speed lines. Frame relay and leased lines are two technologies that can be used at many different speeds, from 56K to T3.
