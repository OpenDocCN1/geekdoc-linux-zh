# Chapter 8: Bringing People to Your Site

# Geographic Boundaries

Government boundaries always remain, but individuals are not nearly eager to separate themselves into groups that exclude others. The size of the world and the rules governing interchange among nations have always played a part in commerce. Customs duties, tariffs, export restrictions, and other similar rules interfere with what the business person would call free trade (this is a very Republican view, but it fits my discussion).

The Internet removes geographic boundaries for both individuals and businesses. Using the Web, a business in Bangkok seems as close to you as the you're in. Particularly in businesses that sell services or information, a company's market can immediately become the entire online world.

Of course, regulations still exist. Netscape can't export its browser freely, and you can't ship products anywhere just because you had a conversation via e-mail. The Internet remove geographic boundaries for both personal relationships and business relationships.

# Pull Versus Push

Advertisers are accustomed to finding the location of a target market and making themselves visible in that location, usually through advertisements. It might be a football field, a classic movie on TV, or a technical magazine. In common parlance, this is called a push: you get in the customer's face.

The Internet doesn't really allow this, because it's a medium where users go only where they want to go. There aren't many opportunities to drop your slogan in front of someone. Instead, the Internet uses what's called a pull: Each user chooses what he or she wants and pulls it to his screen. You can't push anything to the Web page visitor, because he or she is selecting specific information.

> TIP If you've been to business school, you'll recognize that these terms are the reverse of your marketing textbook. A standard push marketing strategy gives you an incentive to push a product to a customer. The salesperson helps the customer decide that he or she wants the product. A standard pull strategy means you advertise to the end user so that he goes into the store and pulls the product off the shelf (pulls through the distribution channel), without any pushing from the salesperson; the customer already knows he or she wants it.

Don't be discouraged by the pull nature of the Internet. It actually provides a great benefit: any users who do see your offerings are likely to be very interested potential customers. And there are ways to push your message to potential customers. Some are great (see the section on search engines in this chapter), and some are deplorable.
