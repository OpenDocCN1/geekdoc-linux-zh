# Providing Cost-Effective, Quality Customer Service

The reason the Web became so popular is not because you can play your favorite game, such as Doom, with someone in Australia. The Web provides a method for providing services that cannot be provided in another cost-effective way.

Companies such as Federal Express didn't just jump on the Web bandwagon because everyone else did. The package-tracking service FedEx provides via its Web site is a much-needed customer benefit that would be more expensive if it were provided by other means.

Other companies focus their entire business around their Web site. Learn2.com, shown in Figure 1-1, is an online training company that provides all its materials via the Web.

In summary, the Web lets you:

+   * Provide information that is updated instantly from your company to the customer's hands.

+   * Do so with almost (but not quite!) zero incremental cost—that is, 10,100 customers cost no service to serve 10,000 customers.

+   * Do so while reducing the need for other traditional costs of printing, mailing, and, so on, making it an environmental win.

In reference to the second reason for having a Web site, your customers are right: if you're not on the Web, you're not using your resources to provide services to them effectively.

# Other Benefits of a Web Site

Of course, there are other reasons for having a Web site. And in outlining these reasons, I would remiss if I overlooked the fact that the nature of the Internet encourages people to communicate. From the first days of the Web, people have been putting useful information resources on the Web to share what they have or what they know.
