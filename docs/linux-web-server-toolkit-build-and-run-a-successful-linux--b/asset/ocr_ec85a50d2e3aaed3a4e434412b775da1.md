# FULL-SERVICE ISP

Many ISPs that make Internet access their mainline business have grown up since the early 1990s, when they had 50 subscribers and a UNIX expert running an old Sun workstation.

A full-service ISP focuses on the larger business market, providing the consulting, high-speed access, and other services that smaller businesses and individual users don't really need. Some full-service ISPs will add Web hosting, HTML design, or related services to their offering; others exclusively on Internet access and refer value-added services, such as HTML design, to a network of specialists who know.

Because full-service ISPs target the business community, they are likely to offer higher speeds, complete service packages, and more competitive rates than a typical reseller ISP. Many new ISPs who want to jump into this business in a big way try become full-service ISPs without the experience to make a real asset in your Web site plans. The challenge is to find an experienced full-service ISP in your area. The items listed in the section "Considing the Right Factors" will help.

# WHOLESALERS

The largest of ISPs do not intend to sell service directly to small businesses, individuals, or even large businesses. That's why I call them wholesalers. Their business is selling large amounts of bandwidth to other ISPs or very busy sites. Examples of wholesalers can even include companies like Sprint, which tries to get large ISPs to connect to the Internet using a Sprint trunk line.

A wholesaler can relocate your Web server on one of its multiple T3 lines or multiple T1 lines into a regional ISP. It has the high-end technical expertise to solve almost any problem. In fact, when part of the Internet has a slowdown or crashes, it's often the people at these very large crossroads who find, diagnose, and solve and problems (OK, they occasionally cause them well).

It's unlikely that you'll work with a wholesaler unless you're creating a very large site. One reason is that you won't need bandwidth in the amounts that these ISPs like to. Another is that a wholesaler expects clients to have a great deal of technical savvy – something like the support staff of a full-service ISP would have. You're likely to want more hand-holding if this is your first venture in providing a Web server of your own.
