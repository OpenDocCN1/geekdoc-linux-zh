# LEASED LINES OR SHARED CIRCUITS

A basic distinction between connection options is determined by how to connect to your ISP, whether a provider is a small reseller with an Internet server or a larger organization such as Sprint or MCI. The two types of connections that you can have are dedicated circuit and a shared circuit.

A dedicated circuit, or leased line, is like renting a physical wire from one location to another. The wire is owned and maintained by your telephone company, but you control everything that goes on that wire. Dedicated circuits are very costly, with that cost usually based on the distance between the two ends of the connection.

You would use a dedicated circuit if you want control over the amount of bandwidth you have at your disposal. No one can place packets on your wire. You also get much tighter security on that dedicated connection, because someone has to have physical access to your specific wire in order to sniff. Leased lines for Internet connections are always digital circuits.

A shared connection is like the one you use to make a telephone call. You momentarily use a series of different wires to reach a selected end point. When your connection is finished, another call uses some of the wires to reach a different end point.

When dealing with shared connections for Internet service, note that Internet traffic (TCP/IP packets) can be mixed on the same wire from several sources without getting mixed up. This is like having several calls going over one line with everyone still to be understood. But you still can say as much as if you had only two people talking.
