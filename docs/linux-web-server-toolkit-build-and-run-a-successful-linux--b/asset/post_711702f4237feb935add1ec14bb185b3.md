### COLOCATING A WEB SERVER

There's a middle ground between having your Web site and having a Web server at your location — it's referred to *colocating* a Web server at your ISP. This option is gaining in popularity because it combines the best aspects of hosting and having your own Web server.

When you colocate a Web server, you provide a separate computer with your Web server and page information on, but place that computer at your ISP's office. You get the control, flexibility, scalability, and so forth having your own server, but can drastically reduce the costs because it doesn't require that you install a high-speed line at your office. Colocating does not provide the flexibility of using the same computer for a Web server and for other things, such as routing, gateway for Web clients, and so on, or allow you to store sensitive, non-Web information on the server (well, you could, but . . . To administer your Web server, you still use some type of Internet access for remote administration using your passwords — others on the Web can't get to those areas, of course.

You should remember, however, that while increases in bandwidth should be much cheaper for a server colocated at your ISP, physical access to the server is usually less flexible, and you have to rely to some extent on the ISP for the security parameters that your server uses. Table 7-2 compares the benefits and drawbacks of colocated and onsite Web servers.

## Connection Technologies

There are many technologies you can use to connect to your Internet Service Provider. Some of the technologies described in the sections that follow won't be available in your area; others will be recommended or discouraged by your ISP. If you've come to trust your ISP and feel your bandwidth and stability requirements are met, you may want to go with your ISP's recommendations. Following your ISP's recommendations will generally make it easy for the ISP's staff to support you, though they are not always experienced in the available options as they should be. The result can be added cost for you.

Becoming an Internet Service Provider is not very difficult. Providing top-quality, informed service is another matter. Research all you can about connection options and prices among ISPs in your area, so you'll be able to recognize the experience level at the ISP you choose. The more the staff know, the more money you'll save in the long run, and the more stable your connection will be.

If you happen upon an ISP that doesn't offer the service you think is best for you reading the sections that follow, you might consider contacting another ISP to see if the service is offered. Your first contact might be either a specialized or limited service ISP that isn't well-suited to meet your needs. More information about choosing an ISP is provided later in this chapter.
