Some of the most useful pages on the Web are sponsored by U.S. government agencies, which have huge stores of information and no profit motive to restrict what they can do with that information. The hottest site in the summer of 1997 was the Mars Lander page, which provided live pictures of the Martian landscape within four to six hours of the pictures being received at NASA. Within the first weeks of the site's launch, NASA had received more than 100 million hits and had set up mirror sites to share the load. To see this, visit http://www.nasa.com.

The Library of Congress was the first government site to start sharing information. I have been visiting the Library's online exhibits since 1994. A popular exhibit is the Treasures exhibit, which describes measures of American heritage that reside in the library, including the Declaration of Independence and some less well-known artifacts. Visit http://1cweb.loc.gov/exhibits/treasures/

The National Institutes of Health have also been using the Internet to share graphical exhibits for the public. The Visible Human Project, shown in Figure 1-6, is like an online anatomy lesson, complete with diagrams and graphics.
