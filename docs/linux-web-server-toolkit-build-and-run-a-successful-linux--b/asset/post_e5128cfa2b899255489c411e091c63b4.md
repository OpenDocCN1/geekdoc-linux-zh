## Choosing an Internet Service Provider

One of the most important decisions you'll make about your Web site is your Internet Service Provider (ISP). A top-notch ISP will make everything go smoothly; an inexperienced or difficult ISP will make even the easy steps to a challenge.

Your ISP not only provides your physical connection to the Internet, but also provide routing to your site, a Domain Name Server, and even the IP addresses you use. In addition, your ISP acts as a technical resource in many areas, particularly if you're paying for a high-speed connection like a partial T1. This consulting expertise can save you lot of money over hiring additional people and can provide valuable insight into how you can make your site run faster and smoother for everyone who visits it.

## Different Types of ISPs

After reading all the connection types in the last section, you may be frustrated to find out that there are as many types of ISPs to decide upon. The following sections discuss the major categories, based on particular business focus. Companies do not advertise themselves according to the designations I've given; you'll have to determine what the ISP offers by asking the questions provided in this section.

### SMALL BUSINESS ISP

The ease of creating an Internet server, especially using Linux, with a very small investment, very little overhead – and means anyone can set up an ISP with very little experience. In fact, you could set up a complete ISP using the CD-ROM in this book. The only additional thing you need is a bunch of modems plugged into your Web server machine. (I'm seeing another book here ...)

Unfortunately, once you choose an ISP, you will find many issues related to running an ISP business can crop up – problems that were perhaps not apparent when your ISP first hung him or her out for business. Things such as major line outages, rampant busy signals, security problems, bandwidth problems, and infrastructure and money concerns are all part of running an ISP. Many of them are not on the mind of the person who only thinks about the monetary lure of the Internet.

I would classify a small business ISP as one that has less than 100 subscribers and has been in business less than 18 months. These companies have Internet access as their mainline business, rather than other computer services, but often offer other related services, such as HTML design and consulting, for an additional charge to supplement revenues. You also identify the experience level of your ISP by asking some hard questions about getting multiple domain names started or altering your routing tables to share your Web traffic among multiple servers with different IP addresses.

Of course, a small business ISP may be the ideal way for internet-savvy users to connect from the office. By providing this service to the company, maybe you can even reduce your fees to zero. Just be certain it has enough modems to meet your needs. In turn, the small business ISP offers low prices and is relatively low maintenance.

### RESELLER ISP

Computer specialists who already resell equipment or services are also starting to become ISPs to their clients. This leverages the expertise they already have and prevents issues. Resellers such as Authorized Resellers or Managed Solutions Providers are generally more experienced than the small business ISP and can therefore provide more consulting and assistance.

### Find a Friend

A kind of ISP that you won't find advertised may be your best choice of all. Many companies have Internet connections, some of fairly speeds. Most don't use their entire bandwidth. If you have a friend in a high place at a company that has a connection, you can approach him or her about selling you part of the bandwidth. You still make a connection to that company's site, with the associated company charges and hardware. But the company is probably not trying to make a profit as an ISP, so your costs could be a lot less.

The caveats are:

- If you use too much bandwidth, you won't be very long and you'll have to start looking for an ISP again.
- You can expect some assistance from the company in setting up your connection if you're paying for it, but don't plan on regular assistance. You should develop a professional relationship with the staff but have expertise in-house.
- Don't try to get a free run unless it's your uncle's company. When others find out the company is subsidizing your access, your friend could be on the street.
