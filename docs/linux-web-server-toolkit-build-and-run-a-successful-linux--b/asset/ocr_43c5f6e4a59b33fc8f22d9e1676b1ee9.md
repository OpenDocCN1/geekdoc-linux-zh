# Immature or Offensive

The glory of the Internet is that everyone gets an equal say. Unfortunately, that includes people whose say you may not want to hear. Web sites that contain offensive language, derogatory comments about people based on religion, race, and so on, and raging accusations do nothing but embarrass and offend the reader who happens on such a site. Articulate arguments for a point of view can be effectively promoted using a Web site. The rest just sends people away.

Web sites for professional organizations are not likely to be full of offensive commentary, but when a site has many people contributing to its content, the occasional offensive comment can slip in. Personal biases or immature attacks on competitors with no factual support shouldn't appear in a professional web site. You might also want to review the legal comments that appear later in this chapter.

# Only an Advertisement

Web sites seek to offer the services and products of an organization, but sites that do nothing other fill the customer's screen with rambling demands to Buy Now! without providing supporting information, ordering details, and guarantees really miss the opportunity to resolve potential customer's concerns. A Web site should provide information about the company that is selling the products or services, provide reinforcement after a sale, and generally be customer-oriented (which increases profits) instead of profit-oriented (which loses customers).

# Examples of Bad Web Pages

Unfortunately, sites like the ones I have described are not hard to find as I wish they were. Several sites collect links to pages of questionable merit. You can visit these sites, follow the links, and see what I am describing. For starters, either Worst of the Web at http://www.worstoftheweb.com or The Useless Pages site at http://www.go2net.com/internet/useless.

Many of the sites that these lists focus on things that just seem like a waste of time to most of us, (as these are real) Banana Labels, Rejected Mottos, and Collecting Sugar Packets.

Aside from the "get a life" aspect of sites like these, you should analyze these sites for lack of visual appeal. Look for hard-to-read text and meaningless graphics. Also consider the irritation they cause because of long waits for initial downloads and of text-only versions. Look for slanderous or foul language (especially on personal home pages; it's their right, but I wouldn't recommend them). Look for misspelled words and bad grammar, and finally, for blatant sales pitches that seem to say: I'm only here so you can give me your money.
