Still, many of the better Web statistics programs do quite well at making an educated guess at the true number of visitors. This is done by collating all hits from a single hostname within an hour of each other, or counting the number of unique hostname accesses to any page with a .html extension (as not all users begin at your home page).

Knowing how many users visit your site gives you a true picture of the influence your Web site has on the overall effects of your organization. It tells you how much you can rely on your Web site to disseminate information to your users and how well your promotions about your site are reaching your target audiences.

# HOW LONG IS AN AVERAGE VISITOR SPENDING AT MY SITE?

Knowing how long visitors browse your site tells you interesting your material is and how much influence the things on your site are likely to be with users. The more time they spend on your site, the more likely they'll act on the things they see.

Unfortunately, it's basically impossible to get true information about the time a user spends looking at your Pages. The reason is simple: The Web is designed to avoid tying up a server while someone is reading a Page. Once the page is downloaded, you have no idea how long a user looks at it or how many times she returns to that page (stored locally in the browser cache directory) and reviews it without ever requesting it from your Web server.

What can be done statistically (with moderate success, mind you), is to examine the number of hits to all html pages by a unique hostname within a center block of time (say, one hour). If you assign an average time to each html file based on its size (for example, every 16K of html file is assumed to be seven minutes of reading/skimming), then you can judge the amount of time spent browsing the site by the number and size of html pages downloaded from your server during a “browsing period” that is defined by the first and last request made by a given hostname within a given period (a single “browsing session”).

This number is not terribly scientific, but taking an average of figures arrived at in this manner will easily tell you whether visitors are stopping at your home page and never going any further, or whether they're spending a while looking over most of your site, downloading pages, and reading one they find.

> Note that this exercise isn't very useful if you have only one or two pages on your site.
