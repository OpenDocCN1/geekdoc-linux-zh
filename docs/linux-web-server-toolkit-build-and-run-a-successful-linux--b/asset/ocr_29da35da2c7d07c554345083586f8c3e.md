# HOW ARE PEOPLE FINDING MY SITE?

After all the talk in Chapter 8, you might well wonder which tools are the most effective in bringing people to your site. The easiest way to determine this is by looking at the referrer field. This piece of data, sent by many browsers but not all, tells the Web server what was being viewed when a link to this site was selected. Studying the referrer field can yield insights like the following:

+   * If you see numerous mentions of Yahoo, InfoSeek, AltaVista, and WebCrawler, you know people are searching for your name or your keywords on those sites.

+   * If you see numerous cases of “no referrer,” you know that people are getting your Web site URL from noncomputer source and entering it in from scratch. Your advertising or promotion in noncomputer areas must be working.

+   * If you see numerous referrals from places you've never heard of, including people's home pages (look for URLs with a tilde (~) after the server host-name), you know people are including a link to your site because they found it fun or informative.

# WHICH PAGES ARE MOST POPULAR ON MY SITE?

Among all the things I'm sharing or promoting on my site, which are of most interest to those who visit?” This question may have been the first one you thought of. One thing that every statistics program generates a list of hits for each item that has been requested from your site. Of course, this list includes every graphic image and every script, as well as every html page. A simple way to sort this out is to use the grep command to pull out lines that mention html files.
