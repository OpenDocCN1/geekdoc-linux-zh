## 36.1. Author's Note

How did I come to write a Bash scripting book? It's a strange tale. It seems that couple of years back, I needed to learn shell scripting, and what better way to do than to read a good book on the subject. I was looking to write a tutorial and reference covering all aspects of scripting. In fact, I was looking for this very book, or something like it. Unfortunately, it didn't exist, so if I wanted it, I had to write it. And so, here, we are, folks.

This reminds me of the apocryphal story about the mad professor. Crazy as a clown, the fellow was. At sight of a book, any book, at the library, at a bookstore, anywhere, he would become totally obsessed with the idea that he could have written it, should have written it, and done a better job of it to boot. He would thereupon rush home and proceed to just that, write a book with the same time. When he did some years later, he had several books to his credit, probably putting even Asimov to the chase. The books might not have been good, who knows, but does really matter? Here's a fellow who lived his dream, even if he was driven by it, and I can't help admiring the old cool...

## 36.2. About the Author

Who is this guy anyhow?

The author claims no credentials or special qualifications, other than a compulsion to write. [64] This book is somewhat of a departure from his other major work, HOW–2 Meet Women: The Shy Man's Guide to Relationships. He has also written the Software–Building HOWTO.

A Linux user since 1995 (Shellack 2.2, kernel 1.2.1), the author has emitted a few software truffles, including the cruft one-time pad encryption utility, the makes mortgage calculator, the judge Scrabble® adjudicator, and the yaw word listing package. He got his start in programming using FORTRAN IV on a CDC 3800, which is not the least nostalgic for those days.

Living in a secluded desert community with wife and dog, he cherishes human frailty.

### 36.3.1. Hardware

A used IBM Thinkpad, model 760XL laptop (P166, 80 meg RAM) running Red Hat 7.1/7.2. Sure, its slow and has a funky keyboard, but it beats the heck out of a No. 2 pencil and a Big Chief tablet.

- i. Bram Molenaar's powerful SGML–aware vim text editor.
