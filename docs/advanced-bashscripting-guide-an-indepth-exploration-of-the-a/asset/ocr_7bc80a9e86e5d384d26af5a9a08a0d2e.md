The -m gives detailed information about the current user. Passing any two arguments to who is the equivalent of who -m, as in who ami or who The Man.

```
bash$ who -m
localhost.localdomainbozo pts/2      Apr 27 17:49
```

whoami is similar to who -m, but only lists the user name.

```
bash$ whoami
bozo
```

Show all logged on users and the processes belonging to them. This is an extended version of who. The output of who may be piped to grep to find a specific user and/or process.

| w | grep startx 4:22pm 6:41 4.47s 0.45s startx |

Show current user's login name (as found in /var/run/utmp). This is a near-equivalent to whoami, above.

```
bash$ logname
bozo
bash$ whoami
bozo
```

However...

```
bash# su
Password: . . . .
bash# whoami
root
bash# logname
bozo
```
