# Open Source Publishing Resources

DocBook isn't the only game in town. Now I happen to think that it' the best game in town, but it's not the only one. In the Open Source world is a variety of typesetting languages and other tools for typesetting that I think should know about.

Typesetting languages are much different in theory and practice from a markup language like DocBook. As mentioned before, DocBook markup is concerned with the structure and content of the document, not the physical presentation.

However, if you're a presentation maniac or you just absolutely have to control over every pixel, you will want to look into typesetting languages like Tex or LaTeX. They're not easy, and they're not WYSIWYG, but they certainly provide fine-grained control over the look and feel of your document.

I would like to point out that I'm not going to provide resources for commercial programs like Adobe Illustrator or QuarkX. They do enough marketing that I'm sure you've heard of them often if you've looked for typesetting solutions for the MacOS or Windows. However, most of the Open Source projects aren't backed by big companies with a lot of capital to publish them. It doesn't mean that the software isn't great, but it does mean that you won't be seeing dual-page ad spreads in MacUser magazine or anything like that.

I'm not necessarily recommending any of these programs or languages, you, but it never hurts to know what the alternatives are.
