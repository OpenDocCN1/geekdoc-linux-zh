To locate an NTP server, you should consult one or more sources:

+   **Your ISP** Many Internet service providers (ISPs), including business networks and universities, operate NTP servers for the benefit of their users. These servers are usually close to you in a network sense, making them good choices for NTP. You should consult your ISP or the networking department at your organization to learn if such a system is available.

+   **Your Distribution's NTP Server** Some distributions operate NTP servers for your users. If you are close to these servers in a network sense, they can be good choices; however, chances aren't the case, you may want to look elsewhere.

+   **Public NTP Server** Lists of public NTP servers are maintained at http://support.ntp.org/bin/view/Servers/Webhome. These servers can be good choices, but you'll need to locate the closest one to you in a network sense and perhaps contact the site you choose to obtain permission to use it.

+   **Public NTP Pool** The pool1.ntp.org subdomain is dedicated to servers that have volunteered to function as public NTP servers. ... you can end up using different servers each time you launch NTP. Thus, using the public NTP server pool can be a bit of a gamble, but the results are usually good enough for casual users or if you don't want to spend time checking and maintaining your NTP configuration. To configure your NTP server to use either the pool1.ntp.org subdomain name or a numbered host within that domain, such as 0.pool1.ntp.org. You can narrow the list geographically by adding a geographic name to the domain name, as in north-america.pool1.ntp.org for servers located in North America. Consult http://support.ntp.org/bin/view/Servers/NTpoolServers for details.

> The closest server in a network may not be the closest in a geographic sense. For instance, a national ISP may route all traffic through just one or two hubs. The result can be that traffic from, Atlanta, Georgia, to Tampa, Florida, may through Chicago, Illinois. Such a detour is likely to increase round-trip time and decrease the accuracy of NTP. In such a situation, a user in Atlanta may be better off using a Chicago NTP server than one in Tampa, even though Tampa is closer geographically.

Once you've located a few possible servers, try using to determine the round-trip time for packets to send them. If any systems have very high ping times, you may want to remove them from consideration.
