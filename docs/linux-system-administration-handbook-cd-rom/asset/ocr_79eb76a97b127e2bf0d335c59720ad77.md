Anyway, CERT's purpose in life is to keep administrators aware of potential security holes in the OS or the software it runs. These security alerts often tell the type of security hole, how it can be exploited, what versions are affected, and (if available) locations of patches or ways to prevent anyone from taking advantage of these holes. comp.linux.announce group posts every now and then from CERT related to Linux and programs it runs. Strang enough, some of the security holes they have are not compiled, but with the way a particular program was compiled, making the hole not a Linux problem at all, but a fault of whoever compiled that program.

If you have an intrusion investigated, you can send an intrusion report to CERT and maybe they can help you track down the person responsible. Their Web site is http://www.cert.org/. It is probably best to give their site a visit before your site gets broken into so you can stay up-to-date with their advisors.

# 8.3 CIAC

The Computer Incident Advisory Capability (CIAC) is run by the U.S. Department of Energy as something similar to CERT, but it primarily provides services for the U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) and its contractors. However, it does have a number of bulletins and advisory notes that may not be at CERT. It is also worthwhile to take a look at their site to keep up-to-date with security issues.
