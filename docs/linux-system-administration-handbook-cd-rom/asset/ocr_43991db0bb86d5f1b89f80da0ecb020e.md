Sather's roots are in Eiffel. Early beta versions of it were nearly subsets of Eiffel 2.0; however, a major release later, both languages have gone their ways. Sather was and is developed at the University of California, Berkeley, and as you might expect a language developed at an educational institution, it is freeware.

Sather has garbage collection, multiple inheritance, is strongly typed, and its code can be compiled into C code. Its public license is very liberal to encourage users to contribute to its library.

# # 14.10 Summary

We literally just skimmed the surface of the scores (at least) of programming languages that are “officially” available and can be compiled for, or easily ported to Linux. Certainly, we've tried to cover the most popular languages and those that come with most Linux distributions. More likely than not, one or more of these languages will meet your needs as a programmer. If not, a search on your favorite Web search engine will turn up collections of links to programming languages for you to try out.
