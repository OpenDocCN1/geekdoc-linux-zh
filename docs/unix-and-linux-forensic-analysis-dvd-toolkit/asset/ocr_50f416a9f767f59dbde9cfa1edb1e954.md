# History

2007, I completed my Master's Degree in Information Security from Capella University. As an Incident Response Analyst by trade, I figured that writing my thesis on UNIX forensic analysis would be a good topic, relevant to my job duties and my course work. With Harlan Carvey being a colleague of mine, you would think I would just write something on Windows forensics and ask him for help. However, this was my thesis, and I wanted to do something that would challenge me, so I chose to write my paper on UNIX forensic analysis.

After about a day of research, I found that my original scope would have to be narrowed drastically. This was due to the vastness of the sheer concept of UNIX forensics, and the fact that there were nothing on it (at least I could find) anywhere. I did find some really good articles and white papers by Barry Grundy,¹ Mariusz Burd,² and Holt Sorenson,³ I also found that there were some chapters in books like “Incident Response: Investigating Computer Crime”“Hacking Exposed: Computer Crime: Forensics” by Davis, Philipp, and Cowen, and “Digital Evidence and Computer Crime: Forensic Science, Computers, and the Internet,” by Casey, but nothing wholly dedicated to UNIX.

At the time I wrote my thesis, I had no idea how many UNIX variants existed. I know personally I have worked with, Solaris, AIX, HP-UX, BSD, Tru64, and several versions of Linux including Ubuntu, Fedora Core, Red Hat, Gentoo, SUSE, and Knopp. Now, writing a book that would include all of these variants and all of the possible architecture and command structure is simply not feasible so I picked one, and stuck with it. This book specifically covers the Linux 2.6.22-14 kernel, and all of our examples are either made using Ubuntu 7.10 Gubby, or Fedora Core 8. However, if you have been around UNIX in any form for any length of time, you can use either the exact command structure we use in this book, or make slight variations.

At the completion of my thesis, I thought long and hard about the knowledge gap existed in the world of UNIX forensics.
