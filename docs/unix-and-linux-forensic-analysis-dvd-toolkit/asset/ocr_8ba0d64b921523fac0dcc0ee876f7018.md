Chapter 6 • The /Proc File System

# sysfs

I felt this chapter would be incomplete without a quick look at the sysfs file system introduced in the 2.6 kernel. Earlier in the chapter, I mentioned that the original purpose of /proc was to provide a meaningful interface to about processes, and over the years more and more non-process data had its under the /proc hierarchy. Sysfs aims to move this non-process data out, into a separate virtual system mounted on /sys. Let's take a quick look at some of the relevant data we can find in sysfs.

The two subdirectories under /sys most likely to be of relevance to an incident response investigation are “modules” and “block.”


/sys/modules contains a subdirectory for every module in the running kernel. This list should be more extensive than the output of the “lsmod” command, since entries are populated for modules that are statically attached into the kernel, or your dynamically loaded kernel module. This can give you a better idea about the capabilities of the kernel you are working with, particularly if it is a custom or otherwise unfamiliar kernel.
