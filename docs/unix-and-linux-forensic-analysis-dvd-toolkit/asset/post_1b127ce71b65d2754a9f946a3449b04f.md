The “exe” entry for this PID tells that the file in question is not hidden, it's deleted! If you weren't already aware, on UNIX systems you can delete a file that's being used by a running process with no consequence. Even though file is not listed in /tmp, can still be recovered thanks to the symbolic link, as long as the process remains running. If Adam had shut the box down and taken a forensic image immediately, he may had a much harder time to get to this point, alone where he wanted to go.

```
[root@localhost 8912]# cat exe > /tmp/mystery-binary
```

Adam was able to dump the executable from the process memory. Now he can do some quick and dirty analysis on the file to see what its purpose is.

# sysfs

I felt this chapter would be incomplete without a quick look at sysfs introduced in the 2.6 kernel. Earlier in the chapter, I mentioned that the original purpose of /proc was to provide a meaningful interface about processes, and over the years more and more non-process data had its under /sys. The two subdirectories under /sys most likely to be relevant to an incident investigation are “modules” and “block.”
