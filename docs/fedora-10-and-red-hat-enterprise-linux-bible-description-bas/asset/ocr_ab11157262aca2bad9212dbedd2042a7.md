# Monitoring CPU usage with top

Start the top utility in a Terminal, and it displays the CPU consuming processes on your computer. Every five seconds, top will determine which processes are consuming the most CPU time and display them in descending order on your screen.

By adding the -s option to top, you can have the display show the cumulative CPU time that the process, as well as child processes that may already have exited, spent. If you want to change how often the screen is updated, you can add the -d secs where secs is replaced by the number of seconds between updates.

By default, processes are sorted by CPU usage. You can sort processes numerically by PID (press N), by age (press A), by resident memory usage (press M), by time (press T), or back to CPU usage (press P). # Monitoring power usage on laptop computers

To effectively use a laptop, you need to be able to monitor and manage your laptop’s power. Using tools provided in Fedora or RHEL, you can configure your laptop to:

+   - The battery is low.
- Notify you when the battery is low.
- Notify you when the laptop is plugged in.
- Show when the laptop is plugged.
- Suspend the current session.

Fedora and RHEL offer two facilities that do power management: APM and ACPI.

+   - Advanced Power Management (APM) — APM can be used to monitor the battery of your notebook and notify user-level programs to tell you your battery is low. It can also be used to put your laptop into sleep mode.
