As audio recording, if you are starting from scratch, there is an open source codec called Theora (http://www.theora.org) that you can use without paying any royalties, as of this writing. Provided you own the content you are recording, which you freely distribute that content and allow others to play it. (See the sidebar "Converting Video to Theora" for details.)

When comes to using video codecs (other than the free Theora), Red Hat, Inc. has taken the cautious approach. While Fedora and RHEL include video players such as Totem (described later in this chapter), they do not include players (such as the MPlayer and Xine media players) that are packaged with convenient codecs. If you want to make commercial movies, post popular video clips, or other video content in a Red Hat system, you have to get those codecs elsewhere.

This section describes some of the issues surrounding and playing video in Linux. It also describes video players that can be used with Fedora and RHEL, as well as those you can obtain to play a wide variety of video content.

# Examining laws affecting video and Linux

I need to start reminding everyone that I am not a lawyer, so you need to take responsibility regarding any software you put on your computers. However, there are several themes that have arisen in regard to playing video content with open source software in Linux:

+   Licensing fees for patented codecs may be required. While many video codecs are covered by patents, some patent holders don't change for personal use. However, you should check current variety of companies who own patents on codecs you plan to use, as the terms of use are constantly changing. For example, this statement that was posted on the DivX Web site is no longer: Personal use of DivX video is free. Commercial requires that you obtain a commercial use license from DivXNetworks.. (Refer to the DivX Web site http://www.divx.com for information on DivX)

Because MPEG-2 and MPEG-4 video formats are covered by patents, groups of patent holders joined together to create licensing fees for related encoders and decoders. These are not sponsored by standards organizations that spearheaded the creation of those formats and may cover every patent holder related to the software you are paying for. See the MPEG Industry Forum for details on MPEG patent issues (http://www.m4if.org/patents).

Remember that there are now ways to purchase codecs for many popular audio/video formats for use with Linux. See the section "Extending Freedom to Codecs" for information on purchasing codecs from Fluendo.
