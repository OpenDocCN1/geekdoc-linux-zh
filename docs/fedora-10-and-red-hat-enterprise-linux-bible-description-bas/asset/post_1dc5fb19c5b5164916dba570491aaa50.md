Typically, an ISP requires you to sign up for a business account if you want to do any kind of business on your Internet service. Here are some excerpts from agreements for several ISPs:

Internet access is the volume business for most ISPs, and they consider business accounts for those who want to manage their own presence as premium services. Although you can technically use a personal account to set up a server (TCP/IP doesn’t care what goes across the wire), the ISP’s Terms of Service or Acceptable Policy may not permit you to do so. Although your DSL can easily handle a handful of hits a day on your Web server, offering that service can result in the termination of your account by the ISP. Check what the ISP considers acceptable before you use any Internet account to connect a public server.

## Getting static IP addresses

Most dial and DSL-access accounts use dynamic IP addresses, whenever you connect to your Internet service, the provider assigns the IP address to that connection. After you disconnect, the ISP can reclaim that IP address to assign to someone else. The next time you connect, it is possible that you get a different IP address.

For a server to have constant, reliable presence on the Internet, it will typically have one or more static IP addresses. Most ISPs charge an additional fee for static IP addresses. Each static IP address costs between $3 and $20 per month.

NOTE: You can assign a public DNS hostname without a static IP address using Dynamic DNS. Dynamic DNS involves installing software on your server that monitors your IP address and notifies the Dynamic DNS provider when its address changes.

The number of static IP addresses that you need varies, depending on how you configure your servers. Most likely, you want at least two static IP addresses, one for each of two DNS servers (if you’re configuring DNS). In a small organization, those same servers may also offer your Web server, mail server, and other services as well. In general, you want one static IP address for each computer that you may publicly accessible to the Internet.

## Choosing a connection speed

Another difficulty with keeping your servers in your home or business is choosing how fast an Internet connection you need. Most data centers at ISPs offer more than enough bandwidth for your needs, high-speed Internet connections may not be available to your location or may be prohibitively expensive.

Although it's technically possible, using a dial connection (up to 56 Kbps) to support an Internet server is generally unacceptable. Always-on connections that you may want to consider the following:

- **Digital Subscriber Line (DSL)** — DSL service is widely available these days. Using the standard telephone wire in your home, you can have an always-on Internet connection and telephone service on the same wires. Speed of 256 Kbps and 7 Mbps are available with DSL service (although the actual speeds that you attain are typically slower than those in the advertisements)

- **Integrated Digital Network (ISDN)** — ISDN has around longer than DSL to offer high-speed network services, and it's comparable to DSL in the speeds that it can offer. ISDN is typically more expensive to implement than DSL with comparable speeds.

- **Cable Internet** — Often, cable television providers offer Internet access as well. Some Cable services offer higher download speeds that you can get with DSL, but may only limit upload speeds (which may restrict its value for use with a server). For example, a major cable television provider currently offers a basic 3 Mbps download speed, but only 256 Kbps upload for for standard service. To use your cable television to run your Internet server, find out about the business Internet it offers.

- **Frame relay** — This is a packet-switching protocol that runs across wide-area networks. Although it can prove much more expensive than DSL or ISDN, it can achieve higher rates of speeds. Speeds can range from 56 Kbps to 1.544 Mbps, or as much as 45 Mbps. Frame relay can operate over a variety of network media. A primary advantage is that it uses virtual circuits that offer a faster rate of speed, because the circuit between you and the ISP is not shared with other ISP customers.

If your business can afford it, you might want T-1 or T-3 lines for your business. A T-1 connection can operate at data rates of 1.544 Mbps. A T-3 line can support rates of 44.736 Mbps. Check with your local ISPs for available connection types and pricing.

## Getting a domain name

Domain names are available from dozens of different domain registrars these days. You can check the availability of domain names from any of these registrar's Web sites, such as GoDaddy, Network Solutions, or others.

At one point, Network Solutions was the only company from which you could get a domain name in the most popular U.S. domains (.com, .net, and .org). Now, there are new TLDs and dozens of registrars from which you can select a domain name.

You can have your ISP register your domain name for you, or you can go to one of the domain registrars yourself and register online to get your domain name.
