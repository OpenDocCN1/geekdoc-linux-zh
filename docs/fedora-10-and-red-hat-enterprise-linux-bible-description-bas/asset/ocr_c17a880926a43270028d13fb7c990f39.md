+   5. Use workspaces — Click different panels in the Workspace Switcher (bottom panel, right side). Open an application, and then click another workspace panel. Workspaces are a great way to have multiple windows and keep your desktop decluttered. Notice that there are tiny representations of each window you open on the panel it is in. Drag and drop the tiny windows among the Workspace Switcher to move applications to different workspaces, without leaving your workspace.

+   6. Add tool to your panel — Right-click an empty place in the panel so that a panel appears. It would say Add Panel at the top. Because real estate is limited on your panel, I recommend adding a drawer, to which you can add little applications that run in the panel and icons that launch other applications. To begin, click Add to Panel, and then from the panel that appears, Select Drawer. A Drawer icon appears on your panel (you can drag it where you want it to go). Click to the drawer, and then right-click the drawer and click Add to Drawer. Here are a few things I suggest adding to your panel:

+   Terminal — From the drawer menu, click Add to Drawer → Application Launcher, and then click Floor. Next, select the down arrow next to System Tools, choose Terminal and click Add. Now, when I ask you to type into a Terminal window, you can launch one from the terminal icon that appears in this drawer.

NOTE: Throughout this book, I give examples that require you to use a Terminal window. Neither the new KDE nor GNOME desktops have a Terminal launcher on the panel or desktop. I strongly suggest that you add a Terminal window to your desktop or panel in order to launch it easily. The alternative is to select Applications → System Tools → Terminal to open a Terminal window.
