# Part III: Administering Fedora and RHEL

A smurfing attack uses a malformed ICMP request to query your computer in network traffic. The attacker does this by bouncing a ping request off an unwitting third party in such a way the reply is duplicated dozens or hundreds of times. An organization with fast Internet connection and a large number of computers is used as the relay. The destination address of the ping is set to entire subnet of a single host. The return address is forged to be your machine's address instead of the actual sender. When the ICMP packet arrives at the relay's network, every host on that subnet replies to the ping! Furthermore, they reply to your computer instead of the actual sender. If the relay's network has hundreds of computers, your Internet connection can be quickly flooded.

The best fix is to contact the organization using a relay and inform them of the abuse. Usually, they only need to reconfigure their Router to stop any future attacks. If the organization is uncooperative, you can minimize the effect of the attack by blocking the ICMP protocol on your router. This will at least keep the traffic off your internal network. If you can convince your ISP to block ICMP packets aimed at your network, it will help even more.

# Protecting against distributed DOS attacks

DDOS attacks are much harder to initiate and nearly impossible to stop. A DDoS attack begins with the penetration of hundreds or thousands of weakly secured machines. These machines can then be directed to attack a single host based on the whims of the attacker.

With the advent of DSL and cable modem, millions of people are enjoying Internet access with virtually no speed restrictions. In their rush to get online, many of people neglect to implement even the most basic security. Because the vast majority of these people run Microsoft operating systems, they tend to get hit with worms and viruses run quickly. After the machine has been infiltrated, often the worm or virus installs a program on the victim's machine that instructs it to call home and it is now ready to do the master's bidding.

At the whim of the infected machines can now be used to focus a concentrated stream of garbage data at a selected host. In concert with thousands of other infected machines, a scriptkiddie(often a youngster, who doesn't have the knowledge to create worms or viruses, but has the inclination and small bit of skill needed to find and launch them) has the power to take down nearly any site on the Internet.

Detecting a DDoS is similar to detecting a DOS attack. One or more of the following are likely to be present:

+   - Saturated data link
- No reduction in link saturation during off-peak hours
- Hundreds or even thousands of simultaneous network connections
- Extremely slow system performance
