# Choosing a connection speed

Another difficulty with keeping your servers in your home or business is choosing how fast an Internet connection you need. Most data centers at ISPs offer more than enough bandwidth for your needs, high-speed Internet connections may not be available to your location or may be prohibitively expensive.

Although it's technically possible, using a dial connection (up to 56 Kbps) to support an Internet server is generally unacceptable. Always-on connections that you may want to consider the following:

+   - **Digital Subscriber Line (DSL)** — DSL service is widely available these days. Using the standard telephone wire in your home, you can have an always-on Internet connection and telephone service on the same wires. Spe of 256 Kbps and 7 Mbps are available with DSL service (although the actual speeds that you attain are typically slower than those in the advertisements)

+   - **Integrated Digital Network (ISDN)** — ISDN has around longer than DSL to offer high-speed network services, and it's comparable to DSL in the speeds that it can offer. ISDN is typically more expensive to implement than DSL with comparable speeds.

+   - **Cable Internet** — Often, cable television providers offer Internet access as well. Some Cable services offer higher download speeds that you can get with DSL, but may only limit upload speeds (which may restrict its value for use with a server). For example, a major cable television provider currently offers a basic 3 Mbps download speed, but only 256 Kbps upload for for standard service. To use your cable television to run your Internet server, find out about the business Internet it offers.

+   - **Frame relay** — This is a packet-switching protocol that runs across wide-area networks. Although it can prove much more expensive than DSL or ISDN, it can achieve higher rates of speeds. Speeds can range from 56 Kbps to 1.544 Mbps, or as much as 45 Mbps. Frame relay can operate over a variety of network media. A primary advantage is that it uses virtual circuits that offer a faster rate of speed, because the circuit between you and the ISP is not shared with other ISP customers.

If your business can afford it, you might want T-1 or T-3 lines for your business. A T-1 connection can operate at data rates of 1.544 Mbps. A T-3 line can support rates of 44.736 Mbps. Check with your local ISPs for available connection types and pricing.
