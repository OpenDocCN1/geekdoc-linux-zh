+   % nl testme

You can accomplish the same thing by specifying nl -bt testme. Try this to verify
that system gives the same results.

3. It's time to use one of the new two-letter command options to number the lines,
including the blank lines:

+   % -ba testme
1 Global.Software News/ history.usen.Z
2 Interactive.Unix Src/ testme
3 Mail/ bin/

+   4 Global.Software News/ history.usen.Z
5 Interactive.Unix Src/ testme
6 Mail/ bin/

4. If you align the contents of my testme file, you can see that two lines contain
t word history. To have nl number just those lines, try the -bp pattern-matching
option:

+   % -bphistory testme
1 Global.Software News/ history.usen.Z
2 Interactive.Unix Src/ testme
3 Mail/ bin/

+   2 Global.Software News/ history.usen.Z
2 Interactive.Unix Src/ testme
3 Mail/ bin/

Notice that the two lines have caused the rest of the lines to fall out of
alignment on the display.

5. This is when the -s, or separator, option comes in handy:

+   % -bphistory -s: testme
1Global.Software News/ history.usen.Z
2 Interactive.Unix Src/ testme
3 Mail/ bin/

+   2Global.Software News/ history.usen.Z
2 Interactive.Unix Src/ testme
3 Mail/ bin/
