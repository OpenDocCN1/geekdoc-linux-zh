The good news is that each line has an associated number of fields, known as the NF variable. Without used a dollar sign, it indicates how many fields are on a line; used with a dollar sign, it's always the value of the last field on the line:

I'm interested in the last field in the /etc/passwd, the best approach for the preceding command would be: awk "{print NF}" explicitly:

```
% grep taylor /etc/passwd | awk -F "{print NF}" | sort | uniq -c
3365 /bin/bash
1 /bin/false
84 /bin/sh
21 /bin/sh
11 /usr/local/bin/ksh
353 /usr/local/bin/tsh
45 /usr/local/lib/mail
```

6. Similar to NF is NR, which keeps track of the number of records (or lines) displayed.
Here's a quick way to number a file: "$0"

```
% ls -l | awk "%$0"

2: drwx- 2 taylor 512 Nov 21 10:30 Archives/
3: drwx- 2 taylor 512 Nov 16 16:55 Infolorld/
4: drwx- 2 taylor 1024 Nov 27 18:02 Mail/

8: -rw-rw-1 taylor 12556 Nov 16 09:49 keyline.pie
```

Here you can see that the zero field of a line is the entire line. This can be useful,
