+   1. The simplest of the commands is the users command, which lists the account names of all people using the system:
% users
david mark taylor
%

+   2. In this example, david and mark are also logged in to the system with me. Try this on your computer and see what other users—if any—are logged in to your computer system.

+   3. A command that you've encountered earlier in this hour can be used to find out who is logged on the system, what line they're on, and how long they've been logged. That command is:
% who
taylor tty0 Oct 8 14:10 (limbo)
david tty2 Oct 4 09:08 (calliope)
mark tty4 Oct 8 12:09 (dent)
%

Here, you can see that three people are logged in, taylor (me), david, and mark. Further, you can see that david is logged in connection ttypp2 and has been connected since October 4 at 9:08 a.m. He is connected from a system calliope. You can see that mark has been connected since just after noon on October 8 on line tty4 and is coming from a computer called dent. Note that I have been logged in in 14:10, which is 24-hour time for 2:10 p.m. UNIX doesn't always indicate a.m. or p.m.
