# Task 2.2: Changing Passwords with passwd

Having logged in to a UNIX system, you can clearly see that there are differences between UNIX and a PA or Macintosh computer. Certainly the style of interaction is different. With UNIX, the keyboard becomes the exclusive method of instructing computer what to do, and the mouse sits idle, waiting for something to happen.

The greatest differences is that UNIX is a multiuser system, as you learned in the previous hour. As you learn more about UNIX, you'll find that this characteristic has an impact on a variety of tasks and commands. The next UNIX command you learn is one that exists because of the multiuser nature of UNIX: passwd.

With the passwd command, you can change the password associated with your individual account name. With the personal identification number (PIN) for your automated-teller machine, the value of password is directly related to how secret it remains.

> UNIX is careful about the whole process of changing passwords. It requires you to enter your current password to prove you're really you. Imagine that you are at a computer center and have the room to make a quick phone call. Without effort, a prankster could learn and quickly change your password to something you wouldn't know. That's why you should log out if you're not going to be near your system, and that's also why passwords are never echoed in UNIX.
