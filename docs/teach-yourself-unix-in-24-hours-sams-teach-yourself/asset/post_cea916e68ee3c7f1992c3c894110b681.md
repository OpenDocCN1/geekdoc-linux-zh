# Task 20.5: The Smarter Electronic Mail Alternative

Just as line editors pale to editor as vi, Berkeley Mail compared to Elm Mail System, or elm. Although it's not available on all systems, the Elm Mail System is widely distributed, and if you don't have it on your system, your system should be able to help out. The basic premise of elm is that the user should be able to focus on the message, not the medium. Emphasis is placed on showing human information. The best way to show how it works is to go straight into it! I'm just a bit biased about elm because I am the author of the program. The widespread acceptance of elm as the design, however, suggests I'm not alone in having sought a friendlier alternative. Another mail with a very similar interface is Pine. If you have access to Elm and Pine, however, I recommend you pick Elm because it lets you work with mail in a much more efficient manner.

- 1. To start up the Mail System, enter elm:
% elm

```
Mailbox is "usr/spool/mail/taylor" with 15 messages
```

- u delete

- f forward

- j move ( "usr/spool/mail/taylor" with 15 messages)

- ? help

The message is indicated by the arrow (or, on some screens, the entire message appears in inverse video). Whenever possible, elm shows the name of the person who sent the message (for example, Mickey Harris rather than Harris as in Berkeley Mail), indicates the number of lines in the message (in parentheses), and shows the subject of the message. The last few lines on the screen indicate the options available at this point. That j and k move the cursor up down the list, just as they move up and down lines in vi.
