## Did Someone Say “Multiple Desktops”?

So, you think running multiple programs is handy? Why not try running multiple desktops! Corel Linux allows you to run up to eight desktops at once, each with its own program windows.

Want to play Asteroids at work when your boss isn't looking? Click the `2` button on the panel to play Asteroids in Desktop 2. When you hear footsteps, click the `1` button to jump back to your work desktop. That's it!

Even when you're not playing games, multiple desktops are a convenient way to reduce clutter and organize tasks in progress. You can use Desktop 1 to display full-screen the report you're writing, Desktop 2 to run your graphics program, Desktop 3 to browse the Internet, and Desktop 4 to run a Web server. The possibilities are endless, limited only by you.

I know, you can't keep a single Windows desktop from crashing, so it's hard to believe Linux can handle multiple desktops gracefully. You'll just have to get used to it! Desktops come in pairs, you can have two, four, six, or eight. Chapter 9 covers how to change this setting and other desktop settings.

## Sticky windows

Now here's a cute trick:

1. On Desktop 1, open the poker game and play with it a bit.
2. Click the pushpin at the upper left to “stick” the poker game on all your desktops.
3. Click the `4` button on the panel to switch to Desktop 4 and then play a few more hands.
4. Go to Desktop 3 and click the pushpin again. This “unsticks” the poker game, so it appears only in Desktop 3.
5. Try this on a Windows machine (just kiddin').

Note that the result (aside from losing $50) was that you moved the poker game from Desktop 1 to Desktop 3. Another way to move a window is to right-click its title bar, click the “Move to Desktop” option, and then select another desktop, as shown in Figure 5-4.

## Desktop Manager tricks

I saved the best desktop tricks for last. Still playing poker on Desktop 3? Well, to the left of the desktop buttons on the panel, find that little button with an arrow. Click it to pop up the graphical Desktop Manager. Then, as shown in Figure 5-5, drag the thumbnail of the poker game in Desktop 3 at the upper right to Desktop 2 at the lower left. Too cool, or whatever superlative you prefer!

![](img/5-5.png)

The Desktop Manager gives you a nice visual display of what's on all your desktops. You can switch desktops by clicking with the mouse in this interface. Again, don't try this on Windows.

## Your Keys to Success

For those of us who hate to memorize keystrokes and birthdays, the graphical interface is like a goddess. Like a kid in a candy store or a tourist in a foreign land, we just point (and click) to get what we want.

Nonetheless, most graphical operations have keystroke equivalents that you may want to use for the following reasons:

* You hate using a mouse or other pointing device.
* Your speed demon hands can't stand the constant interruptions of mousing around.
